15The billboards on the I-10

The short versionEvery spring, artists and labels rent billboards on the interstate between Los Angeles and Indio, because there is one road in and the people driving it are exactly the people they want. The boards cost from about $8K to $40K for a few weeks, and one 2026 campaign reportedly ran to $1M. This is a sourced, year-by-year archive of them, from the first ones on record in 2013 to the 20 that were booked for 2020 and never went up.

In April 2022, a few days before the festival that had dropped him as a headliner, Travis Scott's label put up a sequence of billboards along the I-10. The first said "Psst..." The next said "Looking for UTOPIA?" The third said "Wrong way!"570 Scott had been removed from the lineup after Astroworld. The boards were his way onto the road anyway.570

That is the whole idea in three signs. The festival has a lineup. The highway has a second one.

Why the desert

Coachella's own driving directions funnel almost everyone onto the I-10 into the valley.571 Gino Sesto, whose agency DASH TWO has sold Coachella boards since about 2015, put it simply to Billboard:

"It's kind of a perfect storm... you have one way in."572

Sam Keywanfar, whose company MilkMoney supplied billboard content for 26 advertisers that year, said the festivalgoer is "a very hard-to-reach demographic," and the drive is the chance to catch them "in the early part of the journey."572 Ian Dallimore of Lamar, one of the big billboard owners on the route, said artists get to advertise "on the road leading up to" the festival without advertising at it, and called a 14-by-48-foot roadside board "the biggest flex."572

The real target is the phone. Keywanfar again:

"When fans find a billboard they are drawn to and share it, that's the holy grail."572

The same Billboard piece describes artists stopping on the highway to take selfies with their own boards, and non-music brands, from Florence by Mills to the Elton John film Rocketman, following them onto the route the next year.572

Who owns them, who books them

Two owners come up again and again on this stretch. Outfront's own blog describes the I-10 run as "lined on either side with OUTFRONT billboards."573 Billboard calls Lamar "one of the top billboard operators on this stretch."572 Lamar also says it has had a Palm Springs-area operation since 2002; that is the company's own claim.574

The artists and labels mostly don't call them directly. They go through agencies and marketplaces: DASH TWO, a Los Angeles out-of-home agency; MilkMoney, which makes the content; and AdQuick, an online billboard marketplace, which says the inventory "is fragmented across multiple outdoor media owners" with no public map.572575

Where they are

The boards cluster where the road narrows. Billboard describes about 50 miles of I-10 from Riverside to Indio, and Sesto estimated about 75 boards in the 23 miles from Banning to Palm Springs.572 OOH Today, using StreetMetrics data, counted more than 50 boards between Beaumont and Indio through the San Gorgonio Pass, and measured about 30% more impressions on them during each festival weekend of 2025.580

DASH TWO's own map of the route, which is a sales pitch and should be read as one, starts with the Banning Pass and Cabazon as the first boards drivers from LA and Orange County see, then the split where Highway 111 peels off toward Palm Springs, then the final mile near the polo club.578 The wind turbines are a landmark in their own right; in 2026 Slayyyter staged a bunny-suit stunt there.575

There is also a different kind of board off the interstate. The art biennial Desert X has put billboard works in the valley, starting in 2017 with a work by Jennifer Bolande on the road into Palm Springs.535 The season chapter covers those. One of them sat right in this landscape: in 2019 one of Cara Romero's billboards stood in front of the Palm Springs wind turbines.543

The landmarks between the boards

The boards aren't the only things on the road built to be seen from a car. A lot of people driving the pass every day see the same ones: the Riverside County Transportation Commission puts the traffic through the San Gorgonio Pass at more than 160,000 people a day.383

The dinosaurs. Claude Bell bought land at Cabazon in 1946 for $5,000. The state leveled the hilly lot for free, in exchange for its gravel, which went into an overpass on the new freeway.581 Bell, a sculptor who ran the portrait studio at Knott's Berry Farm for 35 years, started his brontosaurus, Dinny, in 1964. It was built from steel and cement the state had abandoned after a flash flood buried them, girders from a defunct drive-in in Banning, and 1,200 bags of cement. It is 150 feet long, and by Bell's count it took 11 years and $250,000. It had glowing red eyes at night, and it cost 50 cents to go inside.581 He got the idea at about age 10, from Lucy, the elephant-shaped building in Atlantic City.581

The tyrannosaurus, Mr. Rex, started rising in 1981: 65 feet tall, with a staircase to a lookout in its mouth 55 feet up and a channel in the tail meant for a slide that Bell worried was too steep.581 Bell died in 1988 at 91. His obituary listed Dinny at 150 tons and Rex at 100, and noted they were in Pee-wee's Big Adventure.582

Then the dinosaurs changed owners and, for a while, beliefs. Wikipedia's history says Gary Kanter, an Orange County developer, had bought the property by 1995.583 By 2005 the gift shop sold toy dinosaurs labeled "Don't swallow it! The fossil record does not support evolution."584 Kanter told the Los Angeles Times he had first looked up at Dinny's belly and thought: "He's like a movable Golden Gate bridge."584 Visitors have reported the creationist material gone since about 2017, and the Kanters still ran the site as of 2021, by Wikipedia's account. After Paul Reubens died in 2023, Mr. Rex was repainted in Pee-wee's grey suit and red bow tie.583

The outlets. Just east, off the Malki Road exit on Seminole Drive next to Morongo Casino, is Desert Hills Premium Outlets.585 It has been there since 1990 and expanded in 2000, 2008 and 2014, according to a 2025 Patch item.586 The 2014 round added 50 stores and a 1,100-space parking garage, for 650,000 square feet and 180 stores in all.585 KESQ put that expansion at more than $100 million.587

During festival weeks the brands come to the exits instead of the malls coming to the festival. In 2026 Maruchan set up "MaruMart," a convenience-store pop-up at Cabazon Outlets, also on Seminole Drive, from April 9 to 13 and 16 to 20, free to enter.588 Modern Retail reported it was built in a shipping container, with an Airstream trailer for the ramen, and advertised with boards around the exit. Maruchan's marketing director, Katelyn Stokes:

"You can see the pop-up from the highway, and it grounds people with a landmark that's easy to find."589

Her other line explains the whole genre: "Since we're not in the festival, this is a way for the brand to still be present."589 Pacsun paired a black billboard on the freeway with a roadside stand on Highway 111, and Bloom Nutrition wrapped a 7-Eleven near the Palm Springs airport in pink for the Thursday and Friday of arrival.589

The turbines. The San Gorgonio Pass sits between Mount San Gorgonio, at 11,502 feet, and Mount San Jacinto, at 10,786.581 It is the gap where a Mediterranean climate to the west meets the desert to the east, which, in Wikipedia's summary, makes it one of the most consistently windy places in the country.590 The first try came in 1926, when Dew Oliver built a turbine near Whitewater with a generator salvaged from a Seal Beach roller coaster; the wind burned it out.590 Southern California Edison tested two machines near its Devers substation from 1980, and one, an Alcoa vertical-axis turbine, destroyed itself two weeks after installation, on the eve of the first American Wind Energy Conference in Palm Springs.590 By 1987, 14 operators had put up more than 4,200 turbines. Since then old ones have been swapped for fewer, bigger ones, and the federal turbine database counted 667 in May 2025.590

The shovel. The last landmark of 2026 was about 2 miles short of the polo grounds. On the festival's first morning, April 10, Tom Cruise posted a photo on X of a giant shovel, with coordinates and the caption "In DIGGER We Trust."591 It promoted Digger, Alejandro G. Iñárritu's black comedy starring Cruise, his first non-franchise film since 2017; Warner Bros.' teaser had shown him swinging the same shovel.591 AceShowbiz put it at 50 feet and 24,000 pounds, at John Nobles Avenue and Monroe Street.592 The signs beside it said the shovel "can get extremely hot," and that anyone entering the exhibit agreed to be filmed for Warner marketing from April 10 to 26.593 The film opened on October 2 to about $8 million, Cruise's lowest opening since Lions for Lambs in 2007.594

How it started

No one has documented a "first" Coachella billboard, but a label got there by 2013. That April, Republic Records put up eight boards on the I-10 into Indio: one with its logo, five for its acts on the bill, including James Blake and Ben Howard, and two for Black Sabbath and Kid Cudi, who had albums coming and no set.595 The label had tried it the year before. Its marketing chief, Jim Roppo, said:

"We had done a few last year - I think we did three."595

Roppo was also selling the boards to the industry. The festival was where managers and film people would be, and Lamar, the owner, told him the boards got "something like 2 million impressions" in the weeks outside the festival.595 Two Republic acts on the bill got no board, because they had no album coming. "I hope they're not mad," he said. He was already booking for the next year, and he laughed when he said why:

"Coachella has locked in through 2030, and we're doing the same for the billboards."595

That puts the first label boards on the road in 2012. The earliest band photo found for this archive is in a DASH TWO blog post from January 2015, which shows the band Miami Horror "standing proudly in front of their billboard from last year," meaning 2014.596 The same post says only "a handful of billboards" were left for the 2015 festival.596 That is an agency talking about its own work, so it carries a low confidence label below.

Billboard's version is that the boards "first appeared about six years" before its piece, when labels started aiming at festivalgoers and at the performers driving the same road.572 The piece is undated, but it treats 2020 and 2021 as past and 2022 as current, which puts the start at about 2016. By 2019, the outdoor-ad trade body OAAA was writing up Billie Eilish's minimal text boards on the I-10.576

As for how many boards there are, AdQuick tracked 485 on the LA-to-festival route in 2018, 533 in 2019 and 548 in 2022.572 Those count every board on the route, not just festival campaigns.

2020: the boards that never went up

On March 10, 2020, Goldenvoice moved the April festival to October.50 In June, Riverside County cancelled it.51

DASH TWO had booked 20 Coachella billboards for 2020. Sesto told Billboard every one was cancelled.572 Which artists booked them, and whether any creative actually went up that spring, are both unknown. This project found no photo or report of a festival board standing on the I-10 with no festival behind it.

The industry around them fell hard. Out-of-home advertising revenue was down 45% year over year in the second quarter of 2020.597 Outfront's revenue for the year fell 30.6%.598 Lamar's fell 10.5%.599

2022: the comeback

When the festival came back, so did the boards, unevenly. DASH TWO booked 15, five fewer than its cancelled 2020 total. "There's a lot of skepticism still," Sesto said.572 Lamar sold 66 Coachella spots, against 64 in 2019, and Dallimore was upbeat.572

"We're seeing a lot of this business come back bigger, because people feel more comfortable."572

There were fewer faces to go around. Before the pandemic, local car dealers and casinos with year-round contracts sublet their boards to Coachella advertisers at a discount. After the 2020-21 cancellations, they stopped putting those boards in the deal.572

Scott's "Wrong way!" set the tone for what came next: boards built to be photographed, not just read. In 2025, a board reading "KIKI BOY 2025" went up with no explanation, and Rolling Stone asked whether Frank Ocean was teasing something.600 Not every board is welcome. In 2024, Goldenvoice demanded that the AIDS Healthcare Foundation take down its "Catch More Than Vibes?" board on the westbound I-10.601 And in 2026, enough boards were vandalized that officials planned stronger security for them.579

The AIDS Healthcare Foundation is the most persistent of the uninvited. Its 2024 board, "Catch More Than Vibes?", went up on March 26, facing westbound traffic leaving the valley, with the address of its free STD testing site.602 Adweek put it about 15 miles out from the grounds.603 AHF told OOH Today that AEG/Goldenvoice's legal department said the art was too close to its copyrighted imagery and threatened action if the board wasn't down in 48 hours. AHF said the photo came from Getty Images.604 Its marketing VP, Jason Farmer:

"We were ready to fight them if they kept pushing us to take it down."604

The board stayed up and the media owner kept running it.604 In 2025 AHF came back on the same westbound stretch with "Bad Romance?" from March 31 to April 27, the title of a Lady Gaga song in a year she played the first Friday.605564

That spring a board for the DJ Disco Lines read "Accidentally smoked that s***, now I'm playing at Coachella." Amber Royer, who lost her son to fentanyl and runs the nonprofit Out of the Ashes, told KESQ: "It makes it sound like drugs lead to success, fun and fame."606 Lamar, which owns the board, declined to comment.606 The same week, graffiti writers painted GRIM over the whole of a Snow Strippers board. Their label's founder, a graffiti artist himself, covered it with a new promo, and Stereogum thought it looked better than the original.607

How this archive works

The list below is generated from the archive's data, one entry per board or campaign, grouped by year. The rules:

  • Every board has a source. Each entry says who reported it, what it showed, where it stood and what it promoted, as far as the sources say. Gaps are left as gaps.
  • Every board has a confidence label. High, medium or low, with a note on why. A board seen in several independent reports is high. An agency's own blog post, with no date or location, is low.
  • Photos are credited and linked. Official posts by artists, labels and billboard companies are embedded from where they were posted. Fan photos are used only with the photographer's written permission and credit. Nothing is downloaded and rehosted without permission. Where no image can be used, a text card describes the board and links to the source.
  • What gets hosted here. Only three kinds of image: photos taken from the road by Richard Lathrop (marked "From the road", with the date taken), Creative Commons photos with their author and license, and brand or label press-kit images. Everything else stays where it was posted, linked with credit. Boards known from the press or social posts with no usable photo yet are in the still-needed list at the bottom.
  • Street View is cited, not copied. Google's terms don't allow Street View screenshots for any purpose.608 So where an old Street View capture shows a board, the entry links to that view and gives Google's capture date, such as "Apr 2019," and the date it was checked. Street View is corroboration, not proof.
  • It is updated every spring. In 2026, The Desert Sun photographed the boards on March 31, ten days before the first weekend.609

Instagram roundups collect the boards every year too. They are a good place to look, but a roundup alone isn't enough for a high confidence label.
