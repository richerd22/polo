02Every year

The short versionEvery edition on one page, from Pearl Jam's polo-field show in 1993 to the 2026 sellout. A $50 day ticket became a $649 weekend, two days became six, and the gross climbed to $114.6M in 2017 before the published numbers stopped. Three years are missing entirely, and most recent years have no official attendance at all.

The first Coachella charged $50 a day and lost somewhere between $850,000 and a million dollars.317 Eighteen years later the same polo field grossed $114.6M, the first recurring festival to clear $100M.3940 The table below is everything in between, one row per year.

The shape of the curve

It did not go up in a straight line. It went up in steps, and each step was a format change.

Days. Two days in 1999. Nothing in 2000. One day in April 2001, headlined by Jane's Addiction.324 Two days again until 2007, when Coachella went to three days for the first time.29 Then, from 2012, the same three days twice: two weekends, identical lineups, sold separately.33

Prices. A two-day pass cost $142 in 2004.28 The first three-day pass, in 2007, was $249.29 It sat at $269 through the end of the one-weekend era.32 The two-weekend years start at $285 plus fees in 2012, then $349 in 2013, $429 in 2018 and $499 in 2024.43444546 For 2026, general admission started at $649 for the first weekend.47

Grosses. The published numbers run $67.2M in 2013, $78.3M in 2014, $84.3M in 2015, $94.2M in 2016 and $114.6M in 2017.36373839 That is the whole run of figures this project could tie to Billboard or Pollstar. Earlier years in the table come from Wikipedia's summary of Billboard Boxscore and are labeled that way. After 2017, nothing. The money chapter covers why that matters.

The bad years. 2000 was skipped after the first one lost money.3 2010 sold three-day passes only31 and still got overrun at the gates; LA Weekly reported gate-crashing, counterfeit tickets and three-hour entry lines.48 An Icelandic volcano's ash cloud stranded seven acts in England the same weekend.49 The fixes for 2011 were a wrought-iron fence, an extra road lane and the end of the ticket-for-wristband swap.32 Weeks after that festival, on May 31, 2011, Goldenvoice announced the second weekend.33

The gap. Coachella 2020 was moved to October, then cancelled by Riverside County's public health officer in June.5051 The same officer cancelled the April 2021 dates in January 2021.52 The festival came back in April 2022, so the table has two empty rows in a row for the first time.

The slump and the snap-back. In 2024, a first weekend that used to sell out in hours took nearly a month.46 For 2025, second-weekend passes were resold below face value.53 Then 2026 sold out both weekends three days after passes went on sale.54

How to read the table

  • Attendance and gross are as reported. Each cell says who reported it. Where two sources disagree, both are in the row.
  • Some years have no number. No attendance figure was found for 2018 through 2023, and no gross for 2018 onward. An empty cell means nobody published one, not that it was zero.
  • 125,000 is a ceiling, not a count. That is the daily cap Indio has allowed since a 2016 vote.55 It is not how many people came.
  • City counts are their own thing. For 2024, Indio counted 81,690 tickets for the first weekend and 80,148 for the second.56 Those are far below the cap, and may cover only the tickets the city collects a fee on.
  • Admissions are not people. 2014's 579,000 is not a typo. It is 96,500 a day across six days, the way Billboard counted it.36 The number of different people who came was closer to a third of that.
  • Prices are the cheapest general-admission pass at the time it went on sale, before fees. Prices were tiered from about 2022, so the same weekend often sold at more than one price.
  • 1993 is not a Coachella. It is the Pearl Jam show that put Goldenvoice on the polo field. It is in the table because the festival starts there.3

Every row links its sources. If you have a better one, especially a Boxscore printout or a city report, the corrections address is at the bottom of every page.

  • Moved to spring and cut to one day. Jane's Addiction headlined, with Perry Farrell in a white jumpsuit and fedora.
  • Rest of the bill: Weezer, The Roots, Mos Def, Gang Starr, Fatboy Slim (closing with a 90-minute set), Paul Oakenfold, the Chemical Brothers (who worked 'Iron Man' into their set), and Sigur Ros (their first Southern California show).
