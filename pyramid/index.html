06Daft Punk and the pyramid

The short versionIn 2006 Daft Punk said yes to Coachella after a year of saying no, because their manager, who usually turned offers down without asking them, picked up the phone. They played the Sahara tent at 11 p.m. on a Saturday, inside a pyramid of LED blocks built for that one show, and ran nearly 20 minutes past curfew, with the promoter promising to cover any fine. The pyramid went on to a world tour, a live album, two Grammys and, 20 years later, a LEGO set. The crowd count and the bands it started are legends; the rest is on the record.

The pyramid was built for one show. Live Design, the trade magazine that covered it, put it plainly: the set "was first designed for a one-time performance" at Coachella.191 It ended up on a 48-date world tour, a Grammy-winning live album and, announced in 2026, a LEGO set.192193194 All of that started with a phone call their manager almost didn't make.

The offer

Coachella asked twice. According to the 2015 documentary Daft Punk Unchained, the festival offered $250K in 2005 and was turned down, then came back in 2006 with $300K, and the band said yes.195

Goldenvoice tells it with a bigger number. In its own 2024 film, Stacey Vee, Goldenvoice's VP of festival talent, says the 2006 offer was $350K, plus airfare, hotel and ground transportation.196 As far as this research found, it is the first figure the promoter itself has put on it. The two may be counting differently - a fee against a full package - or one of them is wrong. Both are on camera.

"We really didn't want a no; we really wanted this one to happen." - Stacey Vee, Goldenvoice196

The yes is the part that almost didn't happen. Pedro Winter, known as Busy P, managed Daft Punk for 12 years, starting at 20, and founded the label Ed Banger along the way. He describes the job mostly as refusal.196

"My job mostly during those 12 years was to say no to everybody, everything." - Pedro Winter196

Usually he said no without even asking the band.197 The 2006 offer arrived as a short email of a few lines, and this time, he says, he at least took the trouble to call Thomas Bangalter.197 Bangalter said yes. The plan grew quickly into a pyramid made of LEDs.197

Bangalter's own version, from an August 2007 interview that stayed unpublished until 2021, is about money in the useful sense.

"Coachella was a big offer financially, and that triggered the ability to bring the show to the next level." - Thomas Bangalter198

He added that they had "never done anything for the money."198 Water & Music reports the band put advance money into the production, trading profit for a better show.199 It would be their first US show since 1997, and the first time anyone heard Discovery played live.199200

By the numbers · The deal

  • 2005 offer: $250K, declined (Unchained)195
  • 2006 offer: $300K (Unchained)195 or $350K plus flights, hotel and ground transport (Goldenvoice)196
  • Last US show before it: 1997199
  • Set length: about 75 minutes201
  • Over curfew: nearly 20 minutes201
  • Pyramid blocks: more than 1,600 Barco O-Lites202
  • Production cost: not documented anywhere found

The film: Busy P Says "Oui"

The best first-hand account of the booking is a short film the festival made about itself. Busy P Says "Oui" - Ed Banger, Daft Punk & Coachella premiered on Coachella's YouTube channel on December 18, 2024.196203 It runs about ten minutes, was directed by Joey Garfield, and was shot at and around Coachella 2024 as part of Goldenvoice's own content slate.204196

It interviews Winter, Vee, and Wiley Dailey, whom Billboard calls the mastermind of the Sahara tent, and it includes footage of the 2006 set that had not been seen before, alongside Justice's later Coachella shows.196204 Two lines carry it.

"Sometimes a 'Oui' can change your life." - Pedro Winter204

"People showed up and lost their minds." - Wiley Dailey196

It is a promoter's film about its own best night, and it plays like one. It is also where the $350K comes from, and the source for the phone call.196197 (YouTube would not load for this research, so the details here come from Billboard, Radio FG and BPM's coverage of the film, not from a transcript.)

It is one of three films that cover the night. Daft Punk Unchained, directed by Hervé Martin-Delpierre, premiered on Showtime in the US on December 10, 2015, and its Coachella segment is where the $250K-then-$300K story, the locked-out manager and the 40,000 figure all come from.205195206 The festival's own Coachella: 20 Years in the Desert, a 104-minute film by Chris Perkel, went up on YouTube on April 10, 2020, and its director said it included footage of the 2006 set that had never been shown publicly.207 Fans have their own: crowd footage that the uploader says came from an old file shared on Reddit went up on YouTube in 2025, as reported by attendees.208

Building the pyramid

The man who designed it was Martin Phillips of Bionic League, the lighting and set designer, who also co-produced the video and programmed the show.191209 He based it on the video for "Technologic," which he had also designed.199 The duo were closely involved. "Simplicity was a key element for them," he said.209

What he built, by the 2006 trade accounts:

  • The pyramid itself: more than 1,600 Barco O-Lite LED blocks, pixel-mapped into one 3D video surface. Video ran over the bottom two-thirds of the front and a small section above the platform where the duo stood.202 PLSN's 2007 gear list gives 1,800 modules, probably for the touring rig.209 Rolling Stone Australia and The Ringer both call it a 24-foot aluminum pyramid.210200
  • The sides: two 18 x 18 ft frames of criss-crossing hexagons, made from 170 Element Labs VersaTubes.202
  • The back: a pixel curtain 48 ft wide and 16 ft high, built for the show by Element Labs and Daft Arts.202
  • The build: VER Los Angeles made the custom two-faced pyramid screens, with a "top-hat" peak, from Barco templates; XL Video ran video; B&R Scenic later rebuilt the frame to survive the road.209
  • The brains: Catalyst media servers ran about 95% of the video, triggered from a Hog console by timecode sent from the band's own rig. Phillips kept three layers free for "buskable" improvised content.191209

Inside the structure, Bangalter said in 2007, "We have synthesizers and remote controls in the pyramid."198 The look on the surface was the 1970s and 1980s - blocky color, then wireframe graphics, then photographs - on hardware that was new that year.209

None of the parts were invented for it. That was the point, Phillips said.

"What was new was the fact that [they] had taken the existing products and used them as building blocks." - Martin Phillips199

Because production time was short, he "locked them into a set shell" - a fixed beginning, middle and end - so the music cues could fire the lights and video, while the music inside the shell was improvised in Ableton Live.211199 On the sound side, he recalled, "it was really a case of a guy on the console trying to keep up."199

What it cost is the missing number in this chapter. No documented figure turned up.

The people behind the robots

A pyramid that runs on timecode needs someone ready for the night the timecode stops. On the tour that was Eric Belanger, the lighting director, who was set up to run the whole show by hand if it failed.209 The video content came from Baptiste Andrieux and the Santa Monica studio 8VFX, and Cédric Hervet of Daft Arts cut the material to tempo and made most of the footage for the last song and the encore.209 For the encore the duo wore robot suits lit with electroluminescent wire, made by Janet Hansen of Enlighted, with a lighting-control interface built by Anders Nelson.209

Phillips has a line about the job that doubles as advice to every designer who followed him.

"You don't go home humming the lights!" - Martin Phillips199

He also had a simpler review of the work itself: "Everyone should get to work with robots."209

Rehearsal

Even their manager was kept out. Winter first heard the show days before it, as an audio-only rehearsal; he told Unchained, "I wasn't allowed to see the lights yet."195

The duo prepared for the desert in the most literal way available. In 2026 Bangalter told The Times that for the final rehearsal he had them both wear ski jackets, on the theory that "if we get through that anything will be easy."212 His review of the experience: "it was the worst ever."212

The night

Saturday, April 29, 2006. Depeche Mode headlined the main stage, and Daft Punk followed them at 11 p.m. in the Sahara tent.213199 The Sahara then sat at the far edge of the grounds, beside what later became the Rose Garden VIP area, and usually had only a few light panels.94 Why the band went there and not the main stage is not explained in anything found for this chapter.

They opened with the theme from Close Encounters of the Third Kind and went into a "Robot Rock" and "Oh Yeah" mash-up, then ran their catalog as one seamless mix.214 Setlist accounts have "Around the World" laid over "Harder, Better, Faster, Stronger," "One More Time" into "Aerodynamic," "Da Funk," and "Human After All" into "Superheroes."215 NME's report from that night says the crowd "spilled almost a hundred deep" outside the tent.216 The festival had planned for that, adding extra speaker stacks and big screens outside.206

Not everyone outside saw much. One writer, stuck by the side of the tent, recalls his housemate coming home and calling it "the best light show I've ever seen." His reply: "What light show?"206

Billboard's review put the whole weekend at more than 100,000 people over two days.213 A good share of them seemed to be trying to stand in one tent.

The set ran about 75 minutes, nearly 20 minutes past the festival's midnight curfew, which in 2006 could mean a fine.201 Paul Tollett let it run.

"They were so great we told them to keep going, and the festival would cover the possible fine." - Paul Tollett, 2006201

SPIN's live blog had it ending at midnight, with fans heading off to line up for Madonna.217 Las Vegas Weekly's account is more specific and has Tollett on the record, so this page goes with it. Madonna played the Sahara the next day too.94

Bangalter later said the crowd was "five years" behind the duo's ideas, but also that it was "the most synched-up we ever felt," in a 2013 Pitchfork interview as reported by Rolling Stone Australia.210

The lore · 40,000 into a 10,000 tent

The legend goes that 40,000 people tried to get into a tent built for 10,000. The number comes from the band's manager, in Daft Punk Unchained, and it has been repeated widely since.206218 Live Design calls it "rumored."219 Nobody counted at the gate. The Ringer goes one further and says the crowd nearly brought the tent down; no account from 2006 says so.200 What the 2006 reports do say is that the crowd stood almost a hundred deep outside, and that the festival had already put up extra speakers and screens for the people who couldn't get in.216206 However many it was, it was more than the tent.

The tour, the album, the Grammys

The pyramid was not supposed to leave Indio. It did, and XL Video had to rebuild a one-off rig for the road.191

"We've really been on a world tour since that first show at Coachella in April, 2006." - Thomas Bangalter, 2007220

Wikipedia's tour history counts 48 shows on what became Alive 2006/2007, from Coachella to Sydney Showground on December 22, 2007, the last night the pyramid was used.192 It was one of only two official tours the duo ever played; the other was in 1997.210 Along the way it reached Lollapalooza in Chicago in 2007.210

The album came from home. Alive 2007 was recorded at the Palais Omnisports de Paris-Bercy on June 14, 2007, the duo's first hometown show in ten years, and came out that November.221222 In February 2008 they made their first US TV appearance, behind Kanye West on "Stronger" at the Grammys.223 A year later they won two of their own: Best Electronic/Dance Album for Alive 2007, and Best Dance Recording for its live "Harder, Better, Faster, Stronger."193224

How much of the American dance boom that followed traces back to one tent is a matter of framing, not measurement. Fans call the 2006 weekend the "Big Bang," per the LA Times.94 The Rock and Roll Hall of Fame's library calls the set "a sea change for Electronic Dance Music."225 Water & Music traces a production "arms race" from it, through the Tupac hologram and Odesza's drones.199 The music writer Michaelangelo Matos put it more simply in Unchained: "No one had seen that level of production."218

The lore · Everyone there started a band

The legend goes that everyone who was in the Sahara that night went home and started a band, or bought decks, or both. It is the best kind of festival legend, because it cannot be checked and is too nice to want to. It also has a family tree. The original is Brian Eno's line about the Velvet Underground's first album, in the LA Times in 1982: "everyone who bought one of those 30,000 copies started a band!"226 No version naming the 2006 crowd turned up in this research, and no famous DJ is on record as having been in that tent.

The documented case is a cousin. Skrillex saw the pyramid in 2007, on the tour, not at Coachella. "I went alone, bought a ticket off a scalper for $150," he said in 2012. "It changed my life."227 Dancing Astronaut places it at the Alive 2007 show in LA.228 Ten years later he played the Do LaB stage at Coachella as a surprise act, and said he was trying to get the robots to play live again.166228

The robots and Coachella, after

Daft Punk never played Coachella again. They kept turning up anyway.229

In April 2013 the festival screened a teaser of just under two minutes for Random Access Memories on Friday, April 12: "Get Lucky," with Pharrell and Nile Rodgers, followed by a list of collaborators that included Giorgio Moroder, Julian Casablancas and Panda Bear.230231 Daft Punk weren't on the bill.230

In 2016 a site called alive2017.com counted down to October 27, and its code carried coordinates that pointed to LA and Indio. Time Out was skeptical, and nothing happened.232 In 2017 Pharrell answered the reunion rumors with the only line anyone needs: "The robots are, like... they're not repeaters."229

On February 22, 2021, the duo posted "Epilogue," an eight-minute video built from their film Electroma, and ended a 28-year partnership without giving a reason.200

Then, in October 2026, the pyramid came back as plastic. LEGO announced an official set of the Alive 2007 pyramid stage, with more details promised for 2027.194 It started as a LEGO Ideas design by Patrick Harboun, from Luxembourg, and his son, made during lockdown; it won a 2020 music challenge and later passed 10,000 supporters.194233 Cédric Hervet of Daft Arts called the pyramid "one of the most recognisable expressions of Daft Punk's visual world."194 Not bad for something built for one Saturday night.
