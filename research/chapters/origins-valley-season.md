## 1. ORIGINS

### 1.1 Pearl Jam at the Empire Polo Club, 1993
- Pearl Jam played the Empire Polo Club in Indio on **November 5, 1993**, on the Vs. Tour, to almost **25,000** people. The site had never hosted a rock show before. Openers were American Music Club, Weapon of Choice and Eleven.
  - Wikipedia "Coachella", citing Hochman, *Los Angeles Times*, Nov 8, 1993 - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coachella
  - Wikipedia "Vs. Tour" - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vs._Tour
- Why Indio: the band would not play Los Angeles because of a dispute with Ticketmaster over service charges. Band management picked the "untested and under-developed" polo site to avoid the Southern California venues Ticketmaster controlled.
  - Wikipedia "Coachella" (LA Times 1993 citation); Wikipedia "Vs. Tour"
- *Timing:* the formal Pearl Jam vs Ticketmaster fight came after this show. The band complained to the US Justice Department, testified to Congress on **June 30, 1994**, and cancelled its summer 1994 tour "as a result of a Ticketmaster boycott". The 1993 Indio show was an early move in that dispute, not something that grew out of the later complaint.
  - Wikipedia "Vs. Tour", citing the congressional testimony (Jun 30, 1994) and *Entertainment Weekly* (1994)
- Goldenvoice promoted the 1993 show, its first at the polo club. Paul Tollett says it showed the site could hold large events and "sowed the seeds" of the festival.
  - *The Ringer*, Rob Harvilla, Oct 9, 2019 - https://www.theringer.com/music/2019/10/9/20905896/how-coachella-99-lost-a-million-dollars-started-music-festival-empire
  - Wikipedia "Vs. Tour", citing *Billboard* (Flanagan, 2012)

### 1.2 Goldenvoice's punk roots
- Gary Tovar started promoting shows in **1981**. His sister had told him police were shutting down punk shows in Southern California. His first show was in Santa Barbara on **Dec 4, 1981**. The company started as "Golden Voice", later merged into one word.
  - Wikipedia "Gary Tovar" - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gary_Tovar
- He booked UK punk acts (The Damned, GBH, Siouxsie and the Banshees) and US acts (Dead Kennedys, Bad Religion), many at the Grand Olympic Auditorium in LA. Later he promoted early Nirvana and Red Hot Chili Peppers shows.
  - Wikipedia "Gary Tovar"
- The shows were funded by Tovar's marijuana-smuggling money. They often lost money, an estimated **$3-4 million over ten years**, and also helped launder the smuggling profits.
  - Wikipedia "Gary Tovar" (needs a primary source, likely *OC Weekly* or the *LA Times*; see "Unconfirmed")
- Tovar was arrested in **March 1991** on drug trafficking charges. In **October 1992** he was sentenced to seven years. After being denied bail, he handed Goldenvoice to **Paul Tollett** (hired 1986) and **Rick Van Santen** (hired 1985) at no cost.
  - Wikipedia "Gary Tovar"
  - *The Ringer*, Oct 9, 2019 (handover in the "early 1990s")
- Tovar still attends every year, runs a merch tent and is a consultant to the company.
  - Wikipedia "Gary Tovar"
  - *AOL/Desert Sun* profile, "Goldenvoice founder Gary Tovar is Coachella's eternal fan" - https://www.aol.com/goldenvoice-founder-gary-tovar-coachellas-150048995.html (not opened)

### 1.3 The first Coachella - October 9-10, 1999
- Indio City Council approved the festival on **July 16, 1999** and put up **$90,000** for services, with the promoter guaranteeing repayment. The year before, the 1998 "Big Gig" festival had cost the city $16,000.
  - Wikipedia "Coachella"
- The festival was announced July 28 and tickets went on sale Aug 7. The announcement came about a week after Woodstock '99. Tollett later called the timing "financial suicide".
  - Wikipedia "Coachella"
  - *GRAMMY.com*, Nina Frazier, Oct 9, 2024 - https://www.grammy.com/news/first-coachella-valley-music-festival-1999
- Two days at the Empire Polo Club. Tickets were **$50 a day** plus fees. About **70** artists played. It was over 100°F.
  - *The Ringer*, Oct 9, 2019
  - Wikipedia "Coachella"
- Headliners were Beck, Rage Against the Machine, Tool and the Chemical Brothers. Also on the bill: Moby, Morrissey, A Perfect Circle, Jurassic 5, Underworld, DJ Shadow, Carl Craig, Autechre, Richie Hawtin, Cibo Matto and Roni Size. Ninja Tune supplied nine acts, including Kid Koala, Amon Tobin and DJ Food.
  - *The Ringer*, Oct 9, 2019
  - *GRAMMY.com*, Oct 9, 2024
- Attendance was about **17,000 on day one and 20,000 on day two**, roughly 37,000 tickets in total, against a goal of about 70,000.
  - Wikipedia "Coachella"
  - *The Ringer*, Oct 9, 2019 ("17,000 to 20,000 per day")
- Losses: **$850,000**. Tollett has also said "around $1 million", and that he sold his house and car afterward.
  - $850,000: Wikipedia "Coachella"; *GRAMMY.com*, Oct 9, 2024
  - ~$1 million: *The Ringer*, Oct 9, 2019
- Headliners agreed to deferred pay. Tom Morello: "the festival lost so much money they asked us for our fee back, which we gave them because they were friends."
  - Wikipedia "Coachella"
  - *GRAMMY.com*, Oct 9, 2024, quoting an al.com interview
- Tollett quotes, via *The New Yorker* (2017): "We knew we were dust." "A couple bands let us slide."
  - *The Ringer*, Oct 9, 2019

### 1.4 2000 skipped, 2001 one-day return, the AEG deal
- Goldenvoice held October 2000 dates, then cancelled. Tollett blamed too many festivals in Southern California. Goldenvoice instead co-promoted Pasquale Rotella's **Nocturnal Wonderland** at the same site in September 2000.
  - Wikipedia "Coachella"
- **March 2001:** Tollett sold Goldenvoice to **Anschutz Entertainment Group (AEG)** for **$7 million**, keeping control of Coachella at first.
  - Wikipedia "Coachella"
  - *The Ringer*, Oct 9, 2019 ("AEG bought Goldenvoice in 2001")
- **April 28, 2001:** one-day festival, headlined by **Jane's Addiction** after Perry Farrell agreed to reunite the band. Tickets were $65. About 32,000 people came. It lost money again, a "low, low six-figure sum" per Tollett.
  - Wikipedia "Coachella"
  - *The Ringer*, Oct 9, 2019
  - NPR "Word of the Week", Apr 16, 2025 (2001 return in April) - https://www.npr.org/2025/04/16/nx-s1-5365781/coachella-festival-valley-history
- Perry Farrell played the first 12 Coachellas.
  - *The Ringer*, Oct 9, 2019

### 1.5 Growth: two days, three days, two weekends
- **2002:** back to two days (Apr 27-28), with Björk and Oasis. More than 55,000 attended and it nearly broke even.
  - Wikipedia "Coachella"
- **2003:** on-site camping added, with 2,252 sites approved by Indio. 60,000 attended. Co-founder **Rick Van Santen** died in late December 2003, aged 41, of flu-related complications.
  - Wikipedia "Coachella", citing Cromelin, *LA Times*, Jan 3, 2004
- **2004:** Tollett sold **half of Coachella to AEG**. It was the first sellout, at about 110,000.
  - Wikipedia "Coachella"
  - *The Ringer*, Oct 9, 2019 ("half of Coachella in 2004")
  - NPR, Apr 16, 2025 ("2004: Tickets sell out for the first time")
- **2007:** permanently three days, the same year **Stagecoach** launched. About 186,000-187,000 attended in total. Gross was $16.3M.
  - Wikipedia "Coachella"
- **2008:** the year Tollett says he lost the most money (Prince headlined).
  - *The Ringer*, Oct 9, 2019, citing the *LA Times*
- **May 31, 2011:** Goldenvoice announced that 2012 would run over **two weekends with identical lineups**, ticketed separately. *Rolling Stone* called it a "very risky move". 2012 sold out in under three hours. The two weekends grossed **$47.3M** from 158,387 paid attendees.
  - Wikipedia "Coachella"
  - *TicketNews*, June 2011 - https://www.ticketnews.com/2011/06/coachella-2012-to-span-two-weekends-with-identical-lineup/ (not opened)
  - *Tucson Weekly*, May 31, 2011 - https://community.tucsonweekly.com/TheRange/archives/2011/05/31/coachella-expands-to-two-identical-weekends (not opened)
  - Rolling Stone URL failed to load
- **2017:** grossed $114.6M, the first recurring festival over $100M (Billboard Boxscore). Pollstar ranked it the top-grossing festival in the world.
  - Wikipedia "Coachella"
  - *Type Investigations*, Apr 12, 2018 ($114.6M) - https://typeinvestigations.org/investigation/2018/04/12/coachella-underground/
- **By 2019:** about 125,000 people a day, and a yearly gross of "well over $100 million".
  - *The Ringer*, Oct 9, 2019
- **2025:** about 250,000 visitors across the two weekends.
  - *KRON4*, Iman Palm, Apr 14, 2026, citing Gov. Newsom's office - https://www.kron4.com/news/california/newsom-coachella-stagecoach-generate-over-700m-for-ca-annually/amp/
- **2026:** headliners Sabrina Carpenter (Fri), Justin Bieber (Sat), Karol G (Sun). Karol G was the first Latina artist to close the main stage.
  - *NBC Palm Springs*, Apr 13, 2026 - https://www.nbcpalmsprings.com/local-and-community/2026/04/13/coachella-weekend-one-wraps-with-historic-headliner-wind-scares-and-a-few-surprises

### 1.6 The name: Coachella the valley, Coachella the city, the festival in Indio
- The name began as a printer's mistake. In 1901 railroad man Jason Rector proposed "**Conchilla**" ("little shell", after local fossils) for the townsite at what had been Woodspur. The printer set it as "Coachella", and the founders kept it rather than delay the announcement. The valley took the name later. The town became a city in **1946**. Historian Jeff Crider: the word means nothing in Spanish or English beyond the place itself.
  - NPR "Word of the Week", Apr 16, 2025
- The festival takes its name from the **valley**. It has always been held in **Indio**, not the city of Coachella.
  - NPR, Apr 16, 2025
- coachella.com goes to the festival, not the city. The city's motto was "The City of Eternal Sunshine".
  - *The Atlantic*, via Yahoo, Apr 15, 2018 - https://www.yahoo.com/news/other-coachella-won-apos-t-100000485.html
- The city of Coachella is **97.5% Latino**.
  - *Type Investigations* (Gabriel Thompson, with Longreads), Apr 12, 2018

### 1.7 Trademark fights ("-chella")
- **Hoodchella (2016):** Goldenvoice sued this underground LA event and its founder, Kamil Al-Ahdali. The claims were trademark and service-mark infringement, dilution, false designation of origin, unfair competition, and cybersquatting over hoodchella.com. It sought at least **$100,000** in damages plus an injunction.
  - *Hypebot*, Feb 4, 2016 - https://www.hypebot.com/coachella-sues-underground-fest-hoodchella-for-100k-over-name.md
  - *World IP Review*, Feb 1, 2016 - https://www.worldipreview.com/trademark/coachella-sues-underground-music-festival-in-tm-row-9478
- **Urban Outfitters / Free People (2017):** sued in March 2017 over "Coachella"-branded clothing. Settled by October 2017.
  - *Pollstar*, Mar 19, 2017 - https://news.pollstar.com/2017/03/19/goldenvoice-sues-urban-outfitters/
  - *World IP Review*, Oct 2, 2017 - https://www.worldipreview.com/news/coachella-settles-trademark-clash-with-urban-outfitters-14686
- **Filmchella (2017-2018):** a film festival sued in 2017. It settled days before a jury trial. A federal judge approved the settlement on **Oct 4, 2018**, with a permanent injunction against confusingly similar marks.
  - *Law360*, Oct 5, 2018 - https://law360.com/articles/1089724
- **Others reported:** Combschella, Wholechella (a Whole Foods event in Palm Desert), Phillips 66 (over a wristband giveaway), and Carchella (DJ Envy's car show, Sept 2021, later renamed).
  - Wikipedia "Coachella" (each needs a primary source)
- **"Coachella Day One 22" (Dec 2021 - Aug 2022):** a New Year's Eve event at **Coachella Crossroads**, next to Spotlight 29 Casino in the **city of Coachella**, produced by the **Twenty-Nine Palms Band of Mission Indians**. The tribe has sovereign immunity, so Goldenvoice sued **Live Nation/Ticketmaster** and the web host instead. The suit settled in **August 2022** with undisclosed terms and dismissal with prejudice. The tribe said the venue keeps the name "Coachella Crossroads". This is the clearest case of the place name and the festival trademark colliding.
  - *FOX 11 LA*, Dec 21, 2021 - https://www.foxla.com/news/judge-grants-coachella-festival-restraining-order-against-new-years-event-with-similar-name
  - *Festival Insider*, Aug 17, 2022 - https://festivalinsider.com/articles/aeg-live-nation-settle-lawsuit-over-coachella-event-name-dispute
- **Moechella (2023-2024):** Goldenvoice sued the organizers of Moechella, a Washington DC go-go protest-festival, in February 2023. In April 2024 Judge Reggie B. Walton granted a preliminary injunction: "it appears that the defendants intentionally selected Moechella to imitate the Coachella marks."
  - *CMU*, Apr 8, 2024 - https://completemusicupdate.com/coachella-secured-preliminary-injunction-against-moechella-festival/
- **Afrochella (2022):** reported as a trademark dispute; the festival later rebranded. Not opened, needs checking.
  - *Law.com/The Recorder*, Oct 7, 2022 - https://law.com/therecorder/2022/10/07/tucker-ellis-reps-coachella-in-trademark-dispute-with-afrochella

## 2. THE VALLEY

### 2.1 Economic impact studies (who commissioned, what they found)
- **2012 (Development Management Group):** the festivals brought **$254.4M** to the desert region, including $89.2M of consumer spending in Indio and $1.4M in tax revenue.
  - Wikipedia "Coachella"
  - *Hypebot*, Apr 5, 2013 ($89.21M to Indio, DMG analysis) - https://www.hypebot.com/goldenvoice-signs-deal-to-stage-coachella-stagecoach-in-indio-thru-2030.md
- NPR's 2013 coverage called a study putting Coachella's impact on Indio at more than $90M "**Goldenvoice-commissioned**".
  - NPR transcript, April 2013 (published by KUNC Apr 18, 2013) - https://www.npr.org/transcripts/177652870 ; https://www.kunc.org/2013-04-18/coachellas-hometown-aims-to-cash-in-on-fests-rising-tide
- **2016 projections (Michael Bracken, Development Management Group, Palm Desert):** about **$1.4B** worldwide across the five Goldenvoice festivals that year (including Desert Trip), **$805M** to the Coachella Valley and about **$212M** to Indio, based on an average of 273,000 attendees. It was announced in March 2016 by the Palm Springs Desert Resorts CVB and the Coachella Valley Economic Partnership. NBC LA noted Bracken's relationship with AEG/Goldenvoice was "undisclosed and unclear".
  - *NBC Los Angeles*, Apr 13, 2019 - https://www.nbclosangeles.com/news/over-one-billion-to-be-made-worldwide-from-coachella-valley-festivals/164669/
- Another 2016 figure: **$704.75M** globally, **$403.2M** to the Coachella Valley, $106M to Indio (Coachella plus Stagecoach).
  - Wikipedia "Coachella"; *Mixmag*, 2016 - https://mixmag.net/read/coachella-helps-generate-around-704-million-news (not opened)
  - This conflicts with the $805M figure above. See "Unconfirmed".
- **2024:** "about $600 million to the Coachella Valley", credited to the City of Indio. No study was named.
  - *NBC Palm Springs*, Pristine Villarreal, Apr 16, 2024 - https://www.nbcpalmsprings.com/2024/04/16/music-festivals-generate-600-million-for-local-economy-according-to-study
- **2026:** Gov. Newsom's office said Coachella and Stagecoach together generate "more than **$700 million annually**" and support **10,000+ temporary jobs**. No study was named.
  - *KRON4*, Apr 14, 2026
  - *NBC Palm Springs*, Apr 20, 2026 - https://www.nbcpalmsprings.com/2026/04/20/indio-showcases-massive-economic-boost-from-coachella-and-stagecoach-festivals
  - The gov.ca.gov release (Apr 13, 2026) redirected to http and was not fetched.
- *Pattern for the explainer:* the numbers usually come from the city, the tourism bureau or a consultant whose ties to the promoter are unclear. None of the figures found is an independent audit.

### 2.2 Indio's cut: per-ticket fees and the deals
- 1999: the city fronted $90K for services.
  - Wikipedia "Coachella"
- **2012 tax fight:** Indio considered a 5-10% admissions tax on events over 2,500 people, about $36 a ticket. Tollett said the 2013 festivals "could be the last in Indio". Goldenvoice put the cost at $4-6M and threatened to skip 2014 and move. Tollett said the festival already produced $800K+ in ticket fees, tax on about $9M of food and drink sales, and $800K+ in hotel (transient occupancy) tax. Indio had just cut about $4M through layoffs and furloughs.
  - *CBS Los Angeles*, Jul 5, 2012 - https://www.cbsnews.com/losangeles/news/coachella-promoters-threaten-to-move-away-from-the-coachella-valley-over-proposed-tax/
- **2013 deal:** the festivals stay in Indio through at least **2030**. The per-ticket payment to the city rose from **$2.33 to $5.01** from 2014 (NPR: "$5 fee ... about $1 million a year"). The site could go from three festivals a year to five. The vote was 4-0, with Mayor Elaine Holmes abstaining over a property conflict. Councilman Sam Torres had pushed a 6% tax: "I looked at these guys as a way to help us continue to maintain our city."
  - *Hypebot*, Apr 5, 2013
  - NPR/KUNC, Apr 18, 2013
- 2013 curfew terms: a $20,000 fine plus $1,000 per extra minute. Goldenvoice also bought 280 acres for $30M that year.
  - Wikipedia "Coachella"
- 2016: the attendance cap was raised from 99,000 to 125,000 a day, phased in.
  - Wikipedia "Coachella"
- **Aug 2021 extension:** the council voted 3-1 to keep the festivals in Indio "until at least **2050**". Goldenvoice's lease on the Empire Polo Club also runs to 2050 and gives it full operational control of the venue (year-round events), plus the Empire Grand Oasis property in Thermal. Holmes recused (she owns property within 1,000 ft). Councilmember Glenn Miller: "Not one dollar comes out of the city of Indio's pocket."
  - *Digital Music News*, Dylan Smith, Oct 27, 2021 - https://www.digitalmusicnews.com/2021/10/27/coachella-indio-deal/
  - *Business Wire* release, Oct 27, 2021 - https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20211027006159/en (not opened)
- **Not found:** a current (2024-2026) figure for festival money as a share of Indio's general fund. See "Unconfirmed".

### 2.3 Hotels and short-term rentals
- Expedia data: Coachella Valley hotels are more than **90% full** in April, the highest-rate month of the year. Average daily rate was $280 in April 2016 and $340 in April 2017 to date. "April always leads the entire year for Coachella Valley hotels."
  - *Hotel Management*, ~April 2017 - https://www.hotelmanagement.net/operate/coachella-stagecoach-festivals-are-filling-hotel-rooms-southern-california-desert-april
- Example hotel: all 149 rooms at Hotel Paseo (Palm Desert) were expected to sell out on festival weekends. Attendees quoted $4K and $10-15K for a festival weekend.
  - *NBC Palm Springs*, Apr 16, 2024
- Short-term rentals: some luxury homes reportedly rent for "more than $100,000" for one weekend. This is anecdotal, from homeowners and agents, with no data source.
  - *NBC Palm Springs*, Brett Rosen, Mar 24, 2026 - https://www.nbcpalmsprings.com/2026/03/24/festival-season-fuels-surge-in-airbnb-prices-across-coachella-valley
- Reported but not opened: CoStar pieces on how hotel supply affects Coachella demand, and PriceLabs' 2024 Coachella/Stagecoach analysis. These may hold hard figures for average rates and revenue per room.
  - CoStar - https://www.costar.com/article/1821048557/supply-pressures-a-factor-in-coachella-hotel-demand-dip
  - PriceLabs - https://hello.pricelabs.co/analyzing-coachella-stagecoach-2024/

### 2.4 The east valley: agriculture, farmworkers and the city of Coachella
- The valley's two main industries are agriculture (notably dates) and tourism/entertainment. The first major farm labor strike in the valley was in **1965**.
  - NPR, Apr 16, 2025
- Thermal (about 2,396 people) is 99.9% Latino, Mecca (nearly 9,000) is 99.9%, and Oasis is 98.2%. About a third of Thermal's residents live below the poverty line, including nearly half its children. Drinking water on the east side contains arsenic, and there are illegal dumps. In grape season, some migrant workers sleep in cars or parking lots. The festival "has little bearing on the lives of people on the valley's east side."
  - *Type Investigations*, Gabriel Thompson, Apr 12, 2018
- **Farmworkers on the festival cleanup:** grape workers take overnight trash-collection shifts at the polo grounds across all three April weekends. They finish around 3:30 a.m., after about eight hours in the fields, and are paid minimum wage. Supervisor Enriqueta Magaña (20 years in the vineyards, 10 at the festival) said the grounds afterward look like "a garbage dump". Behind the extra shifts: about 1,000 fewer acres of grapes in 2017 than in 2013 (Riverside County Agricultural Commissioner).
  - Rebecca Plevin, *The Desert Sun*, Apr 18, 2019, republished by KQED - https://www.kqed.org/news/11741243/as-grape-industry-changes-farmworkers-pick-up-extra-shifts-collecting-trash-at-coachella-festival
- **"Real Coachella" billboards (2025):** the TODEC Legal Center put up **10 billboards** of local farmworkers around the valley during festival season, through May 1, 2025. One featured Pedro Zacarias of Thermal. TODEC says there are 10,000+ farmworkers in the valley. Director Luz Gallegos said the festival pulls attention away from the east side, where the "real Coachella" is.
  - *NBC Bay Area*, Apr 4, 2025 - https://www.nbcbayarea.com/news/local/billboards-farm-workers-displayed-coachella-valley/3836990/
  - *Note for the editor:* this ties directly to the billboard theme (see 3.3 Desert X).

### 2.5 Thermal / Jacqueline Cochran Regional Airport (private jets)
- The airport is in Thermal, about 20 nautical miles southeast of Palm Springs, and owned by Riverside County. Runways are 8,500 ft and 4,995 ft. It logged 57,093 operations in the 12 months to Apr 30, 2023 (about 156 a day), 80% general aviation and 20% air taxi. Built in 1942 as a WWII Desert Training Center base, it was named Thermal Airport from 1948, Desert Resorts Regional from 1998, and Jacqueline Cochran in 2004. Its busiest passenger season is Dec-Mar (the horse show circuit), not April.
  - Wikipedia "Jacqueline Cochran Regional Airport" - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacqueline_Cochran_Regional_Airport
- Festival air traffic: in 2022, Blade expected to fly about 200 people from Santa Monica to Thermal, cutting a five-hour drive to about 40 minutes. In 2017, XOJET flights to three desert airports were reported "up 40%" for Coachella (citing Quartz). JetSuiteX/JSX and Aero have run pop-up Burbank/LA-to-Thermal flights (about $800 seats were reported in 2025).
  - *KESQ*, Crystal Jimenez, Apr 15, 2022 - https://kesq.com/?p=870505
  - *Private Jet Card Comparisons*, Apr 14, 2017 - https://privatejetcardcomparisons.com/2017/04/14/coachella-attracts-the-private-jet-set
  - *EDMTunes*, Apr 2025 - https://www.edmtunes.com/2025/04/soaring-above-the-gridlock-coachella-attendees-are-paying-800-for-jsx-flights-to-beat-traffic/ (not opened)
- **Gap:** no hard count of private jet landings on festival weekends (FAA or WingX/Argus) was found.

### 2.6 Environment: trash, water, energy
- An Indio environmental report found Coachella, Stagecoach and Desert Trip attendees produce **107 tons of solid waste a day**, with about **20%** recycled. Ten empty bottles could be traded for a reusable one.
  - *Waste360*, Apr 24, 2017 - https://www.waste360.com/waste-reduction/coachella-music-festival-attendees-produce-107-tons-of-waste-each-day
- 2022 diversion: 141 tonnes of mixed recycling and 86 tonnes of compost across Coachella and Stagecoach. Lund University's Kim Nicholas: attendee and artist travel emissions are "way bigger" than on-site energy and waste.
  - AFP, via *The National*, ~April 2023 - https://www.thenationalnews.com/arts-culture/music-stage/2023/04/18/can-music-festivals-actually-go-green
- Festival's own figures (self-reported):
  - 616,278 lb composted or recycled in 2024 (Coachella and Stagecoach)
  - 79,120 lb of materials donated to Galilee Center and 56,051 lb of food to Coachella Valley Rescue Mission (2024)
  - Single-use plastic water bottles replaced with aluminum, refill spigots doubled, non-potable water used on the polo field
  - Renewable diesel in generators, on-site solar and hybrid power, and battery/utility power for the Do LaB stage (new in 2025)
  - Camping for about 35,000 people, Carpoolchella (four or more per car), and Global Inheritance as a partner since 2004
  - coachella.com/sustainability, fetched Oct 31, 2026 - https://coachella.com/sustainability

## 3. THE SEASON

### 3.1 Stagecoach
- First held **May 5-6, 2007**, headlined by George Strait and Kenny Chesney. It runs on the same Empire Polo Club grounds the weekend after Coachella and is produced by Goldenvoice. It first sold out in 2012 (55,000). Attendance was 75,000 in 2018 and about 80,000 in 2019 (Desert Sun estimate). It was cancelled in 2020 and 2021 (COVID). It bills itself as the largest-grossing country festival.
  - Wikipedia "Stagecoach Festival", citing *San Diego Union-Tribune* (Apr 28, 2016), *Desert Sun* (2019) and Mix 100.5 (2018) - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stagecoach_Festival
- The changeover starts as Coachella's Sunday crowd leaves and runs through Monday and the week, turning the grounds into a "country-western venue".
  - *Patch*, Apr 21, 2014 - https://patch.com/california/palmdesert/goodbye-coachella-hello-stagecoach
  - *Gap:* no detailed source found on what stays (main stage structures, fencing, camping) and what changes.

The screens read: "The festival has been postponed until further notice. Please move quickly and calmly to the nearest exit." It resumed about 9:30 p.m. ("Back in the saddle"). **Journey** and **Riley Green** were cancelled, Lainey Wilson was moved to 10:30 p.m., and the night ran to 1 a.m.

### 3.2 Desert Trip ("Oldchella") 2016 and Power Trip 2023
- **Desert Trip, October 2016,** at the Empire Polo Club over two weekends: the Rolling Stones, Bob Dylan, Paul McCartney, Neil Young, Roger Waters and the Who. Expected attendance was 150,000. Tickets ran from $199 (one-day GA) to $1,599 (reserved seat, full weekend). It had 1,000 flushable toilets, against the usual 300. It cost about $100M to stage.
  - *Stereogum*, Sep 29, 2016 - https://stereogum.com/1902051/oldchella-by-the-numbers-1000-per-person-1000-flushable-toilets/news
- Tollett told the NYT the gross was "approximately **$160 million** for the two weekends". *Stereogum* compared that with Coachella's $84M that year. Desert Trip did not return in 2017.
  - *Live for Live Music*, Jul 21, 2016, citing the NYT of Jul 19, 2016 - https://liveforlivemusic.com/news/organizer-of-desert-trip-confirms-event-has-already-grossed-160-million
  - *Consequence*, May 2017 - https://consequence.net/2017/05/desert-trip-festival-is-not-returning-in-2017 (not opened)
- **Power Trip, Oct 6-8, 2023,** at the Empire Polo Club:
  - Fri: Iron Maiden and Guns N' Roses
  - Sat: Judas Priest (replacing Ozzy Osbourne, who cancelled for health reasons) and AC/DC
  - Sun: Tool and Metallica
  - Up to 80,000 a day. GA from $599, the Pit $1,599.
  - *Louder/Metal Hammer*, Rich Hobson, Dec 18, 2023 - https://www.loudersound.com/features/inside-power-trip-festival-2023
- Note: the 2013 Indio deal allowed up to five festivals a year at the site, which is the basis for these fall events.
  - *Hypebot*, Apr 5, 2013

### 3.3 Desert X

  - Wikipedia "Desert X" - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desert_X
- Editions:
  - 2017 (Feb 25 - Apr 30): Doug Aitken, Lita Albuquerque, Richard Prince, Tavares Strachan and others
  - 2019 (Feb 9 - Apr 21)
  - 2021 (Mar 12 - May 16): 650,000+ site visits
  - 2023 (Mar 4 - May 7)
  - 2025 (Mar 8 - May 11): 11 artists, including Agnes Denes (*The Living Pyramid*), Sanford Biggers, Kimsooja, Alison Saar and Ronald Rael
  - Wikipedia "Desert X", citing the *Desert Sun*, *NYT* and *LA Times*
  - *Biennial Foundation*, Jan 31, 2025 - https://biennialfoundation.org/2025/01/desert-x-2025-artists-announced/
- **Timing:** every edition so far has run late winter to spring and covered both Coachella weekends and Stagecoach.
  - Edition dates as above
  - **Desert X 2027** (the 10th anniversary) moves to **Oct 30, 2027 - May 7, 2028**, an opening in fall.
  - desertx.org news page, fetched Oct 31, 2026 - https://desertx.org/learn/news
- **The Gene Autry Trail billboards:** Jennifer Bolande, *Visible Distance / Second Sight* (Desert X 2017).   - desertx.org archive - https://desertx.org/dx/archive/visible-distance-second-sight

## Best stories
1. **A typo, a boycott, a polo field.** The valley's name is a printer's error for "Conchilla" (1901). The festival exists because Pearl Jam, fighting Ticketmaster, played a polo field in Indio in 1993 for almost 25,000 people.
   - Sources: NPR, Apr 16, 2025; Wikipedia, citing the LA Times of Nov 8, 1993
2. **Punk money.** Goldenvoice started in 1981 as a punk promoter funded by its founder's marijuana smuggling. When Gary Tovar went to prison, he gave the company away to Tollett and Van Santen. He still runs a merch tent at the festival.
   - Source: Wikipedia "Gary Tovar"
3. **1999 nearly ended it.** It came a week after the Woodstock '99 fallout, ran two days in over 100°F heat, and drew 37,000 against a 70,000 goal. It lost $850K to $1M, Tollett sold his house, and Rage Against the Machine gave back its fee. "We knew we were dust."
   - Sources: The Ringer; GRAMMY.com
4. **The festival isn't in Coachella.** The festival is in Indio, named after the valley. The city of Coachella is 97.5% Latino and largely agricultural. The sharpest clash came when a tribe's NYE event in the actual city of Coachella ("Coachella Day One 22") was taken to court over the name.
   - Sources: NPR; Type Investigations; FOX 11; Festival Insider
5. **The farmworkers who clean up.** Grape pickers work a field shift, then pick up festival trash overnight until 3:30 a.m. for minimum wage. In 2025, a legal nonprofit put up 10 "Real Coachella" billboards of farmworkers during festival season.
   - Sources: Desert Sun/KQED, Apr 18, 2019; NBC Bay Area, Apr 4, 2025
6. **Billboards that erase themselves.** Jennifer Bolande's Desert X 2017 billboards on Gene Autry Trail show the mountains they block, lined up with the real horizon as you drive in.
   - Source: desertx.org
7. **The $36 ticket-tax standoff (2012).** Indio was broke after the housing crash and floated an admissions tax. Goldenvoice threatened to leave. The compromise, about $5 a ticket and a deal to 2030, later extended to 2050, made the festival permanent.
   - Sources: CBS LA, Jul 5, 2012; NPR/KUNC, Apr 18, 2013; Digital Music News, Oct 27, 2021
8. **Oldchella.** Desert Trip 2016 grossed about $160M, roughly double Coachella's gross that year, with $1,599 seats and 1,000 flushable toilets. Power Trip (2023) repeated the formula for metal.
   - Sources: Live for Live Music, citing the NYT; Stereogum; Louder
9. **Marines on the gate.** In 2022, active-duty Marines worked Coachella security for donations to their birthday ball, and some had no guard license.
   - Source: SF Standard, Nov 11, 2022
10. **2026, the wind year.** A light fixture fell into the Do LaB crowd, Anyma was cancelled, and campground tents were shredded. Two weeks later Stagecoach was evacuated mid-show with 50-65 mph gusts forecast, and Journey and Riley Green were scrapped.
    - Sources: NBC Palm Springs; ABC7; NME
