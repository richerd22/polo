Compiled Oct 31, 2026. Each fact is followed by its source(s): publication, date, URL. "(n.d.)" means the fetched page showed no date. Wikipedia is used only as a pointer and is flagged when it is the sole source. No Reddit/social-only claims are included.

## CHAPTER: THE GROUNDS

### Empire Polo Club - founding, owners, layout

- Alexander "Al" Haagen III founded the Empire Polo Club in 1987. He started playing polo in 1983 and was patron of the Empire team for 30 years. The club hosted the first 40-goal event in the U.S. and the first U.S. Women's Open. Source: Museum of Polo and Hall of Fame, Iglehart Award entry, Feb 17, 2023 - https://polomuseum.org/alexander-haagen-iii/
- Palm Springs Life says "entrepreneur Alexander Haagen" founded the club in Indio in 1987 as a home for his favorite sport. It had a dozen grass polo fields, two exercise tracks and stables. Source: Palm Springs Life (n.d.) - https://www.palmspringslife.com/empire-polo-club/
- Where the family money came from: Alexander Haagen III was vice chairman of Alexander Haagen Properties Inc., a family shopping-center developer that became a REIT. The family built its first center in Rosemead in 1963. He was described as founder, president and owner of Empire Polo Club. Source: Los Angeles Business Journal (n.d., 1990s REIT era) - https://labusinessjournal.com/news/haagen/
- The Rose Garden Field (Field #3) is the main Sunday polo field. The rose garden runs along its south side and holds about 8,000 rose bushes in 107 varieties, plus a life-size bronze, "She Gave Her Best," by Rich Roenisch. Source: Empire Polo Club official site, Aug 26, 2010 - https://empirepolo.com/178/
- The Rose Garden is now rented out as an event venue: 3 acres, about 8,000 rose bushes, statues, fountains, three Victorian gazebos and a 5,000 sq ft indoor pavilion. Source: Empire Polo Club Events (n.d.) - https://empirepoloclubevents.com/the-rose-garden
- Medjhool Lake on the property is named for the dates grown there. Its walkways and footbridges are modeled on Monet's garden at Giverny. Source: Palm Springs Life (n.d.) - https://www.palmspringslife.com/empire-polo-club/

### How Goldenvoice got there, and how the festival rents the site

- In 1993, Goldenvoice's Paul Tollett and Rick Van Santen staged a Pearl Jam concert at the polo grounds. Tollett later produced the first Coachella there. Source: Palm Springs Life (n.d.) - https://www.palmspringslife.com/empire-polo-club/
- Coachella has been at Empire Polo Club since it started in 1999, and Stagecoach since 2007. Before 2021, Goldenvoice's arrangement covered only the festival schedule. Source: Business Wire press release, Oct 27, 2021 - https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20211027006159/en
- Oct 27, 2021: Goldenvoice took year-round operational control of Empire Polo Club under a long-term lease with the Haagen family. The deal also gave Goldenvoice oversight of the Empire Grand Oasis in Thermal. Tollett: "Goldenvoice has had a unique relationship with Empire Polo Club for over twenty-five years." Source: Business Wire, Oct 27, 2021 - https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20211027006159/en
- The lease runs through 2050. Source: Digital Music News (Dylan Smith), Oct 27, 2021 - https://www.digitalmusicnews.com/2021/10/27/coachella-indio-deal/
- After the takeover, polo staff were laid off. Lacrosse, women's field hockey and soccer were planned for the site. Source: Palm Springs Life (n.d.) - https://www.palmspringslife.com/empire-polo-club/ . Wikipedia adds that the polo fields closed in Nov 2021 and cites The Desert Sun (James B. Cutchin, Nov 2, 2021). Not fetched directly - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_Polo_Club
- The 2021 deal also permitted two more large festivals a year on the site. IQ describes the grounds as a "642-plus acre desert oasis." Source: IQ Magazine (n.d., 2021) - https://www.iqmagazine.com/?p=239521

### Land purchases around the grounds

- March 2012: Goldenvoice bought 280 acres around the festival site for an undisclosed sum. The land included the Eldorado Polo Club, Triangle Bar Farms, Fish Creek and Ambassador Glen Holden's property. Tollett said it would "help us put in some infrastructure so we don't have to keep coming back and doing the same things each year." Sources: mxdwn (Carey Uhl), Mar 26, 2012 - https://music.mxdwn.com/2012/03/26/news/goldenvoice-buys-280-acres-of-land-around-the-site-of-the-coachella-festival/ ; HITS Daily Double ("3/26") - https://m.hitsdailydouble.com/news&id=289215&title=Goldenbvoice-buys-acreage-in-Indio
- The seller of the 200-acre Eldorado Polo Club was Eldorado Lake Farms, a group of 19 player-owners. Escrow was expected to close in about 60 days. The Eldorado parcel was used for festival camping, and a source said it would be leased back to polo each winter (about Nov 1 to early April). Source: The Equestrian News, Apr 16, 2012 - https://theequestriannews.com/?p=9708
- Wikipedia dates the 280-acre purchase to 2013 at $30 million. A local blog headline (July 2013) reads "Golden Voice Closes Escrow on 200 Acre Parcels $30+ Million", but that site blocks fetching, so it was not read. Most likely the deal was announced in 2012 and closed in 2013 (see Unconfirmed). Sources: Wikipedia "Coachella" - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coachella ; http://www.carmelaatlaquinta.com/2013/07/golden-voice-closes-escrow-on-200-acre-parcels-30-millon-dollars/ (not read)

### Footprint and growth

- 1999 (Oct 9-10): about 17,000 tickets sold for day one and 20,000 for day two, against a 70,000 goal. There was no festival in 2000. 2001 was a single day with 32,000 attendees. 2012 became two weekends, with 158,387 paid (80,726 and 77,661). Source: Wikipedia "Coachella" (secondary; underlying citations not checked) - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coachella
- Wikipedia's figures: the polo club proper is 78 acres, and with parking and camping the event uses about 642 acres. About 50 acres were added in 2016 along Monroe St, Avenue 50, Avenue 52 and Polo Rd. Source: Wikipedia "Coachella" - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coachella (642 figure also in IQ, above)
- 2016 capacity vote: Goldenvoice asked for 125,000 at Coachella and 85,000 at Stagecoach. On Apr 6 the Indio City Council approved up to 62,000 more attendees in total, phased in gradually. Source: Pollstar, Apr 19, 2016 - http://news.pollstar.com/2016/04/19/coachella-stagecoach-expansion-approved/
- In the same vote, Coachella went from 99,000 to 125,000 and Stagecoach from 75,000 to 85,000, on a 3-0 vote with an abstention. Mayor Glenn Miller said the increase would be "gradual over time." Source: mxdwn (Ryan Fricke), Apr 8, 2016, citing The Desert Sun - https://music.mxdwn.com/2016/04/08/news/coachella-and-stagecoach-festivals-will-expand-attendance-up-to-161000-people-in-the-coming-years/ . My inference: 26,000 more per day across two Coachella weekends plus 10,000 at Stagecoach adds up to 62,000.
- Current capacity is 125,000 people per day. Source: Pollstar (Sarah Pittman), Sept 19, 2025 - http://news.pollstar.com/2025/09/19/snap-crackle-pop-what-coachellas-early-lineup-announcement-means/
- Actual peaks: ABC (state alcohol regulator) counted daily peaks of nearly 110,000 at Coachella and about 82,000 at Stagecoach in 2025, with 30-35 ABC agents working each weekend. Source: California ABC, Jun 26, 2025 - https://www.abc.ca.gov/abc-partners-with-law-enforcement-to-prevent-underage-drinking-at-california-music-festivals/

### Stages and tents - names and history

- Mojave, Gobi and Sahara are named after deserts. Source: Wikipedia "Coachella" - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coachella . No primary Goldenvoice statement on the naming was found.
- The Sahara is "as old as Coachella." Its first lineup included A-Trak, Juan Atkins, Kevin Saunderson and Richie Hawtin. It sat at the far edge of the grounds near what became the Rose Garden VIP area. It is nearly 100 ft tall, with a 250-ft-wide stage, and moved to the center of the grounds in 2018 at 25% larger. Source: Los Angeles Times (August Brown) via AOL, Apr 9, 2023 - https://www.aol.com/news/edm-may-waned-sahara-tent-120013512.html
- Sahara timeline: it stood near the rose garden for years, grew to 80 ft in 2013, moved to the Eldorado Polo Club land by the entrance in 2018 (25% larger), and moved again for 2024 to a new area between Avenue 51 and Polo Road. The 2024 structure is 320 ft long and made of steel instead of aluminum, on about 1,500 tons of concrete poured overnight from December. Goldenvoice's Jason Brown called it a "whole new area of the venue that we've never been into before." Source: The Desert Sun (Ema Sasic) via Yahoo, Feb 6, 2024 - https://www.yahoo.com/entertainment/coachella-2024-sahara-tent-moving-003831144.html
- Early dance programming: in 2002 Coachella expanded the Sahara's production and booking (Hawtin, Moby, Oakenfold, Tiësto, Pete Tong). Yuma opened in 2013 as a second dance stage focused on underground acts. In 2015 Mojave and Gobi started booking more electronic acts. Source: Festival Insider (Jonah Flint), Feb 8, 2022 - https://festivalinsider.com/articles/how-dance-music-grew-to-dominate-coachella
- Was the Sahara ever called the Mojave? No evidence of a name swap was found. Wikipedia's lineup tables (tentative, year labels unclear) list a Mojave Tent and a Sahara Tent side by side from 1999, with DJs in both that year. 2001 also had a one-off "Insomniac Tent", and "Main Stage" became "Coachella Stage" by 2001. What did swap is location: in 2018 the Mojave was enlarged and moved into the Sahara's former spot near the rose garden, and in 2024 the new Quasar stage took over the Sahara's old area. That is probably where the "swap" idea comes from. Sources: Wikipedia "Coachella Festival line-ups" - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coachella_Festival_line-ups ; Wikipedia "Coachella" - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coachella ; Dancing Astronaut (c. Mar 2024) - https://dancingastronaut.com/2024/04/coachella-adds-brand-new-quasar-stage-to-the-2024-lineup/
- Other venues, all per Wikipedia (secondary): Yuma (2013, small indoor tent); Heineken House (2014, redesigned 2019 as an open beer garden); Despacio (2016, with James Murphy); Sonora (2017, punk and Latin acts); Antarctic dome (2017, 120 ft across, seats 500, sponsored by HP); Oasis Dome (mid-2000s). Gobi and Mojave moved behind an access road in 2017. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coachella
- Do LaB: started in 2005 as an art installation by the brothers Dede, Josh and Jesse Flemming. Wind wrecked the dome on day one. One night after the main stages shut off, they kept playing through a few small speakers and a crowd gathered. In 2006 Tollett gave two overbooked DJs space in the dome. In 2014 it moved to a corner with a backstage area and began calling itself the "official sixth stage." Its first big surprise set was Bonobo in 2015, and Billie Eilish's 2024 surprise set drew an estimated 20,000+. Source: The FADER (Steffanee Wang), Apr 11, 2025 - https://www.thefader.com/2025/04/11/history-of-do-lab-stage-coachella-20-anniversary-photos
- Quasar opened in 2024 for extended electronic sets: headliner sets of 3+ hours, two large LED walls, on the old Sahara area. 2024 acts included Jamie xx, Eric Prydz b2b Anyma, RÜFÜS DU SOL and Floating Points. Source: Dancing Astronaut (c. Mar 2024) - https://dancingastronaut.com/2024/04/coachella-adds-brand-new-quasar-stage-to-the-2024-lineup/

### Alcohol, beer gardens and the 21+ wristband

- Until recently, drinkers "were always confined to the beer gardens or craft beer areas" and could not take drinks back onto the field. 2023 was a soft change and 2024 the first clear enforcement of walk-around drinking. IDs are checked and 21+ attendees get a wristband. Beer gardens remain, but guards no longer stop drinks at the exits. Stagecoach has always allowed walk-around drinking. Prices: a 16-oz Heineken was $15. Source: The Desert Sun (Shad Powers) via Yahoo, Apr 12, 2024 - https://www.yahoo.com/entertainment/did-know-totally-walk-around-232623009.html
- In 2024, bars were spread across the grounds for anyone with a 21+ wristband, after complaints from 21+ attendees. Source: The Independent (Kevin E G Perry) via AOL, Apr 13, 2024 - https://www.aol.com/news/coachella-makes-highly-requested-change-020712315.html
- Heineken has been the "official brew" since 2002, and Heineken House has run since 2014. Source: Wikipedia "Coachella" (secondary) - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coachella
- ABC enforcement: agents work Coachella and Stagecoach each weekend looking for minors in possession and false IDs. Source: California ABC, Jun 26, 2025 - https://www.abc.ca.gov/abc-partners-with-law-enforcement-to-prevent-underage-drinking-at-california-music-festivals/
- No source was found stating why alcohol was fenced into gardens, whether by ABC license condition, Indio permit or Goldenvoice policy. See Unconfirmed.

### Art program

- Paul Clemente has been Coachella's main art curator since 2007 and leads its fabricators. He previously spent 18 years in Hollywood effects (Titanic, The Matrix). The festival once ran open calls, with 200+ proposals in a year, but now approaches artists directly. Clemente: "We have this incredible venue like no one else in the country has, usually with zero possibility of rain." Source: Los Angeles Magazine (Steve Appleford), Apr 21, 2023 - https://lamag.com/news/coachellas-monumental-artwork-sets-festival-apart
- In 2016 the program took as many as 250 proposals a year and picked about six or seven. It moved to a shortlist of 15-16 artists. The festival fabricates almost all large pieces itself, working with local firms Rice Construction and White's Steel and an on-site shop, with 11 days to install. Notable 2016 work: the Date Farmers' "Sneaking into the Show", about locals who live beside the festival but can't attend. Clemente also sits on the Desert X board. Source: PBS SoCal Artbound (Matt Stromberg), Apr 15, 2016 - https://pbssocal.org/shows/artbound/paul-clemente-and-the-art-of-coachella
- Early art was often recycled from Burning Man. From 2010 to 2015 the festival moved to commissioned pieces. Poetic Kinetics made the snail (2013), the astronaut "Escape Velocity" (2014), the caterpillar (2015) and "Overview Effect" (2019). Sources: Wikipedia "Coachella" (secondary) - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coachella ; LA Magazine, Apr 21, 2023 (astronaut, 2014 and 2019) - https://lamag.com/news/coachellas-monumental-artwork-sets-festival-apart
- Spectra: a seven-story spiral tower with an observation deck by Patrick O'Mahony's studio Newsubstance (UK), commissioned in 2018. It was due to come down after 2020 but stayed and is now treated as a permanent feature. O'Mahony: "I don't think there's any other festival in the world that would commission something like this." Sources: LA Magazine, Apr 21, 2023 - https://lamag.com/news/coachellas-monumental-artwork-sets-festival-apart ; Newsubstance project page (n.d.) - https://newsubstance.co.uk/journal/spectra-coachella
- Ferris wheel: owned and run by Ray Cammack Shows, a Phoenix-area carnival family operating since 1963. It is 150 ft tall, goes up with a 60-ton crane and a crew over 10 days, and draws more power than any other ride at most fairs. Source: Phoenix New Times (n.d.) - https://www.phoenixnewtimes.com/arts-culture/phoenix-family-owns-and-operates-iconic-coachella-ferris-wheel-21472485/
- The current wheel is the La Grande XL, which replaced the original La Grande Wheel in 2017. It has 36 air-conditioned gondolas and is built by 10 crew over five days with a 70-ton crane (per Ben Pickett, VP of Ray Cammack Shows). Source: LA Times via TNS/Recorder, c. Apr 2026 (photo dated Apr 10, 2026) - https://www.recorderonline.com/features/entertainment_news/what-it-takes-to-build-coachellas-most-recognizable-icon/article_6f4e07a1-1236-5044-ad3c-81251de26b8d.html
- The wheel's manufacturer is Nauta Bussink. CoachellaValley.com calls it the world's largest transportable Ferris wheel. Source: CoachellaValley.com, Oct 1, 2023 - https://coachellavalley.com/did-you-know-coachellas-beautiful-ferris-wheel/print (the page spells it "Brussink/Bussink")

### Rest-of-year use

- Events at the site besides Coachella and Stagecoach: Phish's Festival 8 (Oct-Nov 2009), Desert Trip (Oct 2016), Power Trip (Oct 2023), the Coachella Valley Invitational MLS/NWSL preseason tournament (from 2022, 30,000 attendees by 2024), the 2015 USPA Townsend Cup, and Gay Polo League events. Source: Wikipedia "Empire Polo Club" (secondary; cites Rolling Stone Mar 30, 2023 and NYT Feb 23, 2024) - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_Polo_Club
- The 2000 gap year: Goldenvoice instead staged Nocturnal Wonderland at Empire Polo Club in Sept 2000. Source: Wikipedia "Coachella" (secondary) - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coachella
- Under Goldenvoice the Rose Garden and grounds are rented for weddings and private events. Source: Empire Polo Club Events (n.d.) - https://empirepoloclubevents.com/the-rose-garden ; Business Wire, Oct 27, 2021 (special events, sporting events, private functions)
- June 2025: the owner of Empire Polo Club proposed a mixed-use development on about 218 acres next to the festival grounds, with homes, restaurants, retail and possibly a sports venue. County officials approved a key land-use change, but no designs or timeline had been released. Source: NBC Palm Springs, Jun 9, 2025 - https://www.nbcpalmsprings.com/2025/06/10/major-development-planned-near-coachella-festival-grounds-in-indio

### Indio and the festival - permits, money, agreements

- July 2012: Indio considered a 5-10% admissions tax on events over 2,500 people, which would have added about $36 per ticket and cost Goldenvoice $4-6M. Tollett said 2013 could be the last year in Indio: the festivals would skip 2014 and find a new venue for 2015. Source: CBS Los Angeles, Jul 5, 2012 - https://www.cbsnews.com/losangeles/news/coachella-promoters-threaten-to-move-away-from-the-coachella-valley-over-proposed-tax/
- April 2013: the Indio City Council voted 4-0 (Mayor Elaine Holmes abstaining over a property conflict) to keep the festivals through at least 2030. Goldenvoice's per-ticket payment to the city rose from $2.33 to $5.01 starting in 2014. The deal allowed up to five festivals a year (three were being staged then). A Development Management Group study put the festivals' 2012 impact on Indio at $89.21M. Source: Hypebot via LA Times/CelebrityAccess, Apr 5, 2013 - https://www.hypebot.com/goldenvoice-signs-deal-to-stage-coachella-stagecoach-in-indio-thru-2030/
- Context in 2013: Indio got about $500,000 a year at $2.33 a ticket against a $49M city budget. Councilman Sam Torres had proposed a 6% ticket tax. Coachella threatened to leave and he backed down. Source: KCUR/KPCC (Ben Bergman), Apr 18, 2013 - https://www.kcur.org/2013-04-18/coachellas-hometown-aims-to-cash-in-on-fests-rising-tide
- Aug 18, 2021: the council voted 3-1 to extend the development agreement 20 years, from 2030 to 2050. Holmes recused herself and Oscar Ortiz voted no, citing the length and the lack of outreach. The attendance cap and festival count were unchanged. Source: KESQ (Jesus Reyes), Aug 19, 2021 - https://kesq.com/?p=682929
- Indio made over $3.5M in 2019 from ticket fees and taxes on Coachella and Stagecoach. Source: Big Rick Stuart (radio host page), Aug 19, 2021 - https://www.bigrick.com/amp/indio-extends-development-agreement-with-coachella-music-festival-until-2050 (weak source; verify)
- 2024 city figures: 81,690 tickets for Coachella W1, 80,148 for W2 and 89,003 for Stagecoach, so Stagecoach outsold each Coachella weekend. Indio collected $2.658 "per ticket/per day," or $2,000,206.13 in total. Source: KESQ (Jesus Reyes), Jun 13, 2024 - https://kesq.com/news/festival-season/2024/06/13/coachella-stagecoach-sold-around-250k-tickets-for-2024-festivals-city-reports . By my arithmetic, the total equals tickets × 3 days × $2.658.
- A state report released by Gov. Newsom puts the two festivals at $700M in combined economic activity, more than $100M a year in value to Indio, and 10,000+ temporary jobs. 2025 sales were about 120,000 per weekend. Source: NBC Palm Springs, Apr 13, 2026 - https://www.nbcpalmsprings.com/2026/04/13/coachella-and-stagecoach-generate-700-million-boost-indio-economy-and-jobs

### Presale timing and the shift to earlier drops

- For 2025, presale began Nov 22, 2024 (two days after the lineup). Days later Weekend 1 GA was mostly gone but Weekend 2 GA was widely available. GA started at $599 (W1) and $549 (W2), up from $499 in 2021. "For decades" Coachella had sold out the day tickets went on sale. Source: Digital Music News (Ashley King), Nov 26, 2024 - https://www.digitalmusicnews.com/2024/11/26/coachella-2025-not-sold-out/
- 2024 was the slowest-selling year in a decade (NME, Jan 25, 2024). Weekend 1 took 27 days to sell out (Billboard, Feb 15, 2024). Source: Wikipedia "Coachella 2024", citing both - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coachella_2024

### Poster typography and tiering

- The poster normally uses one standard typeface, Eurostile LT. For the 2025 poster, Goldenvoice set "The Original Misfits" in the band's horror lettering as a tribute. Tollett: "The Misfits are one of the first bands we promoted." Source: Billboard (n.d.; 2025 poster cycle, c. Nov 2024) - https://www.billboard.com/music/concerts/the-misfits-font-coachella-2025-lineup-poster-why-different-1235837141/
- The poster has kept the same format since 2003, with type shrinking line by line to signal an act's standing. Agents negotiate billing hard, and some call it one of the most stressful parts of a festival booking. Source: Wallpaper* (Harry Levin), Jun 14, 2026 - https://www.wallpaper.com/art/why-are-festival-line-up-poster-designs-getting-so-hard-to-read

### How Goldenvoice books

- Tollett reviews 3,000-4,000 submissions a year and aims for 140-150 fresh acts. He calls the festival curated rather than chart-driven, and he defends the radius clause as standard practice that gives Coachella a roughly 100-day exclusive window. Source: Los Angeles Times, Jan 13, 2019 (PressReader edition) - https://www.pressreader.com/usa/los-angeles-times/20190113/282686163384762

### The radius clause and the 2018 lawsuit

- April 2018: Soul'd Out Productions (Nicholas Harris and Haytham Abdulhadi), which runs a Portland festival, sued Goldenvoice and AEG in federal court in Oregon. They said the radius clause propped up "an illegal monopoly" and that SZA and Daniel Caesar turned them down because of it. They also said Goldenvoice waived the clause for its own festivals but not for rivals. Goldenvoice said it would "vigorously defend." Sources: SPIN, Apr 2018 - https://www.spin.com/2018/04/coachella-radius-clause-lawsuit/ ; Dancing Astronaut, Aug 20, 2020 - https://dancingastronaut.com/2020/08/2018-lawsuit-against-restrictive-coachella-radius-clause-cleared-to-proceed-in-oregon-district-court/
- Clause terms in the filings: no other North American festival from Dec 15 to May 1. No shows in LA, Orange, Riverside, San Bernardino, Santa Barbara, Ventura or San Diego counties in that window. No promoting any U.S. festival until after Coachella's lineup announcements. No talking about regional California shows until May 7. Artists may not "advertise, publicize or leak" a performance that interferes with Coachella's marketing. Source: Rolling Stone (Andrew White), 2018 - https://www.rollingstone.com/pro/news/coachella-bans-artists-from-playing-half-the-festivals-competing-with-coachella-666103/
- March 2019: Judge Michael Mosman dismissed the suit with prejudice, finding no antitrust injury. Goldenvoice general counsel Shawn Trell welcomed the ruling. Sources: Digital Music News, Mar 13, 2019 - https://www.digitalmusicnews.com/2019/03/13/coachella-radius-clause-lawsuit-dismissed/ ; Competition Policy International (n.d.) - https://epsilon.competitionpolicyinternational.com/?p=79158
- May 2020: the Ninth Circuit reversed and sent the case back. Aug 19, 2020: Mosman let it proceed to discovery. Sources: Dancing Astronaut (Rugby Scruggs), Aug 20, 2020 - https://dancingastronaut.com/2020/08/2018-lawsuit-against-restrictive-coachella-radius-clause-cleared-to-proceed-in-oregon-district-court/ ; mxdwn, May 13, 2020 - https://music.mxdwn.com/2020/05/13/news/coachella-radius-clause-dismissal-reversed-by-u-s-ninth-circuit-court-of-appeals/
- In its answer, AEG admitted the clause exists and raised 16 affirmative defenses, including that the amount in controversy was under $75,000. Source: Digital Music News (Dylan Smith), Sept 22, 2020 - https://www.digitalmusicnews.com/2020/09/22/coachella-radius-clause-update/
- November 2020: the case ended in a settlement, dismissed with prejudice, "each party to bear its own attorneys' fees." Terms were not disclosed. Source: Digital Music News (Dylan Smith), Nov 25, 2020 - https://www.digitalmusicnews.com/2020/11/25/coachella-sould-out-settle-lawsuit/

## Best stories

1. **The tax standoff (2012-13).** Indio floated a ticket tax of 5-10% (about $36 a ticket). Tollett threatened to skip 2014 and move both festivals. A year later the city instead locked in a deal through 2030, with the per-ticket share rising from $2.33 to $5.01, and a councilman's 6% tax push died. (CBS LA, Jul 5, 2012; Hypebot/LA Times, Apr 5, 2013; KCUR, Apr 18, 2013)
2. **Do LaB's accidental birth.** An art dome in 2005 was wrecked by wind on day one. After the main stages went dark, the crew kept playing through a few small speakers and a crowd showed up. Twenty years later, Billie Eilish's surprise set there drew 20,000+. (FADER, Apr 11, 2025)
3. **The wandering Sahara.** It started at the far edge by the rose garden, grew to 80 ft in 2013, moved onto newly bought Eldorado polo land in 2018, then moved again in 2024 to a 320-ft steel shed on 1,500 tons of concrete poured overnight. Its old home became Quasar, and the Mojave took its rose-garden spot. (Desert Sun, Feb 6, 2024; LA Times, Apr 9, 2023)
4. **The 2024 sales slump, then the lineup moves up.** 2024 was the slowest in a decade and W1 took 27 days to sell out. The next lineup came two months early (Nov 20, 2024) and the one after four months early (Sept 15, 2025), and 2026 sold out in under a week. By 2027, passes are sold in May with no lineup at all. (Wikipedia/Billboard/NME; Pollstar, Sept 19, 2025; NME, Apr 22, 2026)
5. **The radius clause in black and white.** No other North American festival from Dec 15 to May 1, no SoCal shows, and no talking about it until May 7. A small Portland festival sued over SZA and Daniel Caesar, lost, won on appeal, then settled with each side paying its own fees. (Rolling Stone 2018; DMN, Nov 25, 2020)
6. **The Kanye poster.** A 2019 poster with Kanye on it was in production until Jan 1, and the lineup dropped Jan 2. (LA Times, Jan 13, 2019)
7. **Headliners announce themselves.** RATM's reunion dates (2019) and Post Malone's tour announcement (Nov 19, 2024) gave away Coachella headline slots before the poster did, even though the radius clause bars artists from leaking. (Consequence, Dec 30, 2019; HITS, Nov 2024)
8. **The Misfits break the font.** Eurostile LT is the house typeface, broken once for "The Original Misfits" because they were among Goldenvoice's first bookings. (Billboard, c. Nov 2024)
9. **"No one is going to understand it."** Tollett passing on Kate Bush. Also the 2017 headliner pay: $3-4M each, against just under $10K at the bottom of the poster. (SPIN/New Yorker, Apr 2017; mxdwn, Apr 10, 2017)
10. **"Sneaking into the Show" (2016).** The Date Farmers' commissioned piece about Coachella Valley locals who live next to the festival but can't get in. (PBS SoCal Artbound, Apr 15, 2016)
11. **Stagecoach beat Coachella.** In 2024, Stagecoach (89,003) outsold each Coachella weekend (81,690 and 80,148). (KESQ, Jun 13, 2024)
