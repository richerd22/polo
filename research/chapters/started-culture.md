# What Coachella Started - Culture and Business

Research notes for polo.cold.fyi. Compiled Oct 31 2026. Every claim carries URL + publication + date. "Page-read" means the page was fetched and read; "search-result only" means only a headline/snippet was seen (the page was rate-limited, redirected in a loop or failed) - treat those as weaker.

Format per entry: **What happened at Coachella → What it led to → Evidence of the link (sources)**. A "Link strength" line rates how well the causal claim is supported: Strong (a source says so directly), Moderate (timing plus a source naming Coachella as the model), Weak (correlation or our inference).

## 1. FESTIVAL FASHION

### 1.1 "Boho" festival style as "Coachella fashion"
- **At Coachella:** In the mid-2000s, celebrity attendees (Paris Hilton is named) set a boho look that became "Coachella fashion." Then, in the early 2010s, Instagram turned festival clothes into content.
- **Led to:** A recognised "festival look" sold by fast fashion (Forever 21, Topshop, H&M named).
- **Evidence:**
  - Dazed, Sophie Wilson, "When exactly did the Coachella aesthetic become so soulless?", Apr 20 2026 - says Paris Hilton and others set the boho look by the mid-2000s and that fast fashion profited. Quote: "With the rise of Instagram in the early 2010s, Coachella fashion was no longer just about the clothes. It was about content." https://www.dazeddigital.com/fashion/article/70114/1/when-exactly-coachella-aesthetic-soulless-sheinchella-influencers (page-read; "Sheinchella" appears only in the URL, not in the text)
  - Important caveat on origin: boho chic did **not** start at Coachella. CTV News (Apr 18 2022) traces it to Sienna Miller's 2004 **Glastonbury** look and to runway shows (Chloé 2003, Cavalli 2004, Bottega Veneta/DSquared 2005). https://www.ctvnews.ca/entertainment/has-coachella-escaped-the-problematic-legacy-of-boho-chic-1.5866011 (page-read)
  - NYLON Editors, Apr 15 2024 - a year-by-year trend timeline: 2009 forehead headbands, 2017 flower braids, 2018 all-white crochet boho credited to "the Vanessa Hudgens effect," 2019 florals "boosted by the Revolve Festival," 2024 "cowboy cosplay." https://www.nylon.com/fashion/coachella-trends-festival-history (page-read)
- **Link strength:** Moderate. Coachella was the amplifier and the name attached to the look, not where the style was invented.

### 1.2 Flower crowns
- **At Coachella:** Flower crowns were a signature of the festival in the 2010s. NYLON (2018 entry) mentions "a sea of flower crowns."
- **Led to:** Mass-market accessories (ASOS sold flower crowns as festival items, per Retail Brew, Apr 13 2020). By 2022, Glossy reported brands "moving away from flower crowns and boho looks" toward a Euphoria-inspired glitter look.
- **Evidence:** NYLON, Apr 15 2024 (above). Retail Brew, Halie LeSavage, Apr 13 2020, https://www.retailbrew.com/stories/2020/04/13/festival-fashion-revolve-asos-suffers-without-music-festivals (page-read). Glossy, Liz Flora, Apr 5 2022, https://www.glossy.co/beauty/coachellas-comeback-brand-activations/ (page-read). Culture Trip headline "25 Celebrity Instagrams That Prove Coachella Style Has Evolved Past Flower Crowns" (search-result only) https://theculturetrip.com/north-america/usa/california/articles/25-celebrity-instagrams-that-prove-coachella-style-has-evolved-past-flower-crowns
- **Gap:** No source found that names who started the Coachella flower crown or when. See Unconfirmed.

### 1.3 Official retail lines - H&M Loves Coachella (2015)
- **At Coachella:** On Jan 12 2015, H&M announced the "first ever co-branded collection" with Coachella. It was described as "a first of its kind officially branded collection," in H&M's sixth year as a festival sponsor. H&M also supplied part of the official merch and ran its first on-site H&M tent pop-up. The line went on sale Mar 19 on hm.com and in about 350 North American stores, then worldwide on Mar 26.
- **Led to:** A festival brand licensed as a fashion line in mainstream retail. H&M was an eight-time official sponsor, stopped hosting a big customer event in 2018, and flew 22 influencer ambassadors in for 2019 instead (Glossy, Apr 10 2019).
- **Evidence:** H&M Group press release, Jan 12 2015, https://hmgroup.com/news/hm-to-announce-the-first-ever-co-branded-collection-with-the-coachella-valley-music-arts-festival/ (page-read). Glossy, Priya Rao, Apr 10 2019, https://www.glossy.co/fashion/theres-definitely-been-a-decline-fashion-and-beauty-brands-pull-back-on-coachella (page-read).
- **Link strength:** Strong. The line was literally a Coachella product.

### 1.4 Forever 21 / Urban Outfitters "festival shops"
- **At Coachella:** Forever 21 built an online "festival shop" (fringe vests, feathered headpieces, crochet tops) with a Pinterest board. It retweeted Who What Wear: "You can now get everything you need for Coachella at Forever 21."
- **Evidence:** Retail Dive / Mobile Commerce Daily, "Forever 21 celebrates festival season with mobilized shop and social media," date not shown on the archived page (around March, early 2010s). https://www.retaildive.com/ex/mobilecommercedaily/forever-21-celebrates-festival-season-with-mobilized-shop-and-social-media (page-read). Hypebeast (Dylan Kelly, Apr 13 2023) names Levi's, Puma, Guess and Forever 21 as running early-2010s influencer collaborations around the festival. https://hypebeast.com/2023/4/how-coachella-became-influencers-met-gala-style-fashion-evolution-shift (page-read)
- **Gap:** Urban Outfitters' Coachella-specific history was not verified in this pass.

### 1.5 Festival fashion as a retail season
- **At Coachella:** Coachella is treated as the kickoff of "festival season."
- **Led to:** A retail calendar moment big enough to show up in earnings. Revolve "typically earns about 30% of its yearly revenue in the weeks around Coachella." When Coachella 2020 was postponed, Revolve's festival-style orders were about one-fifth of normal. Lyst showed no spike in "festival fashion" searches that year.
- **Evidence:** Retail Brew, Halie LeSavage, Apr 13 2020 (citing NYT reporting), https://www.retailbrew.com/stories/2020/04/13/festival-fashion-revolve-asos-suffers-without-music-festivals (page-read).
- **Link strength:** Strong for Revolve specifically. Note the 30% figure is reported second-hand.

### 1.6 Cultural-appropriation backlash and the headdress bans
- **At Coachella:** In the 2010s, attendees wearing faux Native American war bonnets and bindis drew sustained criticism. Flashpoint: on Apr 9 2014, Victoria's Secret model Alessandra Ambrosio posted an Instagram selfie in a feathered headdress, captioned as getting "inspired for" Coachella. Vanessa Hudgens (bindis) and Kendall Jenner (nath chain) were also criticised. **Coachella itself never adopted a headdress ban** (CTV, 2022).
- **Led to - bans at other festivals (chronological):**
  - **Bass Coast** (Merritt, B.C.): ban posted on Facebook in late July 2014 for the Aug 1-4 festival, the first widely reported ban. "Our core team felt it was time to take a stand." CBC, Aug 2 2014, https://www.cbc.ca/1.2723719 (page-read).
  - **Glastonbury**: banned on-site **sale** of Native American headdresses (wearing them was still allowed), the first UK festival to do so. Organiser Emily Eavis decided Oct 14 2014 after a Change.org petition with only 65 signatures. Dazed, Oct 15 2014, https://www.dazeddigital.com/music/article/22183/1/glastonbury-bans-sale-of-native-american-headdresses (page-read).
  - **Osheaga** (Montreal), plus **Heavy Montreal** and **ÎleSoniq**: bans announced July 2015. NME, Jul 14 2015, https://www.nme.com/?p=1215296 (page-read).
  - **Electric Forest** (Insomniac / Madison House): ban from the 2016 edition. Dancing Astronaut, Lucy Davidson, Nov 17 2015, https://dancingastronaut.com/2015/11/electric-forest-bans-headdresses-festivals-2016-edition/ (page-read).
  - **Outside Lands** (San Francisco): headdresses added to the prohibited-items list. "Out of respect for Native American heritage and culture." SFist, Jul 26 2019, https://sfist.com/2019/07/26/american-indian-headdresses-now-officially-banned-at-outside-lands (page-read).
- **Coachella-specific evidence:** Newspaper Rock, Apr 10 2014, on Ambrosio, https://newspaperrock.bluecorncomics.com/2014/04/alessandra-ambrosio-in-headdress.html (page-read; Native-focused blog). CTV News, Apr 18 2022, says by 2019 "the most offensive examples had largely disappeared from Coachella," and quotes Sage Paul (Indigenous Fashion Arts): "Stealing from other people's nations and calling it creativity is just lazy." https://www.ctvnews.ca/entertainment/has-coachella-escaped-the-problematic-legacy-of-boho-chic-1.5866011 (page-read).
- **Link strength:** Moderate. The bans answered a festival-wide trend that Coachella was the most visible face of. None of the ban statements we read names Coachella.

## 2. INFLUENCER CULTURE

### 2.1 Coachella as "the influencer's Met Gala" - timeline
- **At Coachella:** Heineken became the first major sponsor with branded on-site experiences in 2006, followed by H&M, Amex and Absolut. Early 2010s: fashion brands use the festival for Instagram reach. Around 2014-15: influencers arrive in force with stylists and brand-sponsored houses. 2022: TikTok creators add a new wave.
- **Led to:** The template of the festival as a content shoot. Weekend 1 became the "seen" weekend.
- **Evidence:** Hypebeast, Dylan Kelly, Apr 13 2023, "Coachella has become the influencer's Met Gala." https://hypebeast.com/2023/4/how-coachella-became-influencers-met-gala-style-fashion-evolution-shift (page-read). Variety Australia, Apr 19 2026, quoting an agent: "Weekend 1 is driven heavily by influencer culture." Another insider: "Weekend two is always better... there's more to see and less to be seen." https://au.variety.com/2026/music/news/madonna-sza-why-is-coachella-weekend-2-crushing-weekend-1-35656/ (page-read).
- **Link strength:** Strong as description. "First" is not provable, but Coachella is the case study the trade press uses.

### 2.2 Revolve Festival and paid influencer trips
- **At Coachella:** Revolve first sent influencers to Coachella in 2013 (BoF). Sources disagree on the first Revolve Festival: WWD says Apr 11 **2015** at the Avalon Palm Springs with People StyleWatch (Nikki Reed, Bella Thorne, Victoria Justice; Jhené Aiko performed). BoF says it started in **2016**. It grew into a two-day invite-only festival (Cardi B, SZA, Travis Scott) and peaked at about 5,000 guests in 2022. Revolve's 2019 edition generated over 2 billion social impressions (Glossy 2022).
- **Led to:** BoF's headline argument: "Revolve invented influencer marketing as we know it." The "Revolve Around the World" trips (Dubai, Turks and Caicos) set off a wave of paid influencer travel. Megan Levy of Shine Talent called Raissa Gerona's idea "a genius" one.
- **Evidence:**
  - WWD via Yahoo, Elizabeth Grace Coyne, Apr 12 2024, https://ca.news.yahoo.com/first-ever-revolve-festival-2015-185139744.html (page-read).
  - Business of Fashion, "Revolve invented influencer marketing as we know it. Now it's pulling back," date not shown on the page (it covers 2023 numbers and the 2024 festival, so 2024), https://Businessoffashion.com/articles/marketing-pr/revolve-invented-influencer-marketing-as-we-know-it-now-its-pulling-back/ (page-read).
  - Glossy, Liz Flora, Apr 5 2022, https://www.glossy.co/beauty/coachellas-comeback-brand-activations/ (page-read).
- **Link strength:** Strong.

### 2.3 The Jenners / Hadids / celebrity-as-attendee
- Kendall Jenner's Coachella looks are covered as a recurring style franchise (Who What Wear retrospective, search-result only, undated: https://www.whowhatwear.com/fashion/celebrity-style/kendall-jenner-coachella-fashion-retrospective). CTV (2022) notes Jenner's nath criticism. By 2024, Kendall Jenner's 818 tequila was a sponsor at Revolve Festival (WWD, Apr 12 2024).
- **Gap:** No dated source pins a "first" Jenner/Hadid Coachella or an exact moment the celebrity-attendee era began. Treat it as background, not as a claim.

### 2.4 Off-site party economy and brand houses
- **At Coachella:** By 2012 (the first two-weekend year), BizBash listed a crowded weekend-one circuit: Lacoste L!ve pool parties (with HTC), Armani Exchange/T-Mobile Neon Carnival in a Thermal airport hangar, the Guess Hotel in La Quinta, Nylon/Hugo Boss Escape House, Filter's 10th-year kickoff, Belvedere, the Hard Rock "Music Mansion," Ace Hotel Desert Gold and more. Brands said they stuck to weekend one for the media and the "there first" feeling. Lacoste: the parties "made a bigger splash the first weekend."
- **Neon Carnival:** Brent Bolthouse's invite-only fairground party (ferris wheel, bumper cars, DJs until 4am). "You can't buy your way in." Bolthouse called it "the Vanity Fair party of Coachella's first weekend." In 2019 it expanded to Stagecoach weekend.
  - Founding year conflicts: TheWrap (2014) has Bolthouse dating it to "about 2007" and calls 2014 the eighth edition. BizBash (2012) calls 2012 its "third annual." EARMILK (Apr 4 2019) calls 2019 its 10-year anniversary, which implies 2010.
- **Evidence:** BizBash, Apr 16 2012, https://www.bizbash.com/programming-entertainment/as-coachella-expands-to-two-weekends-brands-stick-to-the-first (page-read). TheWrap, April 2014, https://www.thewrap.com/coachella-neon-carnival-list-get-exclusive (page-read). Hollywood Reporter, 10th-anniversary piece (undated on page; 2019 by context), https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/news/neon-carnivals-brent-bolthouse-shares-partys-10th-anniversary-plans-1201085 (page-read). EARMILK, Apr 4 2019, https://earmilk.com/news/20190404/neon-carnival-celebrates-its-10-year-anniversary-with-expansion-to-stagecoach (search-result only).
- **Link strength:** Strong. The parties exist only because of the festival.

### 2.5 Backlash and decline
- **2018-19 pullback:** Sephora, H&M, Urban Decay, Too Faced, Kiehl's and Maybelline scaled back or dropped VIP experiences. Instead, brands like Anastasia Beverly Hills (185 ambassadors, $1.3M earned media value) and Huda Beauty ($777K EMV) won without a branded venue. Tribe Dynamics' Conor Begley: "There's definitely been a decline in activations, especially larger-branded experiences." Rare Global's Ashley Villa: "A $1,000 VIP wristband doesn't equate to a social campaign that is usually more than $100,000." Glossy, Apr 10 2019 (page-read, URL above).
- **2022, Revolve's "Fyre Fest" weekend (Apr 16-17 2022):** Invited guests were stranded for hours in the heat without water waiting for shuttles, and some were refused entry for not taking the official shuttle. LA Magazine's Joseph Kapsch described being "stranded in the dirt with no water, under the hot sun for hours." Revolve: "As the festival was reaching capacity late Saturday afternoon, shuttle access to the venue was limited... We sincerely apologize to all the guests who were impacted." Hypebae, Apr 19 2022, https://hypebae.com/2022/4/revolve-coachella-festival-fyre-backlash-statement-info (page-read). Also LA Mag on its own viral dispatches: https://www.lamag.com/culturefiles/revolve-festival-apologizes-after-la-mag-coachella-dispatches-went-viral/ (search-result only).
- **2023-24 shrinkage:** The 2024 Revolve Festival was back to one day with about 1,200 guests, down from about 5,000 at the 2022 peak. Revolve marketing spend fell from $181M (2022) to $171M (2023). The 2023 festival drew about half the 2019 EMV (CreatorIQ). CreatorIQ's Alex Rawitz: "they drive [attention]... but there's more negative sentiment." BoF, 2024 (page-read, URL above).
- **2024 ticket softness:** Sales were down 14-17% year over year. Weekend 1 took nearly a month to sell out, against the usual hours. Coachella was still North America's top-grossing annual festival. Billboard, Dave Brooks, Apr 12 2024, https://ca.billboard.com/business/touring/coachella-ticket-sales-lower-than-usual-2024-festival-trend-1235655410 (page-read). Billboard's explanations are brokers, a weaker lineup, prices and dynamic pricing. It does **not** blame influencers, so do not imply that it does.
- **Counterpoint:** Dazed (Apr 20 2026) argues influencer culture has grown, not declined, with top influencer budgets reportedly "high six figures to over a million dollars." So the accurate framing is: big branded venues and Revolve shrank, while influencer content did not clearly decline.

## 3. THE "-CHELLA" SUFFIX (cultural spread, not the lawsuits)

- **What it is:** Linguist Arnold Zwicky classifies "-chella" (meaning "festival") as a "libfix," a liberated affix, building on Nancy Friedman's Apr 25 2022 post. Examples: #Bookchella (LA Times Festival of Books, April 2022), "Combschella" (Sean Combs, blocked 2018) and "Wholechella" (Whole Foods, blocked 2018). Arnold Zwicky's blog, Apr 28 2022, https://arnoldzwicky.org/2022/04/28/bookchella/ (page-read). Friedman's original post was not located.
- **Beychella (2018):** Fans (the BeyHive) coined it for Beyoncé's April 2018 headline set. Homecoming (Netflix, Apr 17 2019) gave it a second wave, and some people use "Beychella" for Coachella itself. Dictionary.com also logs "Arichella" (Ariana Grande, 2019). Dictionary.com pop-culture entry, May 1 2019, https://www.dictionary.com/e/pop-culture/beychella (page-read). NBC News headline "Coachella dubbed 'Beychella' after historic Beyoncé set," https://www.nbcnews.com/pop-culture/music/coachella-dubbed-beychella-after-historic-beyonc-set-n866126 (search-result only).
- **Hoodchella:** An underground one-day LA festival launched 2015 on Coachella's first weekend. Goldenvoice sued in 2016 for an injunction and $100K+. Hypebot, Feb 4 2016, https://www.hypebot.com/coachella-sues-underground-fest-hoodchella-for-100k-over-name (page-read).
- **Brokechella → BROKE LA:** A DIY LA festival started 2011 as a "bargain alternative" to the desert festival, with 4,000+ attendees in 2015. It was renamed on Jan 14 2016. Organiser Negin Singh: "We got too big and *they* wouldn't let us be Brokechella anymore" ("they" is unnamed). Buzzbands.LA, Jan 14 2016, https://buzzbands.la/2016/01/14/brokechella-3/ (page-read).
- **Cowchella → Moo-La-La (Wales):** A Cowbridge festival founded by Ian "H" Watkins (Steps) renamed after a Coachella complaint. Watkins called himself "a threat to their ginormous, juggernaut of a machine." Promotion Centre blog, Jun 4 2025, https://promotioncentre.co.uk/blog/welsh-festival-forced-to-rebrand-after-coachella-complaint/ (page-read; trade blog, so get a news source before publishing).
- **Col-Chella (Collie, Western Australia):** A one-day pub festival on Oct 28 2023 with a stage on the back of a truck. The owner said the name is a play on Coachella. Collie Mail / Daily Liberal (ACM), October 2023, https://www.dailyliberal.com.au/story/8399395/get-sparkly-for-first-col-chella-festival-under-outback-stars/ (page-read). This shows the suffix reached rural Australia.
- **Trademark pressure is ongoing:** World Trademark Review (Nisha Shetty, Jul 25 2025) reports a recent cease-and-desist to a "dog-friendly festival," described as "just the latest legal action against others using the 'chella' suffix." https://www.worldtrademarkreview.com/article/no-you-cant-take-the-chella-out-of-coachella (page-read).
- **"Brochella":** No reliable source found. See Unconfirmed.
- **Link strength:** Strong. Every example derives from the Coachella name.

## 4. BUSINESS MODELS

### 4.1 Two-weekend festival (2012)
- **At Coachella:** In 2011, Paul Tollett explained the move to back-to-back identical weekends for 2012: "We had too many people who wanted to go," and the goal was to "create more room for them." He chose a second weekend over a bigger single weekend. The Boombox, Jun 5 2011 (citing Billboard Biz), https://theboombox.com/coachella-founder-explains-back-to-back-weekends-in-2012/ (page-read).
- **Led to:** **Austin City Limits** went to two weekends in 2013 (Oct 4-6, 11-13). Music Festival Wizard, August 2012: ACL is "Following in the footsteps of mega-festival Coachella." https://www.musicfestivalwizard.com/austin-city-limits-will-be-two-weekends-in-2013/ (page-read). Goldenvoice reused the format for **Desert Trip** (2016), a second weekend added after the first sold out (CNBC, Oct 2016). Even brand parties sorted themselves by weekend (BizBash 2012).
- **Side effect - the "Weekend 1 vs Weekend 2" meme:** Weekend 2 is the running joke as the lesser sequel. By 2026 the trade framing had flipped: Variety asked "Why Is Coachella Weekend 2 Crushing Weekend 1?" after W2 guests including Madonna with Sabrina Carpenter, Olivia Rodrigo with Addison Rae, and SZA and Billie Eilish with Justin Bieber. Variety Australia, Apr 19 2026 (page-read, URL above). The meme's origin is not documented. See Unconfirmed.
- **Link strength:** Strong. ACL coverage names Coachella as the model.

### 4.2 Tiered VIP / Safari tents / glamping
- **At Coachella:** "Coachella Safari Tent" reservations were advertised by Apr 7 2010: a queen bed, AC, outlets, VIP wristbands, shuttle and backstage access, a 2am curfew and one case of beer per person. UrbanDaddy, Apr 7 2010, https://urbandaddy.com/articles/9449/los-angeles/welcome-to-the-jungle-introducing-your-coachella-safari-tent (page-read). A later Palm Springs Life piece (undated) lists 100 VIP Safari Tents at about $6,500 per weekend for two, including passes, golf-cart shuttles and artist-area access. https://www.palmspringslife.com/Haute-Safari/ (page-read). Desert Trip 2016 had safari tents "up to $10,000" (CNBC). In 2017, Marriott ran a Coachella pop-up hotel of tents (Luxury Travel Advisor, Mar 21 2017, search-result only: https://www.luxurytraveladvisor.com/hotels/marriott-unveils-pop-up-hotel-for-coachella-music-festival-2017).
- **Led to:** Festival glamping as a premium tier. The evidence shows Coachella sold it early (2010) and turned it into a hotel-brand partnership.
- **Link strength:** Weak-to-moderate for "pioneered." Glamping existed at UK festivals, and no source here credits Coachella as first.

### 4.3 Payment plans / layaway (2009)
- **At Coachella:** A ticket layaway plan debuted for Coachella 2009 and returned for 2010, with 50% or 10% down and automatic installments through Feb 15. TicketNews, Dec 16 2009, https://www.ticketnews.com/2009/12/coachella-2010-revives-layaway-plan-for-second-year-as-ticket-presales-begin/ (page-read). AOL News, Mar 6 2009, "Pay for concert on the installment plan" (search-result only): https://www.aol.com/news/2009-03-06-pay-for-concert-on-the-installment-plan.html
- **Led to:** Industry-standard festival payment plans (Goldenvoice's Just Like Heaven 2022 listed "Payment plans are available," per Buzzbands Jun 21 2021). By 2025, about 60% of Coachella attendees reportedly used a payment plan (Far Out, search-result only: https://faroutmagazine.co.uk/coachella-2025-ticket-holders-payment-plan/), feeding the buy-now-pay-later debate.
- **Link strength:** Moderate. 2009 is early and well documented, but TicketNews makes no "first" claim.

### 4.4 RFID wristbands - CORRECTION TO THE BRIEF
- **At Coachella:** Intellitix has supplied RFID wristbands since about 2010-11 (Pollstar headline "Intellitix Takes RFID To Coachella," Mar 15 2011, search-result only; the page redirected in a loop). They were used for **access control and anti-counterfeiting, then social check-ins.** In 2014, attendees had to register wristbands online, which gave Goldenvoice demographic data. Intellitix, 2014, https://intellitix.com/hub/coachella-festival-loves-rfid-technology-access-control (page-read; vendor PR).
- **Not cashless RFID:** Coachella was long cash-only. In 2016 it added Square/Apple Pay. By 2017, all vendors took mobile and card payments via Square, but **"Coachella did not use RFID payments in 2017."** Lollapalooza, Mysteryland and ACL had already tried RFID-linked payments. PYMNTS, Apr 18 2017, https://pymnts.com/news/mobile-payments/2017/coachella-goes-cashless-android-pay-samsung-apple-square-pos-vendors-mobile-commerce-rfid-american-express-payment-methods (page-read).
- **So:** Coachella helped popularise RFID **entry** wristbands. It did **not** pioneer RFID **cashless** payments. Lollapalooza went cashless with wristbands in 2014 (Hollywood Reporter headline; the page failed to load).

### 4.5 The AEG/Goldenvoice multi-festival model
Goldenvoice used Coachella's site, staff and brand to spin up genre- and era-specific festivals:
- **Stagecoach** (2007, same Empire Polo Club, the weekend after Coachella): 55K and its first sell-out in 2012, peaking at 80K in 2019. Billed as the highest-grossing country festival. Parts of *A Star Is Born* (2018) were filmed there. Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stagecoach_Festival (page-read; tertiary source).
- **Desert Trip / "Oldchella"** (Oct 7-9 and 14-16 2016): Dylan, Stones, McCartney, Young, The Who, Waters. Tollett told the NYT it grossed about $160M, "the highest-grossing show by far." Ultimate Classic Rock, Jul 22 2016, https://ultimateclassicrock.com/desert-trip-festival-gross/ (page-read). CNBC (Oct 2016) projected about $250M in local impact and quoted Indio Mayor Glenn Miller: "As the concert city of the world..." https://www.cnbc.com/2016/10/07/oldchella-set-to-be-a-boon-of-at-least-250-million-to-coachella-valley.html (page-read).
- **Power Trip** (October 2023, Empire Polo Club): Iron Maiden, Guns N' Roses, Judas Priest, AC/DC, Tool, Metallica. Up to 80K per day, GA from $599. Louder / Metal Hammer, Dec 18 2023, https://www.loudersound.com/features/inside-power-trip-festival-2023 (page-read). It is the Desert Trip format applied to metal.
- **Just Like Heaven** (May 2019, Queen Mary Park, Long Beach; 2022 at Brookside at the Rose Bowl): a lineup of Coachella alumni. Buzzbands.LA, Jun 21 2021, https://buzzbands.la/?p=272873 (page-read).
- **Cruel World** (goth/new wave): announced for May 2 2020, cancelled by COVID. Debuted May 14-15 2022 at Brookside at the Rose Bowl, with a second day added after the first sold out. Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cruel_World_Festival (page-read).
- **Portola** (San Francisco, Pier 80, debut September 2022, Flume and Chemical Brothers). Dancing Astronaut, May 2022, https://dancingastronaut.com/2022/05/san-franciscos-novel-portola-festival-cements-stellar-debut-lineup/ (search-result only).
- **Head in the Clouds**: currently listed by Goldenvoice as "presented with 88rising" (LA, Aug 8 2026).
- **Current portfolio:** Goldenvoice's festivals page (read Oct 31 2026) lists Coachella, Stagecoach, Ocean Way, Portola, **Head Trip** (Oct 10-11 2026, new), Camp Flog Gnaw (partnership), Just Like Heaven, Head in the Clouds, Splash House, Zipangu, Surf Club, Cali Vibes and Cruel World. https://goldenvoice.com/festivals (page-read). C3 Presents is Live Nation's festival arm. Darker Waves (Huntington Beach, debut Nov 18 2023, New Order and Tears for Fears) is better framed as a **competitor copying Goldenvoice's Cruel World nostalgia format.** That supports the "model spread" claim, but it needs a readable confirming source before publishing.

It cites Cruel World, Just Like Heaven and Lovers & Friends, and names All Tomorrow's Parties as an earlier precedent. https://www.insidehook.com/?p=855118 (page-read). So Goldenvoice did not invent the nostalgia festival, but it scaled it.

### 4.6 YouTube livestream as marketing
- **At Coachella:** In 2010 the webcast showed one act at a time and required viewers to "like" sponsor Wrigley on Facebook. In 2011, YouTube streamed three stages on separate channels to nearly 4 million viewers over three days, with Wrigley as sponsor (Ad Age estimated mid-to-high six figures). CNET via CBS News, Daniel Terdiman, Apr 20 2011, https://www.cbsnews.com/news/how-the-coachella-webcast-changed-the-music-biz/ (page-read). Quote from Rob Sheridan (NIN): "An experience is the best kind of scarcity."
- **Led to:** A permanent exclusive. YouTube's multi-year deal (announced January 2023, through 2026) was extended on Sep 23 2026 to run **through 2030**, covering both weekends. YouTube says the 2026 stream set its record for concurrent viewers (no figure given). After his 2026 set, Justin Bieber's video views rose 221% (Vevo). Music Business Worldwide, Sep 23 2026, https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/coachella-stays-exclusive-to-youtube-through-2030-as-goldenvoice-renews-livestream-deal/ (page-read). Unverified: an Indian outlet reports Coachella rejected Netflix and Amazon bids (search-result only: https://madhyamamonline.com/amp/entertainment/coachella-renews-youtube-deal-for-four-more-years-rejects-netflix-and-amazon-bids-1557207).
- **Date discrepancy:** MBW counts "15 years in April 2027," which implies a 2012 start, but streams existed in 2010 and 2011. The likely reading is that the formal Goldenvoice-YouTube partnership dates from 2012 and the earlier streams were sponsor-led.
- **Link strength:** Strong.

### 4.7 Radius clauses and touring
- **At Coachella:** In April 2018, Portland's Soul'd Out festival sued AEG/Goldenvoice. It alleged a radius clause barring Coachella acts from other festivals within more than 1,000 miles (West Coast, Arizona, Nevada) from about Dec 15 to May 7. It said it lost Tank and the Bangas, SZA and Daniel Caesar. Goldenvoice: "will vigorously defend." Scripps News, Apr 12 2018, https://www.scrippsnews.com/us-news/coachella-s-radius-clause-hurting-other-music-festivals (page-read). Pollstar coverage (search-result only): https://news.pollstar.com/2018/04/10/independent-oregon-based-festival-sues-aeg-coachella-for-monopolistic-practices/
- **Effect on touring (culture, not the legal outcome):** The clause shapes the West Coast spring calendar. Headliners route around Coachella, and smaller regional festivals lose bookings. Coachella's late lineup announcements also squeeze other festivals (Sportskeeda headline, search-result only: https://sportskeeda.com/pop-culture/news-how-coachella-s-late-lineup-announcement-impact-u-s-festivals-details-explored-organizers-express-frustration). By 2024, Billboard reported "the number of artists wanting to tour around festivals this year is very small."
- **Caveat:** Radius clauses predate Coachella. Coachella's version became the reference case through the 2018 suit.

## 5. FOOD

- **At Coachella:** Roy Choi's Kogi truck debuted at Coachella around 2009. UPROXX credits it with helping shift festival food toward chef-driven vendors. In 2014, Coachella added LA chefs Josef Centeno (Bäco Mercat) and Kris Yenbamroong (Night + Market), who said he binned about 75% of his food: "people just weren't into it." In 2015 it added Reserve-app sit-down VIP dining ($50) and Outstanding in the Field dinners ($225, two seatings a night, six nights) in the Rose Garden. Vegan chef Nic Adler (Roxy owner) recruited the vendors and later became Culinary Director of Goldenvoice Food. Outstanding in the Field's 2016 chefs included Nyesha Arrington, Michael Voltaggio and Tal Ronnen; prices were $225, $600 with GA or $1,124 with VIP.
- **Led to:** The 2019 "chefs are the new rock stars" era. KAABOO (Richard Blais demos) and BeachLife (Michael Cimarusti pop-up) ran comparable programs. KAABOO CEO Jason Felts: "Chefs are the new rock stars."
- **Evidence:** LA Weekly, Besha Rodell, Apr 15 2015, https://www.laweekly.com/coachella-is-getting-serious-about-food-but-do-festivalgoers-care/ (page-read). Time Out LA, March 2016, https://www.timeout.com/los-angeles/blog/coachella-announces-its-outstanding-in-the-field-chef-lineup-for-2016-030316 (page-read). UPROXX, Steve Bramucci, Oct 17 2019, https://uproxx.com/life/food-at-music-festivals-coachella-kaaboo-beachlife (page-read). KCRW, "Foodchella 2014," search-result only: https://www.kcrw.com/culture/shows/good-food/foodchella-2014-features-a-lineup-of-l-a-chefs-and-upscale-dining
- **Link strength:** WEAK for "a model other festivals copied." LA Weekly (2015) says Outside Lands and Jazzfest rank food just behind music while Coachella ranks it lower, so the food-forward festival predates Coachella's push. UPROXX frames KAABOO and BeachLife as part of a trend, not as copies. Safe framing: Coachella made the LA-chef lineup and the in-festival fine-dining reservation a headline product, while Outside Lands (Taste of the Bay Area) was arguably there first.

## UNCONFIRMED / MYTHS

1. **"Coachella invented boho / festival fashion."** False as stated. The boho chic lineage is Sienna Miller at Glastonbury 2004 plus 2003-05 runways (CTV, 2022). Coachella was the amplifier.
2. **"Coachella banned headdresses."** False. Per CTV (2022), Coachella did not adopt a ban. The bans came from Bass Coast, Glastonbury (sales only), Osheaga, Heavy Montreal, ÎleSoniq, Electric Forest and Outside Lands.
3. **"Coachella pioneered RFID cashless wristbands."** False. It used RFID for entry from about 2011 but was cash-only until about 2016-17, and as of 2017 had no RFID payments (PYMNTS). Lollapalooza did cashless RFID first among majors (2014, per the THR headline).
4. **"Darker Waves is a Goldenvoice festival."** Probably false. The Pollstar headline credits C3 Presents (Live Nation). Needs a readable confirming source.
5. **"Coachella's food program was the model other festivals copied."** Unproven. Outside Lands and Jazzfest were food-forward earlier (LA Weekly, 2015). UPROXX describes a trend, not copying.
6. **Who started Coachella flower crowns.** No dated source found. Vanessa Hudgens is the name most attached to the look, but sources credit her with "boho crochet" (NYLON) and bindis (CTV), not crowns specifically.
7. **First Revolve Festival year.** WWD says 2015 (Avalon Palm Springs). BoF says 2016. Use 2015 with the WWD detail, or say "mid-2010s."
8. **Neon Carnival founding year.** About 2007 (Bolthouse via TheWrap) vs 2010 (BizBash's 2012 "third annual" and EARMILK's 2019 "10th anniversary"). Likely reading: the precursor event dates to about 2007 and the Neon Carnival format to 2010.
9. **Coachella/YouTube start year.** Streams date to 2010 (one act, Facebook-gated) and 2011 (three stages, about 4M viewers). MBW's "15 years in April 2027" implies the formal partnership started in 2012.
10. **"Brochella."** No reliable source found for it as a festival or meme. Leave it out.
11. **"Coachella rejected Netflix and Amazon bids" (2023 renewal).** From a single secondary outlet. Unverified.
12. **"60% of 2025 attendees used payment plans."** Widely repeated (Far Out, mxdwn), but the original source was not read. It is likely a Goldenvoice/AEG statement reported via MSN/LA Times.
13. **Influencers caused the 2024 ticket slump.** Billboard's reporting (Apr 12 2024) names brokers, lineup, price and dynamic pricing, not influencers. Don't imply causation.
14. **Euphoria S3 premiering at Coachella 2026.** Headlines only. Not read.

## BEST STORIES

1. **The 65-signature petition.** Glastonbury, the world's most famous festival, banned headdress sales after a Change.org petition with 65 signatures (Dazed, Oct 15 2014). Six months earlier, a Victoria's Secret model's Coachella-inspired headdress selfie had made the fight mainstream (Newspaper Rock, Apr 10 2014). Coachella itself never banned them.
2. **"Stranded in the dirt with no water."** In 2022, Revolve, the company BoF says "invented influencer marketing as we know it," stranded its own influencers in the desert waiting for shuttles. The internet called it Fyre Fest. Two years later its festival was back to one day and about 1,200 guests, down from 5,000 (Hypebae Apr 19 2022; BoF 2024).
3. **The 2010 like-gate.** To watch Coachella online in 2010, you had to "like" a chewing-gum brand on Facebook, and you saw one act at a time. A year later, 4 million people watched three stages. Sixteen years on, YouTube has locked it up through 2030 (CNET/CBS Apr 20 2011; MBW Sep 23 2026).
4. **Layaway for a music festival, 2009.** In the middle of the recession, Coachella let fans put down 10%. By 2025, a reported 60% of the crowd was paying in installments (TicketNews Dec 16 2009; Far Out 2025).
5. **The "-chella" libfix.** Linguists formally classify "-chella" as a free-floating affix meaning "festival." It shows up in Bookchella, Beychella, Hoodchella, Brokechella, Cowchella (a Welsh village festival run by a member of Steps) and Col-Chella (an Australian pub with a stage on the back of a truck), while Goldenvoice's lawyers keep pushing back (Zwicky Apr 28 2022; Promotion Centre Jun 4 2025; Daily Liberal Oct 2023; WTR Jul 25 2025).
6. **"Oldchella" out-grossed Coachella.** Tollett said Desert Trip's roughly $160M made it "the highest-grossing show by far," ahead of Coachella itself. Seven years later the same field hosted the metal version, Power Trip (UCR Jul 22 2016; Louder Dec 18 2023).
7. **Kris Yenbamroong's 75%.** In 2014, one of LA's most hyped chefs brought his food to Coachella and threw away three-quarters of it: "people just weren't into it." A year later, there were $225 farm dinners in a rose garden (LA Weekly Apr 15 2015).
8. **Weekend 2's revenge.** For a decade "Weekend 2" was the punchline. In 2026, Madonna, Olivia Rodrigo, SZA and Billie Eilish all turned up on W2. An agent's explanation: Weekend 1 "is driven heavily by influencer culture," while W2 has "more to see and less to be seen" (Variety Australia Apr 19 2026).
