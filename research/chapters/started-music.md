# What Coachella Started - Music

Research file for polo.cold.fyi. Compiled Oct 31, 2026. Daft Punk 2006 is left out because it's covered elsewhere.

**Method:** Each entry is laid out as *moment → what it started → evidence*. Every source gives its URL, publication and date. Where the page shows no date, the entry says "undated", with the inferred period in brackets. "(headline only)" means the claim rests on a search-result headline: the page itself wasn't fetched or read. Anything I couldn't confirm is in the Unconfirmed / myths section at the end, not in the main entries.

**Overlap note:** `moments-business.md` in this folder already touches on the hologram, the YouTube stream and the two-weekend format. Those entries here stick to the "spread beyond Coachella" angle.

## A. Reunions launched or proven there

### Pixies - 2004
- **Moment:** The reunited Pixies played the Coachella main stage before Radiohead and "nearly stole the show" (Rolling Stone).
- **What it started:** Coachella's reputation as the place for reunions. The Pixies toured heavily until late 2005 and, by their own account, made more money than they had in their original run ("We all made lots of money").
- **Precision:** Coachella was **not** the first reunion show. The first show back was an unannounced warm-up at the Fine Line Music Café in Minneapolis on Apr 13, 2004, followed by a Canadian run. Coachella was the big public test.
- **Sources:**
  - Rolling Stone, "Flashback: The Pixies Reunite at Coachella in 2004", undated [c. early 2013] - https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-news/flashback-the-pixies-reunite-at-coachella-in-2004-67628/
  - Star Tribune, "Now streaming: Pixies 2004 reunion kickoff at Fine Line", undated - https://www.startribune.com/now-streaming-pixies-2004-reunion-kickoff-at-fine-line-in-minneapolis/600035316
  - Far Out, footage piece, undated - https://faroutmagazine.co.uk/pixies-reunite-coachella-footage/
  - mxdwn: the 2004 Coachella set was later released as a live album, Jan 15, 2021 (headline only) - https://music.mxdwn.com/2021/01/15/news/pixies-release-live-album-of-2004-reunion-at-coachella/

### Bauhaus - 2005
- **Moment:** The band's first show together since 1998. Peter Murphy opened with the nine-minute "Bela Lugosi's Dead" while hanging upside down over the stage like a bat. Afterwards he told the crowd, "Now you can say that you were there!"
- **What it started:** A tour supporting Nine Inch Nails, then the album *Go Away White* (2008), then another split.
- **Sources:**
  - LA Weekly, "Back from the dead: Bauhaus reunited", Oct 30, 2019 - https://www.laweekly.com/back-from-the-dead-bauhaus-reunited/
  - Far Out, undated - https://faroutmagazine.co.uk/bauhaus-performance-coachella/

### The Jesus and Mary Chain - 2007
- **Moment:** The band's first gigs since splitting in 1998. They played a warm-up at the Glass House in Pomona on Apr 26, then the Coachella main stage at dusk on Fri Apr 27. AV Club confirmed Scarlett Johansson as an uncredited backup singer ("she added nothing vocally").
- **What it started:** A long second life for the band, which eventually produced their first album in 18 years (2017, per the mxdwn headline).
- **Date conflict:** mxdwn dates the Coachella set Apr 27. The AV Club byline says "Friday, April 28". Apr 27, 2007 was a Friday, so Apr 27 is probably right.
- **Sources:**
  - mxdwn, Apr 23, 2007 - https://music.mxdwn.com/2007/04/23/news/johansson-teams-with-jesus-mary-chain/
  - AV Club, "Coachella 2007: Friday", 2007 - https://avclub.com/coachella-2007-friday-1798211268
  - mxdwn, Nov 16, 2016 (headline only) - https://music.mxdwn.com/2016/11/16/news/the-jesus-and-mary-chain-announces-the-date-for-their-first-lp-release-in-18-years/amp/

### Rage Against the Machine - 2007, then 2020/2022 attempts
- **Moment:** Coachella confirmed the reunion on Jan 22, 2007. The band headlined the closing night, Apr 29, 2007: their "first show in almost seven years", to "the biggest crowd of the weekend" (Blabbermouth).
  - Two weeks earlier, on Apr 14, 2007, they had played a brief acoustic set at a Chicago rally (Wikipedia).
  - Tom Morello: "the times were right to see if we can knock the Bush administration out in one fell swoop" (Rolling Stone).
- **What it started:** A reunion tour from Apr 29, 2007 to Jul 30, 2011: six legs, 50 shows across North America, Europe, Asia, Australia and South America, ending at L.A. Rising.
- **Later attempts:**
  - RATM were named 2020 headliners (Loudersound, Jan 2020).
  - The pandemic wiped out both the 2020 and 2021 festivals.
  - The 2022 lineup, announced Jan 12, 2022, left them off. Loudwire said they would not "make up their missed headlining slots". They toured on their own instead, starting in El Paso.
  - Zack de la Rocha's Achilles injury (July 2022) led to the 2023 dates being cancelled.
  - On Jan 4, 2024, Brad Wilk wrote that RATM "will not be touring or playing live again."
- **2026:** I found no evidence of a RATM Coachella 2026 booking or attempt. The 2026 headliners were Sabrina Carpenter, Justin Bieber and Karol G. Treat a "2026 attempt" as unconfirmed (see the myths section).
- **Sources:**

  - Rolling Stone, flashback, undated [c. 2020] - https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-news/rage-against-the-machine-tour-reunite-coachella-79633/

### The Police - 2007 (context entry, weak link)
- **Moment:** The reunion launched at the Grammys (Feb 11, 2007). The tour opened in Vancouver on May 27-28, 2007, and grossed about $362M, which made it the third-highest-grossing tour ever at the time.
- **Coachella's role:** Little or none. The Wikipedia tour article doesn't mention a Coachella 2007 appearance, and I didn't verify one.
- **Use:** Don't credit this reunion to Coachella.
- **Source:** Wikipedia, "The Police Reunion Tour", accessed Oct 31, 2026 - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Police_Reunion_Tour

### Pulp - 2012 (not 2011)
- **Moment:** Pulp reformed in 2011 for European shows. Coachella 2012 was the anchor for their first US shows in more than a decade. Radio City (Apr 11, 2012) and the Warfield (Apr 17) were built around the two Coachella sets. It was their first New York show since Jun 16, 1998.
- **What it started:** The US leg of the reunion. Pulp's later North American tour in 2024 is a separate cycle (Rolling Stone headline).
- **Sources:**
  - Spin, Jan 23, 2012 - https://www.spinmagazine.com/2012/01/pulp-confirm-beginnings-first-us-tour-14-years/

  - Rolling Stone, 2024 tour (headline only) - https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-news/pulp-2024-north-american-tour-1234989436/

### At the Drive-In and Refused - 2012
- **Moment:** Both post-hardcore bands reformed with Coachella 2012 as the announced centrepiece. At the Drive-In's first show back was a warm-up at Red 7 in Austin, in April 2012.

### The Stone Roses - 2013 (not 2012)
- **Moment:** The Stone Roses reformed in 2011 and played huge UK and European shows in 2012. Coachella 2013, where they co-headlined with Blur, was "their first scheduled US date since reforming."
- **Sources:**

### Outkast - 2014
- **What it started:** Coachella was the first stop of a 20th-anniversary run of more than 40 festivals (spring and summer 2014). Outkast headlined both Fridays (Apr 11 and Apr 18), with Governors Ball among the later dates. It was described as their first performance together in about a decade.
- **Quote:** Big Boi: "It's truly an honor to celebrate 20 years and still be free to do music the way we choose."
- **Reception:** The weekend-one set had a reportedly mixed reception. I didn't verify this: Pollstar's recap redirect-loops.
- **Sources:**

### LCD Soundsystem - 2016
- **Moment:** Coachella 2016 was announced as the headline slot for the band's un-retirement. A Webster Hall comeback show came first, in March 2016 (FACT).
- **What it started:** A full second act: touring, plus a new album that James Murphy promised.
- **Sources:**

### Guns N' Roses (Axl/Slash/Duff) - 2016 - the biggest commercial payoff
- **Moment:** GN'R were named Coachella headliners on Jan 4, 2016, and headlined both weekends (Apr 16 and Apr 23).
  - The first show back was an unannounced Troubadour gig on Apr 1, 2016.
  - Las Vegas T-Mobile Arena followed on Apr 8-9.
  - Coachella was the festival unveiling.
- **What it started:** The "Not in This Lifetime..." tour, which ran to Nov 2, 2019. It grossed $584.2M from 5.37M tickets, which Wikipedia lists as the fourth-highest-grossing tour ever. Billboard reported $132M and counting early in the run.
- **Sources:**

### Swedish House Mafia - 2012 headline, 2022 return (reunion not launched here)
- **2012:** Festival Insider calls SHM "the first DJ group to headline the main stage."
- **The actual reunion:** It happened at **Ultra Miami, Mar 25, 2018**, on the same stage as their 2013 "final show" (Mixmag, Mar 26, 2018). It did not happen at Coachella.
- **2022:** SHM were already booked for Coachella 2022, and were upgraded to the Sunday headline slot with The Weeknd after Ye dropped out (The Fader, Apr 6, 2022). Coachella was where the reunion went to the main stage, not where it began.
- **Sources:**

### 2NE1 - 2022 (surprise; K-pop reunion)
- **Moment:** CL brought out Dara, Park Bom and Minzy during 88rising's "Head in the Clouds Forever" main-stage set for "I AM THE BEST". It was their first performance together in six years: the group disbanded in 2016, and their last four-member show was the 2015 MAMAs.
- **What it led to:** A full reunion under YG Entertainment and the 15th-anniversary "Welcome Back" tour, announced Jul 21, 2024. ABS-CBN's announcement piece doesn't credit Coachella, so the causal link is inference.
- **Sources:**

### Blink-182 (DeLonge lineup) - 2023
- **Moment:** A surprise late addition to the lineup (Apr 13, 2023). Their Apr 14, 2023 set was the first reunion show with Tom DeLonge, and the first time DeLonge, Hoppus and Barker had played together publicly since 2014. The tour was meant to open in Latin America in March, but that leg was postponed after Barker injured a finger.
  - Hoppus: "Chemo to Coachella. Very much in my feelings today."
- **What it started:** The world tour from May 2023. A week later, Blink-182 also replaced Frank Ocean as weekend-two headliner (see C).
- **Sources:**

### No Doubt - 2024 → Sphere 2026
- **Moment:** Weekend one of Coachella 2024 was No Doubt's first show in nearly a decade (their last was in 2015). Olivia Rodrigo joined for "Bathwater".
  - Gwen Stefani: "I didn't think this was really going to happen, did you?"
- **What it started:** A six-night Las Vegas Sphere residency on May 6, 8, 9, 13, 15 and 16, 2026, announced Oct 10, 2025. AV Club's announcement explicitly references the Coachella reunion.

## B. Careers and scenes it launched or marked

### The Sahara tent and the US EDM boom (beyond Daft Punk)
- **Moment:** The Sahara is nearly as old as the festival. Its first bill was A-Trak, Juan Atkins, Kevin Saunderson and Richie Hawtin, and it started out at the far edge of the grounds.
  - In 2018 Goldenvoice moved it to the centre of the field and made it 25% bigger, with a 250-ft-wide stage.
- **What it started:** Coachella was an early big-stage proving ground, but the evidence says it rode the boom rather than caused it.
  - The LA Times credits the Sahara with helping break pop-EDM wide open (Avicii, Skrillex, Calvin Harris).
  - Festival Insider: electronic music "roared back" in 2011, "coinciding with" the nationwide EDM boom. It doesn't say Coachella caused the boom.
  - Its timeline: 2005 was the low point for electronic bookings; in 2012 SHM became the first DJ group to headline the main stage.
- **Artist datapoints:**
  - **deadmau5** played the Sahara on Friday night in 2010, opposite Jay-Z, with "bombastic lights and razor-edged electro-house" (LA Weekly, Apr 17, 2010).
    - Coda: on Apr 18, 2025 he left a Testpilot b2b with ZHU early while visibly drunk, then posted "Probably my last Coachella" (Stereogum, Apr 21, 2025).
  - **Skrillex** had already played "major music festivals like Coachella" by 2011 (LA Weekly, Oct 20, 2011). He later became a surprise-set regular: in 2017 he replaced Kaskade at Do LaB (The Fader, 2025).
  - **Odd Future** played the Sahara on Apr 15, 2011. It was a chaotic set (Pharrell joined). The LA Times says it "launched or boosted" Frank Ocean, Tyler, the Creator, Syd and Steve Lacy. Tyler later headlined Coachella (Rolling Stone CA, headline only).
- **Sources:**

  - LA Weekly, Apr 17, 2010 - https://www.laweekly.com/deadmau5-blazes-new-trails-in-the-desert/
  - LA Weekly, Oct 20, 2011 - https://www.laweekly.com/skrillex-l-a-dj-producer-is-boy-king-of-electronic-dance-music/

### Do LaB - the "festival inside the festival"
- **Moment:** Brothers Dede, Josh and Jesse Flemming brought an art installation with a water feature (which didn't work well) to the middle of the field in 2005. A late-night party grew out of it when they kept the music playing after the festival's own sound shut off.
  - In 2006 Paul Tollett put DJs in their dome.
  - Dede Flemming: "they give us an inch, we take a mile."
- **What it started:** The surprise-set tradition.
  - The first big surprise from outside their own circle was Bonobo in 2015.
  - Skrillex replaced Kaskade in 2017, a move Goldenvoice weighed in on.
  - Billie Eilish played a surprise Do LaB DJ set on Apr 13, 2024, previewing *Hit Me Hard and Soft* (Stereogum, headline only).
  - Diplo, ZHU and ODESZA have all played the stage.
- **Correction to the brief:** It is not cleanly "independent". The Fader shows the festival deciding Do LaB's placement and weighing in on its plans. Do LaB also runs its own festival, Lightning in a Bottle, so it does have its own scene: Pollstar headline "From Coachella To Lightning In A Bottle", Apr 4, 2024 (headline only).
- **Sources:**
  - The Fader, Apr 11, 2025 - https://www.thefader.com/2025/04/11/history-of-do-lab-stage-coachella-20-anniversary-photos
  - NPR, Apr 22, 2022 (headline only) - https://www.npr.org/2022/04/22/1093356468/coachellas-music-fan-fave-do-lab-is-back
  - Stereogum, 2024 (headline only) - https://stereogum.com/2259288/billie-eilish-previews-new-songs-in-surprise-coachella-dj-set/news

### Bad Bunny - debut 2019 → first Latin headliner 2023
- **Moment:** At his 2019 debut he asked the crowd, "I want to know where the Latinos are!" J Balvin joined him on weekend one (Billboard, Apr 15, 2019, via Wikipedia). In 2023 he became the first Latin artist to headline (Rolling Stone).
- **What it started:** Latin headlining became repeatable. In 2026 Karol G became the first Latina headliner. She closed the festival, was joined by Becky G, Mariah Angeliq and Wisin on weekend one, and by Peso Pluma, J Balvin and others on weekend two.

### BLACKPINK and the K-pop arc
- **Moment:** In 2019 BLACKPINK were the first K-pop girl group to perform at Coachella. Lisa: "Is this real or is this a computer effect?" In 2023 they became the first K-pop and first Korean headliners (Rolling Stone; Wikipedia).
- **Wording caution:** "First Asian headliner" is how Yahoo framed it (headline only). Rolling Stone and ABC7 say first K-pop / first Korean headliner. Use "first K-pop headliner".
- **The arc:**
  - EE in 2011 and Epik High in 2016 were earlier Korean acts (KpopStarz).
  - 2022: aespa made their US festival debut, 2NE1 reunited, and Jackson Wang played the main stage.
  - 2026: KATSEYE performed "Golden" with HUNTR/X (The Fader, Apr 13, 2026).

### 88rising / Head in the Clouds
- **The direction is the reverse of the brief.**
  - 88rising's own festival came first: Head in the Clouds launched Sep 22, 2018 at LA State Historic Park. Outlets nicknamed it the "Asian Coachella", and Goldenvoice organised the 2021 edition (Wikipedia; Rolling Stone headline).
  - The Coachella milestone is **2022**. "Head in the Clouds Forever" on the main stage was "the first label-curated set ever to hit the festival's main stage" (Billboard). Hikaru Utada, who had "never played a festival, even in Asia", appeared, alongside CL, the 2NE1 reunion and Jackson Wang.
  - Afterwards the festival kept expanding: the Rose Bowl, Jakarta and Manila in 2022, Forest Hills in 2024, and Makuhari Messe (Japan), scheduled Mar 28-29, 2026.
- **2019 Coachella set:** Not verified (see myths).
- **Sources:**

### Afrobeats: Burna Boy → Wizkid
- **2019:**
  - Burna Boy and Mr Eazi were the only African acts on the bill, both in the third tier of the poster.
  - Burna Boy on Instagram Live: "I am an AFRICAN GIANT and will not be reduced to whatever that tiny writing means."
  - Wizkid had reportedly missed a 2018 slot because of visa problems. Black Coffee played in 2016 and was rebooked on a bigger stage in 2018 (Quartz).
- **What it started:** Burna Boy named his July 26, 2019 album *African Giant* after the poster complaint, and it was nominated for a Grammy (Best World Music Album). He talked about poster font sizes on The Daily Show (Stereogum, headline only).
  - **2023:** a high-billed, full-band set (Yahoo, headline only). Tems also played that year.
  - **2026:** Wizkid ("Essence") and Tems joined Justin Bieber's headline set.

### Billie Eilish - 2019 → 2022 headliner → 2024 Do LaB → 2026 cameo
- **2019:** She brought *When We All Fall Asleep...* to Coachella in 2019, with Vince Staples as a guest.
- **2022:** She headlined at 20, the youngest headliner ever, with Gorillaz' Damon Albarn as a guest. "This is the craziest thing I've ever experienced."
- **2024:** Surprise Do LaB set previewing *Hit Me Hard and Soft*.
- **2026:** On weekend two Justin Bieber serenaded her; she didn't sing (Variety).

### Beyoncé - "Beychella" 2018 → *Homecoming* 2019 → HBCU visibility
- **Moment:**
  - Beyoncé had been booked for 2017 but pulled out on doctor's orders during her pregnancy. Lady Gaga took the slot, and Beyoncé moved to 2018 (ET; Live for Live Music, headlines only).
  - She performed Apr 14 and 21, 2018, the first Black woman to headline. The set used a full marching band, majorettes, a step show and strolling pledges, all drawn from HBCU and Black Greek life.
  - It drew 458K concurrent viewers, the most-viewed livestreamed performance at the time, and 41M total viewers.
- **What it started:**
  - Netflix released *Homecoming* and a live album on Apr 17, 2019, reported as the first of a three-project, $60M deal. The film won the Grammy for Best Music Film.
  - It increased interest in HBCUs: some high-school seniors cited it, and Beyoncé expanded her HBCU scholarship programme to eight schools (Wikipedia, summarising sources).
- **Spillover:** The bar it set changed how headliners approach Coachella. Ariana Grande's production designer said Beyoncé's set raised expectations so much that the festival had become a "competition" (Hollywood Reporter, Apr 2019).

## C. Production and business firsts that spread

### 2Pac "hologram" - 2012 → the hologram-tour industry
- **Moment:** A digital Tupac performed with Dr. Dre and Snoop Dogg in 2012.
  - Digital Domain made the digital Tupac, and AV Concepts handled the projection.
  - It wasn't really a hologram: it was a 2D Pepper's Ghost projection on Mylar foil, "a theatrical technique first outlined more than 430 years ago".
  - It won the Cannes Lions Titanium award (June 2012).
  - Dr. Dre ruled out touring it: "It was strictly for Coachella."
- **What it started:** A legal and commercial fight over the technique. Hologram USA and Musion Das sued to stop the Michael Jackson projection at the 2014 Billboard Music Awards, claiming patents on "the technology that brought Tupac to Coachella 2012". They lost (Spin, May 2014).
  - BASE Hologram's "In Dreams: Roy Orbison" tour, 28 dates from Oct 1, 2018, was explicitly framed with Tupac at Coachella as precedent (WSB-TV/Cox, Jul 20, 2018). The Whitney Houston hologram tour came from the same company (Philly Mag headline, "It's Official: Dead Musicians Are Now Touring as Holograms").
- **ABBA Voyage:** I found **no** sourced link to Coachella. Voyage uses digital "ABBAtars", not Pepper's Ghost. Don't connect them without a source.
- **Sources:**
  - Andscape, Apr 14, 2017 (Aaron Dodson) - https://andscape.com/features/the-strange-legacy-of-tupacs-hologram-after-coachella
  - Spin, May 2014 - https://www.spin.com/2014/05/michael-jackson-hologram-billboard-music-awards/
  - WSB-TV, Jul 20, 2018 - https://wsbtv.com/living/roy-orbison-30-years-gone-will-tour-as-a-hologram/794222878
  - The Week (headline only) - https://theweek.com/articles/476386/coachellas-astonishing-tupac-shakur-hologram-how-did

### Festival livestreaming at scale - YouTube 2011
- **Moment:** In April 2011 YouTube streamed about 60 acts from three stages, each with its own channel and multiple camera angles, sponsored by Wrigley. Nearly 4M people watched.
  - The 2010 stream showed one act at a time, and viewers had to "like" Wrigley's Facebook page to watch on that site.
  - Industry voices called it "definitely the most significant in terms of artist choice." Nine Inch Nails' Rob Sheridan: "An experience is the best kind of scarcity" (CNET via CBS, Apr 20, 2011).
- **What it started:**
  - Three months later YouTube signed on to stream Lollapalooza and ACL (AP, Jul 23, 2011). It had already streamed Bonnaroo and Outside Lands.
  - Coachella records: Beyoncé's 458K concurrent viewers in 2018 (above), and both weekends streamed for the first time in 2019 (USA Today, Apr 8, 2019, via Wikipedia).
  - The YouTube deal reached its 10th year in 2022 (Pollstar, headline only, page redirect-loops).
- **Sources:**
  - CBS News/CNET, Apr 20, 2011 - https://www.cbsnews.com/news/how-the-coachella-webcast-changed-the-music-biz/

  - Wikipedia, "Coachella 2019" - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coachella_2019

### The two-weekend model - 2012
- **Moment:** Announced Jun 1, 2011: "two identical festival weekends", with the same lineup, art and location but different attendees, because 2011 sold out so fast that many fans couldn't go (CBS, Jun 1, 2011).
- **What it started:**
  - **ACL** went to two weekends in 2013, "following in the footsteps of mega-festival Coachella" (Music Festival Wizard, c. Aug 2012). The City of Austin approved it in Aug 2012 (Consequence headline).
  - **Ultra Miami** ran two weekends in 2013, then returned to one weekend in 2014 (Spin and Music Festival Wizard, headlines only).
  - **Tomorrowland** ran two weekends for its 10th anniversary in 2014 (Dancing Astronaut, Dec 2013, headline only). That is inference: I didn't verify a Coachella reference.
- **Sources:**
  - CBS News, Jun 1, 2011 - https://www.cbsnews.com/news/coachella-expanding-to-two-weekends-in-2012

  - Dancing Astronaut, Dec 2013 (headline only) - https://dancingastronaut.com/2013/12/tomorrowland-set-for-two-weekends-in-2014/

### Bespoke headline production and the stage-design fights
- **Evidence:**
  - Kanye's 2011 set had a stone Pergamon-style mural, ballerinas, and a lift reportedly used on Michael Jackson's last tour (Complex, 10-year retrospective). Complex calls it unprecedented but gives no evidence that it changed industry norms.
  - In 2019 Ye walked away from headlining over the "artistically limiting" 40x60-ft stage, after asking at the last minute for a custom dome (Dancing Astronaut, Jan 2, 2019; Wikipedia).
  - By 2026, Wallpaper* reviewed headline sets as design objects: Stufish built Sabrina Carpenter's "Sabrinawood" and Bieber's minimal set; Karol G had a multi-level cavern with a pool in the crowd. High winds cancelled Anyma's set (Wallpaper*, Apr 13, 2026).
- **Verdict:** Coachella is clearly a showcase for custom headline production. I found no source proving it "set the norm" for festivals generally.

### Frank Ocean - 2023: the anti-headline set
- **Moment:** Frank Ocean on Apr 16, 2023, his first big appearance since *Blonde* (2016):
  - He kept his set off the YouTube stream and started about an hour late, at 10:55pm.
  - An ice rink that had been planned for months was dismantled after he cut the skaters. He had hurt his ankle in rehearsals.
  - Big screens hid him and the band. The set ended abruptly at curfew.
  - Goldenvoice was fined $133K for breaking curfew (Ocean, Bad Bunny and Calvin Harris all contributed).
  - He withdrew from weekend two on Apr 19. Blink-182 took his slot, with Skrillex, Four Tet and Fred again.. added to close.
- **What it started:** A cautionary case study rather than a trend. Billboard reported the withdrawal cost the festival millions (Apr 21, 2023, via Wikipedia). There's no evidence of imitators.
- **Sources:**
  - Wikipedia, "Coachella 2023" (citing Variety, Apr 17; NYT, Apr 19; SFGATE, Apr 19; Billboard, Apr 21, 2023), accessed Oct 31, 2026 - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coachella_2023
  - Rolling Stone (headline only) - https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-news/frank-ocean-cancels-coachella-headliner-weekend-two-1234716648/

### The surprise-guest tradition, and its 2026 shift to weekend two
- **The tradition:** The examples above (2015 Madonna, 2019 *NSYNC, 2022 Shania, 2024 Olivia Rodrigo) show surprise guests as house style.
- **2026:** Nearly every major surprise landed on weekend two:
  - Madonna joined Sabrina Carpenter for three songs, timed to her new single.
  - SZA and Sexyy Red joined Bieber.
  - Olivia Rodrigo joined Addison Rae.
  - Snoop Dogg joined Giveon.
- **Why:** Sources gave several reasons:
  - Weekend one is "driven heavily by influencer culture".
  - Guests protect the headliner's weekend-one spotlight.
  - "Weekend two is always better... more to see and less to be seen."
  - Sources said it was not a coordinated plan by Goldenvoice or YouTube.

## Unconfirmed / myths

- **"Stone Roses reunited at Coachella 2012."** Wrong year. They played Coachella in 2013, their first US date since reforming. The reunion started in the UK in 2011-12.
- **"Pulp reunited at Coachella 2011."** Wrong. The reunion began in 2011 in Europe. Coachella came in 2012 and marked their US return.
- **"Swedish House Mafia reunited at Coachella."** No. The reunion was at Ultra Miami, March 2018. Coachella 2022 was their headline return; they had been co-headliners in 2012.
- **"Pixies' first reunion show was Coachella."** No. The Fine Line in Minneapolis, Apr 13, 2004, came first. The exact Coachella date (probably May 1, 2004) isn't verified.
- **"The Police reunion was launched at Coachella 2007."** Not supported. It launched at the Grammys (Feb 2007) and the tour opened in Vancouver in May 2007. I didn't verify any Coachella appearance.
- **RATM "2026 attempt".** No source found. The last verified Coachella episode is the 2020/2021 cancellations and the 2022 omission. The band said in Jan 2024 it won't play live again.
- **"88rising's Coachella 2019 set led to Head in the Clouds."** The order is backwards: HITC launched in Sep 2018. I couldn't confirm a 2019 88rising Coachella set from any fetched source. The verified Coachella moment is the 2022 main-stage "Head in the Clouds Forever".
- **"BLACKPINK: first Asian act to headline."** Safer wording is "first K-pop / first Korean headliner" (Rolling Stone, Wikipedia, ABC7). "First Asian" appears only in Yahoo's headline.
- **"Do LaB is independent of Goldenvoice."** Only partly true. The Fader shows the festival placing and steering it. It does run its own festival (Lightning in a Bottle).
- **"Coachella caused the US EDM boom."** Unproven. Sources say Coachella's 2011 electronic resurgence "coincided" with the boom, and credit Beatport, YouTube and social media for driving it.
- **"Coachella set the LED/stage production norm for festivals."** No source proves it. Coachella is a showcase, and "the Beyoncé bar" is documented, but only within Coachella.
- **"ABBA Voyage descends from the Tupac hologram."** No sourced link found, and it's a different technology. Leave it out.
- **"Sunday Service's first public performance was Coachella."** Plausible: it was invite-only before. No fetched source says "first public".
- **Outkast 2014 "flopped".** Widely repeated. I didn't verify it with a fetched source (Pollstar's recap redirect-loops).
- **Ariana "youngest headliner".** True in 2019 (25), superseded by Billie Eilish in 2022 (20).

## Best stories

1. **GN'R's money shot.** "Not in This Lifetime" was the band's own joke about ever reuniting. The Coachella unveiling became a $584M tour, the fourth-biggest ever.
2. **"I am an AFRICAN GIANT."** Burna Boy objected to his small type on the 2019 poster, named his next album after the complaint, and got a Grammy nomination for it. Seven years later Wizkid and Tems were guests in a headline set.
3. **Prince vs. Radiohead vs. Prince.** He covered "Creep", had the videos taken down, Thom Yorke said "It's our … song", Prince reposted it, and then his own publishing company took it down again.
4. **Tupac, the 430-year-old trick.** The "hologram" was Pepper's Ghost on Mylar. Dre said it was "strictly for Coachella", but a patent lawsuit over Michael Jackson and a Roy Orbison tour followed anyway.
5. **Peter Murphy upside down.** Bauhaus came back after seven years apart with a nine-minute opener sung hanging like a bat: "Now you can say that you were there!"
6. **"Chemo to Coachella."** Mark Hoppus, after cancer treatment: Blink-182's surprise reunion was added the day before, and a week later they replaced Frank Ocean as weekend-two headliner.
7. **The ice rink that wasn't.** Frank Ocean's rink was planned for months and taken apart on the day. He started an hour late, kept the set off the stream, broke curfew ($133K fine) and was gone by weekend two.
8. **Weekend two's revenge (2026).** Madonna, SZA and Olivia Rodrigo all landed on weekend two, because weekend one is "driven heavily by influencer culture."
9. **Do LaB's inch and mile.** A water feature that didn't work and a party after the official music stopped grew into the stage where Billie Eilish previews albums as a surprise DJ set.
