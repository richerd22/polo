# PART 1 - THE MOMENTS

## 1999 origin, the skipped 2000, the 2001 return

- Before Coachella: Pearl Jam played about 25,000 people at the Empire Polo Club on Nov 5, 1993, after refusing to play Los Angeles during its Ticketmaster dispute. This is the site's first big show.
  - Wikipedia "Coachella", citing Steve Hochman, "Pearl Jam Blossoms in Desert," Los Angeles Times, Nov 8, 1993, p. F3 - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coachella (accessed Oct 31, 2026)
- The first festival ran Oct 9-10, 1999, at the Empire Polo Club in Indio. Headliners were Beck, Tool and Rage Against the Machine. The undercard included the Chemical Brothers, Morrissey, A Perfect Circle, Jurassic 5 and Underworld. It took place in intense heat.
  - Nina Frazier, GRAMMY.com, Oct 9, 2024 - https://www.grammy.com/news/first-coachella-valley-music-festival-1999
- Paul Tollett reported a loss of $850,000.
  - GRAMMY.com, Oct 9, 2024 (above)
- Attendance was about 17,000 on day one and about 20,000 on day two, short of a 70,000 goal. Day tickets cost $50.
  - Wikipedia "Coachella" (footnotes 16, 18 and 22; their titles were not exposed) - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coachella
  - Far Out (n.d.) gives "about 37,000 over two days," which matches - https://faroutmagazine.co.uk/rage-against-the-machine-returned-fee-coachella-festival/
- Tom Morello says the festival "lost so much money they asked us for our fee back." Far Out reports that RATM returned half the fee "because they were friends."
  - GRAMMY.com, Oct 9, 2024; Far Out Magazine, n.d. (URLs above)
- Tollett's New Yorker profile says the loss came to roughly $850K-$1M and nearly bankrupted Goldenvoice. Tollett sold his house and eventually his car, and the festival took four years to turn a profit.
  - John Seabrook, "The Mastermind Behind Coachella," The New Yorker, Apr 17, 2017 issue (author and date from Longform: https://longform.org/posts/the-mastermind-behind-coachella). Details come secondhand from Ryan Fricke, mxdwn, Apr 11, 2017 - https://music.mxdwn.com/2017/04/11/news/coachella-co-founder-discusses-booking-desert-trip-the-anschutz-anti-lgbt-scandal-and-how-the-first-festival-almost-bankrupted-goldenvoice/ (mxdwn misprints the year as "1990")
- In 2000 the festival was skipped. Goldenvoice cancelled a planned October 2000 date, and Tollett blamed Southern California festival oversaturation. Goldenvoice co-staged Nocturnal Wonderland that September instead.
  - GRAMMY.com, Oct 9, 2024 (skipped year); Wikipedia "Coachella" (reason; footnotes 20 and 26)
- The festival returned in April 2001 as a one-day event. Jane's Addiction headlined, tickets were $65 and about 32,000 attended. Far Out says it made a profit.
  - GRAMMY.com, Oct 9, 2024 (single day); Wikipedia "Coachella" (price, attendance and headliner; footnotes 26 and 28-31); Far Out, n.d. (profit)

## Daft Punk's pyramid, 2006

- Daft Punk played the Sahara Tent at 11 PM on Saturday, Apr 29, 2006. The tent was built for about 10,000 people and reportedly held more than 40,000.
  - Kristin Robinson, Water & Music, Apr 29, 2021 - https://www.waterandmusic.com/15-years-ago-daft-punks-coachella-set-helped-spark-an-arms-race-for-live-music-technology
- Martin Phillips designed the stage: an LED pyramid of Barco OLite panels, with VersaTubes and pixel curtains from Element Labs. All of it was bought off the shelf rather than invented for the show. The duo put some of their advance into the production.
  - Water & Music, Apr 29, 2021 (above)
- Water & Music says the show set off an "arms race" in live production tech. It also led to the Alive 2006/2007 tour, whose live album won a Grammy.
  - Water & Music, Apr 29, 2021 (above)

## Prince, 2008

- Prince headlined night two on Apr 26, 2008. He played an eight-minute cover of Radiohead's "Creep" and brought out Sheila E. for "The Glamorous Life."
  - Colleen Cowie, The Current (MPR), Jan 3, 2019 - https://www.thecurrent.org/feature/2019/01/03/remember-when-prince-ruled-coachella-and-controversially-covered-radiohead-in-2008
- Fan video of "Creep" spread the next day and Prince's team had it taken down. Thom Yorke objected that the band should decide. Prince let the clip back online in 2015 by tweeting an NME link to it.
  - The Current, Jan 3, 2019 (above)

## The 2Pac "hologram", 2012

- Tupac appeared during Dr. Dre and Snoop Dogg's set on Apr 15, 2012. It was Dre's idea and he commissioned it. Nick Smith of AV Concepts: "It was Dre's idea to bring Tupac back."
  - Aaron Dodson, Andscape (The Undefeated), Apr 14, 2017 - https://andscape.com/features/the-strange-legacy-of-tupacs-hologram-after-coachella
- Digital Domain (James Cameron's VFX company) built the digital Tupac. VFX supervisors Janelle Croshaw and Steve Preeg led a team of about 20. AV Concepts of San Diego handled the projection and used Mylar foil instead of glass.
  - Andscape, Apr 14, 2017 (above); TIME Techland (n.d., Apr 2012) - https://techland.time.com/?p=128659
- The technique was not a real hologram. It was Pepper's ghost, a 19th-century stage illusion that reflects an image off an angled transparent surface, here a custom 30 x 13 ft screen that could be lowered fast. Hypebeast and Wikipedia name UK firm Musion's "Eyeliner" system as the underlying tech.
  - TIME Techland (above); John Ifejika, Hypebeast, Apr 16, 2012 - https://hypebeast.com/2012/4/tupac-hologram-cost-10-million; Wikipedia "Coachella" (footnotes 63-68)
- It took about four months to make, according to Nick Smith via MTV.
  - Hypebeast, Apr 16, 2012; TIME Techland (both citing MTV)
- On cost: Smith would not disclose the real figure, but said a comparable piece could run from $100,000 to more than $400,000 (via MTV). A "$10 million" figure went around in April 2012 with no named source. Treat it as rumor.
  - Hypebeast, Apr 16, 2012 (above)
- Afeni Shakur reportedly gave permission and said she was thrilled.
  - Wikipedia "Coachella" (footnote 68; primary source not checked)
- Five months later, Digital Domain Media Group filed for Chapter 11 and shut its Port St. Lucie, Florida operation. Most of the 320 staff there were let go and the CEO resigned.
  - CNNMoney, Sep 11, 2012 - https://money.cnn.com/2012/09/11/technology/digital-domain-bankrupt/
- The digital Tupac asset "has been archived," and only the estate has access.
  - Andscape, Apr 14, 2017

## Beyonce, 2018 ("Beychella")

- Beyonce was the first Black woman to headline Coachella. She played both weekends of 2018. GRAMMY.com gives "April 13 and 22"; see the date conflict in "Unconfirmed". About 200 people were on stage, styled as an HBCU homecoming.
  - Jennifer Velez, GRAMMY.com, Apr 19, 2019 - https://grammy.com/news/beyonce-created-space-celebrate-black-culture-coachella-more-things-homecoming-taught
- Rehearsals ran four months for the band, then four months for the dancers.
  - GRAMMY.com, Apr 19, 2019 (above)
- She was first booked for 2017 but withdrew on doctors' advice during her pregnancy.
  - GRAMMY.com, Apr 19, 2019 (above)
- Netflix released the documentary and live album *Homecoming* on Apr 17, 2019.
  - GRAMMY.com, Apr 19, 2019 (above)

  - CNN, "Coachella to be postponed over coronavirus concerns, sources say," Mar 10, 2020 (via Wikipedia footnote 3)

## Frank Ocean, 2023

- The 2023 headliners were Bad Bunny, Frank Ocean and BLACKPINK, the first year every headliner was nonwhite. BLACKPINK were the first K-pop group to headline. Bad Bunny was the first Latin American and first Spanish-language headliner (per Pitchfork).
  - CBS News, Jan 10, 2023 - https://www.cbsnews.com/amp/news/coachella-headlined-entirely-nonwhite-artists-first-time-festival-history-bad-bunny-frank-ocean-blackpink
- Frank Ocean came on about an hour late, wearing slippers. A planned segment with figure skaters on an ice rink was scrapped. A source told Rolling Stone that Ocean hurt his ankle during on-site rehearsals the week before, and the show was reworked at the last minute.
  - Rolling Stone, Apr 2023 (date not shown on page) - https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-news/frank-oceans-coachella-set-derailed-by-ankle-injury-source-claims-1234716595/
- About the skaters: Rolling Stone mentions an LMU hockey player's Instagram Story showing nine men in skating gear captioned "The Boys x Frank." That crew never performed.
  - Rolling Stone (above)
- TMZ reported a bike accident on the festival grounds, with doctors advising the set be changed. X account TheFestiveOwl claimed the rink was built, then melted and torn out, and that about 100 LA hockey players had rehearsed on an elevated rink at Paramount Studios before being moved to background-dancer roles. Stereogum labels the FestiveOwl account unverified. Treat it as reported, not established.
  - Rachel Brodsky, Stereogum, Apr 17, 2023 - https://stereogum.com/2220709/frank-ocean-scrapped-coachella-ice-rink-after-breaking-his-ankle-report/news
- The livestream was pulled. YouTube posted: "Frank Ocean is not scheduled to appear on the Coachella livestream." Bjork's set also came off the stream schedule. No official reason was given.
  - Aaron Chow, Hypebeast, Apr 16-17, 2023 - https://hypebeast.com/2023/4/frank-ocean-coachella-livestream-cancelled-news-info
- His team's statement on the weekend 2 withdrawal: he was injured on festival grounds the week before weekend 1, and "On doctor's advice, [he] is not able to perform weekend 2 due to two fractures and a sprain in his left leg." Blink-182 replaced him, per Variety.
  - Rachel Brodsky, Stereogum, Apr 19, 2023 - https://stereogum.com/2220996/frank-ocean-pulls-out-of-coachella-weekend-2/news
- Ocean said onstage that he was there for his late brother Ryan Breaux, who died in 2020.
  - Rolling Stone, Apr 2023 (above)

## "They built a bunker in the desert this year" (2026) - RESOLVED

The bunker is Radiohead's Motion Picture House - KID A MNESIA: a purpose-built underground film-and-art installation on the polo grounds. It was not a concert, a stage, a VIP space or a brand activation.

- The poster's last line read "The Bunker Debut of Radiohead Kid A Mnesia." It started speculation about whether Radiohead would play.
  - Far Out Magazine (n.d., fall 2025) - https://faroutmagazine.co.uk/what-does-the-bunker-debut-of-radiohead-kid-a-mnesia-mean/
- Permit trail: in September 2025, Goldenvoice applied to the City of Indio for a 16,130 sq ft "commercial foundation." City assessors valued it at $2.5M. Reporters found a large sand pit with steel reinforcement near the Sahara tent. The concrete foundation and steel framing pointed to a permanent structure. A Rolling Stone rep confirmed Radiohead would not perform.
  - Sam Morgen, Palm Springs Desert Sun, syndicated on AOL (date not shown; before Apr 2026) - https://www.aol.com/news/structure-being-built-coachella-festival-224256302.html
- What it was: about 17,000 sq ft with 38 ft ceilings, built beneath the Empire Polo Fields. All festival ticket holders could get in. It held the *KID A MNESIA* film and galleries of full-scale Thom Yorke and Stanley Donwood artwork from the *Kid A*/*Amnesiac* sessions.
  - Will Schube, FLOOD Magazine, Apr 8, 2026 - https://floodmagazine.com/220098/radiohead-kid-a-mnesia-motion-picture-house-coachella
  - Chris DeVille, Stereogum, Apr 8, 2026 - https://stereogum.com/2495193/radiohead-are-taking-their-coachella-bunker-art-installation-on-tour/news
- The sound was a "6-point surround" mix built from the original *Kid A*/*Amnesiac* multitracks. Sean Evans directed the film. *KID A MNESIA* first came out in 2021 as a free digital exhibition for PS5, PC and Mac. Ed O'Brien said the band will play 20 shows a year from 2027.
  - Stereogum, Apr 8, 2026 (above)
- Background: the physical 20th-anniversary installation planned in 2021 was cancelled over logistics and COVID.
  - Far Out (above)
- After Coachella the installation toured. Official dates: Brooklyn (Agger Fish Building) from May 6, 2026; Mexico City (La Maravilla Studios) Oct 27-Dec 27, 2026; San Francisco (Palace of Fine Arts) Jan 14-Feb 7, 2027. Stereogum also listed Chicago (Cinespace) Jul 30-Aug 23. The film runs 75 minutes and visits are booked as 2-hour slots. The official FAQ says "the Coachella Bunker experience" differs in some ways from the touring Motion Picture House.
  - Official site, kida-mnesia.com (accessed Oct 31, 2026) - https://kida-mnesia.com/; Stereogum, Apr 8, 2026
- Desert Sun points to a precedent: Travis Scott's "The Village by Cactus Jack" at Coachella 2025, another artist-designed environment.
  - Desert Sun/AOL (above)

## The livestream (YouTube)

- In 2010 Coachella was webcast one act at a time. In 2011 YouTube streamed three stages on separate channels, with about 61 bands and Wrigley as sponsor. Nearly 4 million people watched. Ad Age's Andrew Hampp estimated the sponsorship cost in the mid-to-high six figures.
  - Daniel Terdiman (CNET), CBS News, Apr 20, 2011 - https://www.cbsnews.com/news/how-the-coachella-webcast-changed-the-music-biz/
- Later milestones: AXS TV broadcast weekend 2 from 2014, SiriusXM carried 2015, YouTube streamed both weekends in 2019, and in Jan 2023 the deal was renewed through 2026 with all six stages streamed.
  - Wikipedia "Coachella" (footnotes 193-200); TechCrunch, Apr 3, 2023 - https://techcrunch.com/2023/04/03/youtube-will-livestream-all-six-coachella-stages-across-both-weekends (not fetched)
- In 2025 YouTube launched a dedicated Coachella Livestream app.
  - Millie Giles, Sherwood News, Apr 11, 2025 - https://sherwood.news/culture/coachella-2025-kicks-off-with-scorching-weather-and-cooling-demand
- In Sep 2026 YouTube extended its exclusive global livestream deal through 2030, covering both weekends. Terms were not disclosed. YouTube says the 2026 stream set its record for concurrent viewers but gave no number. Vevo reported a 221% rise in Justin Bieber video views after his set.
  - Murray Stassen, Music Business Worldwide, Sep 23, 2026 - https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/coachella-stays-exclusive-to-youtube-through-2030-as-goldenvoice-renews-livestream-deal/

# PART 2 - THE BUSINESS

## Goldenvoice, the founders and AEG

- Paul Tollett and Rick Van Santen co-founded Coachella in 1999. Goldenvoice has been an AEG subsidiary since 2001.
  - Wikipedia "Coachella," citing Richard Cromelin, "Rick Van Santen, 41; L.A. Promoter Helped to Advance Punk Rock Bands," Los Angeles Times, Jan 3, 2004, p. B18
- Van Santen died in late December 2003, aged 41, of flu-related complications.
  - LA Times, Jan 3, 2004 (via Wikipedia, above)
- In March 2001, Tollett agreed to sell Goldenvoice to Anschutz Entertainment Group for $7 million. AEG later bought half of Coachella and a controlling interest in 2004.
  - Wikipedia "Coachella" (footnote 32 for the sale; footnote 9, the Seabrook New Yorker profile of Apr 2017, for 2004)
- Leases: the Indio development agreement runs to 2030. An Oct 2021 lease with the Empire Polo Club (owner Alexander Haagen III) extends to 2050, gives Goldenvoice year-round oversight of the site, and allows two more three-day festivals a year.
  - Cameron DeFaria, Dancing Astronaut, Oct 27, 2021 - https://dancingastronaut.com/2021/10/goldenvoice-inks-new-lease-to-keep-coachella-in-indio-through-2050/
- Goldenvoice bought about 280 acres around the site in 2012.
  - mxdwn, Mar 26, 2012 - https://music.mxdwn.com/2012/03/26/news/goldenvoice-buys-280-acres-of-land-around-the-site-of-the-coachella-festival/ (headline only; not fetched)

## The Anschutz controversy (2017)

- From 2010 to 2013 the Anschutz Foundation gave $190,000 combined to Alliance Defending Freedom, the National Christian Foundation and the Family Research Council. In the 2016 cycle Philip and Nancy Anschutz gave $1,012,000 to Republican candidates and PACs, including $500,000 to the Senate Leadership Fund.
  - Myles Tanzer, The FADER, Jan 5, 2017 - https://www.thefader.com/2017/01/05/coachellas-republican-lgbtq-hate-groups
- Anschutz called the claims that he is anti-LGBTQ "fake news." He said he and the foundation stopped funding groups once they learned those groups backed anti-LGBTQ causes. He did not deny the funding.
  - The FADER, Jan 5, 2017 (above)
- Boycott calls followed.
  - Wikipedia "Coachella" (footnotes 86-88); Advocate, Jan 5, 2017 - https://advocate.com/politics/2017/1/05/coachella-owner-denies-homophobia-festival-performer-vows-lgbt-donation (not fetched)
- Tollett told The New Yorker: "I was offended. I run the festival, but it's rude to say that when you're a partner with someone."
  - New Yorker, Apr 17, 2017, via mxdwn, Apr 11, 2017 (above)

## The two-weekend model (since 2012)

- Announced May 31, 2011: a second, separately ticketed weekend with the same lineup from 2012. Tollett's reasons were demand beyond 2011's capacity, not wanting to "ruin the show" by adding about 40,000 people a day, and resale prices of $500-600 that he called unfair.
  - Charley Rogulewski, The Boombox, Jun 5, 2011 - https://theboombox.com/coachella-founder-explains-back-to-back-weekends-in-2012/
- 2012 tickets sold out in under three hours. Rolling Stone called the move "very risky."
  - Wikipedia "Coachella" (footnotes 60 and 62)

## Stagecoach (the weekend after, same grounds)

- Stagecoach started May 5-6, 2007. Attendance was 55,000 in 2012 (its first sellout), 75,000 in 2018 and 80,000 at the 2019 peak.
  - Wikipedia "Stagecoach Festival," citing My Desert (Apr 30, 2012), Mix 100.5 (2018) and Bruce Fessier, Desert Sun (Apr 27, 2019) - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stagecoach_Festival
- In 2024 Stagecoach sold 89,003 tickets and sold out in October. That beat each Coachella weekend (81,690 and 80,148). The three weekends totalled 250,841.
  - Jesus Reyes, KESQ, Jun 13, 2024 - https://kesq.com/news/festival-season/2024/06/13/coachella-stagecoach-sold-around-250k-tickets-for-2024-festivals-city-reports

## The Indio agreement and ticket revenue

- In 2013 the Indio council approved 4-0 Goldenvoice's deal to stay through at least 2030. The city's per-ticket share rose from $2.33 to $5.01 starting in 2014. The deal allows expansion from three to five festivals a year (two more in fall).
  - Bruce Houghton, Hypebot, Apr 5, 2013 - https://www.hypebot.com/goldenvoice-signs-deal-to-stage-coachella-stagecoach-in-indio-thru-2030/
- Before that deal, a proposed Indio ticket tax led promoters to threaten to leave.
  - CBS LA - https://www.cbsnews.com/losangeles/news/coachella-promoters-threaten-to-move-away-from-the-coachella-valley-over-proposed-tax/ (headline only; not fetched)
- A 2013 agreement fines Goldenvoice $20,000 for running five minutes past curfew, plus $1,000 for each extra minute.
  - Wikipedia "Coachella" (footnote 173)
- Indio expected $3.18M in ticket taxes from both festivals in 2016.
  - Wikipedia "Coachella" (footnote 219)
- For 2024, Indio reported $2,000,206.13 from ticket revenue across the three weekends. A city spokesperson said the rate is "$2.658 per ticket/per day this fiscal year."
  - KESQ, Jun 13, 2024 (above)

## Brand activations, houses and influencer culture

## 2024-2026 sales trend

- 2024: Weekend 1, which historically sold out in hours, took nearly a month. About 80% of 250,000 tickets had sold by opening. Sales were down 14-17% year over year, per sources.
  - Dave Brooks, Billboard, Apr 12, 2024 (above)
- 2025: weekend 1 GA had sold out by Dec 2024, but weekend 2 GA was "widely available." By April 2025 weekend 2 passes resold below face, as low as $246 on StubHub, about half of face value.
  - DMN, Dec 2, 2024; DMN, Apr 7, 2025 (above)
- Sherwood News, citing earlier reporting, called 2024 sales the slowest in 10 years. Google search interest in "coachella" peaked in 2018.
  - Sherwood News, Apr 11, 2025 (above)
- 2026 rebounded. The lineup was announced Sep 15, 2025, unusually early. Both weekends' GA and VIP sold out three days after passes went on sale, with only some camping and lodging left.
  - Lauren Boisvert, VICE, Sep 23, 2025 - https://www.vice.com/en/article/both-coachella-2026-weekends-sold-out-just-three-days-after-passes-went-on-sale/

# Best stories

1. The bunker: Goldenvoice quietly permitted a $2.5M, 16,130 sq ft concrete foundation next to the Sahara tent. The poster teased "The Bunker Debut of Radiohead." It turned out to be a 38 ft-tall underground cinema for a Radiohead film, with no band.
2. The founding near-bankruptcy: $850K lost in 1999, Rage Against the Machine handing back half its fee, Tollett selling his house and car, then a sale to AEG for $7M. It became the first recurring festival to gross more than $100M ($114.6M in 2017).
3. Frank Ocean 2023: a built ice rink and a crew of hockey-player skaters were dropped after a rehearsal injury (fractures and a sprain). He started an hour late, the stream was pulled, and Blink-182 replaced him on weekend 2.
4. The Tupac "hologram" was a Victorian theater trick (Pepper's ghost on Mylar). Digital Domain filed for Chapter 11 five months later.
5. Daft Punk's off-the-shelf LED pyramid in a 10,000-capacity tent with a reported 40,000 people kicked off the live-production arms race.
6. Two weekends as anti-scalping logic: Tollett chose to clone the festival rather than add 40,000 people a day. Gross roughly doubled the first year ($24.9M to $47.3M).
7. Stagecoach outsold each Coachella weekend in 2024 (89,003 vs about 81,000 and 80,000). On the slow-sales years: 2024 weekend 1 took a month to sell out and 2025 weekend 2 resold at half face. Then 2026 sold out in three days.
8. Indio's cut is a few dollars a ticket, about $2M in 2024. The contract also sets a curfew fine of $20,000 plus $1,000 a minute.
9. The 2026 Anyma wind cancellation came from his stage build being hit by wind. A light fell at the Do LaB the same night, and he ended up on that stage two days later.
10. Prince's "Creep" cover was taken offline by his own team despite Radiohead's objection, and he put it back up himself in 2015.
