# Daft Punk and the Pyramid - Coachella, Saturday April 29, 2006 (Sahara tent)

Research file for polo.cold.fyi. Compiled Oct 31, 2026. Every claim is followed by its source(s). "Reported by attendees" marks Reddit, social and fan claims. Where sources conflict, the conflict is stated rather than resolved.

---

## 1. Booking

- **Coachella went after them for two years.** In 2005 Coachella offered $250,000 and the band said no. In 2006 it raised the offer to $300,000 and they said yes. - *Daft Punk Unchained* (2015), as summarized in "10 things we learned from Daft Punk Unchained", Ben Cardew, The Guardian (via inkl): https://www.inkl.com/news/10-things-we-learned-from-daft-punk-unchained (date not shown on the mirror; the film came out in 2015)
- **CONFLICT on the fee:** Goldenvoice's own 2024 documentary puts the offer at **$350,000 plus airfare, hotel and ground transportation**. The figure comes from Stacey Vee, Goldenvoice VP of Festival Talent. - Billboard, Katie Bain, Dec 18, 2024 (Yahoo mirror): https://www.yahoo.com/entertainment/daft-punk-booked-coachella-2006-180000503.html ; DJ Mag, Christian Eede, Dec 20, 2024: https://djmag.com/news/daft-punks-2006-coachella-performance-features-new-busy-p-mini-documentary-watch
  - *Possible reconciliation (my inference, not sourced):* $300k may have been the fee and $350k the full package, or one of the two sources is simply wrong. Present both.
- **Vee on making the offer:** "We really didn't want a no; we really wanted this one to happen." - Billboard, Dec 18, 2024 (above)
- **The "oui".** Pedro Winter (Busy P), their manager for 12 years starting at age 20, says his job was mostly turning offers down, often without even asking the band. In 2006 the organizers came back with a short email offering $350k plus transport and hotel. This time Winter called Thomas Bangalter, and the band said yes. The plan quickly grew into the LED pyramid. - Radio FG, Antony Harari, Dec 23, 2024: https://www.radiofg.com/daft-punk-a-coachella-2006-comment-ce-oui-de-busy-p-a-tout-change ; Billboard, Dec 18, 2024 (above)
- **Bangalter on why they said yes:** "Coachella was a big offer financially, and that triggered the ability to bring the show to the next level." He also said: "We were ready to play again - we've never done anything for the money." This comes from an August 2007 interview, unpublished until Feb 2021, conducted the day after their Keyspan Park show in Coney Island. Variety calls it "a six-figure offer". - Variety, Jonathan Cohen, Feb 23, 2021 (Yahoo mirror): https://ca.news.yahoo.com/daft-punk-talks-kanye-west-000436207.html ; Hypebeast, Feb 23, 2021: https://hypebeast.com/2021/2/daft-punk-unpublished-interview-coachella-kanye-west-news
- **The band put advance money into the production**, giving up profit to improve the show. - Water & Music, Kristin Robinson, Apr 29, 2021: https://www.waterandmusic.com/15-years-ago-daft-punks-coachella-set-helped-spark-an-arms-race-for-live-music-technology
- **Why the Sahara tent and not the main stage:** no source found that explains the decision. Context: Depeche Mode headlined the main stage that Saturday, and Daft Punk played after them. - Billboard 2006 festival review: https://www.billboard.com/music/music-news/coachella-valley-music-arts-festival-april-29-30-2006-indio-calif-empire-polo-58544 ; NME, Apr 30, 2006: https://www.nme.com/?p=1359908 ; SPIN, Apr 29, 2006: https://www.spinmagazine.com/2006/04/coachella-06-reviewed/
  - In 2006 the Sahara was pushed to the far edge of the grounds, beside what later became the Rose Garden VIP area, with only a few light panels. - LA Times via AOL, August Brown, Apr 9, 2023: https://www.aol.com/news/edm-may-waned-sahara-tent-120013512.html
- **Who else was involved:** Wiley Dailey ("Sahara tent mastermind") is interviewed in the 2024 film. He recalls that "people showed up and lost their minds." - Billboard, Dec 18, 2024 (above)
- **First US show since 1997.** It was also the first live performance of *Discovery* material. - Water & Music, Apr 29, 2021 (above); The Ringer, Justin Sayles, Feb 23, 2021: https://www.theringer.com/music/2021/2/23/22296993/daft-punk-break-up-announcement
- **The secrecy.** Even Pedro Winter didn't know what the band was planning for much of the run-up. He first saw an audio-only rehearsal days before the show: "I wasn't allowed to see the lights yet." - Guardian/Cardew via inkl (above)
- **Rehearsing for the heat.** Bangalter says that for the final rehearsal he had them both wear ski jackets: "if we get through that anything will be easy... it was the worst ever." - MusicRadar, Ben Rogerson, Jun 10, 2026 (Yahoo mirror), citing an interview with The Times (UK): https://www.yahoo.com/entertainment/music/articles/thomas-bangalter-reveals-daft-punks-104523382.html

## 2. Set time, length, crowd

- **Start: 11:00 PM, Saturday April 29, 2006**, in the Sahara tent. - Water & Music, Apr 29, 2021 (above); Far Out (undated): https://faroutmagazine.co.uk/the-daft-punk-coachella-comeback-set-that-changed-the-course-of-music/
- **Length: about 75 minutes.** - Las Vegas Weekly, Spencer Patterson, May 4, 2006: https://lasvegasweekly.com/news/archive/2006/may/04/noise-you-shoulda-been-there ; The Ringer, Feb 23, 2021 (above)
- **It ran past curfew.** The set went nearly 20 minutes past Coachella's midnight curfew. Paul Tollett: "They were so great we told them to keep going, and the festival would cover the possible fine." - Las Vegas Weekly, May 4, 2006 (above)
  - CONFLICT: a SPIN live-blog entry says the set "ended at midnight", and that fans then began lining up for Madonna. - SPIN, Apr 29-May 1, 2006 (above). The Las Vegas Weekly account is more specific and includes a direct Tollett quote.
- **Crowd: "40,000 tried to get into a 10,000-capacity tent."** The figure traces to the band's manager in *Daft Punk Unchained*. It is not a gate count. - Vice/Thump (undated; written around the Showtime premiere): https://thump.vice.com/en_au/article/daft-punks-legendary-pyramid-is-only-half-the-story-of-that-fateful-night-at-coachella-2006 ; Rolling Stone (undated clip post): https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-news/watch-daft-punks-legendary-2006-coachella-set-in-documentary-clip-225749/ ; Okayplayer, Scott Heins, Dec 10, 2015: https://www.okayplayer.com/news/daft-punk-unchained-coachella-2006-footage.html
  - Live Design calls the 40,000 figure "rumored": https://www.livedesignonline.com/concerts/daft-punk-disbands-duo-set-design-standards
- **The overflow.** Coachella added extra speaker stacks and jumbotrons for people outside the tent. - Vice/Thump (above). NME's same-night report says the crowd "spilled almost a hundred deep outside of the dance tent." - NME, Apr 30, 2006 (above)
- **Festival total:** more than 100,000 people over two days. - Billboard 2006 review (above)
- **Bangalter on the crowd** (Pitchfork interview, 2013, as reported): the audience was "five years" behind the group's ideas, but it was "the most synched-up we ever felt." - Rolling Stone AU, Jon Blistein, Feb 23, 2021: https://au.rollingstone.com/music/music-news/daft-punk-pyramid-lollapalooza-2007-flashback-23420/amp/ (the original Pitchfork interview was not fetched)

## 3. The setlist

Running order of the circulating fan recording, with track timings. - Dancing Astronaut, Andrew Spada, Apr 11, 2013 (SoundCloud upload of the set): https://dancingastronaut.com/2013/04/good-morning-mix-daft-punk-alive-at-coachella-2006/

1. Intro (Close Encounters of the Third Kind theme, per Far Out)
2. Robot Rock (/ Oh Yeah, per Far Out)
3. Technologic
4. Television Rules the Nation
5. Steam Machine
6. Around the World / Harder, Better, Faster, Stronger
7. Too Long
8. Face to Face (Harder remix)
9. Interlude
10. One More Time / Aerodynamic
11. The Brainwashers (Rollin' & Scratchin')
12. Alive (The Prime Time of Your Life)
13. Da Funk
14. Human After All / Superheroes

- Far Out also gives the opener as the *Close Encounters* theme into "Robot Rock"/"Oh Yeah", and the closing run as "Superheroes", "Human After All" and "Rock 'n' Roll". - Far Out (undated, above)
- **How the set worked.** The music was improvised in Ableton Live within a skeleton outline. Mashups included "Around the World/HBFS" and "Touch It/Technologic". (Note: "Touch It/Technologic" is in the Alive 2007 album set, not the 2006 Coachella tracklist above.) Lighting designer Martin Phillips on the sound side: "at the time, it was really a case of a guy on the [sound] console trying to keep up." - Water & Music, Apr 29, 2021 (above)
- **Phillips "locked them into a set shell"** because production time was limited. He wanted "a beginning, middle, and an end." Music cues triggered video and lighting. - The Creators Project / Vice (undated): https://thecreatorsproject.vice.com/blog/meet-the-man-behind-the-daft-punk-pyramid-and-the-presets-new-tour

## 4. The pyramid - who made it and what was in it

**Credits (verified in trade press):**
- **Lighting and set designer: Martin Phillips** (Bionic League). He was in charge of overall visual aesthetics, co-produced the video content, and programmed the show. - Live Design, "One More Time With Daft Punk" (2006, undated online): https://www.livedesignonline.com/concerts/one-more-time-daft-punk ; PLSN production profile, Nov 19, 2007: https://plsn.com/?p=230142 ; Creators Project (above)
- **Inspiration:** Phillips based it on the "Technologic" video, which he had also designed. - Water & Music, Apr 29, 2021 (above)
- **The band:** Bangalter and de Homem-Christo were closely involved. Phillips: "Simplicity was a key element for them." - PLSN, Nov 19, 2007 (above). Bangalter (2007): "We built the custom pyramid... We have synthesizers and remote controls in the pyramid." - Variety, Feb 23, 2021 (above)
- **Video content:** Baptiste Andrieux and 8VFX (Santa Monica). Cédric Hervet of Daft Arts edited the material to BPM and made most of the footage for the final song and the encore. - PLSN, Nov 19, 2007 (above)
- **Video vendor:** XL Video UK, with Phil Mercer as project manager and Simon Pugsley on media servers. - Live Design (above); PLSN (above)
- **O-Lite pyramid screens:** VER Los Angeles (Blake Steury) built custom two-faced pyramid screens with a "top-hat" peak, using Barco CAD templates. - PLSN (above)
- **Set builder:** B&R Scenic (Brian Sullivan) redesigned the structure to make it road-worthy. Eric Beauchamp designed the module layout and the "ninja star" VersaTube structures. - PLSN (above)
- **Lighting rental:** Felix Lighting (US) and PRG (Europe and Japan). - PLSN (above)
- **Crew (2007 profile):** Eric Belanger was lighting director and operator, ready to run the whole show by hand if timecode failed. Aaron Chawla was production manager, Sofia Bjorkman tour manager, and Tom "Biss" Kelleher stage manager. P.J. Maddock-Jones and Ted Cognata were O-Lite techs, and John "The Kid" McGuire was Catalyst/LED tech. Janet Hansen of Enlighted made the EL-wire robot outfits for the encore, and Anders Nelson built the DMX interface for them. - PLSN (above)

**Hardware:**
- **The pyramid:** more than 1,600 Barco O-Lite blocks, 16 ft wide at the base and pixel-mapped into a 3D video surface. Video covered the bottom two-thirds of the front plus a small section above the band platform. - Buildings.com / LEDs Magazine, Aug 21, 2006: https://www.buildings.com/building-systems-om/lighting/article/55258729/leds-create-pyramid-showcase-for-daft-punk ; Live Design (above). PLSN's 2007 gear list gives **1,800** O-Lite modules, which may reflect the touring rig.
- **Height:** "24-foot" aluminum pyramid. - Rolling Stone AU, Feb 23, 2021 (above); The Ringer, Feb 23, 2021 (above)
- **Side frames:** two 18x18 ft criss-crossing hexagon networks made of 170 Element Labs VersaTubes. - Buildings.com, Aug 21, 2006; PLSN
- **Pixel curtain:** 48 ft wide by 16 ft high at 75mm resolution, wrapping the stage. It was a bespoke joint build by Element Labs and Daft Arts. - Buildings.com, Aug 21, 2006
- **Other fixtures (2007 gear list):** 47 Martin MAC 700 profiles, 14 Martin Atomic 3K strobes, 46 Pixel Range PixelLine 1044s, 6 Color Kinetics ColorBlast 12s, 2 JEM ZR33 foggers and 2 Reel EFX DF-50 hazers. - PLSN, Nov 19, 2007
- **Control:** Catalyst V4 media servers (two dual-output units plus two hot backups) ran video for about 95% of the show. A Flying Pig Hog iPC console triggered them from SMPTE timecode sent by the band, and a Barco DVI matrix router provided redundancy. Phillips kept three Catalyst layers free for "buskable" improvised content. - Live Design (above); Buildings.com (above); PLSN (above)
- **No new products.** Phillips: "What was new was the fact that [they] had taken the existing products and used them as building blocks." - Water & Music, Apr 29, 2021
- **Look:** 1970s and 1980s lo-fi computer graphics on new LED hardware. It moved from colour blocks to wireframe/Tron graphics to photographic images. - PLSN
- **Built for one show.** "The pyramid set was first designed for a one-time performance at the Coachella Valley Music Festival." Its success led to the tour, so XL had to adapt a rig never meant for the road. - Live Design, "One More Time With Daft Punk" (above)
- **Cost:** no documented figure found.

## 5. Alive 2006/2007, the album, the Grammys

- **Tour:** Coachella, Apr 29, 2006, was the first show and Sydney Showground, Dec 22, 2007, the last. The Sydney show was the last to use the pyramid. The tour had 48 shows (20 Europe, 14 North America, 5 Asia, 5 Oceania, 4 South America). - Wikipedia, "Alive 2006/2007" (IPFS mirror): https://ipfs.ssi.eecc.de/ipns/en.wikipedia-on-ipfs.org/wiki/Alive_2006/2007 ; Wikipedia, "Alive 2007": https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alive_2007
- It was one of only two official tours in their career; the other was Daftendirektour (1997). - Rolling Stone AU, Feb 23, 2021
- Bangalter (2007): "We've really been on a world tour since that first show at Coachella in April, 2006." - LA Weekly, Randall Roberts, Oct 25, 2007: https://www.laweekly.com/?p=236870
- **Alive 2007 (album):** recorded June 14, 2007 at the Palais Omnisports de Paris-Bercy. Released Nov 19, 2007 (Virgin); North American retail was delayed to Dec 4, 2007. The single "Harder, Better, Faster, Stronger (Alive 2007)" came out Oct 15, 2007. - Wikipedia, "Alive 2007" (above)
- **Grammys (51st, Feb 2009):** Best Electronic/Dance Album for *Alive 2007*, and Best Dance Recording for "Harder, Better, Faster, Stronger (Alive 2007)". - Wikipedia "Alive 2007"; Water & Music (album win). grammy.com did not load, so this is not verified there.
- **The Grammys with Kanye (Feb 10, 2008):** their first US TV performance, playing "Stronger" behind Kanye West. - Star Tribune, Feb 15, 2008: https://www.startribune.com/that-was-daft-punk-behind-kanye/15646512

## 6. Impact

- **"Big Bang."** Fans cite 2006 in the Sahara as the "Big Bang" of the EDM boom. - LA Times/AOL, Apr 9, 2023 (above)
- **Rock Hall:** calls the set "a sea change for Electronic Dance Music (EDM)." - Rock & Roll Hall of Fame Library guide, last updated Aug 14, 2024: https://library.rockhall.com/greatest_festival_moments/coachella_2006
- **Billboard:** it turned the packed Sahara crowd into dance-music believers and set up the genre's rise in the US. - Billboard, Dec 18, 2024 (above). It also appears on Billboard's list of the 50 greatest festival performances (2017): https://www.edmtunes.com/2017/07/billboard-releases-list-of-50-greatest-festival-performances-of-all-time/ (list not opened)
- **LA Weekly:** an "industry-wide wake-up call" for electronic music. - quoted by Live Design (above); the original LA Weekly piece was not located
- **Festival production "arms race".** Brendan Hoffman (TMWRK) on the "arms race to get the latest and greatest technology" that followed. Coachella's later firsts include the Tupac hologram (2012) and Odesza's drone show (2018). - Water & Music, Apr 29, 2021
- **The Sahara's rise:** in 2018 Goldenvoice moved the tent to the centre of the grounds and made it 25% bigger. It is now nearly 100 ft tall with a 250-ft stage, designed and built by AG (Las Vegas; Koen De Puysseleir and Andrew Gumper). - LA Times/AOL, Apr 9, 2023
- **Skrillex** saw the pyramid in 2007, not at Coachella: "I went alone, bought a ticket off a scalper for $150... It changed my life." - Gear Live, Mar 31, 2012: https://www.gearlive.com/music/article/q309-daft-punk-show-changed-skrillex . Dancing Astronaut puts it at the Alive 2007 LA show: https://dancingastronaut.com/2017/02/skrillex-daft-punk-pop-shop-come-guys-lets-get-live-show/
- **deadmau5, Diplo, Kaskade, Porter Robinson:** no sourced quote found tying any of them to *Coachella 2006* specifically. Don't attribute one without a source.
- **Michaelangelo Matos** (author of *The Underground Is Massive*, 2015), in *Unchained*: "No one had seen that level of production." - Rolling Stone; Okayplayer, Dec 10, 2015 (above)

## 7. Coachella afterlife

- **2013: the *Random Access Memories* teaser.** A just-under-two-minute "Get Lucky" clip with Pharrell and Nile Rodgers screened at Coachella on Friday Apr 12, 2013, followed by a call-sheet of collaborators (Panda Bear, Julian Casablancas, Giorgio Moroder, Todd Edwards, Chilly Gonzales, Paul Williams, DJ Falcon). Daft Punk were not on the bill. - SPIN, Kyle McGovern, Apr 13, 2013: https://www.spinmagazine.com/2013/04/daft-punk-coachella-2013-get-lucky-video/ ; Relix, Apr 13, 2013: https://relix.com/blogs/detail/daft-punk-teases-get-lucky-at-coachella/ . Stage and slot are not given in either source.
- **2016: the "Alive 2017" rumour.** A site called alive2017.com with a countdown to Oct 27, 2016 had coordinates in its code that pointed to LA and Indio. Time Out was skeptical, and nothing happened. - Time Out LA, Michael Juliano, Oct 5, 2016: https://www.timeout.com/los-angeles/blog/is-daft-punk-playing-coachella-the-internet-thinks-so-100516
- **2017: Pharrell on the rumours:** "The robots are, like... they're not repeaters." - NME, Luke Morgan Britton, Jun 14, 2017: https://www.nme.com/?p=2088712
- **2017: Skrillex** said he was trying to get them to do a live show again. - Dancing Astronaut, Feb 13, 2017 (above)
- **2021: the split.** "Epilogue", an eight-minute YouTube video built on a clip from *Electroma*, was posted Feb 22, 2021. It ended a 28-year partnership, with no reason given. - The Ringer, Feb 23, 2021
- **2026: LEGO set.** An official LEGO set of the *Alive 2007* pyramid stage was announced around Oct 22-23, 2026, with more details promised for 2027. It started as a LEGO Ideas design by Patrick Harboun (Luxembourg) and his son, made during lockdown; it won the 2020 "Music To Our Ears" challenge and later reached 10,000 supporters. Cédric Hervet called the pyramid "one of the most recognisable expressions of Daft Punk's visual world." - JamBase, Nate Todd, Oct 23, 2026: https://www.jambase.com/article/daft-punk-lego-pyramid-stage-set ; Yanko Design, Oct 22, 2026: https://www.yankodesign.com/2026/10/22/daft-punks-iconic-coachella-pyramid-stage-is-becoming-an-official-lego-set-in-2027/amp/
- **2026: Electronic Sound "Time Machine" feature** "Daft Punk, Coachella, 2006" by Ben Gilbert, Feb 12, 2026, marking 20 years. It is paywalled and was not read. - https://www.electronicsound.co.uk/?p=26434

## Timeline

- **1997:** Daftendirektour. Their last US shows before 2006.
- **2005:** Coachella offers $250k and the band declines (*Unchained*). *Human After All* is released.
- **Early 2006:** a second offer: $300k (*Unchained*), or $350k plus travel (Goldenvoice, 2024). Winter calls Bangalter, and they say yes.
- **Spring 2006:** Martin Phillips designs the pyramid as a one-off. XL Video, Barco O-Lite and Element Labs supply the hardware. Winter hears an audio-only rehearsal days before the show. The ski-jacket rehearsal happens around this time.
- **Sat Apr 29, 2006, about 11:00 PM:** the Sahara tent, after Depeche Mode's main-stage headline set. The set runs about 75 minutes, roughly 20 minutes past the midnight curfew, with Tollett approving.
- **Apr 30, 2006:** Madonna plays Coachella on the Sunday. SPIN reports fans queued for her right after Daft Punk.
- **Aug 21, 2006:** LEDs Magazine/Buildings.com profiles the rig.
- **2006-2007:** the Alive tour, 48 shows.
- **Jun 14, 2007:** Bercy, Paris, where *Alive 2007* is recorded.
- **Nov 19, 2007:** *Alive 2007* released. PLSN production profile published.
- **Dec 22, 2007:** Sydney, the last pyramid show.
- **Feb 10, 2008:** Grammys performance with Kanye West ("Stronger").
- **Feb 2009:** two Grammys at the 51st awards.
- **Apr 12, 2013:** the RAM / "Get Lucky" teaser screens at Coachella.
- **Jun 24 / Dec 10, 2015:** *Daft Punk Unchained* (France / US Showtime).
- **Oct 2016:** the "alive2017" Coachella hoax/rumour.
- **Apr 10, 2020:** *Coachella: 20 Years in the Desert* on YouTube, with previously unseen 2006 footage.
- **Feb 22, 2021:** "Epilogue", the split.
- **Dec 18, 2024:** *Busy P Says "Oui"* on Coachella's YouTube.
- **Mar-Apr 2025:** a crowd recording surfaces on Reddit and YouTube (GotWub).
- **May 27, 2026:** DNCE TRAX's "What Happened At Coachella 2006?"
- **Jun 2026:** Bangalter tells The Times about the ski jackets.
- **Oct 22-23, 2026:** the LEGO pyramid set is announced.

## Unconfirmed / myths

- **"40,000 people in a 10,000 tent."** The figure comes from the band's manager in *Unchained*, and from Busy P generally. There is no gate count, and Live Design calls it "rumored". Say "an estimated" or "reportedly".
- **"Nearly caused the tent to collapse."** Only The Ringer (2021) says this. No contemporary source does. Treat it as lore.
- **The fee: $300k or $350k.** The 2015 doc and the 2024 Goldenvoice doc disagree. Both are "documented".
- **Midnight end vs. 20 minutes over.** SPIN's live-blog and Las Vegas Weekly differ.
- **Tait Towers built the pyramid; grandMA consoles; air conditioners cooling the servers under the pyramid.** These come only from an unofficial fan site (sites.google.com/view/daftpunktoken). The 2007 trade press contradicts the first two: PLSN credits B&R Scenic and VER for the build and Hog iPC consoles. The AC story is unverified. Paul Hahn (Daft Arts) and Chris Holmes ("Shadow") are also fan-site-only credits.
- **Cyril Ryckelynck:** no source links him to the pyramid. Don't credit him.
- **"It launched the American EDM boom."** This is a widely repeated framing (LA Times "Big Bang" from fans, Rock Hall "sea change", Billboard), not a measured fact. Far Out's claims that Google searches peaked, kids bought decks instead of guitars and Leo Sayer came out of retirement are uncited.
- **"Everyone who was there started a band/DJing."** No sourced version of this line was found. It looks like a borrowing of the Velvet Underground/Brian Eno quote. Don't use it as a quote.
- **Named DJs in the 2006 crowd** (deadmau5, Diplo, Kaskade, Porter Robinson, Skrillex): none are documented. Skrillex's life-changing pyramid show was in 2007.
- **Reunion rumours** (2016 "alive2017", plus recurring yearly speculation): none have ever been confirmed. The duo split in 2021.
- **Why the Sahara and not the main stage:** no source explains it.
- **Production cost:** not documented anywhere found.

## Best stories

1. **Two years, one phone call.** They turned down $250k in 2005, then said yes in 2006. Their manager usually said no without even asking them, but this time he picked up the phone.
2. **Even the manager wasn't allowed to see the lights.** Winter heard an audio-only rehearsal days out.
3. **Ski jackets in rehearsal** to get ready for the desert heat in helmets and leather. Bangalter: "it was the worst ever."
4. **Tollett pays the fine.** The set ran about 20 minutes past curfew and the promoter told them to keep going.
5. **"What light show?"** A writer stuck outside the overflowing tent heard about the show of the decade from his housemate.
6. **It was a one-off.** The pyramid was designed for a single Coachella show and became a 48-date world tour, a Grammy-winning live album and, 20 years later, a LEGO set.
7. **Off-the-shelf parts, new idea.** Phillips used no bespoke products. The innovation was using existing LED products as building blocks.
8. **Opening act: Depeche Mode.** Daft Punk followed the main-stage headliner in a side tent on the far edge of the grounds and became the story of the festival.
