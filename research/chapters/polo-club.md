## PART A - HOW POLO WORKS

### A1. The field (outdoor, grass)
- Standard field: **300 x 160 yards**. That is about nine American football fields.
  - USPA Spectator Guide - https://uspolo.org/sport/spectator-guide - US Polo Association, n.d. (current site)
  - Museum of Polo and Hall of Fame FAQ - https://polomuseum.org/faqs - n.d.
  - Britannica, "Polo: The game" - https://www.britannica.com/sports/polo/The-game - n.d. (gives 300 yd / 274.3 m)
- Goal posts are **8 yards apart**, centred on each back line. They have no crossbar, so height is unlimited.
  - Polo Museum FAQ (above); Britannica (above). Snow polo rules give the goal as 7.3 m (8 yd) with "no crossbar" - St. Moritz rules PDF, below.
- Sideboards are optional, no taller than 11 inches, and keep the ball in play along the sides.
  - Polo Museum FAQ (above)
- Lines used in penalties: the 30-, 40- and 60-yard lines, measured out from each back line, and the centre line.
  - USPA Spectator Guide (old) - https://uspolo.org/sport/spectator-guide-old - USPA, n.d.

### A2. Players and positions
- **4 players per team outdoors**, 3 in arena.
  - Polo Museum FAQ; Britannica (above)
- Positions are numbered 1-4 by jersey:
  - **No. 1** - the most forward attacker and main goal-scorer. Marks the other side's No. 4. Often the team's least experienced player.
  - **No. 2** - attacker, the "hustler" who fights for the ball.
  - **No. 3** - the pivot or "quarterback". Turns defence into attack and feeds Nos. 1 and 2. Usually the best player, often captain.
  - **No. 4 ("back")** - defender who protects the goal and sends the ball forward.
  - USPA Spectator Guide (old); Britannica; Polo Museum FAQ (above)
- Positions are fluid during play: players swap roles constantly while keeping the shape. *(Inference from the "pivot" description - no single source states it as a rule.)*

### A3. Chukkers (periods)
- **Length: 7 minutes**, plus up to 30 seconds of overtime. USPA Outdoor Rule 14: "at the end of 7 minutes of elapsed time, a single horn or bell will sound", and "up to 30 seconds of overtime remain in the period". The chukker ends at the first sound of the final horn, wherever the ball is, so a chukker never runs longer than 7.5 minutes.
  - USPA "Rule of the Week: 2019 Outdoor Rule Change - Duration of Periods" - https://uspolo.org/news-social/news/rule-of-the-week-2019-outdoor-rule-change-duration-of-periods - USPA, May 7 2019
- Polo Museum version: a bell at 6.5 minutes warns of 30 seconds left, and a horn ends the chukker at 7 minutes. *(This conflicts with the rule text above. Use the USPA rule.)*
  - Polo Museum FAQ (above)
- FIP International Rules: maximum 7.5 minutes. A single horn at 7 minutes means up to 30 seconds remain, and a double horn ends the period.
  - 2016 International Rules (hosted by USPA) - https://uspolo.org/assets/pdf/2016-International-Rules.pdf - 2016
- **Number of chukkers:**
  - US: typically 6 outdoors, with 4-6 depending on the tournament. Lower-goal leagues often play 4.
    - USPA Spectator Guide; Polo Museum FAQ (above); Eldorado ran "six-chukker 8-goal and ... four-chukker 4-goal leagues" - USPA, Nov 20 2017 (Part B source)
  - Argentina: **8 chukkers** in the Argentine Open at Palermo. The 2025 final (Dec 7 2025, La Natividad La Dolfina 17-13 Ellerstina) recorded an injury "at 1m. 16s. of the eighth chukker".
    - Hurlingham Polo (Argentina) match report - https://hurlinghampolo.com/?p=15130 - Dec 2025
    - Britannica: "Eight are played in Argentina, and four is common in England and on the European continent." (above)
    - FIP 2016 International Rules 1.1: a maximum of 8 for AAP (Argentine Polo Association) open tournaments and 6 for everything else (above)
- **Ties:** play continues after the final chukker until one side scores. USPA Rule 14.c: "until one side obtains a goal which shall determine the game". The Polo Museum describes a 5-minute break and then an extra chukker played as sudden death.
  - USPA Rule of the Week, May 7 2019 (above); Polo Museum FAQ (above)
- **Breaks:** about 3 minutes between chukkers and about 5 minutes at halftime (divot stomp).
  - Polo School Argentina, "Polo rules: an in-depth guide" - https://poloschoolargentina.com/tpost/xkuuxbfar1-polo-rules-an-in-depth-guide - Dec 24 2024 *(secondary source. The USPA rule on intervals, Rule 16, was not retrieved.)*

### A4. Handicaps
- Every player is rated in "goals" from **-2 (USPA shows it as "C") up to 10**. Ten is the best. A rating measures horsemanship, hitting, game sense, pony quality and sportsmanship. It is not a count of goals scored. USPA reviews ratings twice a year.
  - USPA Spectator Guide (above). The Polo Museum FAQ says "-1 to 10" (older wording). Britannica says 0-10 and also mentions minus ratings.
- **Team handicap = the sum of its four players' ratings.** Tournaments set a team cap, e.g. "12-goal" or "22-goal". The Argentine Open requires teams of 28-40 goals.
  - Polo Museum FAQ; Wikipedia, "Campeonato Argentino Abierto de Polo" - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campeonato_Argentino_Abierto_de_Polo - n.d.
- **How handicaps add or subtract in a match:** in a handicap match the lower-rated team starts with goals on the board. USPA formula: take the difference in team ratings, divide by 6, then multiply by the number of chukkers. Example: teams 2 goals apart in a 6-chukker game means the weaker team starts 2-0. A 4-goal team playing a 6-goal team gets 2 goals.
  - USPA Spectator Guide (above); Polo Museum FAQ (above)
- Twist: since May 1 2025, the USPA "American Rule" (Outdoor Rule 1.f) gives teams a 1-goal handicap credit for each "Homegrown" US player in events of 16 goals and up. The credit is capped at 1 or 2 goals.
  - Miami Living Magazine (USPA content), "Get Ready - USPA Outdoor Rule 1.F" - https://www.miamilivingmagazine.com/post/get-ready-uspa-outdoor-rule-1-f-the-american-rule-is-changing-on-may-1-2025 - Feb 27 2025

### A5. Right of way and the line of the ball (the core safety rule)
- **Line of the ball:** an imaginary line through the ball along the direction it is travelling, extending forwards and backwards at any moment.
  - USPA Spectator Guide (old) (above)
- **Right of way:** belongs to the player following closest to that line, at the smallest angle to it. The ball should be on that player's right (offside) when they hit it. Right of way extends ahead of the player in the direction they are riding. Two players riding along the line toward the ball have priority over everyone else. A player riding parallel to the line with the ball on the offside of their pony has priority over a player with it on the nearside. A player who deviates from the ball's direction after hitting it loses priority.
  - FIP International Rules 3.16-3.17 (above); USPA Spectator Guide (old) (above)
- **Crossing:** no player may cross the line of a rider who has right of way unless they are far enough ahead that there is no risk of collision. This is the most common foul.
  - FIP International Rules 3.15 (above); USPA Spectator Guide (old): "ROW violations and ride-off violations are the most frequent" calls
- **Ride-off (bump):** two players ride side by side and push with horse and body to move the other off the line. It is legal only at a shallow angle. The Polo Museum says the angle "must be 45 degrees or less". FIP Interpretation 1 treats more than 45 degrees as dangerous, and also any contact behind the saddle. Two teammates may not ride off one opponent at the same time.
  - Polo Museum FAQ; FIP International Rules 3.18 (above)
- **Hook:** blocking an opponent's swing with your own mallet. Allowed only from the same side as the swing or directly behind it, and only while the opponent is in the act of hitting. Hooking above shoulder height is illegal.
  - FIP International Rules 3.20 (above); USPA Spectator Guide (old) (above)
- Restart nuance: at a throw-in, right of way is set "when the ball leaves the line-up". USPA Outdoor Rule 20(c).
  - USPA "Rule of the Week: How Play Commences" - https://uspolo.org/news-social/news/rule-of-the-week-how-play-commences - Apr 30 2018
- Other fouls: dangerous riding (zig-zagging in front of a player, riding at a dangerous angle or speed, crossing a pony's legs, intimidation), dangerous use of the mallet, reaching across or under a pony, and delay of game.
  - FIP International Rules 3.18; USPA Spectator Guide (old) (above)

### A6. Penalties (outdoor)
Umpires award penalties by number, and the more serious the foul, the closer to goal the hit. Two mounted umpires call fouls. If they disagree, a "referee" sitting at midfield decides.
- **Penalty 1** - a **goal is awarded outright**. Given for a dangerous or deliberate foul near goal made to stop a likely score. Play restarts with a throw-in at the 10-yard line in front of the fouling team's goal (FIP).
- **Penalty 2** - free hit from **30 yards** (the centre of the 30-yard line nearest the fouling team's goal), or from the spot of the foul if the fouled team chooses.
- **Penalty 3** - free hit from **40 yards**, opposite the centre of the goal.
- **Penalty 4** - free hit from **60 yards**, opposite the centre of the goal.
- **Penalty 5a** - free hit from the spot of the foul, at least 4 yards in from the boards or sideline, with the fouling team at least 30 yards away. **Penalty 5b** - free hit from the centre of the field, with the fouling team 30 yards away.
- **Penalty 6 ("safety")** - given when a defender hits the ball over their own back line. The attackers get a free hit from **60 yards** out, level with where the ball crossed, but no more than 40 yards from the goal centre (FIP).
- Penalties 7-10 cover procedural matters: re-taking a botched free hit, failed knock-ins, ordering a pony or player off, and (Penalty 10) ejection for dangerous or deliberate fouls or misconduct.
  - USPA Spectator Guide (old) - https://uspolo.org/sport/spectator-guide-old - n.d.
  - FIP 2016 International Rules 4.27-4.39 (above)
  - USPA "Rule of the Week: Penalty Procedures and Violations" - https://uspolo.org/news-social/news/rule-of-the-week-penalty-procedures-and-violations - Aug 7 2018
  - USPA "Rule of the Week: Selection of Penalties" (Penalty 10, Rule 35.e(3)) - https://uspolo.org/news-social/news/rule-of-the-week-selection-of-penalties - Jul 31 2018
  - Umpires and referee: Polo Museum FAQ (above)

### A7. Restarts and ends
- **Throw-in (bowl-in):** the umpire bowls the ball underhand, hard, between the two teams lined up side by side (FIP: from at least 5 m away). This starts each chukker and restarts play after a goal or after the ball goes out over the sideline.
  - USPA Spectator Guide (old); FIP International Rules 2.4, 2.10 (above)
- **Knock-in:** when the attackers hit the ball over the back line wide of goal, the defenders restart with a free hit from that spot. No attacker may be within 30 yards until the ball is hit.
  - Polo Museum FAQ; FIP 2.7 (above)
- **Teams switch ends after every goal**, which evens out wind and turf. A coin toss sets the first direction. Under FIP rules ends do not change after a Penalty 1 goal, and they change at halftime if no goal has been scored. **In arena polo, ends change each chukker instead.**
  - USPA Spectator Guide; Polo Museum FAQ; FIP 2.5 (above); USPA Arena Rule 8.a (below)
- A goal counts whoever puts the ball through, including a pony.
  - Polo Museum FAQ (above)

### A8. The mallet - right hand only
- Bamboo (manau cane) shaft with a hardwood or bamboo-root cigar-shaped head, **48-54 inches long**. Britannica gives about 7 oz and 48-53 in. Players strike the ball with the **side** of the head, not the end.
  - Polo Museum FAQ; Britannica (above)
- **Every player must hold the mallet in the right hand.** USPA exempts only left-handers registered before **January 1 1974**. The reason is safety: players often ride head-on and pass each other at speed, and if everyone swings on the same side, the paths and swings stay predictable. The line-of-the-ball rules assume an offside (right-side) swing. Left-handed play was banned in the mid-1930s, allowed again after WWII when there were too few players, and banned again by USPA in 1974. Hurlingham's rule reads: "The right hand only is to be used to hold the stick."
  - Polo Museum FAQ; USPA Spectator Guide (above)
  - Dawson & Bell Polo Club, "The Game of Polo Is Right Handed" - https://dbpoloclub.com/polo-played-right-handed/ - Feb 21 2019

### A9. The ball
- Outdoor: hard plastic, **about 3.5 in diameter, about 4.5 oz**. It travels roughly three times faster than the rider who hit it. Historically it was made of willow or bamboo root (Britannica: about 3.25 in, about 4 oz).
  - Polo Museum FAQ; Britannica (above)
- Arena: an inflated ball. USPA Arena Rule 1.b gives 12.5-15 in circumference and 170-182 g.
  - USPA 2025 Arena Rules (below)

### A10. Horses ("ponies")
- They are called ponies by tradition. Early rules capped height at 13.2 hands. There is no limit now, and most stand 14-16+ hands (about 15.1 is typical). Most are Thoroughbreds or crosses (Thoroughbred x Quarter Horse in the US, Thoroughbred x Criollo in Argentina), aged 5-15.
  - Polo Museum FAQ; Wikipedia, "Polo pony" - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polo_pony - n.d.
- The pony is said to account for "50% to 80%" of a player's ability.
  - Polo Museum FAQ (above)
- **A player changes horses between chukkers.** A pony covers 1-2 miles per chukker. High-goal players typically use a fresh horse every chukker, and sometimes change mid-chukker. Every player needs at least two horses per match, and high-goal players bring 6-8 or more (a "string"). FIP rules let a player stop the game to change a lame or tired mount (5 minutes allowed). Snow polo at St. Moritz caps each pony at 2 chukkers per match, never back to back.
  - Polo Museum FAQ; Wikipedia "Polo pony"; FIP 3.24 (above); St. Moritz snow polo rules (below)
  - *(The "6-8 per high-goal player" figure is industry common knowledge and was not pinned to a primary source. Treat as unconfirmed.)*

### A11. Divot stomping
- At halftime spectators walk onto the field and tread back the clods (divots) of turf the hooves have kicked up. It is a social tradition, sometimes with a champagne toast. USPA advises wearing wedges rather than heels.
  - USPA Spectator Guide (above)

### A12. Arena polo vs field polo
- **Arena:** 3 per team (Nos. 1-3), an enclosed dirt or sand arena of ideally **300 x 150 feet** with walls, goals 10 ft wide x 15 ft high, an inflated ball, **4 chukkers of 7.5 min** (or six 5-minute periods), 4-minute breaks plus 10 minutes at halftime. Ends change every chukker. Penalty distances are shorter: Penalty 2 = 15 yd, Penalty 3 = 25 yd, Penalty 4 = 25 yd with one defender in the goal mouth. Arena positions: No. 1 is the most attacking, No. 2 directs play, No. 3 is the hardest-working defender.
  - USPA 2025 Arena Rules - https://d3uxdg7queknzi.cloudfront.net/assets/pdf/2025-2026-Arena-Rules.pdf - USPA, 2025 (Rules 1, 3, 5, 8, 9)
  - USPA Spectator Guide (old) (positions)
- **Field:** 4 per team, 300 x 160 yd grass, hard plastic ball, longer penalty distances, ends change after each goal.

### A13. Snow polo and beach polo
- **Snow polo (St. Moritz, since 1985):** 4 per team, played on a frozen lake. The field is about 90 x 220 m (smaller than grass) because snow and altitude tire the horses. Goals are 7.3 m with no crossbar. High-goal snow polo has 4 chukkas of 7.5 min (the last is 7 min), and no pony plays more than 2 chukkas, which may not be consecutive.
  - Snow Polo World Cup St. Moritz, "The rules of Snow Polo" (2024) - https://WWW.SNOWPOLO-STMORITZ.COM/fileadmin/user_upload/2024_The_rules_of_Snow_Polo.pdf - 2024
- **Beach polo:** 3 per team in an enclosed sand arena with boards about 4 ft high, an inflated ball at least 12.5 in around, and 4 seven-minute chukkers with horses changed after each. Created in Dubai in 2004 (credited to Reto Gaudenzi and Rashid Al Habtoor). Miami Beach followed in 2005.
  - Wikipedia, "Beach polo" - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beach_polo - n.d.

### A14. Governing bodies
- **USPA (United States Polo Association)** writes the Outdoor Rules and Arena Rules, sets US handicaps, and hosts the FIP International Rules text. Its rulebook is published annually, but the full PDFs could not be read through this toolchain (see Unconfirmed). Rule text above comes from the USPA "Rule of the Week" series and the Arena Rules PDF.
  - https://uspolo.org/sport/spectator-guide (above)
- **HPA (Hurlingham Polo Association)** governs polo in the UK, Ireland and other countries. Its rules are similar to USPA's, e.g. the right-hand rule.
  - Dawson & Bell, Feb 21 2019 (above)
- **AAP (Asociación Argentina de Polo)** - Argentina, which plays 8-chukker opens. **FIP (Federation of International Polo)** - international rules and the World Championship.
  - FIP 2016 International Rules (above)

### GAME DESIGN NOTES - minimum ruleset for a 2D browser polo game

*(Design recommendations, not sourced facts. The numbers trace back to the rules above.)*

**World**
- Top-down field with a 300:160 aspect ratio (about 15:8). Goals 8 yd wide at each end with no crossbar, so anything between the posts at any height scores. Optional sideboards: the ball rebounds off them instead of going out.
- Ball physics: low friction, high top speed (about 3x the horse's top speed) and quick decay. Hitting is a directional impulse.

**Beginner mode ("Divot")**
- 2v2 (a No. 1 attacker and a No. 4 back), or 1v1 against AI. 4 chukkers of 60-90 seconds of real time (scaled from 7 minutes). A horn plus a short overtime that ends at the next stoppage or after 5 seconds - this keeps the "30 seconds of overtime" feel.
- **Controls:** steer the horse (turning is momentum-based, so wide arcs), accelerate and brake, and one hit button. The mallet is **always on the right side of the horse**. A forehand swing hits forward-right, and holding back plus hit does a backhand (reverses the ball). Hitting a ball on your left needs a "neck shot" or "under the tail" swing with a smaller hit window and less power. This one constraint captures most of what makes polo polo.
- **One foul only: crossing.** Draw the line of the ball as a faint trail through the ball along its travel direction. The player whose path is closest to that line and most nearly parallel to it holds right of way (highlight that rider). Cutting across the right-of-way rider's path within N horse lengths ahead of them is a foul, and the fouled team gets a free hit from the 40-yard line (Penalty 3).
- Ends switch after every goal (flip the camera, or show an arrow). Throw-in at centre after each goal.
- Divot stomp mini-game at halftime: tap the divots to stamp them back. Optional reward: a smoother field (less random ball bounce) in the second half. This is a natural tie-in to the real tradition.

**Expert mode ("High Goal")**
- 4v4 with roles: AI teammates behave as a No. 1 (stays forward, marks the opposing No. 4), No. 2 (presses), No. 3 (pivot) and No. 4 (stays back).
- 6 chukkers, plus sudden-death extra chukkers on a tie.
- **Horse stamina and a string:** each player picks a horse between chukkers from a string of 4-6. Each horse has speed, turning and stamina stats and tires during a chukker, so a horse used in the previous chukker starts tired. *(Snow polo's "max 2 chukkers, not consecutive" rule is a good optional hard cap.)*
- **Handicap system:** players are rated -2 to 10, and the team rating is the sum. In handicap matches the lower team starts with (difference ÷ 6 × chukkers) goals, rounded (decide whether fractions are rounded or shown as half goals). This makes "fair games between unequal teams" a selectable mode.
- **Contact:**
  - Ride-off: a shove when side by side, legal if the approach angle is 45° or less, and a "dangerous riding" foul if steeper.
  - Hook: a block button that cancels an opponent's swing if you are directly behind or on the same side as the swing while it is happening. A hook from the wrong side or at the wrong time is a foul.
- **Penalty ladder (simplified):** P1 = awarded goal (a cross or dangerous ride inside your own 30 yd that stops a sure goal), P2 = 30 yd hit, P3 = 40 yd, P4 = 60 yd, P5 = hit from the spot, P6 safety = 60 yd hit when a defender knocks the ball over their own back line. The knock-in restart applies when an attacker misses wide.
- **Arena variant:** 3v3 in a walled box, ball rebounds off every wall, ends switch each chukker, shorter penalty distances (15 and 25 yd).
- **Snow variant:** smaller field, higher friction, and a lighter-coloured, bouncier ball (cosmetic).

**UX/AX notes for this page**
- Expose match state as JSON: score, chukker, clock, who holds right of way, last foul and penalty number, and horse stamina. That gives agents and spectator overlays something to read.
- Keep on-screen text to the right-of-way highlight and a penalty number pop-up ("P3 - 40 yards"). That one pop-up teaches the penalty ladder.

### B5. Eldorado Polo Club - where desert polo lives now
- Founded **1957** by a small group of players as a members' club at the original Eldorado Country Club in Indian Wells. Rising land values pushed it to Indio by 1979. It was the valley's top facility until Empire opened next door in 1987.
  - Palm Springs Life, "Polo in Paradise" (above)
- **Goldenvoice bought about 280 acres** around the festival site in March 2012, including Eldorado Polo Club, Triangle Bar Farms and Fish Creek.
  - LAist, "Goldenvoice buys..." - https://laist.com/news/entertainment/goldenvoice-buys-coachella-festival - Mar 24 2012
  - mxdwn, "Goldenvoice buys 280 acres..." - https://music.mxdwn.com/2012/03/26/news/goldenvoice-buys-280-acres-of-land-around-the-site-of-the-coachella-festival/ - Mar 26 2012
- The 2012 sale "included a leaseback that let Eldorado keep hosting polo". The lease ran to May 31 2020, with new terms to be negotiated after that. Management said the fields were "in the best condition in the club's history".
  - USPA, "Eldorado Polo Club continues a desert tradition for years to come" - https://uspolo.org/news-social/news/eldoardo-polo-club-continues-a-desert-tradition-for-years-to-come - Nov 20 2017
- Current setup: **13 fields**, plus 2 at Empire Calhoun and 1 at Mission (Fred Mannix's ranch). Goldenvoice owns the land, and **Eldorado leases it until April, when Goldenvoice hosts Coachella and Stagecoach**. In the 1990s Eldorado hosted 22-goal polo and the US Open, and Governor's Cup tournaments once drew more than 40 teams.
  - USPA/ClickPoloUSA, "Eldorado: the heart of winter polo on the West Coast" - https://uspolo.org/news-social/news/eldorado-the-heart-of-winter-polo-on-the-west-coast-an-article-by-clickpolousa - Jan 14 2025

### B6. Season
- **The desert polo season runs roughly mid-December to late March or early April, so "Dec-Apr" is accurate:**
  - Empire (2016): "mid-December through March", with an official start in late December or early January - USPA, Nov 25 2016
  - Eldorado 2018: tournaments Jan 6 - Apr 1 - USPA, Nov 20 2017
  - Eldorado 2024-25: practices from mid-December, women's tournaments between Christmas and New Year, and four 12-goal tournaments in February and March - USPA, Jan 14 2025
  - About 2015: 13-week seasons January-March, Sunday matches, mostly free entry, "more than 4,000 spectators" each Sunday - Palm Springs Life, "Polo in Paradise"

### B7. Polo season vs festival setup - the overlap
- The handoff is tight: Eldorado's lease runs "until April, when Goldenvoice hosts Coachella and Stagecoach" (USPA, Jan 14 2025). The 2018 Eldorado season ended Apr 1 (USPA, Nov 20 2017), and Coachella runs in mid-April.
- Festival build starts before polo ends. In 2024 the new Sahara footings began in **December**, while polo season was under way on the neighbouring grounds.
  - The Desert Sun (Ema Sasic) via Yahoo, "Coachella 2024: Sahara Tent is moving locations" - https://www.yahoo.com/entertainment/coachella-2024-sahara-tent-moving-003831144.html - Feb 7 2024
- The festival uses Eldorado land too. In 2018 the Sahara moved "from its long-time spot near the rose garden to the neighboring Eldorado Polo Club" (Desert Sun, Feb 7 2024, above). In 2015 The Desert Sun described "the adjacent Eldorado Polo Club, also used by the festival".
  - The Desert Sun (Bruce Fessier and Ian James) via World Polo News, "Coachella in drought: Polo club looks to cut water use" - https://worldpolonews.com/2015/coachella-drought-polo-club-looks-cut-water-use - Apr 2015

### B8. Turf
- Empire irrigated with groundwater and untreated Colorado River canal water ("ag water") through computer-controlled in-ground sprinklers. The polo club watered the fields 11 months a year, and Goldenvoice (Tollett) managed April. Haagen: "Polo ponies run on grass. They don't run on dirt." Grass is "restored after the festivals". The article gives no reseeding details or costs.
  - The Desert Sun via World Polo News, Apr 2015 (above)
- The Sahara has a "permanent concrete footing buried two feet beneath the grass of the polo field". The tent structure itself is dug back up every spring.
  - Los Angeles Times (August Brown) via AOL, "EDM may have waned, but the Sahara Tent remains the beating heart of Coachella" - https://www.aol.com/news/edm-may-waned-sahara-tent-120013512.html - Apr 9 2023
- AEG grows turf about 15 miles away (it supplies the LA Galaxy stadium). Kara Korber of AEG: "Quality turf is really important to the game of soccer."
  - The Desert Magazine (Ameer Mussard-Afcari), "Forget polo - now it's the Empire soccer grounds" - https://thedesertmag.com/our-town/forget-polo-%E2%80%93-now-it%E2%80%99s-the-empire-soccer-grounds/ - n.d. (about 2025, "four years" into CVI)

### B9. Coachella Valley polo history
- Hollywood took up polo by the 1920s. Walt Disney led the Palm Springs horsemen, and Spencer Tracy and Clark Gable "likely" rode early makeshift fields.
  - Palm Springs Life, "Polo in Paradise" (above) - the hedged "likely" is in the source
- **"Polo Capital of the West": NOT verified.** No source found uses that phrase. The closest are USPA's "the heart of winter polo on the West Coast" (Jan 14 2025) and Palm Springs Life's "largest polo complex in America" (Empire + Eldorado combined, about 2015).

### B10. Coachella's origin on the polo grounds
- **Coachella has been at Empire since 1999 and Stagecoach since 2007.** Goldenvoice's relationship with the club goes back "over 25 years" (as of 2021).
  - Business Wire, "Goldenvoice to Assume Operations of Empire Polo Club in Long-Term Agreement" - https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20211027006159/en - Oct 27 2021
- Origin story: Pearl Jam's 1993 Indio show at Empire, staged there because the band was boycotting Ticketmaster venues, showed promoter Paul Tollett the site could work.
  - Far Out Magazine, "How Pearl Jam inspired Coachella Festival" - https://faroutmagazine.co.uk/how-pearl-jam-inspired-coachella-festival/ - n.d.
  - *(Wikipedia's "leased annually to Goldenvoice since 1993" dates the relationship from that show, not from the festival.)*
- Mixed-use plan: in June 2025 Empire's owner proposed a roughly 218-acre mixed-use "lifestyle hub" next to the festival grounds (housing, retail, dining, possibly a sports venue). County officials approved a land-use change, but final approval was pending at the time.
  - NBC Palm Springs, "Major development project proposed near Indio festival grounds" - https://www.nbcpalmsprings.com/2025/06/09/major-development-project-proposed-near-indio-festival-grounds - Jun 9 2025

## PART C - THE GROUNDS YEAR-ROUND

### C1. The 2021 deal
- Announced **Oct 27 2021**. Goldenvoice took "year-round operational control of the site", replacing an arrangement tied to the festival calendar. It also gained oversight of Empire Grand Oasis in Thermal and planned to open both properties to "special events, sporting events, and private functions." Tollett: "It's immeasurable how much we've learned from Al Haagen."
  - Business Wire, Oct 27 2021 (above)
- **The lease runs through 2050.** Goldenvoice can add **two more large festivals a year** on the site (Billboard via Consequence).
  - Consequence (Alex Young), "Coachella to stay in Indio through 2050" - https://consequence.net/2021/10/coachella-indio-2050 - Oct 27 2021
- Lease length: "28-year lease signed in 2021" (2021 + 28 = 2049-2050).
  - The Desert Magazine (above); Wikipedia citing The Desert Sun (Blueskye, 2021)
- Separately, in August 2021 Indio City Council extended its development agreement with Goldenvoice to 2050. Goldenvoice pays the city about **$3.5 million a year**, per city records.
  - EntouragePro (n.d., Oct 2021, citing Billboard) (above)

### C2. Permanent and semi-permanent infrastructure

**Sahara (2024 rebuild)** - steel replaced aluminium, the structure is 320 ft long, and it moved to a "whole new area of the venue" between Avenue 51 and Polo Road. Underground footings began in December 2023, using about **1,500 tons of concrete (about 156 truckloads)** poured overnight to keep trucks off Indio's streets. Neither source says the steel stays up year-round. Earlier evidence (2023) is that the footing is permanent and the structure comes down.
- The Desert Sun (Ema Sasic) via Yahoo, Feb 7 2024 (above)
- LA Times via AOL, Apr 9 2023 (above)

**Quasar (2024)** - a new stage announced Mar 18 2024 for extended DJ sets, placed beside the new Sahara. No source confirms it is a permanent structure.
- The Desert Sun (Brian Blueskye) via Yahoo, "Goldenvoice announces new stage for Coachella" - https://www.yahoo.com/entertainment/goldenvoice-announces-stage-coachella-featuring-184539618.html - Mar 20 2024
- Appeal-Democrat via PressReader, "Coachella fans react to expanded, changed festival grounds" - https://www.pressreader.com/usa/marysville-appeal-democrat/20240418/281883008388583 - Apr 18 2024

**Radiohead bunker (2026) - permanent, underground construction.**
- Goldenvoice applied to Indio in **September 2025** for a building permit for a **16,130 sq ft "commercial foundation"**, valued by city assessors at **$2.5 million**.
  - The Desert Sun (Sam Morgen) via AOL, "What is the structure being built on the Coachella festival grounds?" - https://www.aol.com/news/structure-being-built-coachella-festival-224256302.html - spring 2026, n.d.
- The finished bunker: **17,000 sq ft, 38 ft high, beneath the Empire Polo Fields**. Loose desert sand ruled out piles, so the team dug out the whole footprint, built a concrete retaining-wall box with a steel frame, and backfilled it. The roof uses 12 x 12 in steel truss chords and carries compacted sand, turf and **up to 1,000 people standing on top**. Construction ran from November 2025 to opening weekend. **"The bunker remains on the Coachella site as a permanent fixture."**
  - Wonder Works (designer and production manager), "Radiohead Motion Picture House" - https://wonder.co.uk/mies_portfolio/radiohead-motion-picture-house-kid-a-mnesia/ - 2026, n.d.
- Built into a grassy hill, two storeys underground. The Desert Sun: "It seems inconceivable that the structure would be demolished," but "It's not clear what will become of the newest stage next year."
  - The Desert Sun (Sam Morgen) via Yahoo, "What to expect from Coachella's new underground movie theater bunker" - https://www.yahoo.com/entertainment/music/articles/expect-coachellas-underground-movie-theater-020048709.html - Apr 10 2026
- The exhibit tours (Brooklyn May 6-31, Chicago Jul 30 - Aug 23, Mexico City Oct 27 - Nov 15, San Francisco Jan 14 - Feb 7) in an above-ground version. The bunker itself stays in Indio.
  - Stereogum (Chris DeVille) - https://stereogum.com/2495193/radiohead-are-taking-their-coachella-bunker-art-installation-on-tour/news - Apr 8 2026; Wonder Works (above)

**Spectra** - a seven-storey spiral tower by Patrick O'Mahony / Newsubstance, unveiled 2018. It was planned as a three-year piece through 2020, but Covid and fan affection kept it standing. Newsubstance now calls it "a permanent feature of the festival." It appeared again in 2024 (Press-Enterprise video). Whether it stays standing off-season was **not confirmed** by any source.

## BEST STORIES

1. **The polo club stopped playing polo.** Coachella is named for a polo club where no polo season has been played since November 2021. Staff got three days' notice. Desert polo moved next door to Eldorado, which Goldenvoice also owns. (KESQ Nov 1 2021; Desert Sun Jan 2022; Palm Springs Life)
2. **The landlord's tenant's landlord.** Goldenvoice bought Eldorado in 2012 and leased it back to the polo players. Their lease effectively ends each April, when the festival takes the grass. Polo season runs right up to load-in. (USPA Nov 20 2017, Jan 14 2025)
3. **A movie theatre under the polo field.** In 2026 Goldenvoice buried a 17,000 sq ft, 38 ft-high concrete-and-steel box beneath the Empire Polo Fields for Radiohead's *KID A MNESIA*. Its turfed roof holds 1,000 people, and its designers say it stays as a permanent fixture. The exhibit tours; the bunker doesn't. ($2.5M permit, Sept 2025.) (Wonder Works; Desert Sun; Stereogum Apr 8 2026)
4. **The Sahara's buried foundation.** Its concrete footing sits two feet under the polo grass year-round, and the tent above it is dug back up every spring. The 2024 rebuild poured 1,500 tons of concrete overnight. (LA Times Apr 9 2023; Desert Sun Feb 7 2024)
5. **"Polo ponies run on grass. They don't run on dirt."** Haagen explaining why the polo fields were watered 11 months a year, with Goldenvoice in charge of April. (Desert Sun Apr 2015)
6. **The only lit grass polo field in America** was at Empire, home of Friday "Polo Under the Lights". (USPA Nov 25 2016)
7. **The World Championship that never came.** Indio was set to host the 2021 FIP World Polo Championship. COVID pushed it to 2022 and to Florida. Within months, polo at Empire was gone. (USPA Feb 10 2020; Wikipedia)
8. **Right-handed by law.** Every polo player must swing right-handed, which makes it one of the few sports that bans left-handed play. Only lefties registered before Jan 1 1974 are exempt. The rule exists because riders pass head-on at a gallop. (Polo Museum; Dawson & Bell 2019)
9. **The Forum is a polo field in feet.** AEG's clear-span event building on the grounds is 300 x 160 **feet**, the same proportions as the 300 x 160 **yard** polo field outside. (AEG SEV) *(Probably a coincidence; don't claim intent.)*
10. **Pearl Jam's boycott made the site.** The band's 1993 Ticketmaster-free show at the polo club is the origin story of Coachella at Empire. (Far Out, n.d. Needs a better source.)
11. **Divot stomp.** Halftime at a polo match means spectators treading turf back into the field, with champagne. It is a ready-made halftime mini-game for the browser game. (USPA Spectator Guide)
