# Coachella - Camping, Rituals and the People Who Run It

Research pass, Oct 31 2026. Each fact is followed by its source(s). "Reported by attendees" marks social/Reddit/TikTok material. Where a fetch failed or a tool refused a URL, that is noted and the claim is moved to "Unconfirmed".

## 1. Camping history: tent first, cars in 2010

- On-site camping was first offered in 2003. Indio City Council approved overnight camping for up to four people on each of 2,252 spots.
  - Wikipedia, "Coachella" (citing 2003 coverage, its refs 39-40) - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coachella (accessed Oct 31 2026)
  - Consistent with OC Weekly calling 2004 "the second year camping was permitted" - Mary Carreon, "The Evolution Of Coachella's Campgrounds," OC Weekly, Apr 20 2018 - https://www.ocweekly.com/?p=88797
- Car camping arrived with the 2010 festival. Before that, campers parked and carried gear to the walk-in tent areas. The 2010 car sites were 10 ft x 30 ft, with campers sleeping beside the car.
  - Phoenix New Times, 2010 pre-festival item (no byline or date on page; content dates it to early 2010) - https://www.phoenixnewtimes.com/?p=40532050
- 2010 was also the year of other camping upgrades: a farmers market by the general store, the Down & Derby roller rink, and $5,000 "Safari" tents (furnished, air-conditioned, showers, golf-cart shuttles, private parking).
  - BizBash, "Coachella Sells Out With Three-Day Passes, Towering Ferris Wheel, and Upgraded Camping," Apr 19 2010 - https://www.bizbash.com/event-production-planning/coachella-sells-out-with-three-day-passes-towering-ferris-wheel-and-upgraded-camping
- Campground culture by era (one writer's account, not data): 2004 a party scene; 2007 a campground riot after Rage Against the Machine's Sunday closer (helicopters, police, a Taser, per attendee accounts the writer collected); 2008 heavy multi-agency patrols and a new 2 a.m. curfew, largely ignored; by 2014 much quieter at night.
  - Carreon, OC Weekly, Apr 20 2018 - https://www.ocweekly.com/?p=88797

### What camping is in 2026 (official page)
- Tiers: Car Camping; Preferred (Lot 8); Preferred Front Row; Powered (Lot 5B, one outlet); Group Car Camping (new for 2026, 10-20 spots in Lot 5A, members can arrive separately); Ready-Set Tent (pre-pitched tent in Lot 4B, parking in Lot 2A); La Campana (temperature-controlled tents with private showers, Lot 5A, waitlist); Lake Eldorado (2-person lodge or 4-person tent between Lots 7 and 8, own check-in, concierge and restrooms); plus Safari Camping and "The Resort at Coachella" on separate pages.
- Camping open Thursday 9 a.m. to Monday 9 a.m. Car/tent check-in Thursday 9 a.m.-2 a.m.; Fri/Sat 9 a.m.-8 p.m.; Sun 9 a.m.-3 p.m. Checkout Monday 9 a.m.
- Vehicles cannot leave Fri-Sun 9 p.m.-2 a.m. ("lockdown," may shift with traffic). Once parked, cars don't move all weekend. Cars and campers are searched at check-in. Sites are assigned in order of arrival within each tier. No camping between weekends. Noise curfew 1:30 a.m. 18+ unless with a parent/guardian.
- "Arrive Together to Camp Together": Preferred and Powered groups must drive through the toll booth and search together; saving spots is not allowed.
- General Store (Lot 8): Panera bread delivered every morning, donuts, salsa and guac, sandwiches, fruit, ice. Farmers Market by Flutterby Ranch (produce and live butterflies). Food trucks from 2 p.m. Thursday. Free supermarket shuttle from Lot 7. Mobile ice sales.
  - Coachella, "Camping" (2026 edition, live page) - https://www.coachella.com/camping (accessed Oct 31 2026)
- Campground programming 2026: Activities Tent, Camp Lounge with Wi-Fi, The Oasis recovery zone (massage, ice baths, red-light therapy), Desert Sky with DJs then silent disco 1-3 a.m. Thursday / 1-4 a.m. Fri-Sun, Thursday stargazing on Coyote Hill with NASA-supplied telescopes, Sunday noon water balloon fight, a Euphoria Season 3 screening in the campgrounds Sunday Apr 12 at 11:59 p.m.
  - Coachella, "Activities & Amenities" (2026) - https://www.coachella.com/activities-amenities (accessed Oct 31 2026)

### Safari / luxury
- 2010 Safari tents: $5,000 (see BizBash above).
- A Palm Springs Life piece "Haute Safari" lists about $6,500 per weekend including two wristbands, golf-cart shuttles, A/C tents, a marquee with breakfast and a DJ, and a concierge; $1,500 per extra guest. Page has no date or byline, so year is unknown.
  - Palm Springs Life, "Haute Safari" (undated) - https://www.palmspringslife.com/Haute-Safari/

## 2. The night before: parking lot campouts

### 2018 - "Walmartchella"
- Weekend 2, 2018: a high-wind warning (25-35 mph, gusts to 60) delayed camping from 9 a.m. Thursday to about 3 a.m. Friday (about 18 hours). Thousands gathered in a Walmart lot in Indio and tagged it #Walmartchella; folding chairs and portable barbecues. Indio officers reportedly joined in a beanbag game.
  - CBS Los Angeles, "With Coachella Campground Closed, Festival-Goers Improvise With 'Walmartchella'," Apr 20 2018 - https://www.cbsnews.com/losangeles/news/walmartchella/
- The two cities responded differently. Riverside County Sheriff's deputies told campers to leave the La Quinta Walmart lot early Thursday, citing a La Quinta ordinance against sleeping in vehicles. Indio Police said Indio would be lenient; Sgt. Dan Marshall said campers "may need to seek refuge in parking lots."
  - KESQ, "THURS AM: Stranded Coachella campers have nowhere to go," Apr 19 2018 - https://kesq.com/?p=304240
- Cities then published a list of 12 temporary waiting sites: in Indio, lots at the Indio Fashion Mall, Target (Jackson St), Super Walmart (Ave 42), K-Mart, Home Depot, Fantasy Springs casino and the Walmart Neighborhood Market (Jefferson St); La Quinta City Hall's south lot (500+ spots, restrooms, no tents or open flame); Lake Cahuilla campsites; in Coachella, Village 29 Campground (up to 1,000), Augustine Casino and the Coachella Neighborhood Walmart.
  - NBC Los Angeles, "Stranded Coachella Festival Campers Take Refuge in Temporary Sites," Apr 19 2018 - https://www.nbclosangeles.com/news/stranded-coachella-festival-campers-take-refuge-in-temporary-sites/44081/?amp=1
- OC Weekly also notes La Quinta set up areas for stranded campers that year. - Carreon, Apr 20 2018 (above)

### 2026 - the Ralphs on Jefferson Street
- At 1 a.m. on Thursday ("Day 0") of 2026, the Ralphs grocery lot on Jefferson Street in Indio held a pre-camping gathering: shopping, cars decorated for the Carpoolchella contest, porta-potties, music and a pop-up DJ set by NDA. The gathering has happened "for the past few years" and spread through the Coachella subreddit. Official camping opens at 9 a.m., but the article says security usually lets people in around 3 a.m.; first cars left around 2 a.m.
- One group recalled police patrolling the lot in its first year and knocking on windows if people stayed more than about 30 minutes (reported by attendees).
- The article doesn't name who supplied the porta-potties or quote Ralphs management.
  - Matt King, "Parking-Lot Party: Scenes From One of Coachella's Hottest Pre-Festival Gatherings at the Ralphs in Indio," Coachella Valley Independent, Apr 18 2026 - https://cvindependent.com/2026/04/parking-lot-party-scenes-from-one-of-coachellas-hottest-pre-festival-gatherings-at-the-ralphs-in-indio/
- No Albertsons-specific campout or store ban was found in reporting. The ban that is on record is the La Quinta vehicle-sleeping ordinance enforced at the La Quinta Walmart (KESQ 2018 above).

## 3. The gate and "the line"

- 2025 Weekend 1 entry ("carmageddon"): reported waits of up to 12 hours to reach the campground entrance.
  - NBC Palm Springs, "Coachella organizers apologize for 12-hour traffic delays, promise fixes for weekend two," Apr 16 2025 - https://www.nbcpalmsprings.com/2025/04/16/coachella-organizers-apologize-for-12hour-traffic-delays-promise-fixes-for-weekend-two
- Goldenvoice's explanation at the La Quinta City Council (Tuesday Apr 15 2025, George Cunningham): "Everybody wanted to come early" and the usual midday lull never came. Causes: the first-ever Thursday-night campground concert, checkpoints that weren't fully staffed, and a policy change - campers were not let in before the official 9 a.m. opening, unlike past years, so the queue spilled onto surrounding roads. Fix for W2: more toll booths.
  - Julia Castro and Luis Avila, KESQ, Apr 16 2025 - https://kesq.com/?p=1663117
  - NBC Palm Springs, Apr 16 2025 (above)
- Secondary roll-up of the 2025 incident (cites Business Insider, SFGATE, ABC7, KESQ, LA Times; KESQ figure of 40,000+ campers leaving after W1).
  - Working With Crowds, "Coachella Campground Entry 11 April 2025 - Incident Report" (undated, no byline) - https://www.workingwithcrowds.com/?p=37530
- The early-entry tension is ongoing: in 2026 the Independent reported security "usually" lets cars in around 3 a.m. despite the 9 a.m. official time (King, CV Independent, Apr 18 2026, above). Read with the 2025 policy note: the practice varies year to year.

## 4. Day Zero

- "Day Zero" / "Day 0" is the Thursday before each weekend, when campers arrive. Since 2025 Goldenvoice books a campers-only campground DJ show that night.
- 2025: Chris Lake played the W1 Thursday campground party (the "first-ever Thursday night concert" Goldenvoice blamed in part for the 2025 queue); Disco Dom played W2.
- 2026: Disco Lines headlined W1 Day Zero, announced Tuesday Apr 7 2026, described as an exclusive pre-festival performance for campers, second year running.
  - Brian Blueskye, Palm Springs Desert Sun, "Disco Lines announced as Day Zero performer for Coachella 2026," via AOL, Apr 7 2026 - https://www.aol.com/news/disco-lines-announced-day-zero-183226054.html
  - KESQ, Apr 16 2025 (above)
- 2026 W2 Day Zero: KETTAMA, Prospa and Josh Baker back-to-back-to-back at the campground party.
  - Beatportal, "KETTAMA, Prospa, and Josh Baker to Go b3b at Coachella Weekend 2 Day Zero Party," Apr 14 2026 - https://www.beatportal.com/articles/1374860-kettama-prospa-and-josh-baker-to-go-b3b-at-coachella-weekend-2-day-zero-party
- The Thursday campground also now has official programming from 9 a.m.: Desert Sky DJs from 8 p.m., silent disco, stargazing, vintage merch sale (Coachella Activities & Amenities, above).
- The unofficial Day Zero is the parking-lot party (Ralphs 2026, above). "Day 0" is also r/Coachella's term - its countdown threads are titled "...sleeps until Day 0" (reported by attendees).

Agencies listed that year: Indio PD (lead), Palm Springs PD, Cathedral City PD, Desert Hot Springs PD, Riverside County Sheriff, CHP, Banning PD, Beaumont PD and the FBI. Same piece: armed rifle observation posts above the VIP area and the Outdoor Theatre; drones over traffic. Indio PD Sgt. Daniel Marshall said planning starts a year out.
  - Mary Carreon, "Coachella Sets The Standard Of Security In High Risk Festival Environment," OC Weekly, Apr 16 2018 - https://www.ocweekly.com/?p=88753

  - MyNewsLA/RCNS, "Sheriff's Mounted Patrol-led enforcement operation in San Jacinto...," Jan 14 2022 - https://myrcns.com/2022/01/14/sheriffs-mounted-patrol-led-enforcement-operation-in-san-jacinto-leads-to-28-warrants-served-13-arrests

## 7. Fences, gate crashers and wristbands

- 2010 was the breaking point. Goldenvoice dropped single-day tickets for the first time and sold only three-day passes at $269 (75,000 sold out), with nontransferable wristbands meant to deter scalpers (BizBash, Apr 19 2010, above). Attendance was about 75,000 per day, up about 15,000 per day from 2009 (Michael Mishak, Las Vegas Sun, "Strangechella," Apr 21 2010 - https://lasvegassun.com/news/2010/apr/21/strangechella/; UPI, Apr 18 2010 - https://www.upi.com/Entertainment_News/Music/2010/04/18/Coachella-Fest-draws-record-crowds/UPI-65451271610186/). Reports describe long front-gate holdups and gate-crashing worries.
- 2011 response: a wrought-iron fence around the Empire Polo grounds to deter gate crashers, an extra traffic lane partly funded by Goldenvoice, and no more ticket-for-wristband exchange.
  - Las Vegas Sun, "After rough 2010, Coachella tries to iron itself out," Apr 13 2011 (no byline on fetch) - https://m.lasvegassun.com/news/2011/apr/13/after-rough-2010-coachella-tries-iron-itself-out/
- 2012: two identical weekends, announced May 31 2011, to meet demand without overcrowding; 158,387 paid across both (Wikipedia, citing Billboard/Pollstar, above). The RFID system came from Intellitix: it handled access control, was described as "eliminating" counterfeit tickets, and offered automatic social-media check-ins.
  - SecureIDNews, "Coachella rakes in more fans via RFID social media," May 14 2012 - https://www.secureidnews.com/?p=22280
- Wristband fraud is still the main hustle. In 2026 ("Scamchella"), sellers on Facebook Marketplace and OfferUp sold wristbands and then reported them "lost or stolen," and the wristbands were deactivated after buyers had already entered. Example: Ryan Jimenez paid $2,000+ for three, got in Day 1 and was locked out Day 2. Hundreds lined up at will call.
  - NBC Palm Springs, "Scamchella: surge in ticket fraud...," Apr 17 2026 - https://www.nbcpalmsprings.com/2026/04/17/scamchella-surge-ticket-fraud-casts-shadow-over-festival-season
- Earlier crackdowns: in 2018, staff were told to check wristbands closely (especially for VIP), with zero tolerance and on-the-spot arrests for theft (unnamed festival sources).
  - TMZ, Apr 14 2018 - https://www.tmz.com/2018/04/14/coachella-crackdown-security-counterfeit-passes-wristbands/

## 8. Vendors and the hot dog run

- Bacon-wrapped hot dog vendors used to sell along every entry path at any hour. A Phoenix New Times piece (circa 2016) says they were pushed to end-of-night only, in the northeast lots at the end of the Yellow Path, after AEG/Goldenvoice closed Madison and Monroe between Ave 48 and 52 to foot traffic, citing safety. Kids selling stacked 24-packs of water and locals' shaded cooler stands were described as largely gone.
  - Phoenix New Times, "The Slow Death of Coachella's Local Vendor Economy" (no byline or date on page; context about 2016) - https://www.phoenixnewtimes.com/music/the-slow-death-of-coachellas-local-vendor-economy-8246020/
- The "storming" (Apr 13 2025, end of W1): TikTok videos showed hot dog vendors racing toward the exit to set up for departing crowds. One caption claimed they "literally stormed the venue, breaking down the fence," and that security threatened pepper spray. Distractify notes that the fence-breaking claim is not confirmed. A 2024 r/Coachella post described "the insanity of the hotdog vendors and security." All reported by attendees.
  - Chrissy Bobic, "Hot Dog Vendors Are All About Opportunity When It Comes to Thrifty Coachella Attendees," Distractify, Apr 15 2025 - https://www.distractify.com/p/hot-dog-vendors-storm-coachella

## 9. Heat and medical

- Heat is a problem by design: 1999 was held in October above 100°F, and Goldenvoice moved to April in 2001 "to beat the heat." Recorded festival temperatures range from 43°F (Apr 14 2012) to 106°F (Apr 21 2012).
  - Wikipedia, "Coachella" (citing period coverage and weather data), accessed Oct 31 2026 - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coachella
- JFK Memorial Hospital in Indio is the closest hospital and adds staff each year. It routes overflow to regional partners and sees heat illness plus drug/alcohol complications. Medical director Dr. Andrew Cassinove: "many of these emergency visits are preventable."
  - NBC Palm Springs, Apr 9 2026 - https://nbcpalmsprings.com/2026/04/09/coachella-weekend-one-nears-as-local-hospitals-brace-for-patient-surge
- Deaths on record: Kimchi Truong, 24, collapsed Saturday at Coachella 2014 and died days later; Goldenvoice called it "an apparent overdose," pending toxicology.
  - CBS Los Angeles, Apr 18 2014 - https://www.cbsnews.com/amp/losangeles/news/coachella-festival-concertgoer-dies-days-after-collapsing/
- The 2025 campground queue sat in triple-digit heat (LA Times, Apr 11 2025, as cited by Working With Crowds, above - not fetched directly).

## 10. Indio during the festival: money, rentals, residents

- 2012 tax fight: Indio floated a 5-10% admissions tax on events over 2,500 people (Goldenvoice put it at about $36 per ticket, $4-6M per year). Tollett warned 2013 could be the last year in Indio.
  - CBS Los Angeles, Jul 5 2012 - https://www.cbsnews.com/losangeles/news/coachella-promoters-threaten-to-move-away-from-the-coachella-valley-over-proposed-tax/
- Resolution (2013): Councilman Sam Torres's 6% tax proposal never came to a vote. A new agreement set a $5 fee per ticket starting the following year (about $1M per year) and kept the festival in Indio through at least 2030. Goldenvoice's own study claims $90M+ to Indio's economy.
  - Ben Bergman (KPCC) for NPR Morning Edition, 2013 - https://www.npr.org/transcripts/177652870
- Before that deal, Indio was getting $2.33 per ticket (about $500,000).
  - Matthew Grant Anson, California Forward, "Coachella music festival is the rock star of the Indio economy" (undated, pre-2013 context) - https://cafwd.org/news/coachella-music-festival-is-the-rock-star-of-the-indio-economy/
- 2021: council voted 3-1 to extend through 2050. Mayor Elaine Holmes recused because she owns property near the site; Oscar Ortiz voted no. Goldenvoice also took full operational control of the Empire Polo Club. No per-ticket terms were reported.
  - Digital Music News, Oct 27 2021 - https://www.digitalmusicnews.com/2021/10/27/coachella-indio-deal/
- Residents: neighbors rent rooms, sheds and yard space at premium prices; Goldenvoice has bought nearby houses and one resident said it wants her block; truck parking lots sit on former house lots; houses shake from the bass. Residents can get discounted tickets but "nothing more." Economic impact estimates cited: $90M Indio, $254.4M valley-wide.
  - OC Weekly Staff, "What's It Like Living Right Next to Coachella?," Apr 22 2016 - https://www.ocweekly.com/?p=107674
- Example visitor rental: a condo for $800+ for the weekend (California Forward, above).

## Best stories

1. **Walmartchella (2018).** Wind shuts the campground for 18 hours. Thousands camp in a Walmart lot. Indio cops play cornhole with them, while two miles away sheriff's deputies move campers out of the La Quinta Walmart under a no-sleeping-in-cars law. The cities end up publishing a list of 12 parking lots, a casino and a lake to wait in.
2. **The Ralphs (2026).** A grocery-store lot on Jefferson Street turns into an unofficial Day Zero rave at 1 a.m., with porta-potties, Carpoolchella-decorated cars and a DJ, organized entirely through Reddit.
3. **Day Zero backfires (2025).** Goldenvoice's first campers-only Thursday party (Chris Lake) pulls everyone in early, the gate holds to 9 a.m., and campers sit in up to 12-hour lines in triple-digit heat. Goldenvoice apologizes at the La Quinta City Council and keeps Day Zero for 2026.
4. **Sunday Service on the Mountain (Easter 2019).** A 9 a.m. gospel service on a man-made hill in the campground, about 50,000 people (production sources), robed choir on the slopes, debut of "Water," and a merch tent called "Church Clothes." Tollett's reason: the campers are already there.
5. **The hot dog sprint (2025).** At the end of W1, bacon-wrapped hot dog vendors race the exit crowd on camera. TikTok calls it storming the fence (unconfirmed), and commenters cheer for cheap food after $20 festival meals. The backstory is Goldenvoice gradually pushing local vendors to the end of the Yellow Path.
6. **Scamchella (2026).** People sell wristbands on OfferUp, then report them stolen once the buyer is inside - RFID turned into a refund scam.
7. **The tax standoff (2012-13).** Indio, near bankruptcy, floats a ticket tax. Goldenvoice threatens to leave. They settle on $5 per ticket and a lease that, after the 2021 extension, now runs to 2050 - voted through while the mayor recused herself because she owns land next door.
