## 1. The ritual

### When it became a recognized thing
- No single "first" artist billboard is documented. Here are the earliest documented examples found, in date order:
  - **2014 - Miami Horror.** A DASH TWO blog post dated Jan 20, 2015 has a photo of the band in front of "their billboard from last year," which would be 2014. The post says only "a handful of billboards" were left for the 2015 festival. [read] https://dashtwo.com/blog/coachella-billboard-advertising/ - DASH TWO blog (Gino Sesto / Kim M. Pham) - Jan 20, 2015
  - **Around 2015-2016, labels start doing it.** A Billboard Pro piece says Coachella billboards "first appeared about six years" earlier, when labels began targeting both festivalgoers and the performers driving the same route. The article has no date on its face. It mentions 2019 and the 2020-21 cancellations as past, so it is almost certainly from April 2022, which puts the start at about 2016. DASH TWO's founder says the firm has sold Coachella boards since about 2015. Per the same piece, artists stopped on the highway for selfies with their boards, and the next year non-music brands followed (Florence by Mills, *Rocketman*). [read] https://www.billboard.com/pro/coachella-billboards-interstate-10-festival-advertising-strategy/ - Billboard - date not shown (internal evidence: 2022)
  - **2017 - Desert X.** Jennifer Bolande's "Visible Distance/Second Sight" put art billboards on Gene Autry Trail. This was art, not artist promotion, but it shows the valley's billboards being used as a medium by then. ## 4. Images with credit - US law, plain English (not legal advice)

### Fair use: what it does and does not cover
- Fair use has four factors: the purpose of the use, the nature of the work, the amount used, and the effect on the market. It is decided case by case, with no safe percentage. The Copyright Office does not say that crediting the photographer makes a use fair. **Credit is courtesy, not a defense.** [read] https://www.copyright.gov/fair-use/ - US Copyright Office - current
- *Andy Warhol Foundation v. Goldsmith* (Supreme Court, May 18, 2023) made "transformative" harder to claim when the use serves the same purpose as the original photo, especially a commercial one. https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/us/598/21-869/ [search only] ; summary https://www.copyright.gov/fair-use/summaries/Andy-Warhol-Found-for-the-Visual-Arts-Inc-v-Goldsmith-143-S-Ct-1258-2023.pdf [search only]
- *Otto v. Hearst* (SDNY, Dec 10, 2018): a news site using a guest's iPhone photo to illustrate a story was **not** fair use. That is close to an archive reposting a fan's billboard snapshot. https://www.copyright.gov/fair-use/summaries/otto-hearst-communications-inc.no.1-17-cv-4712-ghw-s.d.n.y.dec.10.2018.pdf [search only]
- **Practical reading (inference):** Rehosting a fan's full photo to show "what the billboard looked like" uses the whole photo for the same purpose the photo serves. That is weak fair-use ground. Real commentary or criticism of the billboard makes the case stronger but does not make it safe.

### Linking vs embedding vs rehosting
- **Linking** (a plain hyperlink to the post) carries the least risk. No case found treats a plain link as infringement.
- **Embedding** (Instagram or X embed code; the image stays on Meta's or X's servers) is split by region:
  - **Ninth Circuit (California, where Coachella is): "server test" protects embedders.** If you don't store the file, you don't "display" it. *Perfect 10 v. Amazon* (2007), reaffirmed in *Hunley v. Instagram*, decided July 17, 2023. [read] https://dwt.com/insights/2023/08/server-test-instagram-case-ninth-circuit - Davis Wright Tremaine - Aug 3, 2023 ; opinion https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca9/22-15293/22-15293-2023-07-17.html [search only]
  - **New York federal courts (SDNY) reject the server test:**
    - *Goldman v. Breitbart* (2018): embedded tweet
    - *Nicklen v. Sinclair* (July 30, 2021): Judge Rakoff called the server rule "contrary to the text and legislative history" of the Act. [read] https://natlawreview.com/article/sdny-again-rejects-server-test-display-embedded-content - Finnegan/NLR - Aug 10, 2021
    - *McGucken v. Newsweek* (Mar 21, 2022): server test rejected; fair use and Instagram sublicense both sent to a jury. [read] https://www.arnoldporter.com/en/perspectives/advisories/2022/03/another-blow-to-ninth-circuits-server-test - Arnold & Porter - Mar 31, 2022
  - ***Sinclair v. Ziff Davis* (Mashable):** dismissed in April 2020 on the theory that Instagram's terms sublicensed the embed. After Instagram said publicly (June 2020) that it grants **no** sublicense for embeds, the judge reconsidered and reinstated the claim. [read] https://copyrightlately.com/legal-embed-instagram-photos-website/ - Copyright Lately (Aaron Moss) - Sep 10, 2020, updated 2021
  - **Still active in 2025-2026:**
    - SDNY *Lynk Media* cases (Sept to Dec 2025): embedding infringed; fair use failed; Twitter/X terms do not sublicense embedders.
    - W.D. Tex. *Urbanimage v. iHeart* (Jul 2025): server test rejected.
    - S.D. Miss. *Emmerich v. Particle* (Jul 2025): server test applied.
    - [read] https://blog.ericgoldman.org/archives/2025/12/copyright-lawsuits-over-embedding-are-still-a-thing.htm - Eric Goldman - Dec 9, 2025
  - **Fifth Circuit:** *Emmerich* argued June 2, 2026, decision pending. [read] https://masslawblog.com/copyright/is-embedding-someone-elses-image-copyright-infringement-the-fifth-circuit-may-finally-tell-us/ - Mass Law Blog (Lee Gesmer) - Jun 9, 2026
  - **Second Circuit, *Richardson v. Townsquare* (Apr 23, 2026):**
    - YouTube embed: licensed by YouTube's terms, which give every user a direct license to use features like embeds.
    - X-post embed: the fair-use dismissal was vacated, because republishing the whole video could be a market substitute.
    - Platform terms decided the outcome; the server test was not adopted.
    - [read] https://www.proskauer.com/blog/second-circuit-sidesteps-server-test-in-embedded-video-copyright-ruling-highlighting-how-platform-terms-matter - Proskauer - May 5, 2026
  - **Instagram embed opt-out:** since Dec 2021, users can switch off "Allow people to embed your posts." If an embed works, the poster has not opted out. That is a good signal, not a license. [read] https://petapixel.com/2021/12/17/instagram-users-can-now-prevent-others-from-embedding-their-photos - PetaPixel - Dec 17, 2021
- **Rehosting** (downloading and serving the image yourself) carries the highest risk. Every theory applies, including in the Ninth Circuit, and it is what *Otto* and most photographer suits are about.

### The billboard artwork is a second copyright
- A photo of a billboard reproduces the billboard's own creative (art, photography, design), which the artist or label owns. The US has no general "freedom of panorama" for 2D art. Section 120(a) lets you photograph *architecture* only.
- *Falkner v. General Motors* (C.D. Cal., Sept 17, 2018): a mural on a parking garage was not part of the architecture, so the 120(a) exception did not cover a photo of it. [read] https://www.loeb.com/en/insights/publications/2018/09/falkner-v-general-motors-llc - Loeb & Loeb - Sep 17, 2018
- **Inference (practical):** Labels and artists rarely object, because the billboard exists to be shared. Still, this is a reason to prefer the artist's or label's own posts, since they own the artwork *and* the photo.

### DMCA safe harbor for a small site
- Register a designated agent with the Copyright Office and post the contact on the site. [read] https://www.copyright.gov/dmca-directory/ - US Copyright Office. It costs $6 and must be renewed every 3 years, or it lapses. [read] https://www.jw.com/news/last-call-your-q4-dmca-agent-designation-reminder - Jackson Walker - Oct 2, 2017
- **Key limit:** Section 512(c) covers material stored "at the direction of a user." It does **not** cover images the operator uploads itself. Curating or screening user submissions can also lose protection. *Mavrix v. LiveJournal* (9th Cir., Apr 7, 2017): moderators who selected posts raised an agency question. [read] https://www.dwt.com/blogs/media-law-monitor/2017/09/9th-circuit-holds-website-can-lose-dmca-safe-har - Davis Wright Tremaine - Sep 2017
  - Inference: a curated archive where Stem or Richard picks and posts every image probably gets little from 512(c) for those images. A takedown address is still worth having for goodwill and fast removal.
- **Small-claims exposure:** The Copyright Claims Board can award up to $30,000 total ($15,000 per timely registered work). A respondent can opt out. [read] https://www.copyright.gov/about/small-claims/quick-facts.pdf - US Copyright Office

### Recommended approach (lowest risk that still works)
1. **Default to embeds of official posts.** Use artist, label, agency or operator Instagram/X posts (Outfront and DASH TWO post their own). The rights holder posted it, wants it seen, and owns both the artwork and usually the photo. Embedding has the strongest footing in the Ninth Circuit; elsewhere, an official post plus an embed is the most defensible position.
2. **Fan photos: ask first, embed second, never rehost without written OK.** DM the photographer, get a one-line "yes, with credit @handle." Log the date and wording. Embed if they prefer.
3. **Own photos going forward.** Shoot April 2027 from the I-10/Banning-Cabazon stretch (passenger seat, like Paste's Matt Mitchell). These are the only images you can rehost freely. Watch the artwork copyright point above; in practice this is low risk for promo boards.
4. **When there's no image you can use, show a text card with a link:** artist, year, location, copy text, source link. The facts about a billboard (who, what it said, where, when) are not copyrightable.
5. **Hygiene:**
   - Credit line on every image
   - Visible takedown/contact address plus a registered DMCA agent ($6)
   - Remove within 24-48 hours on request
   - Per-item rights field in the data: `official-embed` / `permissioned` / `own` / `link-only`. This also meets the AX rule, since agents can read it.
   - Avoid ads next to third-party images; commercial use weighs against fair use.
