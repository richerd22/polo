Chapter 02

02Every year

The short versionEvery edition on one page, from Pearl Jam's polo-field show in 1993 to the 2026 sellout. A $50 day ticket became a $649 weekend, two days became six, and the gross climbed to $114.6M in 2017 before the published numbers stopped. Three years are missing entirely, and most recent years have no official attendance at all.

The first Coachella charged $50 a day and lost somewhere between $850,000 and a million dollars.12 Eighteen years later the same polo field grossed $114.6M, the first recurring festival to clear $100M.34 The table below is everything in between, one row per year.

The shape of the curve

It did not go up in a straight line. It went up in steps, and each step was a format change.

Days. Two days in 1999. Nothing in 2000. One day in April 2001, headlined by Jane's Addiction.15 Two days again until 2007, when Coachella went to three days for the first time.6 Then, from 2012, the same three days twice: two weekends, identical lineups, sold separately.7

Prices. A two-day pass cost $142 in 2004.8 The first three-day pass, in 2007, was $249.6 It sat at $269 through the end of the one-weekend era.9 The two-weekend years start at $285 plus fees in 2012, then $349 in 2013, $429 in 2018 and $499 in 2024.10111213 For 2026, general admission started at $649 for the first weekend.14

Grosses. The published numbers run $67.2M in 2013, $78.3M in 2014, $84.3M in 2015, $94.2M in 2016 and $114.6M in 2017.1516173 That is the whole run of figures this project could tie to Billboard or Pollstar. Earlier years in the table come from Wikipedia's summary of Billboard Boxscore and are labeled that way. After 2017, nothing. The money chapter covers why that matters.

The bad years. 2000 was skipped after the first one lost money.1 2010 sold three-day passes only and still got overrun at the gates; LA Weekly reported gate-crashing, counterfeit tickets and three-hour entry lines.18 An Icelandic volcano's ash cloud stranded seven acts in England the same weekend.19 The fixes for 2011 were a wrought-iron fence, an extra road lane and the end of the ticket-for-wristband swap.9 Weeks after that festival, on May 31, 2011, Goldenvoice announced the second weekend.7

The gap. Coachella 2020 was moved to October, then cancelled by Riverside County's public health officer in June.2021 The same officer cancelled the April 2021 dates in January 2021.22 The festival came back in April 2022, so the table has two empty rows in a row for the first time.

The slump and the snap-back. In 2024, a first weekend that used to sell out in hours took nearly a month.13 For 2025, second-weekend passes were resold below face value.23 Then 2026 sold out both weekends three days after passes went on sale.24

How to read the table

  • Attendance and gross are as reported. Each cell says who reported it. Where two sources disagree, both are in the row.
  • Some years have no number. No attendance figure was found for 2018 through 2023, and no gross for 2018 onward. An empty cell means nobody published one, not that it was zero.
  • 125,000 is a ceiling, not a count. That is the daily cap Indio has allowed since a 2016 vote.25 It is not how many people came.
  • City counts are their own thing. For 2024, Indio counted 81,690 tickets for the first weekend and 80,148 for the second.26 Those are far below the cap, and may cover only the tickets the city collects a fee on.
  • Admissions are not people. 2014's 579,000 is not a typo. It is 96,500 a day across six days, the way Billboard counted it.15 The number of different people who came was closer to a third of that.
  • Prices are the cheapest general-admission pass at the time it went on sale, before fees. Prices were tiered from about 2022, so the same weekend often sold at more than one price.
  • 1993 is not a Coachella. It is the Pearl Jam show that put Goldenvoice on the polo field. It is in the table because the festival starts there.1

Every row links its sources. If you have a better one, especially a Boxscore printout or a city report, the corrections address is at the bottom of every page.

Whose polo club

Empire Polo Club was founded in 1987 by Alexander "Al" Haagen III, a player who had taken up the sport four years earlier and who would be patron of the Empire team for 30 years.29 The money behind it came from shopping centers: Haagen was vice chairman of his family's developer, Alexander Haagen Properties, and as a college-age construction supervisor had helped build its first center, in Rosemead, in 1963.30 One source credits plain "Alexander Haagen" with the founding, which could mean the father.31 The Polo Museum names the son, and so does Paul Tollett, who calls him "Al Haagen."27

Goldenvoice first came to the club in 1993, when Tollett and Rick Van Santen staged a Pearl Jam show on the polo grounds.31 It was Goldenvoice's first show at Empire, booked while the band was at the height of its fight with Ticketmaster.1 Coachella followed in 1999, and Stagecoach in 2007.27 By 2021, Tollett could say Goldenvoice had "a unique relationship with Empire Polo Club for over twenty-five years."27

The club's own address is 81-800 Avenue 51, Indio.32 Everything else in this chapter happens around it.

Buying the neighbors

In March 2012, Goldenvoice announced it had bought about 280 acres around the festival site.33 The package included Eldorado Polo Club, Triangle Bar Farms, Fish Creek and the property of Ambassador Glen Holden.34 The price was not disclosed.33

The city's terms

The tax standoff. In July 2012 the city considered an admissions tax of 5-10% on events over 2,500 people. That would have added about $36 a ticket. Tollett said 2013 could be the last year in Indio, and that the festivals would skip 2014 and find a new home for 2015.41

You pay a lot of money every year to hang out at a polo club. In 2026, a general admission pass for the first weekend cost $649.71 And the polo club stopped playing polo.

On October 27, 2021, Goldenvoice announced it was taking over Empire Polo Club year-round.27 Some of the club's staff had been told the day before that they were being laid off, with three days' notice.49 "These people gave over 30 years of their employment - of their life," one of them told KESQ.49 By November it was official: polo would stop at Empire.72

Palm Springs Life now puts it flatly: polo is no longer played at Empire Polo Club.31 The name stayed. The horses moved next door.

This chapter explains the game you are standing on, the club that played it, and what the grounds do for the rest of the year.

How polo works

Polo is hockey on horseback with a safety system built around a single imaginary line. Here is enough to follow a match, or to play the game at the end of this chapter.

The field

An outdoor polo field is 300 by 160 yards, about nine American football fields.73 The goals sit at each end, 8 yards apart, with no crossbar, so a ball that goes between the posts at any height counts.7475 Low sideboards, no taller than 11 inches, are optional and keep the ball in play along the sides.74

A goal counts no matter who puts the ball through, including a pony.74 After every goal the teams switch ends, which evens out the wind and the turf.7374

Four players, four jobs

Outdoor polo is four a side.74 The positions are numbered on the jerseys:7675

  • No. 1 is the most forward attacker and the main scorer. It is often the team's least experienced player, whose job includes marking the other side's No. 4.
  • No. 2 is the second attacker, the one who fights hardest for the ball.
  • No. 3 is the pivot, usually the best player on the team and often the captain. It turns defense into attack and feeds Nos. 1 and 2.
  • No. 4, the back, defends the goal and sends the ball forward.

Chukkers

A match is divided into periods called chukkers. Under the US Polo Association's (USPA) outdoor rules, a horn sounds after seven minutes of play, and up to 30 seconds of overtime follow. The chukker ends at the next horn, wherever the ball is.77 US matches usually run six chukkers outdoors, with four to six depending on the tournament.73 Argentina plays eight in its biggest opens; four is common in England and Europe.75

A tie is not a tie for long. Play continues after the last chukker "until one side obtains a goal."77

Handicaps

Every player has a rating in "goals," from -2 up to 10, with 10 the best. It is not a count of goals scored. It measures horsemanship, hitting, game sense, the quality of the player's ponies and sportsmanship, and the USPA reviews it twice a year.73

A team's handicap is the sum of its four players' ratings. Tournaments are capped by that total, so a "12-goal" tournament means no team can add up to more than 12.74

In a handicap match, the weaker team starts with goals already on the board. The formula: take the difference in team ratings, divide by six, and multiply by the number of chukkers. In a six-chukker game, a 4-goal team playing a 6-goal team starts 2-0 up.7374 It is one of the few sports that tries to make an unequal game fair before it starts.

The line of the ball

This is the rule that makes polo polo. At any moment, the ball's path through the air or across the grass extends into an imaginary line, forwards and backwards. That is the line of the ball.76

The right of way belongs to the player following that line most closely, at the smallest angle to it, with the ball on their right.7876 Nobody may cross in front of that player unless they are far enough ahead that there is no chance of a collision.78 Crossing is the foul you will see called most; the USPA says right-of-way and ride-off violations are the most frequent calls.76

You can't cut across the line, but you can push your way onto it:

  • The ride-off. Two riders go side by side and shove with horse and body to move each other off the line. It is legal only at a shallow angle; the Polo Museum puts the limit at 45 degrees.74 Two teammates may not ride off one opponent at the same time.78
  • The hook. A player can block an opponent's swing with their own mallet, but only from the same side as the swing or directly behind it, only while the opponent is hitting, and never above shoulder height.7876

Right hand only

Every player must hold the mallet in the right hand. The USPA exempts only left-handers registered before January 1, 1974.7473 The reason is safety. Riders often meet head-on at a gallop, and if everyone swings on the same side, the swings and the paths stay predictable.79 The right-of-way rules assume that swing, which is why the ball should be on your right.78

Left-handed play has been banned before. According to the Dawson & Bell Polo Club, it was barred in the mid-1930s, allowed again after the Second World War when players were scarce, and banned again by the USPA in 1974.79

The mallet is a bamboo cane, 48-54 inches long, with a cigar-shaped head. Players hit with the side of the head, not the end.74 The outdoor ball is hard plastic, about 3.5 inches across and 4.5 ounces, and it travels roughly three times faster than the rider who hit it.74

The ponies

They are called ponies by tradition. Most stand well over pony height and most are Thoroughbreds or crosses.74 The pony is said to account for "50% to 80%" of a player's ability.74

A pony covers one to two miles in a chukker, so players change horses between chukkers. Every player needs at least two horses for a match, and high-goal players bring a whole string and often use a fresh horse every chukker.74

Penalties

Two mounted umpires call fouls. If they disagree, a referee at midfield decides.74 Penalties are numbered, and the worse the foul, the closer to goal the free hit:7678

  • Penalty 1: a goal, awarded outright, for a dangerous or deliberate foul that stops a likely score.
  • Penalty 2: a free hit from 30 yards, or from the spot of the foul.
  • Penalty 3: a free hit from 40 yards, in front of goal.
  • Penalty 4: a free hit from 60 yards, in front of goal.
  • Penalty 5: a free hit from the spot of the foul or from midfield.
  • Penalty 6, the "safety": a 60-yard hit for the attackers when a defender knocks the ball over their own back line.

Penalties 7 to 10 cover the procedural end: re-taking botched hits, sending off a pony or a player, and ejection.7680

Divot stomping

At halftime, the spectators walk onto the field and tread the clods of turf the hooves have kicked up back into place. It is a social tradition, sometimes with champagne. The USPA advises wedges, not heels.73

It is also, if you squint, the only part of polo that resembles Coachella: a few thousand well-dressed people walking around on a polo field, looking at the grass.

Empire's polo years

Al Haagen III founded Empire Polo Club in 1987, four years after he took up the sport.29 The Polo Museum says his name is "nearly synonymous with polo in California."29 The club hosted the first 40-goal event in the US and the first US Women's Open, ran a polo school, fielded at least 15 teams each winter, and drew what the museum calls "record-setting attendance" on Sundays.29 Around 2015, Palm Springs Life put the Sunday crowds at more than 4,000, mostly free of charge, over a 13-week season.81

It also had something no other US club had: the only fully lit grass polo field in the country, used for Friday "Polo Under the Lights."40 Palm Springs Life went further and called it one of only two in the world.81

The grass was the whole point. In 2015 the club watered its fields 11 months a year with groundwater and Colorado River canal water, through computer-controlled sprinklers, and Goldenvoice managed April.66 "Polo ponies run on grass. They don't run on dirt," Haagen told The Desert Sun.66

The lore · Hollywood on horseback

The legend goes that the valley's polo goes back to the movie stars. Palm Springs Life tells it this way: Hollywood had taken up polo by the 1920s, Walt Disney led the Palm Springs horsemen, and Spencer Tracy and Clark Gable "likely" rode the early makeshift fields.81 That "likely" is the magazine's own. The documented history of the clubs starts later, with Eldorado in 1957.

Empire's biggest polo moment never happened. It was chosen to host the XII FIP World Polo Championship in October 2021.82 Covid postponed it, and when it was played, from October 26 to November 6, 2022, it was in Florida, with the final at Wellington.83 By then there was no polo at Empire to go back to.

Eldorado, next door

Desert polo did not end in 2021. It moved over the fence.

Eldorado Polo Club is the older of the two. It was founded in 1957 as a members' club at the original Eldorado Country Club in Indian Wells, and rising land values pushed it to Indio by 1979. It was the valley's top polo facility until Empire opened next door.81 In the 1990s it hosted 22-goal polo and the US Open.84

In 2012 Goldenvoice bought it, along with the farms around it.34 The sale "included a leaseback that let Eldorado keep hosting polo."85 The club now plays on 13 fields, plus two at Empire's Calhoun Ranch and one at a private ranch. Goldenvoice owns the land, and Eldorado leases it until April, when Goldenvoice hosts Coachella and Stagecoach.84

That makes the polo players the festival's tenants, on land that used to be theirs, in a season that ends where the festival's build begins.

The season itself runs roughly mid-December to late March. Eldorado's 2018 tournaments ran from January 6 to April 1.85 In 2024-25 it had practices from mid-December, women's tournaments between Christmas and New Year, and four 12-goal tournaments in February and March.84

The overlap is real. The festival has been spreading onto Eldorado land for years: The Desert Sun described "the adjacent Eldorado Polo Club, also used by the festival" in 2015,66 and the Sahara moved onto it in 2018.60 For 2024, the footings for the new Sahara went in starting in December, while polo season was getting under way next door.60

  • Medjhool Lake. Ten acres with a lake and three tents.32 It is named for the dates grown on the property, and its walkways and footbridges are modeled on Monet's garden at Giverny.31

Palm Springs Life's account of 1999 lists a "main stage" and a "second stage."100 Wikipedia says the Outdoor Theatre has been in use since the start, next to the main stage, and its lineup tables use the name from early on without saying when it was first used.36 So the second stage of 1999 is probably the Outdoor Theatre under a plainer name, but nothing found here says so outright.

The main stage and the Outdoor Theatre

The main stage, now the Coachella Stage, is the one on the posters and the livestream thumbnails. In 2013 it was built by Brown United on six legs, and the stagehands called it "Optimus Brown." It carried about 165,000 pounds of gear on a structure rated for 500,000.104 Rat Sound has handled the audio almost from the start - 18 of the first 19 years, as of 2018 - and that year the main stage field alone had 168 L-Acoustics K2 cabinets.105

The Outdoor Theatre sits next to it, and it is the stage people mean when they say "sunset set."106 In 2013 it had a 65 x 45 ft roof and sliding trusses so one act's lights could roll off while the next act's rolled on.104 It also has the strangest second life of any stage on the grounds. When the country festival Stagecoach takes over the polo fields after Coachella, its main stage goes up on the Outdoor Theatre site, according to a 2014 report.68

The Sahara keeps moving

The Sahara is the tent that changed the festival, and it has spent most of its life being moved around the grounds.

In 2002 Coachella put more money into its production and bookings, with Hawtin, Moby, Paul Oakenfold, Tiësto and Pete Tong.107 In 2006 it held the show the next chapter is about, plus a surprise Madonna set the following day; fans now date the American dance boom to that weekend.59 In 2011 Odd Future played one of the tent's first live, non-DJ sets.59

By 2013 the Sahara was 80 ft tall.60 PLSN described it as a hangar holding about 20,000 people, built and lit by Andrew Gumper's AG Lighting and Sound.104 Then the festival bought more land, and the tent started to travel:

  • Until 2018: near the Rose Garden at Empire Polo Club, its home for years.60
  • 2018: onto the newly bought Eldorado Polo Club land next door, by the entrance, built 25% bigger.60 The LA Times gives the result as nearly 100 ft tall with a 250-ft stage, rated for 90 mph winds, and anchored to permanent concrete footings about two feet under the grass.59
  • 2024: south again, to land between Avenue 51 and Polo Road that Goldenvoice's Jason Brown called a "whole new area of the venue that we've never been into before."60

The 2024 version is not a tent in any camping sense. The old frame was aluminum; the new one is steel, which Brown said allows "more weight capacity and production elements." Work started in December. Crews poured about 156 truckloads of concrete, roughly 1,500 tons, into footings underground, and worked overnight so the trucks would not clog Indio's streets.60

How big is it? The Desert Sun gives 320 ft long.60 Elation, the lighting maker that supplied the rig, gives 400 x 410 ft, nearly 100 ft tall, on 16 steel ribs and 40 ft wider than the old one.108 The two may be measuring different things. Either way, it hung 956 Elation fixtures, designed by Todd Roberts of Production Design Works, and ran four headliner-level acts a day with changeovers as short as 10 to 15 minutes.108

The move came with a new grass hill and a "Sahara Express Walkway," and some fans said the walk from the main stage took about 15 minutes.109 The tent's old ground did not stay empty. That is where Quasar went.110

Mojave and Gobi

The two other 1999 tents are the quieter siblings. The Mojave is the second-largest tent and books indie rock and rising acts; the Gobi is the smallest of the classic tents, shaded and rarely packed.106 In 2013 the Mojave hung motion-controlled "noodles" - long tubes that moved and doubled as a 3D projection surface - from a 60 x 40 ft truss grid, while the Gobi used a 60 x 40 ft Versa Stage with a roof.104

According to Wikipedia, both moved behind an access road in 2017, and in 2018 the Mojave was enlarged and moved into the Sahara's old spot near the Rose Garden.36 From 2015 they also started booking more electronic acts, as dance music spread out of the Sahara.107

Every spring the tents also change jobs. When Stagecoach takes over the grounds, the Mojave becomes the Palomino, Yuma becomes Diplo's Honkytonk, Sonora becomes the air-conditioned Bud Light Backyard, and the Gobi becomes the Yellowstone Dutton Ranch.61

Yuma: a nightclub in a tent

Yuma opened in 2013 as the sixth stage, in the terrace food area near the Heineken Dome.104 Dancing Astronaut called it "the mysterious new Yuma Tent" and found an indoor nightclub inside: wood floors, disco balls, fake Baroque portraits, couches and air conditioning. Four Tet opened it with a 90-minute improvised set, followed by DJ Harvey, Seth Troxler and Luciano.101

The idea, per Wikipedia, was a room that "dials down the noise and strobe lights" for underground acts.36 Its longtime lighting designer, Steve Lieberman, kept it that way: lights only, no video wall, dark and minimal.111 In a festival built around LED screens the size of buildings, the room's selling point is that you can barely see.

Sonora: the punk tent

Sonora arrived in 2017, not 2016 or 2018 as some lists say. LAist announced it with the headline "New stage, who dis?" and a rock-leaning bill with a "bilingual slant": Allah-Las, T.S.O.L., Guided By Voices and Downtown Boys.102 It is fully enclosed and club-sized, and its booker, Rene Contreras, put the brief simply.112

"We just wanted to have an intimate space for guitar bands." - Rene Contreras112

It has since booked rock, hardcore, early hip-hop and Latin acts, from Los Mirlos to The Linda Lindas, Viagra Boys, PUP and Los Bitchos.112

Do LaB: the stage that let itself in

Do LaB is the best story on the grounds, because nobody planned it.

It is run by three brothers, Dede, Josh and Jesse Flemming; Josh and Jesse are twins. Jesse books the music, Josh designs the structures and Dede runs production and deals with Goldenvoice.113 They started with renegade parties in the late 1990s, going back to a birthday party in the mountains in 1999.114115 A downtown LA warehouse they ran was shut down by police and fire officials, which Josh says is what made them go legal.116

The broken water feature

Sources argue about the first year. Variety and NPR say 2004; the brothers' own accounts to The FADER, Pollstar and Entertainment Weekly say 2005, when a friend who curated art for the festival invited them to build something.117116118115 The likeliest reading is that 2004 is when the company and its own festival began, and 2005 is when it first came to Coachella.

What they built in 2005 was a dome with a water feature that did not work very well.118

"Everybody was showering in it and drinking from it." - Josh Flemming118

The wind mangled everything they set up on the first day.118 The budget was tiny; in 107-degree heat they misted people and handed out orange slices.115 Then, around midnight, the main music shut off. The brothers kept playing, and the crowd walked over - roughly a thousand people, by EW's account.118114 Jesse says that was the first time they saw it "could be more than an installation."118

In 2006 Paul Tollett overbooked two DJs and parked them in the Do LaB dome, with extra sound and lights. Dede called it "the green light for us to go for it and actually play music."118

An inch, then a mile

From there it grew, one negotiation at a time. Dede describes 2008 as the year they went to 360-degree environments: "they give us an inch, we take a mile."118 In 2009 Tollett asked them to take their sign down, because Coachella had no branding on site at all; they talked him into keeping it, which made it one of the first branded things at the festival.118 By 2010 a performance stage was pushing it toward "more of a stage and less of an art installation."118 In 2011 Josh's Calatrava-inspired "fish fry" structure was, in his words, their lowest point; it "barely stood up."118

In 2014 it moved to a corner with a backstage, and the brothers started calling it "the official sixth stage at Coachella."118 The festival still does not put Do LaB on the set-time grid. It lists it on the poster, in small type, as an art installation, and the lineup comes out separately on Do LaB's own channels, usually in late March.117 Dede called the tiny poster credit a "badge of honor."114

Water stayed the theme. Misters, water cannons and performers with flower-covered water guns have been cooling the crowd since the start; as Dede put it, "we knew how intense it was out here."113116 By 2022 the structure was a 200 x 200 ft tent of colored fabric triangles holding about 10,000 people at night.116 In 2025 the stage ran on battery power, according to the festival.119

The surprise sets

Do LaB books itself. Jesse runs it apart from the festival-wide booking, and the aim is to "catch artists right before they're about to pop."113 Dede: "We're not trying to sell tickets."117 Closing slots are sometimes booked hours before they start.117

The surprises began, Jesse says, because "we left it open to see what would happen."120 The first big one was Bonobo in 2015, the first major artist from outside their circle of friends, and the reaction made them leave a couple of open slots every year after.118 Since then:

  • 2017: Kaskade was booked as the surprise and tweeted about it. Security said the area could not hold the crowd that would follow, so the slot went to Skrillex instead.118
  • 2018: FISHER and Chris Lake drew the biggest afternoon crowds Do LaB had seen, and both credit those sets with lifting their careers.118
  • 2019: Major Lazer, with Diplo.121
  • 2022: Diplo again, plus Rebecca Black and MEUTE.116
  • 2024: Billie Eilish, two years after headlining the main stage. Josh's estimate: "It must've been over 20,000 people," the biggest crowd the stage has had. Anderson .Paak played a surprise set as DJ Pee .Wee the same year.118

The run has had one bad night. On Friday, April 10, 2026, Do LaB closed after a light reportedly fell on a woman during a surprise John Summit set, and later sets were cancelled; at the time Mixmag reported it, details came from attendees and there was no official statement.122

Lightning in a Bottle

Do LaB is the only stage at Coachella that also runs its own festival. Lightning in a Bottle took its name from a friend's private July birthday party in the early 2000s, and Wikipedia's history dates the first event under that name to 2004 in the Angeles National Forest.123 Other outlets count from 2006, the first three-day public edition.114124 It moved around California for years, and per Wikipedia has been at Buena Vista Lake in Kern County since 2019.123

The two festivals run back to back. The brothers start building Lightning in a Bottle about five days after Coachella's second weekend.115 In 2024 Jesse told Pollstar the independent festival was up against Live Nation, AEG and their radius clauses - the contract terms that stop artists playing nearby festivals.115 It is still running: the 2026 edition was set for May 20-24 at Buena Vista Lake, with Empire of the Sun, Zeds Dead and Chase & Status.125

Quasar and the Red Bull Mirage

Quasar is the newest stage, and it exists because 60 to 90 minutes is not long enough for some DJs. Goldenvoice announced it in March 2024 as a stage for sets of three hours or more, on both weekends.126 Its designer, Heather Shaw of Vita Motus - who also helped design Do LaB and has been to every Coachella since 1999 - links the idea to 2023, when Fred again.., Skrillex and Four Tet replaced Frank Ocean and showed what a long, shared set could do.127

Shaw heard about the project at the end of January 2024, which gave her under four months to build it, the tightest timeline of her career.127 The result is two asymmetric LED walls showing a live Unreal Engine model of the Coachella landscape, rendered in real time.127 It sits on the Sahara's former ground.110 The 2024 bookings set the format: Honey Dijon with Green Velvet, a Jamie xx, Floating Points and Daphni b3b, and a sunset set from Eric Prydz b2b Anyma.126110

In 2025 Red Bull built a place to watch it from. KTLA described the Red Bull Mirage as a two-story, 20,000-square-foot installation near Quasar, with bars and shade, open to any GA wristband.128 By 2026 Flaunt was calling it three tiers, with DJ sets, a daytime happy hour and a Nobu omakase dinner on the Saturday; the top level was mostly kept for artists and guests.129 Whether it grew a floor between years, or the two outlets counted differently, is not clear.

Despacio: seven stacks and one disco ball

Despacio is a sound system, not a stage, and it is the opposite of everything else at the festival. James Murphy of LCD Soundsystem and David and Stephen Dewaele of Soulwax built it with engineer John Klett; the idea dates to around May 2010.130 It made its debut at the Manchester International Festival in July 2013: three vinyl-only nights, seven 11-ft speaker stacks around the room, and nearly 50,000 watts.131 It is vinyl only because, its makers say, the system "would definitely notice" anything else.130

It came to Coachella in 2016, in its own tent, with the stacks arranged around a circular lighting rig and a big disco ball. On April 22, the day after Prince died, they played "Purple Rain."132

It came back in 2023, open 3 to 9 p.m. both weekends, with 44 McIntosh amplifiers, about 950 people on the floor, a target of 105 dB, and the disco ball as the only visual.133

The Antarctic

The one room named for a cold place was a dome. In 2017 Goldenvoice put up a 120-ft geodesic projection dome, billed as the largest in the world, built by Obscura with Pacific Domes. It had 15 Christie projectors, 108 L-Acoustics speakers in three tiers and an eight-minute, 360-degree program called "Chrysalis," for 500 people at a time.134 In 2019 the air-conditioned dome came back as YouTube's 360 dance dome, and the Antarctic program returned with it.135

Heineken House

Heineken has been Coachella's official beer since 2002.5253 Before the House there were the Heineken Domes; PLSN used one as a landmark in 2013.104 In 2014 Heineken House replaced them, with sets from Skrillex, Outkast, Lorde and Girl Talk, Snapchat clues to secret performers, and beer lockers opened by fingerprint.52 In 2019 it moved, got a new structure and its first 21-plus beer garden, and booked De La Soul and The Roots.53 By 2025 it was air-conditioned, with Pusha T and Afrojack.136

Retired, temporary and never built

Some rooms did not last.

  • The Oasis Dome: an open-air dome "built into a makeshift tree," with 360-degree sound; Breakage played it in 2011.137 Pacific Domes describes a 44-ft dome with misters overhead.138
  • The Heineken Domes: replaced by Heineken House in 2014.52
  • Despacio and the Antarctic: pop-ups that come back when someone wants them to, so far in 2016 and 2023, and 2017 and 2019.132134135
  • The Kanye dome: for 2019, Kanye West reportedly wanted a huge dome in the middle of the grounds. Goldenvoice said no, partly because it would have meant moving stages and a toilet area tied to sewer and water lines, and Ariana Grande took the slot within 24 hours. That account comes from Billboard's reporting, via Digital Music News; neither side confirmed it.139

Chapter 07

07The moments

The short versionThe sets people still bring up, and what the paperwork says happened. A Prince cover that kept getting pulled offline, a dead rapper reflected off a sheet of foil, a homecoming with about 200 people on stage, an ice rink nobody skated on, and a $2.5M concrete bunker for a band that never showed up.

Several of Coachella's best-known moments didn't happen quite the way people remember. The Tupac on the main stage in 2012 was a reflection on a sheet of foil.140 Frank Ocean's ice rink was built, and the first people to stand on it were Skrillex, Fred again.. and Four Tet, a week after Ocean had left the bill.141 In 2026 the poster promised "The Bunker Debut of Radiohead," and Radiohead stayed home.86 The festival is very good at making moments. This chapter is about what was underneath them.

Daft Punk's 2006 pyramid has a chapter of its own. Everything else is here.

Prince plays "Creep" (2008)

Prince headlined the second night, April 26, 2008. Somewhere in the set he played an eight-minute cover of Radiohead's "Creep," and he brought out Sheila E. for "The Glamorous Life."142

Fan videos of "Creep" were online by the next day, and Prince's team had them taken down.142 Since the song was Radiohead's, Radiohead had a view on this. Thom Yorke's, as reported by Consequence:

"Really? He's blocked it? Well, tell him to unblock it. It's our … song."143

Seven years later Prince let it back up. In December 2015 a new upload appeared with Radiohead's blessing, and Prince tweeted a link to it.142 On December 16, 2015, it was taken down again, this time after a claim from NPG Music Publishing, which is Prince's own publishing company.143 So the Prince side won both rounds, the second one against itself.

The hologram that wasn't (2012)

On April 15, 2012, during Dr. Dre and Snoop Dogg's headline set, Tupac Shakur appeared on stage. Dre had come up with it and paid for it. "It was Dre's idea to bring Tupac back," said Nick Smith of AV Concepts, the San Diego company that projected it.144

Everybody called it a hologram. It wasn't one. It was Pepper's ghost, a 19th-century theater illusion: you project an image onto an angled transparent surface, and from the audience it looks like a person standing in the room.140 Victorian theaters did it with glass. AV Concepts used Mylar foil.144 Hypebeast named the UK firm Musion and its "Eyeliner" system as the technology underneath.140

The digital Tupac came from Digital Domain, James Cameron's visual-effects company. VFX supervisors Janelle Croshaw and Steve Preeg led a team of about 20 on it.144 Smith told MTV it took about four months.140

The lore · The $10 million Tupac

The legend goes that the Tupac cost $10 million. That figure went around in April 2012 and never came with a name attached.140 The only on-the-record number is Smith's, via MTV, and it's for a different thing: he wouldn't give the real cost, but said a comparable piece could run from $100,000 to more than $400,000.140 Nobody has published an invoice. The $10M lives on because it sounds like what a dead man on a stage ought to cost.

Then came the strange part. On September 11, 2012, five months after Coachella, Digital Domain Media Group filed for Chapter 11. It shut its operation in Port St. Lucie, Florida, let most of the 320 staff there go, and its CEO resigned.145 No source ties the bankruptcy to Tupac. It's just the order things happened in.

As for the Tupac, the digital asset "has been archived," Andscape reported in 2017, and only the estate has access.144 Dre has said it was "strictly for Coachella."144 It didn't stay strictly there for long, though. The next chapter follows it.

Beychella (2018)

Beyoncé was booked to headline in 2017. She withdrew on her doctors' advice while she was pregnant, and came back in 2018 as the first Black woman ever to headline Coachella.146 She played both Saturdays. The first was April 14.147 (GRAMMY.com gives "April 13 and 22," but those were a Friday and a Sunday.)

The show was built as an HBCU homecoming, with about 200 people on stage.146 Rehearsals ran four months for the band, then four more for the dancers.146 Fans named it Beychella.148

On YouTube, her weekend-one set peaked at about 458,000 people watching at once. That was the highest since the festival started streaming, according to TMZ's figures as reported by The FADER.147 A year later, on April 17, 2019, Netflix released Homecoming, a film of the set with a live album alongside.146 It won the Grammy for Best Music Film.149

It also raised the bar for every headliner after her. Ariana Grande's production designer told The Hollywood Reporter that after Beyoncé, the festival had turned into a "competition."150 TMZ's numbers, as The FADER reported them, had her peak about 75% above Lady Gaga's the year before, with 43.1 million live-stream views across 232 countries for the whole festival.147

Kanye, the dome and the hill (2019)

He had form. Kanye West's 2011 set came with a stone mural in the style of the Pergamon Altar, a troupe of ballerinas and a lift that was reportedly used on Michael Jackson's last tour. Complex, looking back ten years later, called it unprecedented.151

He was due to headline again in 2019. In January he walked away. He called the standard 40-by-60-foot stage "artistically limiting" and had asked, late in the day, for a custom dome. The festival didn't build it.152

He came back anyway, without a headline slot. On Easter Sunday, April 21, 2019, Sunday Service, which had been an invite-only gathering since January, went up on a hill inside the campground at 9 a.m.153154 The "Church Clothes" merch went with it: sweatshirts from $165 to $225 and "Jesus Walks" socks at $50.155 The hill, the crowd, and the arguments over the size of the crowd all belong to the camping chapter.

Frank Ocean's rink (2023)

That year's headliners were Bad Bunny, BLACKPINK and Frank Ocean. It was the first time every headliner was nonwhite.156 Ocean closed the first Sunday, and it went wrong in several directions at once.

He came on about an hour late, in slippers.157 There was meant to be a segment with skaters on an ice rink, and it didn't happen. A source told Rolling Stone he had hurt his ankle in on-site rehearsals the week before, and the show was reworked at the last minute.157 Rolling Stone also found an LMU hockey player's Instagram Story showing nine men in skating gear, captioned "The Boys x Frank." That crew never performed.157 On stage, Ocean said he was there for his brother Ryan Breaux, who died in 2020.157

The YouTube stream was pulled. "Frank Ocean is not scheduled to appear on the Coachella livestream," YouTube posted, and nobody gave a reason. Björk's set came off the stream schedule the same weekend.158 On April 19 he withdrew from weekend two. His team said he had been hurt on the festival grounds the week before, and "on doctor's advice" couldn't perform, "due to two fractures and a sprain in his left leg."159 Blink-182, who had played their first reunion show with Tom DeLonge that Friday, took his slot.159160

According to Billboard, as reported by The FADER, Ocean was contracted for $8M across both weekends. His weekend-one production reportedly cost more than his $4M fee, the rink was the most expensive part of it, and the festival stood to lose "several million dollars."161 Goldenvoice went looking for someone else to use the rink. On the second Sunday, Skrillex, Fred again.. and Four Tet stood on it to close the night.141

The lore · The melted rink

Two stories went around that week. TMZ's, as Stereogum summarized it: Ocean hurt himself in an accident involving bikes on the polo grounds, doctors advised changing the set, and the skating went. Stereogum adds that about 100 LA-based hockey players had rehearsed on a raised rink at Paramount Studios and were told on arrival that the routine was off.162 The second came from TheFestiveOwl, an anonymous X account. Its version was that the rink was "constructed and ready to go," that Ocean dropped it at the last minute, and that Coachella had to "melt the entire ice rink" and rebuild the stage.162 Stereogum called the account unverified.162 The documented version is the injury his team described, and a rink that was still there a week later.159141

The bunker (2026)

The 2026 poster ended with a line nobody could explain: "The Bunker Debut of Radiohead Kid A Mnesia." Fans started guessing whether that meant Radiohead were playing.163

The permit file had the better answer. In September 2025 Goldenvoice applied to the City of Indio for a 16,130-square-foot "commercial foundation." City assessors valued it at $2.5M. Reporters found a large sand pit with steel reinforcement near the Sahara tent, and the concrete and steel framing suggested something permanent. A Rolling Stone representative confirmed Radiohead would not perform.86

What got built was Radiohead's Motion Picture House: about 17,000 square feet with 38-foot ceilings, under the Empire Polo Fields, open to anyone with a festival wristband.164 Inside were the KID A MNESIA film and galleries of full-scale Thom Yorke and Stanley Donwood artwork from the Kid A and Amnesiac sessions.164 The sound was a "6-point surround" mix built from the original multitracks, and Sean Evans directed the film.89

It had a long run-up. KID A MNESIA first came out in 2021 as a free digital exhibition for PS5, PC and Mac.89 A physical 20th-anniversary installation planned that year was cancelled over logistics and COVID.163 The desert finally got it built, five years late and underground.

So Radiohead made the 2026 poster without playing a note. Ed O'Brien did tell reporters the band plans to play 20 shows a year from 2027.89 After the festival the Motion Picture House went on tour: Brooklyn from May 6, Mexico City from October 27 and San Francisco's Palace of Fine Arts from January 14, 2027.165 The film runs 75 minutes, and visits are booked in two-hour slots. Stereogum also listed a Chicago run at Cinespace in the summer, which the official site doesn't show.16589 The official FAQ says "the Coachella Bunker experience" was different in some ways from the touring version.165 It wasn't the first artist-built world on the grounds, either. Desert Sun points to Travis Scott's "The Village by Cactus Jack" in 2025.86

Weekend two gets the guests (2026)

For most of the two-weekend era, weekend two was the sequel. Surprise guests were part of the house style, and the famous ones above (Madonna in 2015, \*NSYNC in 2019, Shania Twain in 2022) all came on the first weekend. In 2026 almost all of the big ones landed a week later. Madonna joined Sabrina Carpenter for three songs, timed to her new single. SZA and Sexyy Red came out for Justin Bieber, and Bieber serenaded Billie Eilish. Olivia Rodrigo joined Addison Rae, and Snoop Dogg joined Giveon.178

Variety's Jem Aswad asked the industry why. One agent said weekend one "is driven heavily by influencer culture." Another insider put it as: "Weekend two is always better... more to see and less to be seen."178 Others said guests on weekend two protect a headliner's first-weekend spotlight. Sources said it was not a plan coordinated by Goldenvoice or YouTube.178 The festival has two identical weekends on paper. In practice they've started to split: weekend one for being seen, weekend two for whoever turns up.

Chapter 09

09Getting there

The short versionAbout 40,000 people a day ride a shuttle into Indio, and the company that runs it isn't Goldenvoice - it's a travel outfit owned, three layers up, by private equity. The drivers come from all over, the train ran exactly once, and the rich fly into a WWII airfield in Thermal. Everyone else sits on Jefferson Street, then leaves on Monday at 9 a.m.

The bus you ride to Coachella belongs to a company named after a man named Dan. Not literally - the drivers are subcontracted - but follow the shuttle pass up the chain and you reach CID Entertainment, which stands for "Consider It Dan," started by the tour manager of a Philadelphia jam band.179 Above that sits a sports hospitality firm, and above that, four private-equity investors.180 None of them is Goldenvoice.

For two weekends every April, Indio has to take in a crowd the size of a small city every afternoon, and send it back out after midnight. The 2019 traffic plan assumed 125,000 people a day.181 This chapter is about how they get there: the buses, the roads, the train that ran once, and the private jets.

The shuttle and the man named Dan

Valley Music Travel (VMT) runs the official shuttles. It sells the passes, staffs the stops and answers the phone.182 It calls itself the "Official Enhanced Experience Partner of Coachella," and sells black cars and in-venue carts on the side.183 Its own About page describes it as "a subsidiary of CID Entertainment."184

CID's founder, Dan Berkowitz, became tour manager for the Disco Biscuits in 2004. He noticed most bands didn't offer travel and VIP packages to fans, so he started a company that would. He worked out of an office in the band's studio, and in 2008 sold a small stake for $100,000 to grow it.179 Pollstar puts the founding at 2007.185 By 2015 CID had about 60 full-time staff in Philadelphia, and up to 300 people working for it over a big festival weekend. At Coachella, it would "build you a 14-by-14-foot safari tent with full amenities on site."179

In October 2018, On Location Experiences bought CID, according to Berkowitz.185 On Location now calls CID "a division of On Location," and lists its own owners as RedBird Capital Partners, Bruin Sports Capital, 32 Equity and the Carlyle Group.180 So the chain runs: your shuttle pass, VMT, CID, On Location, private equity. No source shows Goldenvoice or AEG holding a stake in any link of it, and no source rules it out either.

VMT has done the job since at least 2010. That spring Pollstar wrote that "Coachella partnered with Valley Music Travel" for hotel shuttles. 2010 was also the first year everyone got in-and-out privileges, which is what made a hotel shuttle worth having.186 Goldenvoice used VMT again for Desert Trip in 2016: a $25 pass, over 20 stops, and a shuttle coordinator at every pickup.187

The pass

The product is the "Any Line" pass: one pass, good from any stop, all weekend. Stops sit about 5 to 25 miles from the polo grounds, and not every hotel is one of them. Peak inbound is 2 to 5 p.m. Peak outbound is midnight to 2 a.m., when VMT says it runs "its full fleet."182

The price has gone up about two and a half times in eleven years.

The drivers come from everywhere

Here is what isn't public: how many coaches there are, how many drivers, which charter companies supply them, and where they sleep. Nothing published gives a fleet count. The closest thing to one came by accident.

On Sunday night, April 24, 2022, the shuttle drivers ate a catered dinner of pasta and pre-packaged salad at the Riverside County fairgrounds in Indio. Calls started shortly after midnight. Responders treated drivers at the fairgrounds, at "the shuttle bus lot," at hotels and at pickup points across the valley. Forty-six went to hospital: 23 to Eisenhower, 12 to Desert Regional and 11 to JFK Memorial. County health said "more than 100 drivers report being sick."196 The drivers were subcontracted. The caterer was never named, and officials said it had nothing to do with the festival.196

So the driver pool is well over 100 people. That's an inference from a food-poisoning count, which tells you how little is published about the fleet. What the outbreak does show is the shape of the thing: a festival-travel company running a subcontracted driver pool for two weekends, then a third for Stagecoach, which VMT also runs on the same hotel-and-park-and-ride model.192

The train that ran once

Indio used to be a railroad town. Southern Pacific reached it in 1876. Amtrak's Sunset Limited stopped there until October 1998, when, by Wikipedia's account of the station, it closed for "low ridership."197

Ten years later, Coachella got one train. On Thursday, April 24, 2008, a chartered Amtrak train left Los Angeles Union Station for a temporary platform in Indio, arriving at 5 p.m. It had seven cars, including a business-class coach, a café car and a Superliner lounge, and was led by F59PH locomotive No. 458.198 About 300 festivalgoers were aboard.198 Riders who had bought three-day passes rode free, and Goldenvoice handed everyone VIP passes for all three days.199

It was a party. DJs set up laptops on the stainless steel counters in the back cars, and Junkie XL played breakbeats to a packed car. Rosanna Arquette hosted, followed by a camera crew Goldenvoice had hired. The writer on board for LA Weekly said "the three hours flew by."199 At the platform, Indio's mayor said a transportation center would be built there, and an Amtrak vice president agreed that desert passenger service "could and should happen."198

It didn't. In 2016 the county transportation commission floated festival trains for 2017 and 2018, on a budget of about $1.2M, under the name "Choo-Choo-Chella." They never ran.200 In 2019 the state awarded the Riverside County Transportation Commission $5.9M in rail money, and the commission added $2.7M, for $8.6M in all. That would have paid for a 900-foot temporary platform at the Indio Transit Center, with festival trains from Union Station "as early as 2020."201 Then 2020 happened. Wikipedia, citing the Desert Sun, says the plan was cancelled that year, amid the pandemic, after arrangements with Union Pacific fell through.197 The platform was never built.

The long game is Coachella Valley Rail, a proposed daily service on about 144 miles from Los Angeles with nine stops. In January 2026 the commission awarded an $80M contract for its environmental review. The project will likely cost more than $1.5B, and service could start "by 2040."202 Nobody has promised DJs.

The fast way in

If you have the money, you skip Interstate 10 altogether and land in Thermal.

Jacqueline Cochran Regional Airport, in Thermal, was built in 1942 as a WWII Army airfield. It has an 8,500-foot runway, and Wikipedia's entry on it says its busy season is the horse show circuit, December to March - not April.203 In April, the pop-ups arrive.

  • 2016: Uber and Blade flew helicopters from Van Nuys for $4,170 one way, or $695 a seat with six aboard, with an Uber for the last leg and a tequila bar before takeoff. Uber's pitch: "There's no traffic 1,000 feet in the air."204
  • 2017: XOJET flights to three desert airports were up 40%, according to a Quartz report cited by an industry site.205
  • 2022: Blade said it was flying roughly 200 passengers into Thermal from Santa Monica, turning "a five-hour car ride into only 40 minutes in the air."206
  • 2026: JSX ran Santa Monica to Thermal on April 9, back on April 13, nonstop in 50 minutes, for $606 each way.207

No one publishes how many private jets land on festival weekends.

The commercial airport, Palm Springs International, feels it in fares rather than records. In 2019, round trips from San Francisco ran about $600 to $800 for weekend 1 and $500 to $600 for weekend 2, against about $320 in early April.208 The airport's busiest month ever, with nearly 500,000 passengers, was March 2025 - the month before the festival.209

Down on the ground, the top of the price ladder is a staging pass for your hired car. For 2026, the festival charged $3,500 for a vehicle with 10 seats or fewer, and $5,000 for anything bigger, up to a 35-foot bus - "for drivers to service pre-booked clients only."195 The sky above the grounds is closed: a federal flight restriction banned drones from April 9 to 13, 2026.191

The roads

Most people drive. Indio prepares by closing its own streets.

The long closure is Avenue 50 between Monroe and Madison, shut for about six weeks, through both Coachella weekends and Stagecoach. In 2024 it ran from March 25 to May 6. "The closure of Avenue 50 is essential to ensure the safety of workers, residents, and commuters alike," said Jim Curtis, the city's community services manager.210 In 2026 it closed March 23 and reopened after May 4.211

Then, Friday to Monday of each festival weekend, four more go:212

  • Avenue 49, between Hjorth and Monroe
  • Avenue 50, between Madison and Jackson
  • Hjorth Street, between Avenues 49 and 50
  • Madison Street, between Avenues 49 and 52

It's the same list Indio published in 2019.190 Inbound traffic peaks from about 3 to 8 p.m., Friday to Sunday, on Jefferson, Washington and Monroe. Outbound runs from about midnight to 3 a.m.193 Caltrans watches the climb in from the coast: I-10 from Beaumont and SR-60 from Moreno Valley, eastbound peaking Friday and westbound Sunday.181

The police side is a numbers game. Indio PD's traffic division runs festival enforcement. In a crackdown briefing in April 2025, it cited 112 arrests and 83 citations for misused disabled placards from a second weekend, and a 40% spike in arrests on the year before.213 Weekend 2 of 2026 ended with 106 arrests and 87 placard citations, and when festival lots filled, police sent the overflow to the Tennis Garden.214

The last mile

Wherever you start, you finish on foot. The festival sorts arrivals by lot. Rideshare goes to Lot 1D at Avenue 49 and Monroe, open 11 a.m. to 3 a.m., with the longest waits from midnight to 2:30. Friends and taxis drop at Lot 13, at Avenue 52 and Madison. Walkers and cyclists get four color-coded entries - Red, Green, Yellow and Blue - and no pedestrians are allowed on Monroe between Avenues 49 and 52. A bike locked anywhere but a rack "will be removed and forfeited." Day parking isn't guaranteed, and "Cars left after 2am will be towed."195

The cheerful exception is Carpoolchella, a program run with the nonprofit Global Inheritance. Cars with four or more people, decorated and spotted on the way in, can win backstage passes, VIP upgrades or "VIP tickets for life."195215 It's an incentive to fill the car, wrapped as a contest. In 2026, cars in a grocery store lot in Indio were already decorated for it at 1 a.m. on Thursday.216 Global Inheritance also organized the 2008 train.215

On the way in, you'll pass the billboards. They're the subject of their own chapter: the I-10 billboard archive.

The line

The worst wait in the festival's recent history wasn't for a stage. It was for the campground.

On Thursday, April 10, 2025, campers sat in their cars for 8, 10, 12 hours. One told KESQ she'd arrived at 3 a.m. and it was almost 11. Another said, "Seven hours into this." The first: "I've seen people pass out through heat exhaustion, it's been chaos."217 There were no restrooms, and some people relieved themselves on the roadside, in a heat wave.218

Five days later, Goldenvoice's head of public safety, George Cunningham, went to the La Quinta City Council. His explanation: the usual midday lull never came. "Everybody wanted to come early." Three things had stacked up. The festival's first-ever Thursday night campground concert pulled people in early. Unlike some past years, nobody was let in before the official 9 a.m. opening, so the queue backed onto public roads. And not every checkpoint was fully staffed.219 Then he apologized: "Again, [we] apologize for what took place to your residents, the impact that it had to the community."218

The fix was more booths. For weekend 2, Goldenvoice added 12 toll plazas near Madison and Avenue 50, for 44 in all.219 The camping chapter picks up the line from inside the car.

Monday

The festival ends on Sunday night. The exodus is Monday morning.

More than 40,000 campers leave after each weekend.191 It's been about that many for years: Indio expected "nearly 40,000" in 2019.190 Camper gates open around 8:30 to 9 a.m., and Washington, Jefferson and Monroe fill at once. Things usually ease by about 11:30. The city's advice for anyone heading west is to be on I-10 "before 9 a.m." After that, expect the freeway to be gridlocked for most of the day.193

Then the roads reopen, the buses go back to wherever they came from, and Indio does it again the next Thursday.

Chapter 11

11The lineup and the poster

The short versionOne man reads thousands of pitches a year, picks about 150 acts, and sets every name on the poster in one typeface at one of several sizes - a size agents fight over. The poster used to land in January. Now it lands in the fall, and the 2027 passes went on sale with no names on them at all. Behind it sits a radius clause that keeps those names off other stages from December to May, and a small Portland festival that sued over it.

Until Jan. 1, 2019, Goldenvoice was making a Coachella poster with Kanye West at the top of it. The lineup came out on Jan. 2 without him.220221 Paul Tollett told the Los Angeles Times that West's production ideas were "great," but "we just weren't able to pull them off right now."222 The poster is the festival's whole pitch on one sheet, and it gets argued over, reworked and, now and then, thrown out the night before.

One man's inbox

Coachella's lineup is not a chart printout. Tollett, who co-founded the festival and still runs it as owner-operator in partnership with AEG, told the LA Times in 2019 that agents send him "another 3,000 or 4,000" acts a year on top of what he chases himself, and that he is "looking for 140 or 150 artists that are fresh."220 That year's anniversary bill came to 160 acts.220

"Fresh" is the point. He wants something unique for what he calls a "100-day window" around the festival, and he books against acts that have just played nearby.220 Some of what comes in he waves off. When WME's Marc Geiger pitched Kate Bush, Tollett's answer, as The New Yorker told it in 2017, was "No one is going to understand it."223

The same profile carried the clearest numbers ever published on what the bill costs. Tollett said each of the 2017 headliners - Radiohead, Lady Gaga and Kendrick Lamar - was paid about $3-4M, and the lowest-billed acts a little under $10,000.224 That is a gap of at least 300 times between the top line and the bottom line of the same poster. Pay is only part of what an agent is fighting over.

Twenty percent of the job

An agent negotiating a Coachella slot is negotiating four things at once: the fee, the stage, the time slot and the billing - where the name sits on the poster and how big it is. Headliners can demand top billing more than a year ahead.225 Wasserman's Lee Anderson told The Ringer that billing is "probably 20 percent of what I do for a living."225

The reason is that the poster reads like a ranking. The format has barely changed since 2003: names in rows, with the type shrinking line by line to show where an act stands.226 Agents bargain over that hard, and some call it one of the most stressful parts of booking a festival.226

The size is Goldenvoice's guess at an act's draw, and the guess can be wrong. 2 Chainz was billed small in 2013 and drew more people than his stage could hold.227 Tollett himself told The New Yorker that streaming has made it harder to tell whether an act belongs on the second line or the third.223

And sometimes the act reads the poster too. In 2019 Burna Boy, on the third tier, went on Instagram Live: "I am an AFRICAN GIANT and will not be reduced to whatever that tiny writing means."228 The Ringer cites his complaint as a case of an artist going public about type size.225 What he did next is in the chapter on what Coachella started.

Eurostile, and the one exception

Every name on the modern poster is set in one typeface, Eurostile LT, a wide, squared-off sans that looks like the label on a 1970s stereo.229 Its sameness is the point. If everyone gets the same letters, the only thing left to compare is size.

Goldenvoice broke the rule once. On the poster for 2025, "The Original Misfits" appeared in the band's own horror-movie lettering, dripping and jagged among the clean Eurostile names.229 Tollett's reason was history, not hype: "The Misfits are one of the first bands we promoted."229 The font was a thank-you note to one of Goldenvoice's own first bookings.

The lore · The font-size Hunger Games

Fans read the poster as a pay chart. Stereogum's 2014 headline called the scramble the "font-size Hunger Games."227 The documented version is less tidy: the size is Goldenvoice's estimate of an act's draw, bargained over alongside fee, stage and slot, and it is not the fee itself.227225 Fans have a second ritual too, the fake posters that circulate every winter before the real one.230

When the poster drops

For most of the festival's life the lineup came out in January. Music Festival Wizard's tally puts every drop from 2005 to 2012 somewhere between Jan. 9 and Jan. 31; that list is a single undated page, and at least one of its weekdays is wrong, so treat those early dates as approximate.231 From 2013 the dates are firmer, and they kept creeping earlier into the new year until the drop settled on the first days of January.232

Then the calendar broke. The 2025 lineup came out Nov. 20, 2024, about two months ahead of the old schedule.233 The 2026 lineup came out Sept. 15, 2025, about four months early.44 And for 2027 Goldenvoice skipped the poster altogether, at least at first: the dates (Apr. 9-11 and 16-18) were confirmed in April 2026 and advance passes went on sale before any names were announced.234 That sale opened May 1, 2026, with weekend 1 GA from $599 and a $49 down payment.235 As of Oct. 30, 2026, there was still no official 2027 lineup. TMZ reported SZA, Dua Lipa and Tame Impala as headliners, and Consequence wrote that the announcement was "expected soon."236

Why the drop moved

Goldenvoice has not said publicly why it moved the date, so everything here is the industry reading the tea leaves. The obvious context is 2024. Tickets for that year went on sale Jan. 19 and were still available a week later, which SFGate called the "slowest ticket sales in ten years."240 Weekend 1 sold out about a month in.240 Weekend 2 never did.44 For a festival whose 2015 passes had reportedly gone in 40 minutes, that was a long month.240

The next on-sale did not fully fix it. Days after the Nov. 22, 2024 presale for 2025, weekend 1 GA was mostly gone, but weekend 2 GA was "widely available." Digital Music News noted that "for decades" Coachella had sold out the day tickets went on sale.241

Then the fall drop worked. The 2026 bill - Sabrina Carpenter, Justin Bieber and Karol G, with Anyma in a special slot - came out in mid-September, and both weekends sold out in about three days.24 By Pollstar's count it was the first time three pop acts had headlined, and Karol G was the first Latina headliner.44 Pollstar asked people in the business why. WME's Josh Kurfirst said "announcing early allows them to capture the hype around fall tours currently playing out."44 A venue owner, Stephen Chilton, offered a blunter guess: "they are trying to get out an onsale while things are as good as they are."44

The radius clause

The other reason the poster matters is what signing it costs an artist. Court filings in 2018 laid out the terms of Coachella's radius clause, the contract rule that limits where a booked act can play.242 According to Rolling Stone's reading of the filings, an act playing Coachella could not play any other North American festival from Dec. 15 to May 1. It could not play shows in Los Angeles, Orange, Riverside, San Bernardino, Santa Barbara, Ventura or San Diego counties in that window. It could not promote another U.S. festival until Coachella's lineup was out. And it could not "advertise, publicize or leak" a show in a way that interfered with Coachella's marketing.242

Tollett defends it as ordinary. He compared it to car dealerships, each with its own region, and pointed out that artists usually come back to the area later in the year.220 In Tollett's telling, that 100-day window is the clause's whole purpose: for that stretch, the acts on the poster are something you can only see in Indio.220

In April 2018, Soul'd Out Productions, which ran a festival in Portland, Ore., sued Goldenvoice and AEG in federal court there. It said the clause propped up "an illegal monopoly," that SZA and Daniel Caesar had turned it down because of it, and that Goldenvoice waived the clause for its own festivals but not for rivals. Goldenvoice said it would "vigorously defend."243

The case took two and a half years. In March 2019 Judge Michael Mosman dismissed it with prejudice, finding no antitrust injury.244 In May 2020 the Ninth Circuit reversed and sent it back,245 and in August Mosman let it go to discovery.246 AEG's answer admitted the clause exists and raised 16 defenses, among them that the amount in dispute was under $75,000.247 In November 2020 the two sides settled. The case was dismissed with prejudice, "each party to bear its own attorneys' fees," and no terms were disclosed.248 No court ever decided whether the clause itself was legal.

The leaks

The radius clause says artists may not leak. In practice the biggest names keep giving themselves away. Rage Against the Machine announced 2020 reunion dates in November 2019 that more or less confirmed their headline slot, and Consequence named the 2020 headliners three days before the poster - though its first version wrongly included My Chemical Romance.249 In November 2024, Post Malone's own tour announcement revealed his Coachella headline a day before the poster.250

The ones who said no

The top line is also the hardest to fill, and the misses only surface as reports. For 2025, Digital Music News reported that Kendrick Lamar and Rihanna had declined headline offers.241 For 2027 the rumor cycle started in September 2026 with HITS, which named Tame Impala and Dua Lipa,251 and by late October TMZ was reporting that BTS and Rage Against the Machine had turned the festival down.236 None of that is confirmed by Goldenvoice, and none of it has to be. A festival that sells its passes before the poster is out has already been paid for the suspense.

When the top line falls out

The top line is the line that most often changes. Beyoncé withdrew from 2017 on her doctor's orders during her pregnancy. Lady Gaga took her slot, and Beyoncé was announced for 2018.252253 Justin Timberlake dropped out of 2019 weeks before the announcement, after a vocal cord injury disrupted his tour.220

Then came West. According to Billboard's report, relayed by Digital Music News, his team asked on New Year's Day for a huge dome in the middle of the grounds, designed and supervised by John McGuire, to be ready by the April 12 start. Goldenvoice said it could not be built in four months; it would also have meant moving a large portable-toilet area tied into sewer and water lines. West's reported answer was no dome, no show.254 Within about 24 hours, the same report says, Ariana Grande had the slot.254 Tollett confirmed to the LA Times that the poster with West on it was in production until Jan. 1.220 West did come to Coachella that year, on Easter Sunday, for Sunday Service on a hill built between weekends.255 That story is in the camping chapter.

The dome would have stood about where the toilets were. Every poster is a set of decisions like that one, made months early and quietly undone when they don't work out.
