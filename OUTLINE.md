# polo.cold.fyi - outline (v1, Oct 31 2026)

A cited long-form deep dive on how Coachella works, plus an archive of the billboards on the road in. Independent: not affiliated with Coachella, Goldenvoice or AEG.
The rule: every claim has a source, and where sources disagree, the page says so.
So far the research covers about 830 distinct source URLs (27 of them Wikipedia pages), 94 billboard entries (2013-2026) and 29 year records (1993, then 1999-2026).

## Chapters

### 0 - Prologue: a polo field and a boycott
Premise: the festival exists because a band fighting Ticketmaster needed somewhere that wasn't LA.
- Nov 5, 1993: Pearl Jam play Empire Polo Club to almost 25,000 (LA Times 1993, via Wikipedia)
- Goldenvoice promoted it, and it "sowed the seeds" (Tollett, The Ringer 2019)
- "Coachella" was a printer's error for "Conchilla" in 1901 (NPR 2025)
- Named for the valley, and held in Indio from the start (NPR 2025)
- Al Haagen III founded the club in 1987 (Museum of Polo 2023); the Rose Garden has about 8,000 bushes (Empire Polo Club 2010)

Still needs: the 1993 LA Times original; which Haagen founded the club.

### 1 - The beginning
Premise: a punk promoter nearly went broke on the first one, then built an empire with AEG.
- Goldenvoice was a punk promoter running on Gary Tovar's smuggling money, later handed to Tollett and Van Santen (Wikipedia - verify)
- 1999: $50 a day, 37,000 people against a 70,000 goal, vinyl melting in the heat (The Ringer 2019)
- It lost $850K-$1M, and Rage gave its fee back: "We knew we were dust" (GRAMMY.com 2024; The Ringer 2019)
- 2001: a reported $7M sale to AEG (Wikipedia); 2004: first sellout, and AEG takes half (The Ringer 2019)
- 2017: $114.6M, the first recurring festival over $100M (Billboard 2017)

The lore: Tollett sold his house and his car after 1999 (New Yorker 2017). It's the founding legend, and it holds up.
Still needs: primary sources for the AEG price and the Tovar story.

### 2 - The year-by-year
Premise: every edition on one page, fed by data/years.json.
- GA: $50 a day (1999) to $649 (2026 W1) (The Ringer 2019; California Today 2025)
- Gross: $67.2M (2013) to $114.6M (2017), none published since (Billboard; Pollstar)
- 2010: the ash cloud strands seven acts; 2011: the iron fence (UPI 2010; Las Vegas Sun 2011)
- 2014's 579,000 is daily admissions, 96,500 x 6 (Billboard via SPIN 2014)
- 2024's W1 took 27 days to sell out; 2026 sold out in about three (Billboard 2024; VICE 2025)

Still needs: attendance for 2018-26; a Boxscore check of the 2006-11 grosses.

### 3 - The grounds
Premise: a polo club, the land around it, and how Goldenvoice came to run all of it.
- About 642 acres in use, around about 78 acres of polo club (Wikipedia; IQ 2021)
- 2012: 280 acres bought, including Eldorado Polo Club (mxdwn 2012; Equestrian News 2012)
- 2021: year-round control on a lease to 2050; the polo staff laid off (Business Wire 2021; Palm Springs Life)
- Cap of 125,000 a day; the state counted peaks near 110,000 in 2025 (Pollstar 2016; California ABC 2025)
- 2024: the first clear year of walk-around drinking (Desert Sun 2024)

Still needs: current acreage; the date the land deal closed; why drinks were fenced in.

### 4 - The stages and the spaces within
Premise: three tents named for deserts became a dozen spaces, some of them festivals of their own.
- Mojave, Gobi and Sahara all date to 1999 (Palm Springs Life)
- The Sahara moved from the rose garden to the Eldorado land (2018), then into a 320-ft steel shed (2024) (LA Times 2023; Desert Sun 2024)
- Do LaB, 2005-06: a broken water feature, a party after the main stage shut, then Tollett's overbooked DJs (The FADER 2025)
- Do LaB surprises: Kaskade's tweeted set went to Skrillex (2017), and Billie Eilish drew 20,000-plus (2024). Their own festival is Lightning in a Bottle (The FADER 2025; Pollstar 2024)
- Yuma (2013); Sonora and the Antarctic (2017); Despacio's "Purple Rain" (2016); Quasar (2024) (Dancing Astronaut 2013; LAist 2017; MusicTech 2026; EW 2024)

The lore: who chose the desert names? Nobody on record says. A good question to put to readers.
Still needs: the naming origin; where the Outdoor Theatre came from.

### 5 - Daft Punk and the pyramid
Premise: one Saturday night in a side tent, and everything after it.
- Said no to $250K in 2005, then yes to $300K (*Daft Punk Unchained* 2015) or $350K plus travel (Goldenvoice, via Billboard 2024)
- The "oui": their manager usually turned offers down unasked, but this one he took to Bangalter (*Busy P Says "Oui"*, Coachella YouTube, Dec 2024)
- 11 p.m., Apr 29, 2006, after Depeche Mode; about 20 minutes over curfew, with the fine on Tollett (Las Vegas Weekly 2006)
- The pyramid: 1,600-plus Barco O-Lite blocks, built for one show (Live Design 2006)
- Ski jackets in rehearsal, and a manager who wasn't allowed to see the lights (The Times 2026; *Unchained*)
- Then the tour and *Alive 2007* (Wikipedia - verify), and in 2026 a LEGO set (JamBase 2026)

The lore: "40,000 tried to get into a 10,000-capacity tent" (their manager, *Unchained*). The legend goes that everyone there started a band. Skrillex is the documented one, though his pyramid show was in 2007. Fans call it the Big Bang of American EDM (LA Times 2023).
Still needs: timestamps for the doc; the cost; why the Sahara.

### 6 - The moments
Premise: the sets people still bring up, and what actually happened.
- Prince's 2008 "Creep" cover, and the takedowns that followed (The Current 2019; Consequence 2015)
- Tupac 2012: Pepper's ghost on Mylar, and Digital Domain in Chapter 11 five months later (Andscape 2017; CNNMoney 2012)
- Beychella 2018: the first Black woman to headline (GRAMMY.com 2019)
- Frank Ocean 2023: the ice rink, the fractures, Blink-182 on weekend 2 (Rolling Stone 2023; Stereogum 2023)
- The 2026 bunker: a $2.5M permit and a 38-ft underground Radiohead cinema, with no band (Desert Sun 2026; FLOOD 2026)

The lore: "the hologram" is the name everyone uses, and the trick behind it is the better story. The $10M price tag is part of the legend (Hypebeast 2012).
Still needs: who built the bunker, and whether it stays.

### 7 - What Coachella started
Premise: the formats, careers and words that left the polo field.
- Reunions: Pixies (2004), Rage (2007), Guns N' Roses (2016, ahead of a $584M tour), No Doubt (2024) (Rolling Stone; Wikipedia; AV Club 2025)
- Two weekends, copied by ACL in 2013 (Music Festival Wizard 2012)
- The livestream went from a like-gated webcast to a YouTube exclusive through 2030 (CNET 2011; MBW 2026)
- Layaway began in 2009; by 2025 about 60% of buyers used payment plans (TicketNews 2009; Entrepreneur 2025)
- "-chella" spread to Brokechella, Cowchella, Col-Chella; Burna Boy named *African Giant* for his small poster type (Zwicky 2022; Quartz 2019)

The lore: boho and the EDM boom. Coachella gave both their name and their biggest stage (CTV 2022; Festival Insider 2022). Two dates to fix: the Stone Roses played in 2013, not 2012, and Pulp's US return was 2012, not 2011.
Still needs: whether Darker Waves is a Goldenvoice or a C3 festival.

### 8 - Getting there
Premise: who moves 125,000 people a day into a farm town.
- The shuttles chain: VMT, then CID ("Consider It Dan," founded by a Disco Biscuits tour manager), then On Location (VMT; Inquirer 2015; Pollstar 2019)
- A shuttle pass cost $60 in 2015 and $150 in 2026; about 40,000 riders (VMT; NBC Palm Springs 2026)
- 2022: one catered dinner put 46 subcontracted drivers in hospital (KESQ 2022)
- 2008: the one-off Amtrak Coachella Express; a rail platform funded in 2019 was never built (RailPAC 2008; RCTC 2019)
- 2025: 12-hour camping lines, and an apology at La Quinta council (KESQ 2025)
- Richard's story: a Boston-to-NH coach driver who used to drive for VMT, told first-person and labeled as his

Still needs: fleet size and driver count.

### 9 - Camping and rituals
Premise: the festival before the festival.
- Walmartchella 2018: an 18-hour delay, with Indio police joining in and La Quinta moving people on (CBS LA 2018; KESQ 2018)
- 2026: a 1 a.m. party in the Ralphs lot on Jefferson, organized on Reddit (CV Independent 2026)
- Day 0: Goldenvoice's campers-only Thursday party, since 2025 (KESQ 2025; Desert Sun 2026)
- Sunday Service, Easter 2019, on a hill built between weekends (The FADER 2019; LA Times 2019)
- Scamchella 2026: wristbands sold, then reported stolen (NBC Palm Springs 2026)

The lore: the 2007 riot (OC Weekly 2018), the hot dog "storming" (Distractify 2025), Sunday Service's "over 50,000" (TMZ 2019) - each told as a legend, with its source.
Still needs: who rides the horses; Richard's own Day 0.

### 10 - The lineup and the poster
Premise: how the bill gets made, and why the type shrinks.
- The drop moved from January to November (2024), then September (2025); 2027 passes sold with no lineup (Pollstar 2025; NME 2026)
- Eurostile LT, broken once, for the Misfits (Billboard 2024)
- Billing is "20 percent of what I do," says one agent (The Ringer 2022)
- 2017: $3-4M per headliner, under $10K at the bottom (New Yorker via mxdwn 2017)
- The radius clause, and the Portland suit that settled (Rolling Stone 2018; DMN 2020)

Still needs: the 2005-12 drop dates; Goldenvoice's own reason for dropping early.

### 11 - The build, the art, the ticket, the food
Premise: five weeks of construction, an ex-Hollywood effects man, a wristband and a $225 dinner.
- The 2025 stagehand window ran Mar 30 - May 3 (AEG posting 2025)
- Paul Clemente's rule: the biggest thing that goes up in ten days (LA Magazine 2023)
- The snail, then the astronaut "burnt from reentry" (PBS SoCal 2013; Time Out 2019)
- The curfew meter: $20,000 at five minutes over, then $1,000 a minute (Hypebeast 2023)

Still needs: crew totals, power figures, any dB limit.

### 12 - The money and Indio
Premise: what the city gets, and who does the counting.
- A $36 ticket-tax threat ended as $5.01 a ticket and a deal to 2030, since extended to 2050 (CBS LA 2012; Hypebot 2013; KESQ 2021)
- 2024: $2.658 per ticket per day, about $2M (KESQ 2024)
- Economic impact claims run $254M-$1.4B, with no independent study behind them (Wikipedia; NBC LA 2019; KRON4 2026)
- Grape workers clean the grounds overnight (Desert Sun/KQED 2019); Marines have worked security (SF Standard 2022)
- A tribe's NYE event in the actual city of Coachella went to court over the name (FOX 11 2021; Festival Insider 2022)

Still needs: the festival's share of Indio's general fund.

### 13 - The valley and the season
Premise: Coachella is two weekends in a longer desert season.
- Stagecoach outsold each Coachella weekend in 2024 and was evacuated for wind in 2026 (KESQ 2024; ABC7 2026)
- Desert Trip grossed about $160M (2016); Power Trip followed (2023) (Live for Live Music 2016; Louder 2023)
- Desert X billboards line up with the mountains behind them; the 2027 edition opens in fall (desertx.org)
- Neon Carnival, and Revolve's 2022 "Fyre" weekend (BizBash 2024; Hypebae 2022)
- The east valley: 99.9% Latino, arsenic in the water (Type Investigations 2018)

Still needs: the year Splash House started.

## Build plan

- **Stack:** static, like the other cold.fyi deep dives. No dependencies, built with build.py, served by GitHub Pages from `main` (richerd22/polo).
- **Pages:** one per chapter, plus a one-page /all.
- **Data feeds for agents:** /data/years.json, a sources list, and /data/billboards.json (reviewed entries only, each with a rights field: `official-embed` / `permissioned` / `own` / `link-only`).
- **Images:**
  - Official posts are embedded by default.
  - Fan photos go in only with written permission and a credit.
  - Our own photos are hosted on the site.
  - Street View is linked, never screenshotted.
  - Where no image works, a text card stands in.
- **Housekeeping:** a takedown address, a $6 DMCA agent registration, a corrections log, and an independence line in the footer.
- **The polo game:** one self-contained page (`/play`), keyboard and touch, beginner mode (simple rules) and expert mode (line of the ball, fouls, chukkers, handicaps). No dependencies, no tracking.
- **Launch:** Mar 22 - Apr 2, 2027, while the boards go up. Coachella 2027 is Apr 9-11 and 16-18.
- **Upkeep:** the billboard chapter is updated every spring.
