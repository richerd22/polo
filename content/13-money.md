In 2024, the city of Indio collected $2,000,206.13 from three weekends of festivals on the polo field.[^mon-kesq24] Seven years earlier, the festival on that field grossed $114.6M on its own.[^mon-pollstar17] Those are the two hardest numbers in this chapter. Almost everything else is an estimate, and it matters who made it.

## What the festival grosses

A gross is total ticket sales, the figure Billboard Boxscore and Pollstar chart from box office reports. For Coachella those charts run through 2017, and the figures climb every year:

:::numbers Reported grosses
- 2013: $67.2M[^mon-spin14]
- 2014: $78.3M[^mon-spin14]
- 2015: $84.3M, on 198,000 tickets[^mon-pollstar15]
- 2016: $94.2M, on 198,000 tickets[^mon-pollstar16]
- 2017: $114.6M, on 250,000 tickets[^mon-pollstar17]
:::

2017 made Coachella the first recurring festival to gross over $100M.[^mon-da17] Then the numbers stop. This project found no Boxscore or Pollstar gross for any year after 2017. By 2019, The Ringer could say only that the yearly gross was "well over $100 million."[^mon-ringer] Nobody outside the company has published a better number since.

The years before 2013 are murkier. Wikipedia's table, which cites Boxscore, puts 2011 at about $25M and the first two-weekend year, 2012, at about $47.3M.[^mon-wiki] If those hold, the second weekend nearly doubled the gross in a single year, which is roughly what you'd expect from selling the same show twice.

Before that, the numbers came from Tollett himself. He told Billboard in 2009 that the 2007 festival, then the biggest yet, drew 186,636 people over three days and grossed $16.3M. The 2008 edition drew 151,666 and grossed $13.8M.[^mon-bb09] So the gross grew roughly sevenfold from 2007 to 2017, on a ticket count that only went from about 187,000 to 250,000.[^mon-bb09][^mon-pollstar17] Most of that growth was price.

## Selling the same show twice

The biggest financial decision in the festival's history was announced in a sentence: from 2012, a second weekend, same lineup, separate tickets.[^mon-boombox] Tollett's reasons, as he gave them in 2011, were about scarcity. Demand was already past what the 2011 site could hold. Adding about 40,000 people a day would "ruin the show." And resellers were getting $500-600 for passes, which he called unfair.[^mon-boombox]

So instead of a bigger festival, he cloned it. The same artists play the same stages a week apart. Every gross in the panel above is two weekends' worth.

Goldenvoice did the same sum again in October 2016 with Desert Trip, six classic-rock acts over two weekends on the same field. Tollett told The New York Times it would gross "approximately $160 million for the two weekends."[^mon-l4lm] Stereogum set that against $84M for Coachella, which is the 2015 figure; Pollstar's chart has Coachella 2016 at $94.2M.[^mon-stereogum16][^mon-pollstar16] Either way, two weekends of the Stones, Dylan and McCartney out-grossed the festival. Stereogum put the cost of staging it at about $100M.[^mon-stereogum16]

Some sense of where the money goes came from Paul Tollett himself. He told The New Yorker in 2017 that each of that year's headliners was paid $3-4M, and that acts at the bottom of the bill got a little under $10,000.[^mon-mxdwnfees] For 2026, Rolling Stone reported that Justin Bieber's fee was "north of $10 million for the two weekends," about $5M a weekend.[^mon-rs25] That figure came from one unnamed source.[^mon-complex]

## The country festival's books

Stagecoach is the cheap date that pays for itself. The same Billboard piece put it plainly: Coachella "shares costs" with Stagecoach, held on the same site the next weekend.[^mon-bb09] Most of the build is already there. Pollstar's editor made the same point in 2012, when Goldenvoice bought the land next door: Stagecoach worked from the start because the site had already been prepared for Coachella.[^mon-laweekly12]

Its grosses, while anyone was still publishing them, kept climbing:

- 2008: $11.4M, on an aggregate attendance of 120,903[^mon-bb09]
- 2015: $21.9M, on 70,000 tickets, third biggest festival gross in the world that year[^mon-pollstar15]
- 2016: $25.4M, on 72,000 tickets, fifth in the world[^mon-pollstar16]

The tickets climbed too. A two-day pass for the second Stagecoach, in 2008, started at $175.[^mon-pollstar07] Pollstar lists the 2016 price range as $269-899.[^mon-pollstar16] Three-day GA passes for Stagecoach 2027, set for April 23-25 with Riley Green, Zach Top and Garth Brooks at the top, start at $599 once the early discounts run out.[^mon-sc27]

:::numbers One polo field, 2016
- Desert Trip: $160.1M on 150,000 tickets, No. 1 festival gross in the world[^mon-pollstar16]
- Coachella: $94.2M on 198,000 tickets, No. 2[^mon-pollstar16]
- Stagecoach: $25.4M on 72,000 tickets, No. 5[^mon-pollstar16]
- Together: about $279.7M, by our addition, from one patch of grass in Indio
:::

Pollstar's 2016 chart gives the Desert Trip price range as $399-1,599, and Coachella's as $399-899.[^mon-pollstar16]

## Who owns it

Goldenvoice runs Coachella. Goldenvoice is owned by AEG, the Anschutz Entertainment Group. AEG bought Goldenvoice in 2001.[^mon-kcrw] Wikipedia puts the price at $7 million; this project hasn't found that figure in the original reporting, so treat it as Wikipedia's.[^mon-wiki] Coachella itself came later. Tollett sold AEG half of the festival in 2004, the year it first sold out.[^mon-ringer]

## The Anschutz donations

In January 2017, The FADER reported what the Anschutz Foundation had given to. Between 2010 and 2013 it gave $190,000 combined to Alliance Defending Freedom, the National Christian Foundation and the Family Research Council, groups The FADER described as anti-LGBTQ.[^mon-fader] In the 2016 election cycle, Philip and Nancy Anschutz gave $1,012,000 to Republican candidates and PACs, including $500,000 to the Senate Leadership Fund.[^mon-fader]

Anschutz called the claims that he is anti-LGBTQ "fake news." He said he and the foundation stopped giving to groups once they learned those groups backed anti-LGBTQ causes. He did not deny the donations.[^mon-fader]

Tollett, asked about it by The New Yorker that spring, did not defend the politics. He was blunt about the position he was in:

> "I was offended. I run the festival, but it's rude to say that when you're a partner with someone."[^mon-mxdwnny]

The festival went ahead that April as planned, and grossed its record $114.6M.[^mon-pollstar17]

## Indio's cut: the standoff

In 2012, Indio was short of money. The city had just cut about $4M through layoffs and furloughs.[^mon-cbs12] So the council looked at the biggest event in town and floated an admissions tax: 5-10% on any event over 2,500 people. Goldenvoice worked it out at about $36 a ticket, or $4-6M a year.[^mon-cbs12]

Tollett's answer was to threaten to leave. He said the 2013 festivals could be the last in Indio, that Goldenvoice would skip 2014 and find a new site for 2015.[^mon-cbs12] He also listed what the festival already paid: more than $800,000 in ticket fees, sales tax on about $9M of food and drink, and more than $800,000 in hotel tax.[^mon-cbs12]

At the time, the city's per-ticket share was $2.33. That came to about $500,000 a year, in a city budget of about $49M.[^mon-kcur] Councilman Sam Torres had pushed for a 6% tax.

> "I looked at these guys as a way to help us continue to maintain our city."[^mon-kcur]

## The 2013 deal

The tax never passed. In April 2013 the council voted 4-0 for a new agreement instead, with Mayor Elaine Holmes abstaining over a property conflict.[^mon-hypebot] The terms:

- Coachella and Stagecoach stay in Indio through at least 2030.
- The city's share rises from $2.33 to $5.01 a ticket, starting in 2014.
- The site can go from three festivals a year to five.[^mon-hypebot][^mon-spin13]

NPR's summary was "$5 a ticket," or about a million dollars a year.[^mon-npr13] The five-festival clause is the one that later made room for Desert Trip and Power Trip in October (see the next chapter).

In 2021 the deal was stretched to 2050. The council voted 3-1, with Holmes recusing because she owns property within 1,000 feet of the site.[^mon-dmn21] Councilmember Glenn Miller made the case for it:

> "Not one dollar comes out of the city of Indio's pocket."[^mon-dmn21]

The same year, Goldenvoice signed a lease with the Haagen family that runs to 2050 and gives it the polo club year-round, with room for two more large festivals a year.[^mon-bw21][^mon-da21]

## Indio's cut: the 2024 numbers

For 2024, a city spokesperson gave KESQ the details. Indio counted 81,690 tickets for Coachella's first weekend, 80,148 for the second and 89,003 for Stagecoach, 250,841 in all. It collected $2,000,206.13, at a rate of "$2.658 per ticket/per day this fiscal year."[^mon-kesq24]

The city's arithmetic checks out exactly: 250,841 tickets, times three days, times $2.658, is $2,000,206. That works out to about $7.97 per three-day ticket. That is our arithmetic, not the city's. It is well above the $5.01 of the 2013 deal, and the public record found for this piece doesn't say how the rate got there or when it switched to a per-day basis. The amended agreements would.

Notice the order of those ticket counts. Stagecoach, the country festival the weekend after, sold more tickets than either Coachella weekend that year.[^mon-kesq24]

The city also gets paid when the music runs late. The curfew carries a $20,000 fine once a set goes five minutes over, then $1,000 a minute, and the money goes into Indio's general fund.[^mon-hb23] For the first weekend of 2023, the city fined Goldenvoice $117,000.[^mon-abc23]

:::numbers What Indio gets
- 1999: $90,000 fronted by the city for services, per Wikipedia[^mon-wiki]
- 2012: $2.33 a ticket, about $500,000 a year[^mon-kcur]
- 2014 on: $5.01 a ticket under the 2013 deal[^mon-hypebot]
- 2024: $2.658 per ticket per day, $2,000,206.13 in all[^mon-kesq24]
- 2023: $117,000 in curfew fines, first weekend alone[^mon-abc23]
:::

What none of these numbers say is what share of Indio's budget the festivals cover today. The 2013 figure, about $500,000 against $49M, is about 1%.[^mon-kcur] No current equivalent turned up.

## What the festivals are worth to City Hall

The city's own budget answers part of that. Indio's mid-year report for fiscal 2024-25 projected $5.37M in "Musical Festival Services," the money the festival promoters pay back for the police and other agencies the city contracts to cover the crowds.[^mon-indio25] A further $2.6M was booked as "festival activities," under the city's income from its property and assets. The report doesn't break that line down.[^mon-indio25] Together that is about $8M, or about 6.6% of a $120.9M general fund. That is our arithmetic, and it leaves out sales tax and hotel tax.

A 2016 Desert Sun study, as relayed by the Grammys and Hypebot, put the city's ticket-tax take at $3.18M and called it 5% of the city's total fund.[^mon-hypebot16] Ten years apart, the share is in the same neighborhood.

The hotel tax is where the festivals show up indirectly. Indio's transient occupancy tax, charged on stays of up to 30 days including festival campers, was about $3.1M a year a decade earlier. The 2024-25 report projected $15.3M, with residential vacation rentals supplying about 60% of it.[^mon-indio25] One festival moves that number. The report said hotel tax was down that year "mainly" because Power Trip, the October 2023 metal weekend, did not come back.[^mon-indio25]

A year later the city was more cheerful. In February 2026 its finance director, Ruby Walla, raised the general fund revenue target to $129.8M and the hotel tax estimate to $16.5M, about 75% of it from short-term rentals, by her count.[^mon-indiopost26] The 60% and 75% may measure different things; neither document says. Walla was not shy about where it comes from:

> "We also rely on the festivals. If they succeed, we succeed."[^mon-indiopost26]

For scale, Bonnaroo's home county in Tennessee was getting $3 a ticket in 2011, more than $5M that year, according to LA Weekly.[^mon-laweekly12] And Riverside County's date crop was worth $145.4M in 2024.[^mon-crop24] That is more than Coachella's last published gross.

## The impact numbers, and who made them

Every spring, a big number goes around: what Coachella is "worth" to the valley. It has been $254M, $600M, $700M and $1.4B. Here is where each one came from.

- **$254.4M (2012).** An economic-impact analysis said Coachella brought that much to the region, with more than a third of it landing in Indio.[^mon-spin13] Hypebot credits the analysis to Development Management Group, which put Indio's share at $89.2M.[^mon-hypebot] NPR described a study putting the festival's Indio impact at more than $90M as "Goldenvoice-commissioned."[^mon-npr13]
- **$1.4B (2016).** A projection by Michael Bracken of Development Management Group, of Palm Desert, for all five Goldenvoice festivals that year, Desert Trip included. It put $805M in the Coachella Valley and about $212M in Indio, and was announced by the valley's tourism bureau and its economic partnership. NBC Los Angeles noted that Bracken's relationship with AEG and Goldenvoice was "undisclosed and unclear."[^mon-nbcla]
- **About $700M lost (2020).** When the festivals were cancelled, the Coachella Valley Economic Partnership estimated the valley would lose about $700M.[^mon-ticketnews20]
- **About $600M (2024).** NBC Palm Springs credited this one to the City of Indio. No study was named.[^mon-nbcps24]
- **More than $700M a year (2026).** Gov. Gavin Newsom's office said Coachella and Stagecoach together generate that, plus more than 10,000 temporary jobs.[^mon-kron4] NBC Palm Springs added a figure of more than $100M a year in value to Indio.[^mon-nbcps26] Neither report named the study behind it.

- **$704M (2016).** A separate projection that spring, reported by The Desert Sun, for Coachella and Stagecoach alone: $403M to Coachella Valley businesses and $106M to businesses in Indio.[^mon-hypebot16][^mon-mixmag16]

None of these is an independent audit. Two come from the same consultant, at least one was paid for by the promoter, and the recent ones cite no method at all. They may all be roughly right. A valley that fills its hotels for three weekends running is clearly getting something. But the figures tell you more about who wanted to say a number than about the number.

In April 2026 the local business paper GPS Business Insider went looking for the study behind the governor's $700M, under the headline "Pick a Number, Any Number." Its publisher, Bob Marra, said the governor's office had not answered by press time.[^mon-gpsbi26] He traced the earlier $600M figure to a July 2024 KESQ report in which Goldenvoice executive Mark Girton cited it. The study Girton likely meant, Marra wrote, counts ticket purchases that go to the Los Angeles promoter and airfare to the desert.[^mon-gpsbi26] Girton described the crowd as 125,000 a day, "or 750,000 people over six days." Marra's correction: they are three-day passes, so closer to 250,000 people.[^mon-gpsbi26]

The governor's release had a third number: $908M in "Media Impact Value" for Coachella in 2025.[^mon-gov26] It also said nearly 250,000 people came to Coachella that year.[^mon-gov26] The most modest estimate around came from two tourism data firms, The Data Appeal Company and Mabrian, who projected just over $20M in direct tourism spending, leaving out tickets and multipliers.[^mon-gpsbi26] So that is $20M and $700M, published the same spring, for roughly the same three weekends.

:::lore A billion-dollar festival
The legend goes that Coachella is a billion-dollar event for the desert, and you'll see "$1.4 billion" quoted as if it were one festival's yearly haul. That figure is real, but it is a 2016 projection, by a consultant whose ties to the promoter were never disclosed, for five festivals at once, worldwide.[^mon-nbcla] Indio's share in that same projection was about $212M. Nobody has measured it since.
:::

## The rooms

The clearest independent signal is the hotels. Expedia's data showed Coachella Valley hotels more than 90% full in April, the most expensive month of the year, with the average nightly rate climbing from $280 in April 2016 to $340 in April 2017.[^mon-hotel17] "April always leads the entire year for Coachella Valley hotels," the report said.[^mon-hotel17]

Short-term rentals are where the stories get wild. Homeowners and agents told NBC Palm Springs in 2026 that some luxury homes rent for more than $100,000 for a single weekend.[^mon-nbcps26b] That's anecdote, not data. Nobody published the bookings.

## Who does the work

The festival's labor is a mix of stagehands, private security, temporary crews and, at night, farmworkers.

**The overnight cleanup.** In 2019, The Desert Sun's Rebecca Plevin followed grape workers who take overnight trash shifts at the polo grounds across all three April weekends. They finish around 3:30 a.m., after about eight hours in the vineyards the day before, and are paid minimum wage.[^mon-kqed] The reason more of them need the shifts is in the fields: the county counted about 1,000 fewer acres of grapes in 2017 than in 2013.[^mon-kqed] Enriqueta Magaña, a supervisor with 20 years in the vineyards and 10 at the festival, described the grounds the morning after as "a garbage dump."[^mon-kqed]

**The Marines.** In 2022, the San Francisco Standard reported that active-duty Marines, many from Camp Pendleton, had worked security shifts at Coachella for C.I. Security Specialists. In exchange, the company donated toward Marine Corps birthday-ball tickets. One Marine estimated that about half of roughly 200 of the company's officers there were volunteer Marines.[^mon-sfstd] The company said its personnel were all paid employees. State regulators said the Marines needed guard licenses, and two of those interviewed had none.[^mon-sfstd]

**The guards.** By 2025, close to 1,000 Allied Universal guards were posted around the clock inside the gates, according to NBC Palm Springs.[^mon-nbcps25]

**The build.** In April 2019, lead rigger Christopher Griffin, 49, a 20-year Goldenvoice employee, died after falling from scaffolding during setup.[^mon-mxdwn19] In 2025, a former stagehand filed a wage suit against the companies behind the festival, alleging that hourly workers drive up to three hours to job sites without being paid for the travel.[^mon-law360]

**The guests, sometimes.** In 2019, the rapper Belly sued Coachella, Goldenvoice and the security firm IPS, saying up to 25 guards attacked him during the Weeknd's set the year before.[^mon-belly]

## Selling it: the slump and the snap-back

For most of the 2010s, selling out was the easy part. Then it wasn't.

In 2024, the first weekend took nearly a month to sell out. About 80% of the year's 250,000 tickets had sold by opening day, and sales were down 14-17% from the year before, according to Billboard's sources.[^mon-bb24] The second weekend never fully sold out.[^mon-pollstar25]

2025 was split. First-weekend general admission was gone by December 2024; second-weekend passes were "widely available."[^mon-dmn24] By April, second-weekend passes were reselling on StubHub for as little as $246, less than half of face value.[^mon-dmn25]

Inside the fence, the prices kept their own pace. In 2025, Digital Music News priced three beers at $45 and two tacos at $16, and hotel packages from about $3,300 to more than $9,000.[^mon-dmn24]

Goldenvoice's response was to move the calendar. The 2026 lineup came out on September 15, 2025, months earlier than usual.[^mon-pollstar25] Both weekends' GA and VIP passes sold out three days after going on sale.[^mon-vice] The chapter on the lineup covers why an early poster changes the math.

## The brands

Hypebeast dates the first big branded spaces on the grounds to Heineken in 2006, followed by H&M, American Express and Absolut.[^mon-hb23b] When the festival split into two weekends in 2012, the brand parties off-site mostly stuck to the first one.[^mon-bizbash12]

The off-site houses peaked and then thinned out. By 2019, Glossy reported Sephora, H&M, Urban Decay and others scaling back or dropping their VIP experiences. Conor Begley of Tribe Dynamics:

> "There's definitely been a decline in activations, especially larger-branded experiences."[^mon-glossy19]

The money moved inside the fence instead. SponsorUnited counted the 2026 on-site sponsors by number of branded assets: Heineken led with 23, then Aperol with 19 and Gap with 14. Heineken's cans lit up when they touched, Gap built a "Hoodie House," Pinterest ran a phone-free zone, and HBO screened the *Euphoria* season three premiere in the campgrounds. No sponsorship fees were disclosed.[^mon-su26]

The company with the most riding on the festival doesn't sponsor it at all. Revolve, the online fashion retailer, "typically earns about 30% of its yearly revenue in the weeks around Coachella," Retail Brew reported in 2020, citing New York Times reporting. When the 2020 festival was postponed, Revolve's festival-style orders dropped to about a fifth of normal.[^mon-retailbrew] Revolve's own party, and what happened to it, is in the next chapter.
