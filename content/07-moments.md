short: The sets people still bring up, and what the paperwork says happened. A Prince cover that kept getting pulled offline, a dead rapper reflected off a sheet of foil, a homecoming with about 200 people on stage, an ice rink that outlasted its headliner, and a $2.5M concrete bunker for a band that never showed up.
---

Several of Coachella's best-known moments didn't happen quite the way people remember. The Tupac on the main stage in 2012 was a projection bounced off a sheet of foil.[^mo-andscape] Frank Ocean's ice rink was built, and the first people to stand on it were Skrillex, Fred again.. and Four Tet, a week after Ocean had left the bill.[^mo-okayplayer] In 2026 the poster promised "The Bunker Debut of Radiohead," and Radiohead stayed home.[^mo-desertsun] The festival is very good at making moments. This chapter is about what was underneath them.

Daft Punk's 2006 pyramid has [a chapter of its own](/pyramid/). Everything else is here.

## Prince plays "Creep" (2008)

Prince headlined the second night, April 26, 2008. Somewhere in the set he played an eight-minute cover of Radiohead's "Creep," and he brought out Sheila E. for "The Glamorous Life."[^mo-current]

Fan videos of "Creep" were online by the next day, and Prince's team had them taken down.[^mo-current] Since the song was Radiohead's, Radiohead had a view on this. Thom Yorke's, as reported by Consequence:

> "Really? He's blocked it? Well, tell him to unblock it. It's our … song."[^mo-cos]

Seven years later Prince let it back up. In December 2015 a new upload appeared with Radiohead's blessing, and Prince tweeted a link to it.[^mo-current] On December 16, 2015, it was taken down again, this time after a claim from NPG Music Publishing, which is Prince's own publishing company.[^mo-cos] So the Prince side won both rounds, the second one against itself.

## The hologram that wasn't (2012)

On April 15, 2012, during Dr. Dre and Snoop Dogg's headline set, Tupac Shakur appeared on stage. Dre had come up with it and paid for it. "It was Dre's idea to bring Tupac back," said Nick Smith of AV Concepts, the San Diego company that projected it.[^mo-andscape]

Everybody called it a hologram. It wasn't one. It was Pepper's ghost, a 19th-century theater illusion: you project an image onto an angled transparent surface, and from the audience it looks like a person standing in the room.[^mo-hypebeast12] Victorian theaters did it with glass. AV Concepts used Mylar foil.[^mo-andscape] Hypebeast named the UK firm Musion and its "Eyeliner" system as the technology underneath.[^mo-hypebeast12]

The digital Tupac came from Digital Domain, James Cameron's visual-effects company. VFX supervisors Janelle Croshaw and Steve Preeg led a team of about 20 on it.[^mo-andscape] Smith told MTV it took about four months.[^mo-hypebeast12]

:::lore The $10 million Tupac
The legend goes that the Tupac cost $10 million. That figure went around in April 2012 and never came with a name attached.[^mo-hypebeast12] The only on-the-record number is Smith's, via MTV, and it's for a different thing: he wouldn't give the real cost, but said a comparable piece could run from $100,000 to more than $400,000.[^mo-hypebeast12] Nobody has published an invoice. The $10M lives on because it sounds like what a dead man on a stage ought to cost.
:::

Then came the strange part. On September 11, 2012, five months after Coachella, Digital Domain Media Group filed for Chapter 11. It shut its operation in Port St. Lucie, Florida, let most of the 320 staff there go, and its CEO resigned.[^mo-cnn] No source ties the bankruptcy to Tupac. It's just the order things happened in.

As for the Tupac, the digital asset "has been archived," Andscape reported in 2017, and only the estate has access.[^mo-andscape] Dre has said it was "strictly for Coachella."[^mo-andscape] It didn't stay strictly there for long, though. The next chapter follows it.

## Beychella (2018)

Beyoncé was booked to headline in 2017. She withdrew on her doctors' advice while she was pregnant, and came back in 2018 as the first Black woman ever to headline Coachella.[^mo-grammy19] She played both Saturdays. The first was April 14.[^mo-fader18] (GRAMMY.com gives "April 13 and 22," but those were a Friday and a Sunday.)

The show was built as an HBCU homecoming, with about 200 people on stage.[^mo-grammy19] Rehearsals ran four months for the band, then four more for the dancers.[^mo-grammy19] Fans named it Beychella.[^mo-dictionary]

On YouTube, her weekend-one set peaked at about 458,000 people watching at once. That was the highest since the festival started streaming, according to TMZ's figures as reported by The FADER.[^mo-fader18] A year later, on April 17, 2019, Netflix released *Homecoming*, a film of the set with a live album alongside.[^mo-grammy19] It won the Grammy for Best Music Film.[^mo-grammyaward]

It also raised the bar for every headliner after her. Ariana Grande's production designer told The Hollywood Reporter that after Beyoncé, the festival had turned into a "competition."[^mo-thr] TMZ's numbers, as The FADER reported them, had her peak about 75% above Lady Gaga's the year before, with 43.1 million live-stream views across 232 countries for the whole festival.[^mo-fader18]

## Kanye, the dome and the hill (2019)

He had form. Kanye West's 2011 set came with a stone mural in the style of the Pergamon Altar, a troupe of ballerinas and a lift that was reportedly used on Michael Jackson's last tour. Complex, looking back ten years later, said nobody had ever done it like him.[^mo-complex11]

He was due to headline again in 2019. In January he walked away. He called the standard 40-by-60-foot stage "artistically limiting" and had asked, late in the day, for a custom dome. The festival didn't build it.[^mo-da19]

He came back anyway, without a headline slot. On Easter Sunday, April 21, 2019, Sunday Service, until then an ultra-exclusive private gathering, went up on a hill at the festival at 9 a.m.[^mo-fader19][^mo-complex] The "Church Clothes" merch went with it: sweatshirts from $165 to $225 and "Jesus Walks" socks at $50.[^mo-variety19] The hill, the crowd, and the arguments over the size of the crowd all belong to the camping chapter.

## Frank Ocean's rink (2023)

That year's headliners were Bad Bunny, BLACKPINK and Frank Ocean. It was the first time every headliner was nonwhite.[^mo-cbs23] Ocean closed the first Sunday, and it went wrong in several directions at once.

He came on about an hour late, in slippers.[^mo-rs23] There was meant to be a segment with skaters on an ice rink, and it didn't happen. A source told Rolling Stone he had hurt his ankle in on-site rehearsals the week before, and the show was reworked at the last minute.[^mo-rs23] Rolling Stone also found an LMU hockey player's Instagram Story showing nine men in skating gear, captioned "The Boys x Frank." That crew never performed.[^mo-rs23] On stage, Ocean said he was there for his brother Ryan Breaux, who died in 2020.[^mo-rs23]

The YouTube stream was pulled. "Frank Ocean is not scheduled to appear on the Coachella livestream," YouTube posted, and nobody gave a reason. Björk's set came off the stream schedule the same weekend.[^mo-hypebeast23] On April 19 he withdrew from weekend two. His team said he had been hurt on the festival grounds the week before, and "on doctor's advice" couldn't perform, "due to two fractures and a sprain in his left leg."[^mo-stereogum19] Blink-182, who had played their first reunion show with Tom DeLonge on the first Friday, took his slot.[^mo-stereogum19][^mo-loudwire]

According to Billboard, as reported by The FADER, Ocean was contracted for $8M across both weekends. His weekend-one production reportedly cost more than his $4M fee, the rink was the most expensive part of it, and the festival stood to lose "several million dollars."[^mo-fader23] Goldenvoice went looking for someone else to use the rink. On the second Sunday, Skrillex, Fred again.. and Four Tet stood on it to close the night.[^mo-okayplayer]

:::lore The melted rink
Two stories went around that week. TMZ's, as Stereogum summarized it: Ocean hurt himself in an accident involving bikes on the polo grounds, doctors advised changing the set, and the skating went. Stereogum adds that about 100 LA-based hockey players had rehearsed on a raised rink at Paramount Studios and were told on arrival that the routine was off.[^mo-stereogum17] The second came from TheFestiveOwl, an anonymous X account. Its version was that the rink was "constructed and ready to go," that Ocean dropped it at the last minute, and that Coachella had to "melt the entire ice rink" and rebuild the stage.[^mo-stereogum17] Stereogum called the account unverified.[^mo-stereogum17] The documented version is the injury his team described, and a rink that was still there a week later.[^mo-stereogum19][^mo-okayplayer]
:::

## The bunker (2026)

The 2026 poster ended with a line nobody could explain: "The Bunker Debut of Radiohead Kid A Mnesia." Fans started guessing whether that meant Radiohead were playing.[^mo-farout]

The permit file had the better answer. In September 2025 Goldenvoice applied to the City of Indio for a 16,130-square-foot "commercial foundation." City assessors valued it at $2.5M. Reporters found a large sand pit with steel reinforcement near the Sahara tent, and the concrete and steel framing suggested something permanent. A Rolling Stone representative confirmed Radiohead would not perform.[^mo-desertsun]

What got built was Radiohead's Motion Picture House: about 17,000 square feet with 38-foot ceilings, under the Empire Polo Fields, open to anyone with a festival wristband.[^mo-flood] Inside were the *KID A MNESIA* film and galleries of full-scale Thom Yorke and Stanley Donwood artwork from the *Kid A* and *Amnesiac* sessions.[^mo-flood] The sound was a "6-point surround" mix built from the original multitracks, and Sean Evans directed the film.[^mo-stereogum26]

It had a long run-up. *KID A MNESIA* first came out in 2021 as a free digital exhibition for PS5, PC and Mac.[^mo-stereogum26] A physical 20th-anniversary installation planned that year was cancelled over logistics and COVID.[^mo-farout] The desert finally got it built, five years late and underground.

Getting in cost nothing but planning. Visits were booked as free reservations in the festival's app, and the core was the 75-minute film built from thousands of Yorke and Donwood sketches, paintings, collages, recordings and notes.[^mo-vau26sets] Yorke's own description of what it's about:

> "a monster trapped in a derelict museum of the lost and forgotten."[^mo-needle26]

It was air-conditioned, which in Indio in April may have been the bigger draw.[^mo-showbams26]

So Radiohead made the 2026 poster without playing a note. Ed O'Brien did tell reporters the band plans to play 20 shows a year from 2027.[^mo-stereogum26] After the festival the Motion Picture House went on tour: Brooklyn from May 6, Mexico City from October 27 and San Francisco's Palace of Fine Arts from January 14, 2027.[^mo-kidamnesia] The film runs 75 minutes, and visits are booked in two-hour slots. Stereogum also listed a Chicago run at Cinespace in the summer, which the official site doesn't show.[^mo-kidamnesia][^mo-stereogum26] The official FAQ says "the Coachella Bunker experience" was different in some ways from the touring version.[^mo-kidamnesia] It wasn't the first artist-built world on the grounds, either. Desert Sun points to Travis Scott's "The Village by Cactus Jack" in 2025.[^mo-desertsun]

## Other nights people bring up

**Bauhaus, upside down, 2005.** It was the band's first show together since 1998. Peter Murphy opened with the nine-minute "Bela Lugosi's Dead," sung while hanging upside down over the stage like a bat.[^mo-laweekly] When it was over he told the crowd: "Now you can say that you were there!"[^mo-laweekly] The reunion bought them a tour opening for Nine Inch Nails and a 2008 album, *Go Away White*, and then they split again.[^mo-laweekly]

**Madonna and Drake, 2015.** On April 12 Madonna joined Drake's headline set for "Human Nature," "Hung Up" and "Bitch I'm Madonna," then kissed him for a long time. His recoil is the clip everyone remembers.[^mo-stereogum15] His Instagram caption afterward: "Moments to write home about."[^mo-stereogum15]

**Lady Gaga, twice.** In 2017 she took the headline slot Beyoncé had given up. On April 15 she debuted "The Cure" live and put it out on Spotify, iTunes and Tidal that night. It was an early case of a festival set doubling as a release.[^mo-avclub17] In 2025 she came back to open the *Mayhem* live show: a "demonic opera house" set, four acts and a finale, the first live "Garden of Eden," with Gesaffelstein on synths.[^mo-bv25] BrooklynVegan's reviewer thought it was built more for the livestream and the screens than for the field.[^mo-bv25]

**Ariana Grande and most of \*NSYNC, 2019.** On April 14, Joey Fatone, JC Chasez, Chris Kirkpatrick and Lance Bass joined her for "Tearin' Up My Heart" and "Break Up With Your Girlfriend." She sang Justin Timberlake's parts herself.[^mo-ap19] Nicki Minaj, Diddy and Mase turned up too. Grande told the crowd: "I've been rehearsing my whole f---ing life for this moment."[^mo-ap19]

**Harry Styles and Shania Twain, 2022.** On the first Friday, Styles brought out Twain for "Man! I Feel Like a Woman!" and "You're Still the One." The same set was the first live performance of "As It Was."[^mo-fader22] His introduction: "This lady taught me to sing. She also told me that men are trash."[^mo-fader22]

**Odd Future in the Sahara, 2011.** On April 15, 2011, the LA collective played the Sahara tent, and Pharrell came out near the end.[^mo-mxdwn11] The LA Times later called it a "ramshackle set" of barely half an hour that "yielded two headline-caliber artists," Tyler, the Creator and a singer named Frank Ocean, and boosted Syd and, later, Steve Lacy.[^mo-lat23] Twelve years later Ocean was the one closing the main stage.

**deadmau5, last call, 2025.** He was one of the Sahara's early big nights, on a Friday in 2010 opposite Jay-Z.[^mo-laweekly10] Fifteen years later, on April 18, 2025, his back-to-back set with ZHU was cut short while he was visibly drunk; he apologized and posted: "Probably my last Coachella."[^mo-stereogum25]

**Justin Bieber, 2026.** Bieber went from cameo regular (with Ariana Grande in 2019, with Daniel Caesar and Giveon in 2022) to Saturday headliner, on a deal he negotiated himself. Rolling Stone's Canadian edition thought the crowd might have been "the biggest ever" at Coachella, and also that "Bieber missed the mark": a minimal set heavy on nostalgia, with people leaving during the acoustic deep cuts.[^mo-rsca]

**Sabrinawood, 2026.** Two years after telling the crowd "see you back here when I headline," Sabrina Carpenter did.[^mo-cos26sab] Her first Friday was a tour of fake Hollywood, under a giant "Sabrinawood" sign: Sam Elliott as a traffic cop in the opening film, dancers dressed as poodles for "Manchild," Will Ferrell as an electrician, and the voice of Samuel L. Jackson.[^mo-nme26sab] Susan Sarandon played an older Carpenter in a monologue that ran nearly seven minutes. For weekend two the role went to Geena Davis, Sarandon's *Thelma & Louise* co-star, in a shorter cut, and Terry Crews took Ferrell's part.[^mo-wrap26sab] The same firm, Stufish, designed both Carpenter's show and Bieber's bare one the next night.[^mo-wp26]

**Karol G, 2026.** On the first Sunday Karol G opened with "Latina Foreva," brought out an all-female mariachi band, Becky G and Wisin, and premiered a song with Greg González of Cigarettes After Sex.[^mo-rs26karol] She told the crowd who she was:

> "I am Carolina Giraldo from Medellin, Colombia. And today, I am the first Latina woman to headline Coachella."[^mo-cx26karol]

Wallpaper's critic noted a cave-painting creation myth, a parrot-shaped float and a shallow pool built in the middle of the crowd.[^mo-wp26] A week later she added Peso Pluma, J Balvin and Ryan Castro, and ended with "Nos Vamos de Tour" on the screen.[^mo-mx26karol]

**The Strokes, 2026.** On the first Saturday Julian Casablancas joked about the military draft. On the second, the band played "Oblivius" for the first time in a decade over a video accusing the CIA of political killings, which ended on footage of bombings in Gaza and Iran. The festival's YouTube stream showed it in full.[^mo-ind26]

The surprise slot changed hands too. Jack White, who had headlined 11 years earlier, was added for 45 minutes at 3 p.m. in the Mojave on the first Saturday.[^mo-vau26sets] On the second, the same slot went to Kacey Musgraves.[^mo-showbams26]

**Anyma and the wind, 2026.** Anyma was due to follow Sabrina Carpenter at midnight on the first Friday. The festival called it off: "Due to strong wind conditions affecting Anyma's stage build, he is unable to perform tonight."[^mo-stereogum26b] The same winds brought down a light fixture during John Summit's Do LaB set, injuring a festivalgoer, and that stage was closed.[^mo-stereogum26b] By Sunday the Do LaB had a new Instagram post: "Let's end at the beginning. Anyma b2b Marlon Hoffstadt close out the Do LaB at 10:45pm tonight."[^mo-variety26] His full *ÆDEN* show moved to weekend two.[^mo-stereogum26b] On a site that stages a different spectacle every year, the weather got a credit.

## Weekend two gets the guests (2026)

For most of the two-weekend era, weekend two was the sequel. Surprise guests were part of the house style, and the famous ones above (Madonna in 2015, \*NSYNC in 2019, Shania Twain in 2022) all came on the first weekend. In 2026 almost all of the big ones landed a week later. Madonna joined Sabrina Carpenter for three songs, timed to her new single. SZA and Sexyy Red came out for Justin Bieber, and Bieber serenaded Billie Eilish. Olivia Rodrigo joined Addison Rae, and Snoop Dogg joined Giveon.[^mo-varietyau]

Up close: Madonna rose out of the stage during Carpenter's "Juno," as it turned into "Vogue." Then came an unreleased song, then "Like a Prayer." She told the crowd it was "20 years ago today" that she had played the dance tent, in the same boots and corsets: "it's like a full circle moment."[^mo-bbca26mad] It was close. The 2006 set was on April 30.[^mo-spin06] Bieber kept the laptop bit but brought Billie Eilish for "One Less Lonely Girl," SZA for "Snooze," Sexyy Red, Dijon and Big Sean, who did two songs Bieber hadn't sung in public since 2017.[^mo-cos26bieb] And Anyma finally got his main-stage *ÆDEN* show, with Lisa of BLACKPINK, Muse's Matt Bellamy, Swae Lee and Joji.[^mo-mx26anyma]

Variety's Jem Aswad asked the industry why. One agent said weekend one "is driven heavily by influencer culture." Another insider put it as: "Weekend two is always better... more to see and less to be seen."[^mo-varietyau] Others said guests on weekend two protect a headliner's first-weekend spotlight. Sources said it was not a plan coordinated by Goldenvoice or YouTube.[^mo-varietyau] The festival has two identical weekends on paper. In practice they've started to split: weekend one for being seen, weekend two for whoever turns up.
