In January 2019, Burna Boy found his name in the third line of the Coachella poster, in small type. "I am an AFRICAN GIANT and will not be reduced to whatever that tiny writing means," he wrote on Instagram.[^ws-fader19] Later that year he had an album out called *African Giant*.[^ws-cnn19] It was nominated for Best World Music Album at the 2020 Grammys.[^ws-notjustok] The complaint about the small type had become the name of a record.

That's the pattern of this chapter. Coachella rarely invents things outright. It's where they get big, or get named, or get a price tag. Daft Punk's pyramid, the obvious first entry, has [its own chapter](/pyramid/).

## Reunions that became tours

The festival's reputation for reunions starts in 2004. The re-formed Pixies played the main stage before Radiohead and, in Rolling Stone's words, "nearly stole the show."[^ws-rs04] It wasn't their first show back. That was a warm-up added on a week's notice at the Fine Line in Minneapolis on April 13, 2004.[^ws-startrib] Coachella was the big public test, and they toured on it until late 2005.[^ws-rs04]

Then came the bigger ones.

**Rage Against the Machine, 2007.** Coachella confirmed the reunion on January 22, 2007. On April 29 the band closed the festival, their first full show in almost seven years, to "the biggest crowd of the weekend."[^ws-blabber07] Tom Morello's explanation: "the times were right to see if we can knock the Bush administration out in one fell swoop."[^ws-rsrage] By Wikipedia's count the tour that followed ran to July 2011, six legs and 50 shows on five continents.[^ws-wikirage] They were named 2020 headliners too. The pandemic took 2020 and 2021, the 2022 lineup didn't include them, and in January 2024 drummer Brad Wilk wrote that the band "will not be touring or playing live again."[^ws-loudwire22][^ws-fader24]

**Pulp and the Stone Roses.** Two dates get mixed up a lot. Pulp re-formed in 2011 for European shows. Their US return came in 2012, built around the two Coachella sets, with Radio City Music Hall on April 11 and the Warfield on April 17.[^ws-spin12] The Stone Roses re-formed in 2011 and played their giant UK shows in 2012. Coachella 2013, where they shared the top of the bill with Blur, was their first scheduled US date since.[^ws-bv13]

**Outkast, 2014.** Coachella was the first stop of a 20th-anniversary run of more than 40 festivals, with both Fridays to themselves. Big Boi: "It's truly an honor to celebrate 20 years and still be free to do music the way we choose."[^ws-billboard14]

**Guns N' Roses, 2016.** This is the reunion with the biggest receipt. Axl Rose, Slash and Duff McKagan played a $10 warm-up at the Troubadour in LA on April 1, 2016, then two nights at the new T-Mobile Arena in Las Vegas, then Coachella on April 16 and 23.[^ws-ucr16] The festival was the big unveiling. The tour, "Not in This Lifetime...," ran until November 2019 and grossed $584.2 million from 5,371,891 tickets. At the time, Billboard ranked it the third-highest-grossing tour in Boxscore history.[^ws-blabber19]

**2NE1, 2022.** During 88rising's main-stage set, CL brought out Dara, Park Bom and Minzy for "I AM THE BEST." It was the group's first performance together in six years.[^ws-kpopstarz] In July 2024 they announced a full reunion and a 15th-anniversary tour.[^ws-abscbn] The announcement doesn't credit Coachella. The timeline does the talking.

**Blink-182, 2023.** They were added to the bill late, and their April 14 set was the first reunion show with Tom DeLonge. Mark Hoppus, back from cancer treatment, posted: "Chemo to Coachella. Very much in my feelings today."[^ws-loudwire23] A world tour started that May, and a week after the reunion set they were closing weekend two in Frank Ocean's slot.[^ws-loudwire23]

**No Doubt, 2024.** Weekend one was the band's first show in nearly a decade, and Olivia Rodrigo joined them for "Bathwater." "I didn't think this was really going to happen, did you?" said Gwen Stefani.[^ws-fox13] It didn't stop at the polo field. In October 2025 they announced six nights at the Sphere in Las Vegas for May 2026, and the AV Club's report on it pointed straight back to Coachella.[^ws-avclub25]

## Two weekends, then everyone

In 2011 the festival sold out so fast that a lot of fans couldn't go. Paul Tollett's choice was to clone the festival instead of growing it. On May 31, 2011, he announced a second, separately ticketed weekend with the same lineup for 2012.[^ws-boombox] He gave three reasons. More people wanted in than the site could hold. He didn't want to "ruin the show" by adding about 40,000 people a day. And resale prices of $500-600 struck him as unfair.[^ws-boombox]

Austin City Limits went to two weekends in 2013. Music Festival Wizard's report described it as "following in the footsteps of mega-festival Coachella."[^ws-mfw] Goldenvoice reused the trick itself: Desert Trip, the 2016 classic-rock festival on the same field, added its second weekend after the first sold out.[^ws-cnbc16] The other thing the format produced, the running joke about weekend two, is covered in [the moments chapter](/moments/).

## The stream

In 2010, if you wanted to watch Coachella online, you had to "like" the sponsor, Wrigley, on Facebook, and you got one act at a time.[^ws-cnet] A year later YouTube streamed three stages on separate channels, about 60 bands over three days, and nearly 4 million people watched. Ad Age's Andrew Hampp estimated the sponsorship cost in the mid-to-high six figures.[^ws-cnet] Nine Inch Nails' Rob Sheridan summed up the logic: "An experience is the best kind of scarcity."[^ws-cnet]

Within three months YouTube had signed up to stream Lollapalooza and ACL as well.[^ws-ap11] Coachella kept setting the records. Beyoncé's 2018 set peaked at about 458,000 people watching at once, by TMZ's figures as The FADER reported them.[^ws-fader18] In September 2026 YouTube extended its exclusive global deal through 2030, both weekends. It said the 2026 stream had set its record for concurrent viewers, though it didn't give the number.[^ws-mbw]

## Holograms after Tupac

The 2012 Tupac was a Pepper's ghost on Mylar, not a hologram, and it's told in [the moments chapter](/moments/). Dr. Dre said it was "strictly for Coachella."[^ws-andscape] The idea didn't stay there.

In 2014, Hologram USA and Musion Das went to court to block a Michael Jackson projection at the Billboard Music Awards. They claimed patents on "the technology that brought Tupac to Coachella 2012." They lost.[^ws-spin14] In 2018, BASE Hologram put a projected Roy Orbison on a 28-date tour, with Tupac at Coachella explicitly cited as the precedent.[^ws-wsb18] People sometimes connect all this to ABBA Voyage. No source we found makes that link, and Voyage uses different technology, so it stays out.

## The Sahara and the boom

:::lore The tent that started the boom
The legend goes that Coachella's dance tent made American EDM. There's a lot of truth in it. The Sahara is almost as old as the festival, and its first bill was A-Trak, Juan Atkins, Kevin Saunderson and Richie Hawtin, out at the far edge of the grounds.[^ws-lat23] In 2010 deadmau5 played it on a Friday night opposite Jay-Z, with "bombastic lights and razor-edged electro-house."[^ws-laweekly10] The LA Times credits the tent with helping pop-EDM break wide open: Avicii, Skrillex, Calvin Harris.[^ws-lat23] In 2012 Swedish House Mafia became the first DJ group to headline the main stage.[^ws-fi22] By 2018 the tent had been moved to the middle of the field and made 25% bigger.[^ws-lat23]

The careful version comes from Festival Insider. Electronic music at Coachella "roared back" in 2011, it says, in a timeline it describes as "coinciding with" the national EDM boom.[^ws-fi22] Coinciding is a modest word for a 250-foot-wide stage.[^ws-lat23] Beatport, YouTube and social media did a lot of the pushing.[^ws-fi22] The Sahara was where the boom came to be seen, which is most of what a festival can do for anything.
:::

## Firsts on the main stage

**Bad Bunny.** At his 2019 debut, by Wikipedia's account of Billboard's report, he asked the crowd, "I want to know where the Latinos are!"[^ws-wiki19] In 2023 he came back as the first Latin artist to headline.[^ws-rs23] Three years later Karol G became the first Latina headliner.[^ws-remezcla] She closed the 2026 festival, with Becky G, Mariah Angeliq and Wisin as guests on weekend one.[^ws-fader26]

**BLACKPINK.** In 2019 they were the first K-pop girl group to play the festival. In 2023 they were the first K-pop group to be billed as headliners.[^ws-rs23] (Some outlets said "first Asian headliner." The careful sources say K-pop.) The 2022 lineup had already shown where things were going. 88rising's "Head in the Clouds Forever" was, per Billboard, "the first label-curated set ever to hit the festival's main stage," and it brought out Hikaru Utada, who had "never played a festival, even in Asia."[^ws-billboard22] It went the other way round from what people assume. 88rising's own festival, Head in the Clouds, came first, in 2018. Wikipedia notes that outlets nicknamed it the "Asian Coachella."[^ws-wikihitc]

**Afrobeats.** In 2019, Burna Boy and Mr Eazi were the only African acts on the bill.[^ws-quartz] Quartz noted that Wizkid had been booked the year before and didn't appear, citing visa trouble, and that Black Coffee's 2016 debut got him rebooked on a bigger stage in 2018.[^ws-quartz] Seven years after the small type, Wizkid and Tems came out during Justin Bieber's headline set.[^ws-rsca]

## The look

Coachella did not invent boho. CTV traces the look to Sienna Miller at Glastonbury in 2004 and to the runways of 2003-05: Chloé, Cavalli, Bottega Veneta.[^ws-ctv] What Coachella did was give it a name and a backdrop. By the mid-2000s, Dazed writes, celebrity attendees including Paris Hilton had made boho "Coachella fashion." Then Instagram arrived:

> "Coachella fashion was no longer just about the clothes. It was about content."[^ws-dazed26]

NYLON's year-by-year timeline runs from forehead headbands (2009) through "a sea of flower crowns," all-white crochet credited to "the Vanessa Hudgens effect" (2018) and florals "boosted by the Revolve Festival" (2019), to "cowboy cosplay" (2024).[^ws-nylon] In 2015 H&M made it official with what it called the "first ever co-branded collection" with the festival, in about 350 North American stores.[^ws-hm15]

The look also had a problem: faux Native American war bonnets. On April 9, 2014, Victoria's Secret model Alessandra Ambrosio posted a selfie in a feathered headdress, captioned as getting inspired for Coachella.[^ws-rock] The bans that followed came from other festivals. Coachella itself never adopted one, per CTV.[^ws-ctv]

- **Bass Coast** (British Columbia) posted the first widely reported ban, in July 2014. "Our core team felt it was time to take a stand."[^ws-cbc14]
- **Glastonbury** banned the *sale* of headdresses in October 2014, after a Change.org petition with 65 signatures.[^ws-dazed14]
- **Osheaga**, Heavy Montreal and ÎleSoniq followed in July 2015.[^ws-nme15]
- **Electric Forest** banned them from 2016.[^ws-da15]
- **Outside Lands** added them to its prohibited list in 2019, "out of respect for Native American heritage and culture."[^ws-sfist]

By 2019, CTV wrote, "the most offensive examples had largely disappeared from Coachella."[^ws-ctv]

## The influencers, and the brand houses

Heineken was the first big sponsor with branded experiences on site, in 2006, according to Hypebeast. H&M, Amex and Absolut followed, and around 2014-15 the influencers arrived with stylists and brand-paid houses. Hypebeast's verdict: "Coachella has become the influencer's Met Gala."[^ws-hypebeast23] By 2012, the first two-weekend year, BizBash listed a crowded weekend-one party circuit: Lacoste pool parties, the Guess Hotel in La Quinta, Neon Carnival in a hangar in Thermal. The brands stuck to weekend one for the media and the feeling of being there first.[^ws-bizbash12]

The biggest player was Revolve. It first flew influencers to the festival in 2013.[^ws-bof] Its own Revolve Festival started in 2015 at the Avalon in Palm Springs, according to WWD; Business of Fashion dates it to 2016.[^ws-wwd][^ws-bof] By 2022 it was a two-day invite-only festival with headliners like Cardi B, SZA and Travis Scott, peaking at about 5,000 guests. BoF's headline put it plainly: "Revolve invented influencer marketing as we know it."[^ws-bof] The season was worth real money. Revolve "typically earns about 30% of its yearly revenue in the weeks around Coachella," Retail Brew reported in 2020, citing the New York Times.[^ws-retailbrew]

Then the pullback. In 2019 Sephora, H&M, Urban Decay, Too Faced, Kiehl's and Maybelline scaled back or dropped their VIP experiences. One consultant told Glossy: "A $1,000 VIP wristband doesn't equate to a social campaign that is usually more than $100,000."[^ws-glossy19] In 2022 Revolve's own invited guests were left waiting for shuttles in the heat, and the internet called it Fyre Fest. LA Magazine's Joseph Kapsch described being "stranded in the dirt with no water, under the hot sun for hours." Revolve apologized.[^ws-hypebae] By 2024 the festival was down to one day and about 1,200 guests.[^ws-bof] Big branded venues shrank. Influencer budgets, Dazed reported in 2026, did not, and the top ones reportedly run from "high six figures to over a million dollars."[^ws-dazed26]

## "-chella"

The linguist Arnold Zwicky files "-chella" as a libfix, a piece of a word that broke off and now means "festival" on its own. His examples include #Bookchella for the LA Times Festival of Books.[^ws-zwicky] Fans coined Beychella in 2018, and Dictionary.com logged Arichella for Ariana Grande a year later.[^ws-dictionary]

Goldenvoice's lawyers have kept busy with it. The festival sued Hoodchella, an underground LA one-dayer, in 2016, seeking an injunction and more than $100K.[^ws-hypebot16] Brokechella, a DIY LA festival started in 2011 as a "bargain alternative," became BROKE LA in January 2016. "We got too big and *they* wouldn't let us be Brokechella anymore," its organizer said, without naming who "they" were.[^ws-buzzbands16] In Wales, a Cowbridge festival called Cowchella became Moo-La-La after a complaint from Coachella, according to a UK promotions trade blog. Its founder, Ian "H" Watkins of Steps, called himself "a threat to their ginormous, juggernaut of a machine."[^ws-promo] In 2025 a dog-friendly festival got a cease-and-desist, which World Trademark Review called "just the latest" over the suffix.[^ws-wtr] Some got away with it. In October 2023, a pub in Collie, Western Australia, held Col-Chella, a one-day festival with a stage on the back of a truck.[^ws-dailyliberal]

## Paying for it

In 2009, in the middle of a recession, Coachella offered a layaway plan. It came back for 2010 with a choice of 50% or 10% down, open to buyers through February 15, with the balance taken automatically in scheduled installments.[^ws-ticketnews] By 2025 about 60% of the crowd was using the payment plan, according to a Billboard report as relayed by Entrepreneur.[^ws-entrepreneur]

The other direction was up. By April 2010 you could reserve a "Coachella Safari Tent": a queen bed, air conditioning, power outlets, VIP wristbands, a shuttle, backstage access, a 2 a.m. curfew and one case of beer per person.[^ws-urbandaddy] Palm Springs Life later counted 100 VIP safari tents at about $6,500 a weekend for two.[^ws-psl] By 2026 the ladder ran from $160 car camping through GA at $649 for weekend one to Lake Eldorado lodges at $2,300 and tents at $3,000.[^ws-caltoday] Nobody we read credits Coachella with inventing glamping. It did sell it early, and it turned it into a product line.

## A family of festivals

Goldenvoice used Coachella's field, crew and name to start a run of other festivals, each with a narrower audience.

- **Stagecoach**, the country festival on the same grounds the weekend after, started in 2007, per Wikipedia.[^ws-wikistage] In 2024 it sold 89,003 tickets, more than either Coachella weekend that year.[^ws-kesq24]
- **Desert Trip**, October 2016: Dylan, the Stones, McCartney, Neil Young, The Who and Roger Waters, nicknamed "Oldchella." Tollett told the New York Times it grossed about $160M, "the highest-grossing show by far."[^ws-ucrdt][^ws-cnbc16]
- **Power Trip**, October 2023: the same format for metal, with Iron Maiden, Guns N' Roses, Judas Priest, AC/DC, Tool and Metallica.[^ws-louder]
- **Just Like Heaven** (2019), a lineup of Coachella alumni, and **Cruel World**, the goth and new wave one, which Wikipedia dates to 2022 after a cancelled 2020 debut.[^ws-buzzbands21][^ws-wikicruel]

Goldenvoice's own festivals page now lists more than a dozen, from Portola in San Francisco to Head in the Clouds and Splash House.[^ws-gv] It didn't invent the nostalgia festival. InsideHook points to All Tomorrow's Parties as an earlier example. It did help turn the idea into an industry.[^ws-insidehook]

## The food

Roy Choi's Kogi truck came to Coachella around 2009, and UPROXX credits it with helping push festival food toward chef-driven vendors.[^ws-uproxx] The festival's real food push came later, and it started rough. In 2014 Coachella brought in LA chefs including Josef Centeno and Kris Yenbamroong of Night + Market. Yenbamroong said he threw away about 75% of his food: "people just weren't into it."[^ws-laweekly15] A year later the festival had $225 Outstanding in the Field dinners in the Rose Garden.[^ws-laweekly15] By 2016 those cost $225 on their own, $600 with GA or $1,124 with VIP.[^ws-timeout16]

Other festivals took up the idea. KAABOO's CEO, Jason Felts: "Chefs are the new rock stars."[^ws-uproxx] Coachella wasn't first, though. LA Weekly noted in 2015 that Outside Lands and Jazz Fest already ranked food just behind music, and that Coachella ranked it lower.[^ws-laweekly15] What Coachella did was give the chefs top billing and put the dinners in a garden.
