title: The billboards on the I-10
short: Every spring, artists and labels rent billboards on the interstate between Los Angeles and Indio, because there is one road in and the people driving it are exactly the people they want. The boards cost from about $8K to $40K for a few weeks, and one 2026 campaign reportedly ran to $1M. This is a sourced, year-by-year archive of them, from the first one on record in 2014 to the 20 that were booked for 2020 and never went up.
---

In April 2022, a few days before the festival that had dropped him as a headliner, Travis Scott's label put up a sequence of billboards along the I-10. The first said "Psst..." The next said "Looking for UTOPIA?" The third said "Wrong way!"[^bb-complex22] Scott had been removed from the lineup after Astroworld. The boards were his way onto the road anyway.[^bb-complex22]

That is the whole idea in three signs. The festival has a lineup. The highway has a second one.

## Why the desert

Coachella's own driving directions funnel almost everyone onto the I-10 into the valley.[^bb-directions] Gino Sesto, whose agency DASH TWO has sold Coachella boards since about 2015, put it simply to Billboard:

> "It's kind of a perfect storm... you have one way in."[^bb-bbpro]

Sam Keywanfar, whose company MilkMoney supplied billboard content for 26 advertisers that year, said the festivalgoer is "a very hard-to-reach demographic," and the drive is the chance to catch them "in the early part of the journey."[^bb-bbpro] Ian Dallimore of Lamar, one of the big billboard owners on the route, said artists get to advertise "on the road leading up to" the festival without advertising at it, and called a 14-by-48-foot roadside board "the biggest flex."[^bb-bbpro]

The real target is the phone. Keywanfar again:

> "When fans find a billboard they are drawn to and share it, that's the holy grail."[^bb-bbpro]

The same Billboard piece describes artists stopping on the highway to take selfies with their own boards, and non-music brands, from Florence by Mills to the Elton John film *Rocketman*, following them onto the route the next year.[^bb-bbpro]

## Who owns them, who books them

Two owners come up again and again on this stretch. Outfront's own blog describes the I-10 run as "lined on either side with OUTFRONT billboards."[^bb-outfront] Billboard calls Lamar "one of the top billboard operators on this stretch."[^bb-bbpro] Lamar also says it has had a Palm Springs-area operation since 2002; that is the company's own claim.[^bb-lamar]

The artists and labels mostly don't call them directly. They go through agencies and marketplaces: DASH TWO, a Los Angeles out-of-home agency; MilkMoney, which makes the content; and AdQuick, an online billboard marketplace, which says the inventory "is fragmented across multiple outdoor media owners" with no public map.[^bb-bbpro][^bb-adquick]

## What it costs

The published prices, in order:

:::numbers The going rate
- 2019: $8,000-$20,000 for four weeks, sold out by January, with I-10 prices up 31% in late March and early April[^bb-oaaa19]
- 2019: about $3M booked through AdQuick alone from February to April, across 378 boards it evaluated[^bb-bbinsider19]
- About 2022: $15,000-$40,000 per board, per MilkMoney's Keywanfar[^bb-bbpro]
- 2025-26: $10,000-$18,000 for four weeks on a standard board near Indio or La Quinta, $20,000 or more for digital boards or the Banning Pass and Cabazon, with a festival premium of 25-40%, per DASH TWO[^bb-dash25]
- 2026: Addison Rae's campaign reportedly cost about $1M, per AdQuick[^bb-adquick]
- 2026: about $900 to replace one vandalized vinyl panel[^bb-nbcps26]
:::

Book early, the brokers say: six to nine months ahead, according to DASH TWO, or four to six, according to AdQuick.[^bb-dash25][^bb-adquick]

## Where they are

The boards cluster where the road narrows. Billboard describes about 50 miles of I-10 from Riverside to Indio, and Sesto estimated about 75 boards in the 23 miles from Banning to Palm Springs.[^bb-bbpro] OOH Today, using StreetMetrics data, counted more than 50 boards between Beaumont and Indio through the San Gorgonio Pass, and measured about 30% more impressions on them during each festival weekend of 2025.[^bb-oohtoday]

DASH TWO's own map of the route, which is a sales pitch and should be read as one, starts with the Banning Pass and Cabazon as the first boards drivers from LA and Orange County see, then the split where Highway 111 peels off toward Palm Springs, then the final mile near the polo club.[^bb-dash25] The wind turbines are a landmark in their own right; in 2026 Slayyyter staged a bunny-suit stunt there.[^bb-adquick]

There is also a different kind of board off the interstate. The art biennial Desert X has put billboard works in the valley, starting in 2017 with a work by Jennifer Bolande on the road into Palm Springs.[^bb-dx] The season chapter covers those.

## How it started

No one has documented a "first" Coachella billboard. The earliest found for this archive is in a DASH TWO blog post from January 2015, which shows the band Miami Horror "standing proudly in front of their billboard from last year," meaning 2014.[^bb-dash15] The same post says only "a handful of billboards" were left for the 2015 festival.[^bb-dash15] That is an agency talking about its own work, so it carries a low confidence label below.

Billboard's version is that the boards "first appeared about six years" before its piece, when labels started aiming at festivalgoers and at the performers driving the same road.[^bb-bbpro] The piece is undated, but it treats 2020 and 2021 as past and 2022 as current, which puts the start at about 2016. By 2019, the outdoor-ad trade body OAAA was writing up Billie Eilish's minimal text boards on the I-10.[^bb-oaaa19]

As for how many boards there are, AdQuick tracked 485 on the LA-to-festival route in 2018, 533 in 2019 and 548 in 2022.[^bb-bbpro] Those count every board on the route, not just festival campaigns.

## 2020: the boards that never went up

On March 10, 2020, Goldenvoice moved the April festival to October.[^bb-timeout20] In June, Riverside County cancelled it.[^bb-kesq20]

DASH TWO had booked 20 Coachella billboards for 2020. Sesto told Billboard every one was cancelled.[^bb-bbpro] Which artists booked them, and whether any creative actually went up that spring, are both unknown. This project found no photo or report of a festival board standing on the I-10 with no festival behind it.

The industry around them fell hard. Out-of-home advertising revenue was down 45% year over year in the second quarter of 2020.[^bb-bbinsider20] Outfront's revenue for the year fell 30.6%.[^bb-outfront8k] Lamar's fell 10.5%.[^bb-advocate]

## 2022: the comeback

When the festival came back, so did the boards, unevenly. DASH TWO booked 15, five fewer than its cancelled 2020 total. "There's a lot of skepticism still," Sesto said.[^bb-bbpro] Lamar sold 66 Coachella spots, against 64 in 2019, and Dallimore was upbeat.[^bb-bbpro]

> "We're seeing a lot of this business come back bigger, because people feel more comfortable."[^bb-bbpro]

There were fewer faces to go around. Before the pandemic, local car dealers and casinos with year-round contracts sublet their boards to Coachella advertisers at a discount. After the 2020-21 cancellations, they stopped putting those boards in the deal.[^bb-bbpro]

Scott's "Wrong way!" set the tone for what came next: boards built to be photographed, not just read. In 2025, a board reading "KIKI BOY 2025" went up with no explanation, and Rolling Stone asked whether Frank Ocean was teasing something.[^bb-rs25] Not every board is welcome. In 2024, Goldenvoice demanded that the AIDS Healthcare Foundation take down its "Catch More Than Vibes?" board on the westbound I-10.[^bb-nbcps24] And in 2026, enough boards were vandalized that officials planned stronger security for them.[^bb-nbcps26]

## How this archive works

The list below is generated from the archive's data, one entry per board or campaign, grouped by year. The rules:

- **Every board has a source.** Each entry says who reported it, what it showed, where it stood and what it promoted, as far as the sources say. Gaps are left as gaps.
- **Every board has a confidence label.** High, medium or low, with a note on why. A board seen in several independent reports is high. An agency's own blog post, with no date or location, is low.
- **Photos are credited and linked.** Official posts by artists, labels and billboard companies are embedded from where they were posted. Fan photos are used only with the photographer's written permission and credit. Nothing is downloaded and rehosted without permission. Where no image can be used, a text card describes the board and links to the source.
- **What gets hosted here.** Only three kinds of image: photos taken from the road by Richard Lathrop (marked "From the road", with the date taken), Creative Commons photos with their author and license, and brand or label press-kit images. Everything else stays where it was posted, linked with credit. Boards known from the press or social posts with no usable photo yet are in the still-needed list at the bottom.
- **Street View is cited, not copied.** Google's terms don't allow Street View screenshots for any purpose.[^bb-geo] So where an old Street View capture shows a board, the entry links to that view and gives Google's capture date, such as "Apr 2019," and the date it was checked. Street View is corroboration, not proof.
- **It is updated every spring.** In 2026, The Desert Sun photographed the boards on March 31, ten days before the first weekend.[^bb-desertsun26]

Instagram roundups collect the boards every year too. They are a good place to look, but a roundup alone isn't enough for a high confidence label.

**Send a sighting.** Seen a board that isn't here, or have a better photo of one that is? Email hello@thirdpolararch.com with the photo, where you saw it (road, direction, nearest exit) and when. Say whether you'd like to be credited, and how. Either is fine.
