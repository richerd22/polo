Coachella names its tents after deserts, and nobody on record has said why. Mojave, Gobi and Sahara were there in October 1999, three "rave tents" beside a main stage and a second stage.[^st-psl] Yuma joined in 2013 and Sonora in 2017.[^st-da13][^st-laist17] Since then the festival has added a dance room with no desert in its name, a stage for three-hour sets, a dome, a beer house and a stage that books itself.

:::lore The desert names
The festival's tent names make a tidy set: Sahara, Mojave, Gobi, Yuma, Sonora. Every guide repeats that they're named for deserts, and Wikipedia confirms the desert sources for the first four.[^st-wiki] What nobody has published is the meeting where someone said "let's name them after deserts." No interview with Paul Tollett or anyone at Goldenvoice on the subject turned up in this research. Two later rooms seem to break the rule. Quasar does. The Antarctic, a projection dome from 2017, may not: by Britannica's count, the largest desert on Earth is the Antarctic Ice Sheet.[^st-brit] Whether whoever named the dome knew that is another thing nobody has said. If you know who named the Sahara, the corrections address is in the footer.
:::

## Three tents and a polo field

The 1999 Sahara was a dance tent from the first day. Its opening bill included A-Trak, Juan Atkins, Kevin Saunderson and Richie Hawtin, and it was pushed to the far edge of the grounds, beside what later became the Rose Garden VIP area, with only a few light panels.[^st-lat23] That first weekend was hot enough that Kid Koala's records started to warp on the turntables.[^st-ringer19]

The two open-air stages are a smaller mystery. Palm Springs Life's account of 1999 lists a "main stage" and a "second stage."[^st-psl] Wikipedia says the Outdoor Theatre has been in use since the start, next to the main stage, and its lineup tables use the name from early on without saying when it was first used.[^st-wiki] So the second stage of 1999 is probably the Outdoor Theatre under a plainer name, but nothing found here says so outright.

## The main stage and the Outdoor Theatre

The main stage, now the Coachella Stage, is the one on the posters and the livestream thumbnails. In 2013 it was built by Brown United on six legs, and the stagehands called it "Optimus Brown." It carried about 165,000 pounds of gear on a structure rated for 500,000.[^st-plsn13] Rat Sound has handled the audio almost from the start - 18 of the first 19 years, as of 2018 - and that year the main stage field alone had 168 L-Acoustics K2 cabinets.[^st-foh18]

The Outdoor Theatre sits next to it, and it is the stage people mean when they say "sunset set."[^st-discotech] In 2013 it had a 65 x 45 ft roof and sliding trusses so one act's lights could roll off while the next act's rolled on.[^st-plsn13] It also has the strangest second life of any stage on the grounds. When the country festival Stagecoach takes over the polo fields after Coachella, its main stage goes up on the Outdoor Theatre site, according to a 2014 report.[^st-soundgirls]

## The Sahara keeps moving

The Sahara is the tent that changed the festival, and it has spent most of its life being moved around the grounds.

In 2002 Coachella expanded the Sahara's production and bookings, with Hawtin, Moby, Paul Oakenfold, Tiësto and Pete Tong.[^st-fi22] In 2006 it held the show the next chapter is about, plus a surprise Madonna set the following day; fans now date the American dance boom to that weekend.[^st-lat23] In 2011 Odd Future played one of the tent's first live, non-DJ sets.[^st-lat23]

By 2013 the Sahara was 80 ft tall.[^st-ds24] PLSN described it as a hangar holding about 20,000 people, built and lit by Andrew Gumper's AG Lighting and Sound.[^st-plsn13] Then it started to travel:

- **Until 2018:** near the Rose Garden at Empire Polo Club, its home for years.[^st-ds24]
- **2018:** onto the newly bought Eldorado Polo Club land next door, by the entrance, built 25% bigger.[^st-ds24] The LA Times gives the result as nearly 100 ft tall with a 250-ft stage, rated for 90 mph winds, and anchored to permanent concrete footings about two feet under the grass.[^st-lat23]
- **2024:** south again, to land between Avenue 51 and Polo Road that Goldenvoice's Jason Brown called a "whole new area of the venue that we've never been into before."[^st-ds24]

The 2024 version is not a tent in any camping sense. The old frame was aluminum; the new one is steel, which Brown said allows "more weight capacity and production elements." Work started in December. Crews poured about 156 truckloads of concrete, roughly 1,500 tons, into footings underground, and worked overnight so the trucks would not clog Indio's streets.[^st-ds24]

How big is it? The Desert Sun gives 320 ft long.[^st-ds24] Elation, the lighting maker that supplied the rig, gives 400 x 410 ft, nearly 100 ft tall, on 16 steel ribs and 40 ft wider than the old one.[^st-elation] The two may be measuring different things. Either way, it hung 956 Elation fixtures, designed by Todd Roberts of Production Design Works, and ran four headliner-level acts a day with changeovers as short as 10 to 15 minutes.[^st-elation]

The move came with a new grass hill and a "Sahara Express Walkway," and some fans said the walk from the main stage took about 15 minutes.[^st-ad24] The tent's old ground did not stay empty. That is where Quasar went.[^st-mixmagasia]

## Mojave and Gobi

The two other 1999 tents are the quieter siblings. The Mojave is the second-largest tent and books indie rock and rising acts; the Gobi is the smallest of the classic tents, shaded and rarely packed.[^st-discotech] In 2013 the Mojave hung motion-controlled "noodles" - long tubes that moved and doubled as a 3D projection surface - from a 60 x 40 ft truss grid, while the Gobi used a 60 x 40 ft Versa Stage with a roof.[^st-plsn13]

According to Wikipedia, both moved behind an access road in 2017, and in 2018 the Mojave was enlarged and moved into the Sahara's old spot near the Rose Garden.[^st-wiki] From 2015 they also started booking more electronic acts, as dance music spread out of the Sahara.[^st-fi22]

Every spring the tents also change jobs. When Stagecoach takes over the grounds, the Mojave becomes the Palomino, Yuma becomes Diplo's Honkytonk, Sonora becomes the air-conditioned Bud Light Backyard, and the Gobi becomes the Yellowstone Dutton Ranch.[^st-lat24]

## Yuma: a nightclub in a tent

Yuma opened in 2013 as the sixth stage, in the terrace food area near the Heineken Dome.[^st-plsn13] Dancing Astronaut called it "the mysterious new Yuma Tent" and found an indoor nightclub inside: wood floors, disco balls, fake Baroque portraits, couches and air conditioning. Four Tet opened it with a 90-minute improvised set, followed by DJ Harvey, Seth Troxler and Luciano.[^st-da13]

The idea, per Wikipedia, was a room that "dials down the noise and strobe lights" for underground acts.[^st-wiki] Its longtime lighting designer, Steve Lieberman, kept it that way: lights only, no video wall, dark and minimal.[^st-lsi25] In a festival built around LED screens the size of buildings, the room's selling point is that you can barely see.

## Sonora: the punk tent

Sonora arrived in 2017, not 2016 or 2018 as some lists say. LAist announced it with the headline "New stage, who dis?" and a rock-leaning bill with a "bilingual slant": Allah-Las, T.S.O.L., Guided By Voices and Downtown Boys.[^st-laist17] It is fully enclosed and club-sized, and its booker, Rene Contreras, put the brief simply.[^st-cvi25]

> "We just wanted to have an intimate space for guitar bands." - Rene Contreras[^st-cvi25]

It has since booked rock, hardcore, early hip-hop and Latin acts, from Los Mirlos to The Linda Lindas, Viagra Boys, PUP and Los Bitchos.[^st-cvi25]

## Do LaB: the stage that let itself in

Do LaB is the best story on the grounds, because nobody planned it.

It is run by three brothers, Dede, Josh and Jesse Flemming; Josh and Jesse are twins. Jesse books the music, Josh designs the structures and Dede runs production and deals with Goldenvoice.[^st-law25] They started with renegade parties in the late 1990s, going back to a birthday party in the mountains in 1999.[^st-ew23][^st-poll24] A downtown LA warehouse they ran was shut down by police and fire officials, which Josh says is what made them go legal.[^st-npr22]

### The broken water feature

Sources argue about the first year. Variety and NPR say 2004; the brothers' own accounts to The FADER, Pollstar and Entertainment Weekly say 2005, when a friend who curated art for the festival invited them to build something.[^st-var23][^st-npr22][^st-fader25][^st-poll24] The likeliest reading is that 2004 is when the company and its own festival began, and 2005 is when it first came to Coachella.

What they built in 2005 was a dome with a water feature that did not work very well.[^st-fader25]

> "Everybody was showering in it and drinking from it." - Josh Flemming[^st-fader25]

The wind mangled everything they set up on the first day.[^st-fader25] The budget was tiny; in 107-degree heat they misted people and handed out orange slices.[^st-poll24] Then, around midnight, the main music shut off. The brothers kept playing, and the crowd walked over - roughly a thousand people, by EW's account.[^st-fader25][^st-ew23] Jesse says that was the first time they saw it "could be more than an installation."[^st-fader25]

In 2006 Paul Tollett overbooked two DJs and parked them in the Do LaB dome, with extra sound and lights. Dede called it "the green light for us to go for it and actually play music."[^st-fader25]

### An inch, then a mile

From there it grew, one negotiation at a time. Dede describes 2008 as the year they went to 360-degree environments: "they give us an inch, we take a mile."[^st-fader25] In 2009 Tollett asked them to take their sign down, because Coachella had no branding on site at all; they talked him into keeping it, which made it one of the first branded things at the festival.[^st-fader25] By 2010 a performance stage was pushing it toward "more of a stage and less of an art installation."[^st-fader25] In 2011 Josh's Calatrava-inspired "fish fry" structure was, in his words, their lowest point; it "barely stood up."[^st-fader25]

In 2014 it moved to a corner with a backstage, and the brothers started calling it "the official sixth stage at Coachella."[^st-fader25] The festival still does not put Do LaB on the set-time grid. It lists it on the poster, in small type, as an art installation, and the lineup comes out separately on Do LaB's own channels, usually in late March.[^st-var23] Dede called the tiny poster credit a "badge of honor."[^st-ew23]

Water stayed the theme. Misters, water cannons and performers with flower-covered water guns have been cooling the crowd since the start; as Dede put it, "we knew how intense it was out here."[^st-law25][^st-npr22] By 2022 the structure was a 200 x 200 ft tent of colored fabric triangles holding about 10,000 people at night.[^st-npr22] In 2025 the stage ran on battery power, according to the festival.[^st-coachsus]

### The surprise sets

Do LaB books itself. Jesse runs it apart from the festival-wide booking, and the aim is to "catch artists right before they're about to pop."[^st-law25] Dede: "We're not trying to sell tickets."[^st-var23] Closing slots are sometimes booked hours before they start.[^st-var23]

The surprises began, Jesse says, because "we left it open to see what would happen."[^st-edmh23] The first big one was Bonobo in 2015, the first major artist from outside their circle of friends, and the reaction made them leave a couple of open slots every year after.[^st-fader25] Since then:

- **2017:** Kaskade was booked as the surprise and tweeted about it. Security said the area could not hold the crowd that would follow, so the slot went to Skrillex instead.[^st-fader25]
- **2018:** FISHER and Chris Lake drew the biggest afternoon crowds Do LaB had seen, and both credit those sets with lifting their careers.[^st-fader25]
- **2019:** Major Lazer, with Diplo.[^st-edmtunes19]
- **2022:** Diplo again, plus Rebecca Black and MEUTE.[^st-npr22]
- **2024:** Billie Eilish, two years after she headlined Coachella.[^st-rs22] Josh's estimate: "It must've been over 20,000 people," the biggest crowd the stage has had. Anderson .Paak played a surprise set as DJ Pee .Wee the same year.[^st-fader25]

The run has had one bad night. On Friday, April 10, 2026, Do LaB closed after a light reportedly fell on a woman during a surprise John Summit set, and later sets were cancelled; at the time Mixmag reported it, details came from attendees and there was no official statement.[^st-mixmag26]

### Lightning in a Bottle

Do LaB also runs its own festival. Lightning in a Bottle took its name from a friend's private July birthday party in the early 2000s, and Wikipedia's history dates the first event under that name to 2004 in the Angeles National Forest.[^st-wikilib] Other outlets count from 2006, the first three-day public edition.[^st-ew23][^st-dt15] It moved around California for years, and per Wikipedia has been at Buena Vista Lake in Kern County since 2019.[^st-wikilib]

The two festivals run back to back. The brothers start building Lightning in a Bottle about five days after Coachella's second weekend.[^st-poll24] In 2024 Jesse told Pollstar the independent festival was up against Live Nation, AEG and their radius clauses - the contract terms that stop artists playing nearby festivals.[^st-poll24] It is still running: the 2026 edition was set for May 20-24 at Buena Vista Lake, with Empire of the Sun, Zeds Dead and Chase & Status.[^st-bww25]

## Quasar and the Red Bull Mirage

Quasar is the newest stage, and it exists because 60 to 90 minutes is not long enough for some DJs. Goldenvoice announced it in March 2024 as a stage for sets of three hours or more, on both weekends.[^st-djmag24] Its designer, Heather Shaw of Vita Motus - who also helped design Do LaB and has been to every Coachella since 1999 - links the idea to 2023, when Fred again.., Skrillex and Four Tet stepped in for Frank Ocean, and to the problem that 60 to 90 minutes is too short for a full arc.[^st-ew24]

Shaw heard about the project at the end of January 2024, which gave her under four months to build it, the tightest timeline of her career.[^st-ew24] The result is two asymmetric LED walls showing a live Unreal Engine model of the Coachella landscape, rendered in real time.[^st-ew24] It sits on the Sahara's former ground.[^st-mixmagasia] The 2024 bookings set the format: Honey Dijon with Green Velvet, a Jamie xx, Floating Points and Daphni b3b, and a sunset set from Eric Prydz b2b Anyma.[^st-djmag24][^st-mixmagasia]

In 2025 Red Bull built a place to watch it from. KTLA described the Red Bull Mirage as a two-story, 20,000-square-foot installation near Quasar, with bars and shade, open to any GA wristband.[^st-ktla25] By 2026 Flaunt was calling it three tiers, with DJ sets, a daytime happy hour and a Nobu omakase dinner on the Saturday; the top level was mostly kept for artists and guests.[^st-flaunt26] Whether it grew a floor between years, or the two outlets counted differently, is not clear.

## Despacio: seven stacks and one disco ball

Despacio is a sound system, not a stage, and it is the opposite of everything else at the festival. James Murphy of LCD Soundsystem and David and Stephen Dewaele of Soulwax built it with studio designer John Klett, who dates the first conversations to around May 2010.[^st-djmagdesp] It made its debut at the Manchester International Festival in July 2013: three vinyl-only nights, seven 11-ft speaker stacks around the room, and nearly 50,000 watts.[^st-mcintosh13] It is vinyl only because, its makers say, the system "would definitely notice" anything else.[^st-djmagdesp]

It came to Coachella in 2016, in its own tent, with the stacks arranged around a circular lighting rig and a big disco ball. On April 22, the day after Prince died, they played "Purple Rain."[^st-musictech]

It came back in 2023, open 3 to 9 p.m. both weekends, with 44 McIntosh amplifiers, about 950 people on the floor, a target of 105 dB, and the disco ball as the only visual.[^st-groove]

## The Antarctic

The one room named for a cold place was a dome. In 2017 Goldenvoice put up a 120-ft geodesic projection dome, billed as the largest in the world, built by Obscura with Pacific Domes. It had 15 Christie projectors, 108 L-Acoustics speakers in three tiers and an eight-minute, 360-degree program called "Chrysalis," for 500 people at a time.[^st-ld17] In 2019 the air-conditioned dome came back as YouTube's 360 dance dome, and the Antarctic program returned with it.[^st-pacdomes19]

## Heineken House

Heineken has been Coachella's official beer since 2002.[^st-talents14][^st-prn19] Before the House there were the Heineken Domes; PLSN used one as a landmark in 2013.[^st-plsn13] In 2014 Heineken House replaced them, with sets from Skrillex, Outkast, Lorde and Girl Talk, Snapchat clues to secret performers, and beer lockers opened by fingerprint.[^st-talents14] In 2019 it moved, got a new structure and its first 21-plus beer garden, and booked De La Soul and The Roots.[^st-prn19] By 2025 it was air-conditioned, with Pusha T and Afrojack.[^st-edmid25]

## Retired, temporary and never built

Some rooms did not last.

- **The Oasis Dome:** an open-air dome "built into a makeshift tree," with 360-degree sound; Breakage played it in 2011.[^st-mxdwn11] Pacific Domes describes a 44-ft dome with misters overhead.[^st-pacdomes]
- **The Heineken Domes:** replaced by Heineken House in 2014.[^st-talents14]
- **Despacio and the Antarctic:** pop-ups that come back when someone wants them to, so far in 2016 and 2023, and 2017 and 2019.[^st-musictech][^st-ld17][^st-pacdomes19]
- **The Kanye dome:** for 2019, Kanye West reportedly wanted a huge dome in the middle of the grounds. Goldenvoice said no, partly because it would have meant moving stages and a toilet area tied to sewer and water lines, and Ariana Grande took the slot within 24 hours. That account comes from Billboard's reporting, via Digital Music News; neither side confirmed it.[^st-dmn19]
