short: Camping began in 2003 and cars joined in 2010; now about 35,000 people sleep on the grounds. The festival starts for them on Thursday - in a Walmart lot, a Ralphs lot, a 12-hour line, and since 2025 at Goldenvoice's own campers-only party, Day 0. Along the way: a gospel service on a hill built in a week, horses, hot dogs, an iron fence and a wristband scam.

At 1 a.m. on the Thursday of Coachella 2026, one of the best parties in Indio was in the parking lot of a Ralphs. There were porta-potties, cars painted for the Carpoolchella contest, people doing their last grocery run, and a DJ set by NDA. Nobody official organized it. Word spread on the Coachella subreddit, and it has happened "for the past few years."[^cp-cvi26] The campground opened eight hours later.

For the roughly 35,000 people who camp,[^cp-sustain] Coachella doesn't start with a headliner. It starts with a line, a lot and a Thursday. This chapter is the festival before the festival.

## Tents first, cars later

Camping came early, but not at the start. Wikipedia, citing coverage from the time, says Indio approved overnight camping for 2003, for up to four people on each of 2,252 spots.[^cp-wiki] An OC Weekly writer's history of the campgrounds backs the timing: 2004 "was the second year camping was permitted, changing the entire dynamic of the festival."[^cp-ocw18] Her account of that era is a party where nobody slept.[^cp-ocw18]

Cars arrived with the 2010 festival. Before that, you parked and hauled your gear to the tent area. From 2010 you could pitch beside your car on a 10-by-30-foot space, or just sleep in it.[^cp-pnt10] The same year brought a farmers market by the general store, a roller rink called the Down & Derby, and $5,000 "Safari" tents with air conditioning, showers and golf-cart shuttles.[^cp-bizbash10]

The 2026 menu runs from plain car camping up through Preferred, Front Row and Powered sites (one outlet), group sites new that year, pre-pitched tents, temperature-controlled La Campana tents with private showers, and Lake Eldorado, with its own check-in, concierge and restrooms.[^cp-camping] For 2027, a car site is $160 ($40 a night), and Lake Eldorado runs $2,300 to $3,000.[^cp-passes]

The rules shape the weekend. Camping opens at 9 a.m. Thursday and closes at 9 a.m. Monday. Once your car is parked, it doesn't move. From 9 p.m. to 2 a.m., Friday to Sunday, no vehicle leaves - the "lockdown." Groups in the better tiers must "Arrive Together to Camp Together," driving through the toll booth and the search as one, and saving spots isn't allowed. Quiet hours start at 1:30 a.m.[^cp-camping] The one loophole is new: the 2026 Group Car Camping tier, 10 to 20 spots in Lot 5A, lets members arrive separately.[^cp-camping]

Then it's a small town for four days. The general store in Lot 8 sells Panera bread delivered every morning, plus donuts, sandwiches, fruit and ice. The farmers market, run by Flutterby Ranch, sells produce and live butterflies. Food trucks open at 2 p.m. Thursday, a free shuttle runs from Lot 7 to the supermarket, and ice comes to you on wheels.[^cp-camping] There's a lounge with Wi-Fi.[^cp-amen] Nobody is roughing it, exactly, but nobody is moving their car either.

## The expensive end

At the top, camping stops being camping. The 2010 Safari tents cost $5,000.[^cp-bizbash10] A Palm Springs Life feature, undated, put a Safari weekend at about $6,500, with two wristbands, golf-cart shuttles, air-conditioned tents, a marquee with breakfast and a DJ, a concierge, and $1,500 for each extra guest.[^cp-psl] For 2026, one guide put a Safari cabana at "upwards of $10,000."[^cp-forty5]

Some of the tents are built by the same company that runs the shuttles. CID Entertainment, which Valley Music Travel's own site names as its parent,[^cp-vmtabout] told the Philadelphia Inquirer in 2015 that at Coachella it would "build you a 14-by-14-foot safari tent with full amenities on site."[^cp-inquirer] Your bus and your bed can share an owner.

## The night before

The reason for the waiting is in the rules. Cars and campers are searched at check-in, and sites are handed out in order of arrival within each tier.[^cp-camping] Show up first, camp closest. Check-in isn't always quick: a long-time camper told OC Weekly in 2018 that "there were only about 4 workers directing everyone where to park."[^cp-ocw18] So people arrive early and wait somewhere. Where they wait has become its own tradition.

The first famous version was an accident. On weekend 2 of 2018, a high-wind warning, with gusts forecast to 60 mph, pushed camping from 9 a.m. Thursday to about 3 a.m. Friday - roughly 18 hours. Thousands of people waited in a Walmart lot in Indio with folding chairs and portable barbecues, and tagged it #Walmartchella. Indio officers reportedly joined in a beanbag game.[^cp-cbs18]

In La Quinta, next door, it went differently. Riverside County deputies told campers to leave the La Quinta Walmart early Thursday, citing a La Quinta ordinance against sleeping in vehicles. Indio said it would be lenient; a police sergeant said campers "may need to seek refuge in parking lots."[^cp-kesq18] By the end of the day the cities had published a list of 12 temporary waiting sites, including a fashion mall, a Home Depot, two casinos and the campsites at Lake Cahuilla. La Quinta City Hall offered its south lot - 500-plus spots and restrooms, but no tents and no open flames. In the city of Coachella, the Village 29 campground took up to 1,000.[^cp-nbcla18]

By 2026 the waiting had a home. The Ralphs on Jefferson Street is the unofficial Day 0: the 1 a.m. party, the porta-potties, the decorated cars.[^cp-cvi26] One regular, KC, remembered their first year there, when police "couldn't stay here for longer than 30 minutes without them knocking on your windows" (reported by attendees).[^cp-cvi26] Officially, camping opens at 9 a.m. In practice, the Independent reported, security "usually lets people in around 3 a.m."[^cp-cvi26]

## The line

That "usually" matters. In 2025 it didn't happen.

On Thursday, April 10, 2025, campers waited in their cars for up to 12 hours or more, in a heat wave, with no restrooms.[^cp-patch25] One said she'd arrived at 3 a.m. and was still waiting near 11: "I've seen people pass out through heat exhaustion, it's been chaos."[^cp-kesq25a] Goldenvoice's head of public safety later told La Quinta's council that the usual midday lull never came, that not every checkpoint was fully staffed, and that, unlike some past years, nobody was let in before 9 a.m. The other cause was new: the first-ever Thursday night concert in the campground, which made everyone want to arrive early.[^cp-kesq25b] He apologized, and weekend 2 got 12 more toll plazas.[^cp-kesq25b] The full story is in [Getting there](/getting-there/).

## Day 0

That Thursday concert is the newest ritual, and and one Goldenvoice built on purpose.

"Day 0" is what campers call the Thursday they arrive; the Independent's Ralphs report was filed from "Day 0 of Coachella 2026."[^cp-cvi26] In 2025, Goldenvoice gave it a stage. Chris Lake played the first-ever Thursday night show inside the campgrounds on weekend 1, and on weekend 2 he was swapped for Disco Dom, Dombresky's disco project, at 8:30 p.m.[^cp-patchdz] It was the party that helped cause the 12-hour line.[^cp-kesq25b] Goldenvoice kept it anyway. For 2026, Disco Lines was announced as the weekend 1 Day Zero performer, an exclusive pre-festival set for campers, second year running.[^cp-ds26] Weekend 2 got KETTAMA, Prospa and Josh Baker playing back to back to back.[^cp-beatportal]

Around it, the Thursday campground now has a full program: DJs at Desert Sky from 8 p.m., a silent disco until 3 a.m., and stargazing on Coyote Hill through NASA-supplied telescopes.[^cp-amen] The name isn't Coachella's alone.[^cp-tulum]

The rest of the weekend has its own campground calendar. In 2026 that meant an activities tent, a recovery zone with massage and ice baths, a silent disco until 4 a.m. Friday to Sunday, a water balloon fight at noon on Sunday, and a *Euphoria* season 3 screening in the campgrounds at 11:59 p.m. on Sunday, April 12.[^cp-amen]

## Sunday Service on the Mountain

The campground's most famous morning was Easter Sunday, April 21, 2019, at 9 a.m.

Kanye West brought his Sunday Service to a spot in the campground dubbed "the Mountain." Paul Tollett told Billboard where the idea came from: "Why don't we try this in camping, since it's in the morning and all the other campers are already there?"[^cp-fader19] The hill was new. Tollett said the dirt went in on the Tuesday morning between weekends, and the sod on Thursday night and Friday. His older brother, Perry, designed the hidden loudspeakers.[^cp-lat19]

Variety described a circular mound beside a cordoned-off stretch of the campground. A robed choir sang on the slopes, with dancers on the surrounding hills, a harp, horns and at least five percussionists. West debuted "Water," and the set ran through "Jesus Walks," "Ultralight Beam," Kirk Franklin's "Brighter Day" and Stevie Wonder's "As." The merch read "Sunday Service at the Mountain."[^cp-variety19] The merch tent was called "Church Clothes."[^cp-world19]

:::lore The 50,000
The legend goes that "over 50,000" people climbed out of their tents for a 9 a.m. church service. That number comes from TMZ, citing unnamed Coachella production sources, the day after.[^cp-tmz19] No other count has turned up. On a hill built in four days, on Easter, with the campers already there, it doesn't need one.
:::

It hasn't been repeated. No other gospel or church service in the campgrounds has turned up, and the 2026 program lists none.[^cp-amen]

## Horses, guards and drones

The campground has its own police presence, some of it on horseback. In 2018, OC Weekly reported mounted officers patrolling the campgrounds. The agencies on site that year ran from Indio PD, in the lead, through Palm Springs, Cathedral City, Desert Hot Springs, Banning and Beaumont police, the Riverside County Sheriff, the CHP and the FBI. There were armed observation posts above the VIP area and the Outdoor Theatre, and drones over traffic.[^cp-ocwsec] Which agency the horses belonged to, the piece doesn't say.

Indio PD still flew drones for crowd monitoring in 2026.[^cp-kesq26] Inside the gates, in 2025, close to 1,000 Allied Universal guards worked around the clock, and Indio PD announced "zero tolerance."[^cp-nbc25sec] Weekend 1 of 2026 ended with 97 arrests, mostly for drug possession and fake IDs, among more than 200,000 attendees.[^cp-nbc26w1]

:::lore The 2007 riot
The legend goes that the campground rioted after Rage Against the Machine closed the 2007 festival. The source is one OC Weekly writer's 2018 history, built from attendee posts on the Coachella forum: helicopters overhead, SWAT nearby, a Taser.[^cp-ocw18] The next year, by the same account, brought heavy multi-agency patrols and a 2 a.m. curfew, which people mostly partied through.[^cp-ocw18] Research for this chapter found no police report on the night, and no second account of it. By 2014, the same writer says, the campground had gone much quieter at night.[^cp-ocw18]
:::

## The hot dogs

Outside the fence, the most local ritual is a bacon-wrapped hot dog at 2 a.m.

Local vendors used to sell along every path, at any hour: hot dogs, stacked 24-packs of water, shaded cooler stands. A Phoenix New Times piece from around 2016 tracked their decline. Once Madison and Monroe between Avenues 48 and 52 were closed to foot traffic, citing safety, the hot dog carts were pushed to the end of the night and to the northeast lots at the end of the Yellow Path.[^cp-pnt16] The water kids were mostly gone.[^cp-pnt16]

In 2025, they were still there, and still fast.

:::lore The storming
The legend goes that on April 13, 2025, as weekend 1 let out, the hot dog vendors "stormed" Coachella. TikTok videos show vendors racing toward the exit to set up for the crowd. One caption said they were "breaking down the fence," and that security threatened pepper spray. Another said they knocked down an entire wall. Distractify, which collected the videos, notes "the destruction claims aren't confirmed" (reported by attendees).[^cp-distractify] What the videos do show is a sprint, and a market.
:::

## Fences and wristbands

The festival learned to lock itself in the hard way.

In 2010, Goldenvoice dropped single-day tickets for the first time and sold only three-day passes, at $269, with wristbands meant to stop scalpers. All 75,000 sold.[^cp-bizbash10] Daily attendance ran about 75,000, up about 15,000 a day from 2009,[^cp-lvsun10] and reports from that year describe long holdups at the front gates and worries about gate-crashing.[^cp-lvsun10]

The response came in 2011: a wrought-iron fence around the Empire Polo grounds to stop gate crashers, an extra traffic lane partly paid for by Goldenvoice, and the end of exchanging a ticket for a wristband at the gate.[^cp-lvsun11] The wristband became the ticket.

Then it got a chip. By 2012, RFID wristbands from Intellitix handled access at both weekends, which a trade site said "eliminates counterfeit tickets," and let fans check in on social media automatically.[^cp-secureid12] The vendor itself said in 2014 that it had supplied Coachella's RFID for four years, which puts the start around 2010 or 2011.[^cp-intellitix] 2014 was the first year everyone had to register their wristband online.[^cp-intellitix]

Binding a wristband to its buyer opened a new hustle. As early as 2014, the CV Independent described the scam: a seller reports a delivered pass as "never received," gets a replacement, and sells the original, which then gets switched off.[^cp-cvi14] In 2026 it got a name. "Scamchella," NBC Palm Springs called it: sellers on Facebook Marketplace and OfferUp sold real wristbands, then reported them lost or stolen. One buyer, Ryan Jimenez, paid more than $2,000 for three. They worked on day one and were dead on day two. Hundreds lined up at will-call, unsure their passes would work.[^cp-scam26] He did get his money back.[^cp-scam26]

## The heat

The weather is why Coachella is in April at all. The first festival, in early October 1999, ran in "blazing triple-digit temperatures."[^cp-npr25] When it came back in 2001, it was one day, "scheduled for the milder weather of April."[^cp-npr25] Britannica says the move was partly about avoiding the heat.[^cp-britannica]

April is milder, not mild. JFK Memorial Hospital in Indio, the closest one, adds staff every year and sees heat illness alongside drug and alcohol cases. "Many of these emergency visits are preventable," its medical director said in 2026.[^cp-nbcjfk] The 2025 camping line happened in a heat wave.[^cp-patch25] And the desert has another trick: wind. It's what kept the campground shut for 18 hours in 2018.[^cp-cbs18]

## The neighbors

For the people who live next to the polo grounds, the rituals are economic. In 2016, OC Weekly talked to neighbors who rent out rooms, sheds and yard space at premium prices, and who say their houses shake from the bass. One said Goldenvoice wanted her block. Residents could get discounted tickets, but "nothing more."[^cp-ocw16]

Hotels across the valley run more than 90% full in April, which Expedia data says is the highest-rate month of the year.[^cp-hotelmgmt] Some luxury homes reportedly rent for "more than $100,000" for one weekend - an anecdote from homeowners and agents, not data.[^cp-nbcairbnb]

The camper, meanwhile, leaves on Monday morning with more than 40,000 others,[^cp-nbc0407] and by the next Thursday somebody else is waiting in a parking lot.
