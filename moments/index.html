07The moments

The short versionThe sets people still bring up, and what the paperwork says happened. A Prince cover that kept getting pulled offline, a dead rapper reflected off a sheet of foil, a homecoming with about 200 people on stage, an ice rink that outlasted its headliner, and a $2.5M concrete bunker for a band that never showed up.

Several of Coachella's best-known moments didn't happen quite the way people remember. The Tupac on the main stage in 2012 was a projection bounced off a sheet of foil.233 Frank Ocean's ice rink was built, and the first people to stand on it were Skrillex, Fred again.. and Four Tet, a week after Ocean had left the bill.234 In 2026 the poster promised "the bunker debut of Radiohead," and Radiohead stayed home.134 The festival is very good at making moments. This chapter is about what was underneath them.

Daft Punk's 2006 pyramid has a chapter of its own. Everything else is here.

Prince plays "Creep" (2008)

Prince headlined the second night, April 26, 2008. Somewhere in the set he played an eight-minute cover of Radiohead's "Creep," and he brought out Sheila E. for "The Glamorous Life."235

Fan videos of "Creep" were online by the next day, and Prince's team had them taken down.235 Since the song was Radiohead's, Radiohead had a view on this. Thom Yorke's, as reported by Consequence:

"Really? He's blocked it? Well, tell him to unblock it. It's our … song."236

Seven years later Prince let it back up. In December 2015 a new upload appeared with Radiohead's blessing, and Prince tweeted a link to it.235 On December 16, 2015, it was taken down again, this time after a claim from NPG Music Publishing, which is Prince's own publishing company.236 So the Prince side won both rounds, the second one against itself.

The hologram that wasn't (2012)

On April 15, 2012, during Dr. Dre and Snoop Dogg's headline set, Tupac Shakur appeared on stage. Dre had come up with it and paid for it. "It was Dre's idea to bring Tupac back," said Nick Smith of AV Concepts, the San Diego company that projected it.233

Everybody called it a hologram. It wasn't one. It was Pepper's ghost, a 19th-century theater illusion: you project an image onto an angled transparent surface, and from the audience it looks like a person standing in the room.237 Victorian theaters did it with glass. AV Concepts used Mylar foil.233 Hypebeast named the UK firm Musion and its "Eyeliner" system as the technology underneath.237

The digital Tupac came from Digital Domain, James Cameron's visual-effects company. VFX supervisors Janelle Croshaw and Steve Preeg led a team of about 20 on it.233 Smith told MTV it took about four months.237

The lore · The $10 million Tupac

The legend goes that the Tupac cost $10 million. That figure went around in April 2012 and never came with a name attached.237 The only on-the-record number is Smith's, via MTV, and it's for a different thing: he wouldn't give the real cost, but said a comparable piece could run from $100,000 to more than $400,000.237 Nobody has published an invoice. The $10M lives on because it sounds like what a dead man on a stage ought to cost.

Then came the strange part. On September 11, 2012, five months after Coachella, Digital Domain Media Group filed for Chapter 11. It shut its operation in Port St. Lucie, Florida, cut about 300 of the staff there and kept about 20, and its CEO resigned.238 No source ties the bankruptcy to Tupac. It's just the order things happened in.

As for the Tupac, the digital asset "has been archived," Andscape reported in 2017, and only the estate has access.233 Dre has said it was "strictly for Coachella."233 It didn't stay strictly there for long, though. The next chapter follows it.

Beychella (2018)

Beyoncé was booked to headline in 2017. She withdrew on her doctors' advice while she was pregnant, and came back in 2018 as the first Black woman ever to headline Coachella.239 She played both Saturdays. The first was April 14.240 (GRAMMY.com gives "April 13 and 22," but those were a Friday and a Sunday.)

The show was built as an HBCU homecoming, with about 200 people on stage.239 Rehearsals ran four months for the band, then four more for the dancers.239 Fans named it Beychella.241

On YouTube, her weekend-one set peaked at about 458,000 people watching at once. That was the highest since the festival started streaming, according to TMZ's figures as reported by The FADER.240 A year later, on April 17, 2019, Netflix released Homecoming, a film of the set with a live album alongside.239 It won the Grammy for Best Music Film.242

It also raised the bar for every headliner after her. Ariana Grande's production designer told The Hollywood Reporter that after Beyoncé, the festival had turned into a "competition."243 TMZ's numbers, as The FADER reported them, had her peak about 75% above Lady Gaga's the year before, with 43.1 million live-stream views across 232 countries for the whole festival.240

Kanye, the dome and the hill (2019)

He had form. Kanye West's 2011 set came with a stone mural in the style of the Pergamon Altar, a troupe of ballerinas and a lift that was reportedly used on Michael Jackson's last tour. Complex, looking back ten years later, said nobody had ever done it like him.244

He was due to headline again in 2019. In January he walked away. He called the standard 40-by-60-foot stage "artistically limiting" and had asked, late in the day, for a custom dome. The festival didn't build it.245

He came back anyway, without a headline slot. On Easter Sunday, April 21, 2019, Sunday Service, until then an ultra-exclusive private gathering, went up on a hill at the festival at 9 a.m.246247 The "Church Clothes" merch went with it: sweatshirts from $165 to $225 and "Jesus Walks" socks at $50.248 The hill, the crowd, and the arguments over the size of the crowd all belong to the camping chapter.

Frank Ocean's rink (2023)

That year's headliners were Bad Bunny, BLACKPINK and Frank Ocean. It was the first time every headliner was nonwhite.249 Ocean closed the first Sunday, and it went wrong in several directions at once.

He came on about an hour late, in slippers.250 There was meant to be a segment with skaters on an ice rink, and it didn't happen. A source told Rolling Stone he had hurt his ankle in on-site rehearsals the week before, and the show was reworked at the last minute.250 Rolling Stone also found an LMU hockey player's Instagram Story showing nine men in skating gear, captioned "The Boys x Frank." That crew never performed.250 On stage, Ocean said he was there for his brother Ryan Breaux, who died in 2020.250

The YouTube stream was pulled. "Frank Ocean is not scheduled to appear on the Coachella livestream," YouTube posted, and nobody gave a reason. Björk's set came off the stream schedule the same weekend.251 On April 19 he withdrew from weekend two. His team said he had been hurt on the festival grounds the week before, and "on doctor's advice" couldn't perform, "due to two fractures and a sprain in his left leg."252 Blink-182, who had played their first reunion show with Tom DeLonge on the first Friday, took his slot.252253

According to Billboard, as reported by The FADER, Ocean was contracted for $8M across both weekends. His weekend-one production reportedly cost more than his $4M fee, the rink was the most expensive part of it, and the festival stood to lose "several million dollars."254 Goldenvoice went looking for someone else to use the rink. On the second Sunday, Skrillex, Fred again.. and Four Tet stood on it to close the night.234

The lore · The melted rink

Two stories went around that week. TMZ's, as Stereogum summarized it: Ocean hurt himself in an accident involving bikes on the polo grounds, doctors advised changing the set, and the skating went. Stereogum adds that about 100 LA-based hockey players had rehearsed on a raised rink at Paramount Studios and were told on arrival that the routine was off.255 The second came from TheFestiveOwl, an anonymous X account. Its version was that the rink was "constructed and ready to go," that Ocean dropped it at the last minute, and that Coachella had to "melt the entire ice rink" and rebuild the stage.255 Stereogum called the account unverified.255 The documented version is the injury his team described, and a rink that was still there a week later.252234

The bunker (2026)

The 2026 poster ended with a line nobody could explain: "The Bunker Debut of Radiohead Kid A Mnesia." Fans started guessing whether that meant Radiohead were playing.256

The permit file had the better answer. A permit issued by the City of Indio assessed the structure at $2.5M.132 And Stereogum spelled it out: "That's not a stealth Radiohead concert."134

What got built was Radiohead's Motion Picture House: about 17,000 square feet with 38-foot ceilings, under the Empire Polo Fields, open to anyone with a festival wristband.257 Inside were the KID A MNESIA film and galleries of full-scale Thom Yorke and Stanley Donwood artwork from the Kid A and Amnesiac sessions.257 The sound was a "6-point surround" mix built from the original multitracks, and Sean Evans directed the film.134

It had a long run-up. KID A MNESIA first came out in 2021 as a free digital exhibition for PS5, PC and Mac.134 A physical 20th-anniversary installation planned that year was cancelled over logistics and COVID.256 The desert finally got it built, five years late and underground.

Getting in cost nothing but planning. Visits were booked as free reservations in the festival's app, and the core was the 75-minute film built from thousands of Yorke and Donwood sketches, paintings, collages, recordings and notes.258 Yorke's own description of what it's about:

"a monster trapped in a derelict museum of the lost and forgotten."259

It was air-conditioned, which in Indio in April may have been the bigger draw.177

So Radiohead made the 2026 poster without playing a note. Ed O'Brien did tell reporters the band plans to play 20 shows a year from 2027.134 After the festival the Motion Picture House went on tour: Brooklyn from May 6, Mexico City from October 27 and San Francisco's Palace of Fine Arts from January 14, 2027.260 The film runs 75 minutes, and visits are booked in two-hour slots. Stereogum also listed a Chicago run at Cinespace in the summer, which the official site doesn't show.260134 The official FAQ says "the Coachella Bunker experience" was different in some ways from the touring version.260 It wasn't the first artist-built world on the grounds, either. In 2025 Travis Scott had the Village by Cactus Jack, which Time Out called "a sort of sculpture park on the festival grounds."261

Other nights people bring up

Bauhaus, upside down, 2005. It was the band's first show together since 1998. Peter Murphy opened with the nine-minute "Bela Lugosi's Dead," sung while hanging upside down over the stage like a bat.262 When it was over he told the crowd: "Now you can say that you were there!"262 The reunion bought them a tour opening for Nine Inch Nails and a 2008 album, Go Away White, and then they split again.262

Madonna and Drake, 2015. On April 12 Madonna joined Drake's headline set for "Human Nature," "Hung Up" and "Bitch I'm Madonna," then kissed him for a long time. His recoil is the clip everyone remembers.263 His Instagram caption afterward: "Moments to write home about."263

Lady Gaga, twice. In 2017 she took the headline slot Beyoncé had given up. On April 15 she debuted "The Cure" live and put it out on Spotify, iTunes and Tidal that night. It was an early case of a festival set doubling as a release.264 In 2025 she came back to open the Mayhem live show: a "demonic opera house" set, four acts and a finale, the first live "Garden of Eden," with Gesaffelstein on synths.265 BrooklynVegan's reviewer thought it was built more for the livestream and the screens than for the field.265

Ariana Grande and most of \*NSYNC, 2019. On April 14, Joey Fatone, JC Chasez, Chris Kirkpatrick and Lance Bass joined her for "Tearin' Up My Heart" and "Break Up With Your Girlfriend." She sang Justin Timberlake's parts herself.266 Nicki Minaj, Diddy and Mase turned up too. Grande told the crowd: "I've been rehearsing my whole f---ing life for this moment."266

Harry Styles and Shania Twain, 2022. On the first Friday, Styles brought out Twain for "Man! I Feel Like a Woman!" and "You're Still the One." The same set was the first live performance of "As It Was."267 His introduction: "This lady taught me to sing. She also told me that men are trash."267

Odd Future in the Sahara, 2011. On April 15, 2011, the LA collective played the Sahara tent, and Pharrell came out near the end.268 The LA Times later called it a "ramshackle set" of barely half an hour that "yielded two headline-caliber artists," Tyler, the Creator and a singer named Frank Ocean, and boosted Syd and, later, Steve Lacy.95 Twelve years later Ocean was the one closing the main stage.

deadmau5, last call, 2025. He was one of the Sahara's early big nights, on a Friday in 2010 opposite Jay-Z.269 Fifteen years later, on April 18, 2025, his back-to-back set with ZHU was cut short while he was visibly drunk; he apologized and posted: "Probably my last Coachella."270

Justin Bieber, 2026. Bieber went from cameo regular (with Ariana Grande in 2019, with Daniel Caesar and Giveon in 2022) to Saturday headliner, on a deal he negotiated himself. Rolling Stone's Canadian edition thought the crowd might have been "the biggest ever" at Coachella, and also that "Bieber missed the mark": a minimal set heavy on nostalgia, with people leaving during the acoustic deep cuts.271

Sabrinawood, 2026. Two years after telling the crowd "see you back here when I headline," Sabrina Carpenter did.272 Her first Friday was a tour of fake Hollywood, under a giant "Sabrinawood" sign: Sam Elliott as a traffic cop in the opening film, dancers dressed as poodles for "Manchild," Will Ferrell as an electrician, and the voice of Samuel L. Jackson.273 Susan Sarandon played an older Carpenter in a monologue that ran nearly seven minutes. For weekend two the role went to Geena Davis, Sarandon's Thelma & Louise co-star, in a shorter cut, and Terry Crews took Ferrell's part.274 The same firm, Stufish, designed both Carpenter's show and Bieber's bare one the next night.151

Karol G, 2026. On the first Sunday Karol G opened with "Latina Foreva," brought out an all-female mariachi band, Becky G and Wisin, and premiered a song with Greg González of Cigarettes After Sex.275 She told the crowd who she was:

"I am Carolina Giraldo from Medellin, Colombia. And today, I am the first Latina woman to headline Coachella."276

Wallpaper's critic noted a cave-painting creation myth, a parrot-shaped float and a shallow pool built in the middle of the crowd.151 A week later she added Peso Pluma, J Balvin and Ryan Castro, and ended with "Nos Vamos de Tour" on the screen.277

The Strokes, 2026. On the first Saturday Julian Casablancas joked about the military draft. On the second, the band played "Oblivius" for the first time in a decade over a video accusing the CIA of political killings, which ended on footage of bombings in Gaza and Iran. The festival's YouTube stream showed it in full.278

The surprise slot changed hands too. Jack White, who had headlined 11 years earlier, was added for 45 minutes at 3 p.m. in the Mojave on the first Saturday.258 On the second, the same slot went to Kacey Musgraves.177

Anyma and the wind, 2026. Anyma was due to follow Sabrina Carpenter at midnight on the first Friday. The festival called it off: "Due to strong wind conditions affecting Anyma's stage build, he is unable to perform tonight."279 The same winds brought down a light fixture during John Summit's Do LaB set, injuring a festivalgoer, and that stage was closed.279 By Sunday the Do LaB had a new Instagram post: "Let's end at the beginning. Anyma b2b Marlon Hoffstadt close out the Do LaB at 10:45pm tonight."280 His full ÆDEN show moved to weekend two.279 On a site that stages a different spectacle every year, the weather got a credit.

Weekend two gets the guests (2026)

For most of the two-weekend era, weekend two was the sequel. Surprise guests were part of the house style, and the famous ones above (Madonna in 2015, \*NSYNC in 2019, Shania Twain in 2022) all came on the first weekend. In 2026 almost all of the big ones landed a week later. Madonna joined Sabrina Carpenter for three songs, timed to her new single. SZA and Sexyy Red came out for Justin Bieber, and Bieber serenaded Billie Eilish. Olivia Rodrigo joined Addison Rae, and Snoop Dogg joined Giveon.281

Up close: Madonna rose out of the stage during Carpenter's "Juno," as it turned into "Vogue." Then came an unreleased song, then "Like a Prayer." She told the crowd it was "20 years ago today" that she had played the dance tent, in the same boots and corsets: "it's like a full circle moment."282 It was close. The 2006 set was on April 30.283 Bieber kept the laptop bit but brought Billie Eilish for "One Less Lonely Girl," SZA for "Snooze," Sexyy Red, Dijon and Big Sean, who did two songs Bieber hadn't sung in public since 2017.284 And Anyma finally got his main-stage ÆDEN show, with Lisa of BLACKPINK, Muse's Matt Bellamy, Swae Lee and Joji.285

Variety's Jem Aswad asked the industry why. One agent said weekend one "is driven heavily by influencer culture." Another insider put it as: "Weekend two is always better... more to see and less to be seen."281 Others said guests on weekend two protect a headliner's first-weekend spotlight. Sources said it was not a plan coordinated by Goldenvoice or YouTube.281 The festival has two identical weekends on paper. In practice they've started to split: weekend one for being seen, weekend two for whoever turns up.

By the numbers · The stream, 2025-2026

  • 2025: six stages live, plus the Quasar stage in vertical video for Shorts, and the first "Watch With" streams, such as Lady Gaga alongside the creator Zach Campbell286287
  • More than half the views on the official channel came from outside the US, three years running, per YouTube in 2025287
  • 2025: more than half of livestream watch time came from TVs, per YouTube152
  • 2026: seven stages, the Coachella Stage, Outdoor Theatre and Sahara in 4K for the first time, plus a 24/7 "Coachella TV" channel152
  • 2026: "the most viewers watching at one time in the history of Coachella on YouTube," per YouTube, with no number given288
  • The last published peak is still Beyoncé's, about 458,000, in 2018240
